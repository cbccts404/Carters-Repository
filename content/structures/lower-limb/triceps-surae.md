name: Triceps surae and calcaneal tendon
type: muscle
region: lower-limb
subregion: Leg
taName: Musculus triceps surae; Tendo calcaneus
aka: [Gastrocnemius and soleus, Calf muscles, Achilles tendon]
summary: Gastrocnemius and soleus form the calf and insert via the Achilles tendon on the calcaneus to plantarflex the ankle. They carry the S1 ankle reflex; Achilles rupture and tendinopathy are common.
tags: [leg, ankle, sports-medicine]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Superficial posterior compartment of the leg (see [[leg-compartments]]).
  group: Posterior compartment of the leg, superficial layer
  origin: >-
    **Gastrocnemius**: medial head from above the medial femoral condyle; lateral head from the lateral femoral condyle
    of the [[femur]]. **Soleus**: soleal line and posterior upper [[tibia]] and the head and posterior upper
    [[fibula]]. (Plantaris: small muscle above the lateral condyle with a long thin tendon.)
  insertion: '**Calcaneal (Achilles) tendon** into the posterior surface of the calcaneus ([[bones-of-the-foot]]).'
  innervation: "[[tibial-nerve]] (S1–S2)"
  bloodSupply: Sural arteries (from the [[popliteal-artery]]) and the posterior tibial artery.
  action:
    - "**Plantarflexion** of the ankle (push-off in walking, running, jumping)"
    - "Gastrocnemius also **flexes the knee** (it crosses the knee); it is most effective with the knee extended"
    - "Soleus keeps the leg from falling forward when standing, and acts as a **venous pump** (soleal sinuses)"
  relations: The **soleal venous sinuses** within soleus are a common starting point for calf DVT (see [[deep-veins-of-lower-limb]]). The sural nerve and small saphenous vein run alongside the Achilles tendon.
clinical:
  - title: Achilles tendon rupture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Sudden forceful push-off or eccentric loading, typically a middle-aged **"weekend warrior"**; risk with **fluoroquinolones** and corticosteroids.
    presentation: >-
      A **"pop"** or sensation of being kicked in the heel, then weak push-off; a palpable gap about 2–6 cm above the
      insertion; **positive Thompson test**. Patients can often still plantarflex weakly (other flexors). Treated with
      functional bracing or surgical repair.
  - title: Achilles tendinopathy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Overuse (running), with midportion degeneration or insertional disease; fluoroquinolone association.
    presentation: Morning stiffness and pain in the tendon, a thickened tender tendon about 2–6 cm above the heel; treat with **eccentric calf strengthening**, avoid steroid injection into the tendon.
  - title: Medial gastrocnemius strain ("tennis leg")
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Sudden push-off with the knee extended.
    presentation: Sharp pain in the medial calf; distinguish from Achilles rupture (Thompson negative) and DVT (ultrasound).
pance:
  - "Pop at the heel + **positive Thompson test** (no plantarflexion with calf squeeze) → **Achilles rupture**."
  - "Ankle reflex = **S1** (tibial nerve)."
  - "Fluoroquinolones → tendinopathy and rupture."
  - "Achilles tendinopathy → eccentric strengthening; **no** steroid injection into the tendon."
imaging:
  ultrasound: Confirms and measures a tendon gap; shows tendinopathy (thickening, neovascularity).
  mri: Partial vs complete tears and chronic tendinopathy.
  xray: Usually normal; excludes calcaneal avulsion fracture.
  keyViews:
    - "Dynamic ultrasound of the Achilles tendon"
exam:
  landmarks: The Achilles tendon is palpable from the calcaneus to the calf; the two gastrocnemius heads form the bulge of the upper calf.
  palpation: Palpate along the tendon for a gap, nodularity or tenderness.
  testing: Single-leg heel raise; **ankle reflex (S1)**.
  specialTests:
    - name: Thompson (Simmonds) test
      technique: Prone with the feet over the edge of the table (or kneeling on a chair); squeeze the calf.
      positive: No passive plantarflexion of the foot.
      significance: Complete Achilles tendon rupture.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 42-year-old man playing tennis feels as though someone kicked him in the back of the ankle. He can walk with a
      limp. When his calf is squeezed while he lies prone, the foot does not move. What is the diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Gastrocnemius strain
      - Achilles tendon rupture
      - Ankle sprain
      - Calcaneal fracture
      - Deep vein thrombosis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The sensation of being kicked, a limp with weak push-off, and a **positive Thompson test** indicate **Achilles
      tendon rupture**. Weak active plantarflexion may persist through the long toe flexors and tibialis posterior.
  - stem: The ankle jerk reflex tests which nerve root?
    choices:
      - L3
      - L4
      - L5
      - S1
      - S3
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      The **ankle (Achilles) reflex** is mediated by **S1** via the tibial nerve to the triceps surae. The patellar reflex
      is L4; there is no reliable routine reflex for L5.
