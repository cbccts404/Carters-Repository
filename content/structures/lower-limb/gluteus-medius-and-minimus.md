---
name: Gluteus medius and minimus
type: muscle
region: lower-limb
subregion: Gluteal region
taName: Musculus gluteus medius; Musculus gluteus minimus
aka: [Hip abductors]
summary: Fan-shaped hip abductors that keep the pelvis level when standing on one leg. Weakness or superior gluteal nerve injury produces the Trendelenburg sign; their tendons cause lateral hip pain.
tags: [gluteal, hip, gait]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Deep to gluteus maximus (medius) and deep to medius (minimus), on the outer surface of the ilium.
  group: Gluteal region, intermediate and deep layers (abductors and medial rotators)
  origin: External surface of the ilium of the [[hip-bone]] — medius between the anterior and posterior gluteal lines; minimus between the anterior and inferior gluteal lines.
  insertion: '**Greater trochanter** of the [[femur]] — medius on its lateral surface; minimus on its anterior surface.'
  innervation: "**Superior gluteal nerve** (L4–S1), from the [[sacral-plexus]]"
  bloodSupply: Superior gluteal artery (from the [[internal-iliac-artery]]).
  action:
    - "**Abduction** of the hip"
    - "**Stabilize the pelvis** on the stance limb during walking, keeping the opposite side from dropping"
    - "Medial rotation of the hip (anterior fibers)"
  relations: The superior gluteal nerve and vessels run between the two muscles; tensor fasciae latae (same nerve) lies anteriorly.
clinical:
  - title: Trendelenburg sign and gait
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Weakness of gluteus medius and minimus — **superior gluteal nerve** injury (injection, hip surgery), **L5
      radiculopathy**, hip OA, DDH or a fractured greater trochanter.
    presentation: >-
      When standing on the affected leg, the **opposite side of the pelvis drops** (positive Trendelenburg sign).
      Walking, the patient lurches the trunk **toward the affected side** (compensated Trendelenburg/"waddling" gait
      if bilateral).
  - title: Gluteal tendinopathy (greater trochanteric pain syndrome)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Degeneration or tears of the medius/minimus tendons at the greater trochanter, often with bursitis; common in middle-aged women.
    presentation: Lateral hip pain, trochanteric tenderness, pain lying on the affected side or standing on one leg; treat with load management, physical therapy (abductor strengthening) and sometimes injection.
pance:
  - "Positive Trendelenburg = weak **gluteus medius/minimus** (superior gluteal nerve, L5) on the **stance** side; the **opposite** hip drops."
  - "Lateral hip pain with trochanteric tenderness → gluteal tendinopathy/bursitis."
imaging:
  mri: Gluteal tendon tears and tendinopathy; denervation edema after nerve injury.
  ultrasound: Tendinopathy, tears, bursitis; guides injection.
exam:
  landmarks: The muscles lie between the iliac crest and the greater trochanter on the lateral hip.
  palpation: Tenderness at the greater trochanter.
  testing: Resisted hip abduction in side-lying (hip slightly extended); observe gait.
  specialTests:
    - name: Trendelenburg test
      technique: The patient stands on the tested leg and lifts the other foot off the ground for about 30 seconds.
      positive: The pelvis drops on the side of the lifted leg (or the trunk leans toward the stance side).
      significance: Weak hip abductors on the stance side (superior gluteal nerve, L5 root, hip joint disease).
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      After an intramuscular injection into the right buttock, a patient's left hip drops whenever she stands on her
      right leg. Which muscle is weak?
    choices:
      - Right gluteus maximus
      - Right gluteus medius
      - Left gluteus medius
      - Right iliopsoas
      - Left adductor longus
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      When standing on the **right** leg, the **right gluteus medius and minimus** hold the pelvis level. If they are
      weak (right superior gluteal nerve injury), the unsupported **left** side drops.
  - stem: Which nerve root lesion most commonly weakens the hip abductors?
    choices:
      - L2
      - L3
      - L4
      - L5
      - S2
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      Hip abduction is mainly **L5** (superior gluteal nerve L4–S1). An L5 radiculopathy can cause a Trendelenburg sign
      along with weak great toe extension and ankle dorsiflexion.
flashcards:
  - front: Nerve to gluteus medius and minimus?
    back: Superior gluteal nerve (L4–S1)
  - front: Positive Trendelenburg — which side is weak?
    back: The stance side; the opposite hip drops
related: [gluteus-maximus, hip-joint, sacral-plexus]
