---
name: Sciatic nerve
type: nerve
region: lower-limb
subregion: Gluteal region
taName: Nervus ischiadicus
summary: The largest nerve in the body (L4–S3), running from the buttock down the back of the thigh and dividing into the tibial and common fibular nerves. It supplies the hamstrings and everything below the knee except a strip of skin on the medial leg.
tags: [gluteal, thigh, sciatica, nerve-injury]
systems: [neurologic, musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Gluteal region and posterior compartment of the thigh.
  roots: Ventral rami of **L4–S3**.
  origin: "[[sacral-plexus]], on the anterior surface of piriformis"
  course: >-
    Leaves the pelvis through the **greater sciatic foramen below [[piriformis]]**, descends deep to
    [[gluteus-maximus]] midway between the **greater trochanter and ischial tuberosity**, crosses the short lateral
    rotators, and runs down the posterior thigh deep to the long head of biceps femoris. It usually divides at the
    **apex of the [[popliteal-fossa]]** (the division level varies, and it may divide in the pelvis).
  branches:
    - "[[tibial-nerve]] (L4–S3; anterior divisions)"
    - "[[common-fibular-nerve]] (L4–S2; posterior divisions)"
    - "**Muscular branches in the thigh**: [[hamstrings]] and the hamstring part of adductor magnus (tibial division); short head of biceps femoris (common fibular division)"
  motor: The hamstrings and hamstring part of adductor magnus, and — through its branches — **all muscles of the leg and foot**.
  sensory: Through its branches, the skin of the leg and foot except the medial side (saphenous nerve).
  relations: The posterior femoral cutaneous nerve lies superficial to it in the buttock; the inferior gluteal vessels accompany it.
clinical:
  - title: Sciatica (lumbosacral radiculopathy)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Most often compression of the **L5 or S1** nerve root by a herniated disc (see [[lumbar-vertebrae]] and [[intervertebral-disc]]) — not the sciatic nerve itself.
    presentation: Pain radiating from the buttock down the back or side of the leg below the knee, often with paresthesias; **positive straight-leg raise**. Most improve with conservative care; red flags (cauda equina, progressive weakness) need urgent MRI.
  - title: Sciatic nerve injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Posterior hip dislocation** and acetabular fractures, hip arthroplasty, misplaced gluteal **intramuscular injections**, prolonged compression (e.g. in intoxication).'
    presentation: >-
      Weak knee flexion and **all muscles below the knee** (flail foot) with sensory loss below the knee except the
      medial leg; ankle reflex lost. The **common fibular division** is more vulnerable, so partial injuries often
      present as **foot drop**.
pance:
  - "Sciatica is usually an **L5/S1 root** problem → positive straight-leg raise."
  - "Posterior hip dislocation → sciatic (fibular division) injury → foot drop."
  - "Gluteal IM injections go in the **upper outer quadrant**."
  - "Sciatic nerve divides into **tibial** and **common fibular** nerves, usually at the popliteal fossa."
imaging:
  mri: Lumbar MRI for radiculopathy; MR neurography of the pelvis and thigh for nerve lesions, tumors and entrapment.
  ultrasound: Shows the nerve in the thigh; guides sciatic (popliteal or subgluteal) nerve blocks.
  keyViews:
    - "MRI lumbar spine (if red flags or persistent symptoms)"
exam:
  landmarks: The nerve enters the thigh at about the midpoint between the greater trochanter and the ischial tuberosity.
  testing: >-
    Knee flexion, ankle dorsiflexion (deep fibular), eversion (superficial fibular), plantarflexion and inversion
    (tibial), great toe extension (L5); ankle reflex (S1); sensation of the sole, dorsum and lateral leg.
  specialTests:
    - name: Straight-leg raise (Lasègue) test
      technique: Supine; raise the extended leg passively.
      positive: Radiating pain **below the knee** between about 30° and 70° of hip flexion (thigh tightness alone doesn't count).
      significance: Sensitive for L5/S1 radiculopathy; a **crossed** straight-leg raise (pain in the affected leg when the other leg is raised) is more specific.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A patient received an intramuscular injection into the lower medial part of the left buttock. He now has weakness
      of ankle dorsiflexion and eversion with numbness on the dorsum of the foot. Which nerve fibers were most likely
      injured?
    choices:
      - Superior gluteal nerve
      - Common fibular division of the sciatic nerve
      - Tibial division of the sciatic nerve
      - Femoral nerve
      - Obturator nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The sciatic nerve lies in the lower medial buttock. Its **common fibular division** is more vulnerable, so partial
      injuries cause **foot drop** and dorsal foot numbness. Injections belong in the upper outer quadrant.
  - stem: A crossed straight-leg raise test is positive. What is the significance?
    choices:
      - Highly specific for lumbar disc herniation
      - Diagnostic of hip osteoarthritis
      - Indicates hamstring strain
      - Indicates sacroiliitis
      - Rules out radiculopathy
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      Pain in the affected leg when the **opposite** leg is raised is **specific** for a herniated lumbar disc (usually a
      large central/axillary herniation); the ordinary SLR is sensitive but less specific.
