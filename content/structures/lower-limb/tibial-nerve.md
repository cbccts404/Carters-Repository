name: Tibial nerve
type: nerve
region: lower-limb
subregion: Leg
taName: Nervus tibialis
summary: Larger terminal branch of the sciatic nerve, running through the popliteal fossa and deep posterior leg to the sole. It supplies the plantarflexors and inverters and the skin of the sole, and is compressed in the tarsal tunnel.
tags: [leg, foot, nerve-injury]
systems: [neurologic, musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Center of the [[popliteal-fossa]], the deep posterior compartment of the leg, then behind the medial malleolus into the sole.
  roots: L4–S3.
  origin: "[[sciatic-nerve]], at the apex of the popliteal fossa"
  course: >-
    Descends vertically through the popliteal fossa (the most **superficial** of the three main structures there:
    nerve, vein, artery), passes deep to the soleus arch, runs in the deep posterior compartment with the posterior
    tibial vessels, and passes **behind the medial malleolus** under the flexor retinaculum (**tarsal tunnel**),
    dividing into the **medial and lateral plantar nerves**.
  branches:
    - "**Muscular**: gastrocnemius, soleus, plantaris, popliteus ([[triceps-surae]]); tibialis posterior, flexor digitorum longus, flexor hallucis longus"
    - "**Medial sural cutaneous nerve** → joins the fibular communicating branch to form the **sural nerve** (lateral foot and heel)"
    - "**Medial calcaneal branches** (heel)"
    - "**Medial and lateral plantar nerves**: intrinsic foot muscles and the skin of the sole"
  motor: '**Plantarflexion**, **inversion** (tibialis posterior), toe flexion and the intrinsic muscles of the sole; also knee flexion (gastrocnemius, popliteus).'
  sensory: Sole of the foot (plantar nerves), heel (calcaneal branches), lateral foot (sural nerve, with a fibular contribution).
  relations: 'Tarsal tunnel contents, anterior to posterior: tibialis posterior, flexor digitorum longus, posterior tibial artery and veins, **tibial nerve**, flexor hallucis longus ("Tom, Dick And Very Nervous Harry").'
clinical:
  - title: Tarsal tunnel syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Compression under the flexor retinaculum behind the medial malleolus (ganglia, varicosities, tenosynovitis, flat foot, trauma).
    presentation: Burning pain, tingling and numbness of the **sole and toes**, worse with standing, walking or at night; **Tinel sign behind the medial malleolus**; the heel may be spared (calcaneal branches arise proximally).
  - title: Tibial nerve injury
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Posterior knee dislocation, popliteal trauma, deep posterior compartment syndrome.
    presentation: Loss of plantarflexion and inversion, inability to stand on tiptoe, loss of the **ankle reflex**, numbness of the sole (risk of ulcers), a calcaneovalgus foot.
  - title: Sural nerve biopsy and grafts
    highYield: false
    mechanism: The sural nerve is purely sensory and easily found beside the small saphenous vein behind the lateral malleolus.
    presentation: Used for nerve biopsy (e.g. vasculitic neuropathy) and as a nerve graft; leaves a numb patch on the lateral foot.
pance:
  - "Tibial nerve: **plantarflexion + inversion**, ankle reflex (**S1**), sole sensation."
  - "Burning sole pain with Tinel sign behind the medial malleolus → **tarsal tunnel syndrome**."
  - "Can't stand on tiptoe → tibial nerve (or S1/Achilles) problem."
imaging:
  mri: Masses, ganglia or varicosities in the tarsal tunnel; MR neurography.
  ultrasound: Shows compressive lesions in the tarsal tunnel and the nerve in the popliteal fossa.
exam:
  landmarks: The tibial nerve and posterior tibial pulse lie midway between the medial malleolus and the Achilles tendon.
  palpation: Tinel sign over the tarsal tunnel.
  testing: Plantarflexion (tiptoe standing), inversion, toe flexion; **ankle reflex (S1)**; sensation of the sole and heel. Nerve conduction studies confirm tarsal tunnel syndrome.

      A 50-year-old runner has burning pain and numbness on the sole of his left foot, worse at night. Tapping behind
      the medial malleolus reproduces the symptoms. Which nerve is compressed?
    choices:
      - Deep fibular nerve
      - Sural nerve
      - Tibial nerve
      - Saphenous nerve
      - Superficial fibular nerve
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Tarsal tunnel syndrome** is compression of the **tibial nerve** (or its plantar branches) under the flexor
      retinaculum behind the medial malleolus, causing burning and numbness of the sole with a positive Tinel sign.
  - stem: A lesion of the tibial nerve in the popliteal fossa would cause loss of which movement?
    choices:
      - Ankle dorsiflexion
      - Foot eversion
      - Foot inversion and plantarflexion
      - Knee extension
      - Hip abduction
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The tibial nerve supplies the posterior leg muscles — **plantarflexion** (triceps surae) and **inversion** (tibialis
      posterior) — and the sole. Dorsiflexion and eversion are fibular nerve functions.
  - stem: Which structure is the most posterior in the tarsal tunnel behind the medial malleolus?
    choices:
      - Tibialis posterior tendon
      - Flexor digitorum longus tendon
      - Posterior tibial artery
      - Tibial nerve
      - Flexor hallucis longus tendon
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      From anterior to posterior: **T**ibialis posterior, flexor **D**igitorum longus, posterior tibial **A**rtery and
      **V**eins, tibial **N**erve, flexor **H**allucis longus ("Tom, Dick And Very Nervous Harry").
  - stem: >-
      A purely sensory nerve, found beside the small saphenous vein behind the lateral malleolus, is commonly used for
      nerve biopsy. Which nerve is it?
    choices:
      - Saphenous nerve
      - Superficial fibular nerve
      - Sural nerve
      - Medial plantar nerve
      - Deep fibular nerve
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **sural nerve** (formed by the medial sural cutaneous branch of the tibial nerve and the fibular communicating
      branch) is purely sensory to the lateral foot and heel, so it is used for **biopsy** (e.g. vasculitic neuropathy)
      and as a nerve graft, leaving only a numb patch.
  - stem: In tarsal tunnel syndrome, sensation over the heel is often preserved. Why?
    choices:
      - The heel is supplied by the saphenous nerve
      - The medial calcaneal branches arise proximal to the tunnel
      - The sural nerve supplies the entire heel
      - The plantar nerves are purely motor
      - Heel skin has no sensory innervation
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **medial calcaneal branches** of the tibial nerve arise **before** it passes under the flexor retinaculum, so
      heel sensation can be spared while the sole and toes (medial and lateral plantar nerves) are numb.
