---
name: Piriformis
type: muscle
region: lower-limb
subregion: Gluteal region
taName: Musculus piriformis
summary: Pear-shaped lateral rotator of the hip and the key landmark of the gluteal region — structures leave the pelvis above or below it. It can irritate the sciatic nerve (piriformis syndrome).
tags: [gluteal, hip, sciatica]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Leaves the pelvis through the **greater sciatic foramen**, which it almost fills, deep to [[gluteus-maximus]].
  group: Gluteal region, deep layer (short lateral rotators)
  origin: Anterior (pelvic) surface of the [[sacrum]] (S2–S4 region) and the [[sacrotuberous-and-sacrospinous-ligaments|sacrotuberous ligament]].
  insertion: Upper border of the greater trochanter of the [[femur]].
  innervation: "Nerve to piriformis (S1–S2), from the [[sacral-plexus]]"
  bloodSupply: Superior and inferior gluteal arteries and the lateral sacral arteries.
  action:
    - "Lateral rotation of the extended hip"
    - "Abduction of the flexed hip"
    - "Holds the femoral head in the acetabulum"
  relations: >-
    **Above piriformis**: superior gluteal nerve and vessels. **Below piriformis**: [[sciatic-nerve]], inferior gluteal
    nerve and vessels, posterior femoral cutaneous nerve, [[pudendal-nerve]] and internal pudendal vessels, and the
    nerves to obturator internus and quadratus femoris. The sciatic nerve (or its common fibular division) passes
    through or above the muscle in a minority of people.
clinical:
  - title: Piriformis syndrome
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Irritation or compression of the sciatic nerve by piriformis (spasm, hypertrophy, anatomic variants, trauma); a diagnosis of exclusion.
    presentation: >-
      Deep buttock pain radiating down the back of the thigh, worse with **prolonged sitting** and hip flexion–adduction–
      internal rotation; tenderness over the muscle; no back pain or root signs. Treat with stretching and physical
      therapy; injection if refractory. {{verify: diagnostic criteria are debated}}
pance:
  - "Piriformis is the landmark of the gluteal region: superior gluteal vessels/nerve **above**, everything else **below**."
  - "Buttock pain worse with sitting, normal back exam → consider piriformis syndrome (exclude radiculopathy first)."
imaging:
  mri: Excludes lumbar disc herniation and shows piriformis asymmetry, variants or masses; MR neurography for the sciatic nerve.
  ultrasound: Guides injections.
exam:
  landmarks: Lies along a line from the upper sacrum (about 2 cm lateral to the midline at S2) to the top of the greater trochanter.
  palpation: Deep tenderness in the buttock along that line.
  specialTests:
    - name: FAIR test
      technique: Side-lying with the tested side up; the hip is flexed, adducted and internally rotated.
      positive: Buttock pain or sciatic symptoms.
      significance: Suggests piriformis syndrome (limited specificity).

    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      Only the **superior gluteal nerve and vessels** pass above piriformis; all other nerves and vessels leave the pelvis
      below it.
  - stem: What is the main action of piriformis with the hip extended?
    choices:

      - Lateral rotation

    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Piriformis is one of the short **lateral rotators** of the hip (with the obturators, gemelli and quadratus
      femoris). With the hip flexed beyond about 60–90°, it acts as an abductor (and its rotation reverses).
  - stem: Which structure leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen below piriformis in most people?
    choices:
      - Superior gluteal nerve
      - Obturator nerve
      - Femoral nerve

    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      Piriformis is the landmark of the gluteal region: only the superior gluteal nerve and vessels pass **above** it.
      The **sciatic nerve**, inferior gluteal nerve and vessels, posterior femoral cutaneous nerve and pudendal nerve
      pass **below** it.
