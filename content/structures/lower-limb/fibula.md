taName: Fibula
summary: Slender lateral bone of the leg that bears little weight but forms the lateral malleolus of the ankle. The common fibular nerve wraps around its neck, where fractures and pressure injure it.
tags: [leg, ankle, nerve-injury]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Lateral bone of the leg, parallel to the [[tibia]]; not part of the knee joint but forms the lateral side of the [[ankle-joint]].
  classification: Long bone
  features:
    - "**Head**: articulates with the lateral tibial condyle (proximal tibiofibular joint); attachment of biceps femoris and the fibular collateral ligament"
    - "**Neck**: the [[common-fibular-nerve]] winds around it — palpable and vulnerable"
    - "**Shaft**: slender, attachment of many leg muscles and the interosseous membrane"
    - "**Lateral malleolus**: extends lower than the medial malleolus; attachment of the [[lateral-ankle-ligaments]]; the fibularis tendons run in a groove behind it"
  ossification: Primary center in the shaft (8th fetal week); secondary centers in the distal end (first year) and head (about 3–4 years).
  relations: The distal tibiofibular **syndesmosis** (anterior and posterior tibiofibular ligaments, interosseous ligament) holds the fibula to the tibia at the ankle mortise.
clinical:
  - title: Ankle fractures (lateral malleolus)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Twisting (usually supination–external rotation) injuries; Weber classification by level of the fibular fracture relative to the syndesmosis (A below, B at, C above).
    presentation: Lateral ankle pain, swelling and bony tenderness at the posterior edge or tip of the lateral malleolus (Ottawa rules). Weber C and unstable (bimalleolar, widened medial clear space) fractures need fixation.
  - title: Maisonneuve fracture
    highYield: false
    mechanism: External rotation injury tearing the deltoid ligament or fracturing the medial malleolus, disrupting the syndesmosis, and fracturing the **proximal fibula**.
    presentation: Medial ankle pain with proximal fibular tenderness — always palpate the whole fibula in an ankle injury.
  - title: Fibular neck fracture and common fibular nerve injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Direct blow to the lateral knee, fracture, a tight cast, prolonged leg crossing or squatting.
    presentation: '**Foot drop** (weak dorsiflexion and eversion) with numbness of the dorsum of the foot and lateral leg — see [[common-fibular-nerve]].'
  - title: Free fibula flap
    highYield: false
    mechanism: The middle fibular shaft can be harvested with the fibular artery for reconstruction (e.g. of the mandible) because the fibula is not weight-bearing.
    presentation: The distal fibula is preserved to keep the ankle stable.
pance:
  - "Lateral malleolus tenderness → **Ottawa ankle rules** for X-ray."
  - "Medial ankle injury + **proximal fibular** tenderness → Maisonneuve fracture."
  - "Blow to the fibular neck → **foot drop** (common fibular nerve)."
imaging:
  xray: Ankle AP, lateral and mortise views (Weber level, medial clear space, syndesmotic widening); full-length tibia/fibula when the proximal fibula is tender.
  ct: Complex ankle fractures and syndesmotic injury.
  mri: Syndesmotic ligament injury (high ankle sprain) and occult fractures.
  keyViews:
    - "Ankle mortise view (15–20° internal rotation)"
exam:
  landmarks: The fibular head is palpable on the lateral knee at about the level of the tibial tuberosity; the neck just below it; the lateral malleolus at the ankle.
  palpation: Palpate the whole length of the fibula after an ankle injury.
  specialTests:
    - name: Squeeze test
      technique: Compress the tibia and fibula together at mid-calf.
      positive: Pain at the distal tibiofibular syndesmosis.
      significance: Suggests a syndesmotic (high ankle) sprain.
