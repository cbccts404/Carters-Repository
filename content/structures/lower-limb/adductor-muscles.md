---
name: Adductor muscles of the thigh
type: muscle
region: lower-limb
subregion: Thigh
taName: Musculi adductores
aka: [Hip adductors, Medial thigh muscles, Groin muscles]
summary: Medial compartment muscles — adductor longus, brevis and magnus, gracilis, pectineus and obturator externus — that adduct the hip. They are supplied mostly by the obturator nerve, and adductor strains are the classic "groin pull".
tags: [thigh, groin-pain, obturator-nerve]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Medial compartment of the thigh.
  group: Medial (adductor) compartment of the thigh
  origin: >-
    Body and inferior ramus of the pubis and the ischiopubic ramus of the [[hip-bone]]; the hamstring part of adductor
    magnus from the **ischial tuberosity**.
  insertion: >-
    The linea aspera and pectineal line of the [[femur]]; the hamstring part of adductor magnus to the **adductor
    tubercle**; gracilis to the upper medial tibia (pes anserinus).
  innervation: "[[obturator-nerve]] (L2–L4) for most; **pectineus** mainly by the [[femoral-nerve]]; the hamstring part of **adductor magnus** by the tibial division of the [[sciatic-nerve]]"
  bloodSupply: Obturator artery and branches of the deep femoral and medial circumflex femoral arteries.
  action:
    - "**Adduction** of the hip"
    - "Assist hip flexion (longus, brevis, pectineus) or extension (hamstring part of magnus)"
    - "Stabilize the pelvis and control lateral sway during gait"
  relations: Adductor longus forms the medial border of the [[femoral-triangle]]; the **adductor hiatus** in adductor magnus lets the femoral vessels pass into the [[popliteal-fossa]].
clinical:
  - title: Adductor (groin) strain
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Forceful abduction or eccentric contraction in kicking, skating and change-of-direction sports; adductor longus is most often injured.
    presentation: Medial groin pain and tenderness near the pubic origin, pain on resisted adduction (squeeze test). Distinguish from hip joint pathology, osteitis pubis, inguinal hernia and athletic pubalgia.
  - title: Obturator nerve injury
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Pelvic surgery, pelvic fracture, childbirth, obturator hernia.
    presentation: Weak adduction, a wide-based gait (circumducted), and numbness of a small area of the medial thigh.
pance:
  - "Groin pull in hockey/soccer players = **adductor longus** strain."
  - "Adductors = **obturator nerve** (except pectineus — femoral; hamstring part of magnus — tibial)."
imaging:
  mri: Adductor strains, avulsions and associated pubic bone marrow edema.
  ultrasound: Tendon and muscle injury.
exam:
  landmarks: The adductor longus tendon is the prominent cord in the upper medial thigh when the hip is resisted in adduction; it leads to the pubic tubercle region.
  palpation: Tenderness at the adductor longus origin.
  testing: Resisted adduction (squeeze test with knees flexed), passive abduction stretch.
quiz:
  - stem: Which nerve supplies most of the adductor muscles?
    choices:
      - Femoral nerve
      - Obturator nerve
      - Sciatic nerve
      - Superior gluteal nerve
      - Genitofemoral nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **obturator nerve** (L2–L4) supplies the medial compartment. Pectineus is usually supplied by the femoral
      nerve, and the hamstring part of adductor magnus by the tibial division of the sciatic nerve.
  - stem: Which muscle forms the medial border of the femoral triangle?
    choices:
      - Sartorius
      - Adductor longus
      - Pectineus
      - Gracilis
      - Iliopsoas
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The femoral triangle is bounded by the inguinal ligament (superior), **sartorius** (lateral) and the medial border
      of **adductor longus** (medial).
  - stem: Through which opening do the femoral vessels pass from the thigh into the popliteal fossa?
    choices:
      - Obturator canal
      - Saphenous opening
      - Greater sciatic foramen
      - Adductor hiatus in adductor magnus
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      The **adductor hiatus** in adductor magnus lets the femoral vessels pass into the [[popliteal-fossa]], where the
      femoral artery becomes the popliteal artery.
