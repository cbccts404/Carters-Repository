---
name: Femoral nerve
type: nerve
region: lower-limb
subregion: Thigh
taName: Nervus femoralis
summary: Largest branch of the lumbar plexus (L2–L4), supplying the hip flexors and knee extensors and the skin of the anterior thigh and medial leg. Lesions cause knee buckling and a lost patellar reflex.
tags: [thigh, lumbar-plexus, nerve-injury]
systems: [neurologic, musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Anterior thigh; enters the thigh under the inguinal ligament lateral to the femoral artery.
  roots: Posterior divisions of the ventral rami of **L2–L4**.
  origin: "[[lumbar-plexus]], within [[psoas-major]]"
  course: >-
    Emerges from the lateral border of psoas, runs between psoas and iliacus (supplying iliacus), passes **under the
    inguinal ligament** lateral to the femoral sheath (it is **not** in the sheath) into the [[femoral-triangle]], and
    divides after a few centimeters into anterior and posterior branches.
  branches:
    - "**Muscular**: iliacus, pectineus, sartorius and [[quadriceps-femoris]]"
    - "**Anterior cutaneous nerves of the thigh**"
    - "**Saphenous nerve**: the longest cutaneous branch; runs through the adductor canal, emerges at the medial knee, and runs with the great saphenous vein to the medial leg and medial foot"
    - "Articular branches to the hip and knee"
  motor: Hip flexion (iliacus, sartorius, rectus femoris) and **knee extension** (quadriceps).
  sensory: Anterior and medial thigh; **medial leg and medial foot** (saphenous).
  relations: 'Order in the femoral triangle from lateral to medial: **N**erve, **A**rtery, **V**ein, (**E**mpty space), **L**ymphatics — **NAVEL**.'
clinical:
  - title: Femoral neuropathy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      **Retroperitoneal/iliopsoas hematoma** (anticoagulation, hemophilia), pelvic or hip surgery (retractor
      compression, lithotomy positioning), **diabetic amyotrophy**, femoral artery catheterization.
    presentation: >-
      Weak knee extension (**knee buckles**, trouble with stairs), weak hip flexion (if the lesion is proximal),
      **absent/reduced patellar reflex**, and numbness of the anterior thigh and medial leg. **Hip adduction is
      preserved** — weakness there suggests an L3/L4 root or plexus lesion instead.
  - title: Saphenous nerve injury
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Great saphenous vein harvest or stripping, knee arthroscopy or surgery.
    presentation: Numbness or pain of the medial leg and foot without weakness.
  - title: Femoral nerve block
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Local anesthetic injected lateral to the femoral artery below the inguinal ligament (ultrasound guided).
    presentation: Analgesia for hip fractures and knee surgery; causes temporary quadriceps weakness (fall risk).
pance:
  - "Femoral nerve: **L2–L4**, knee extension, **patellar reflex (L4)**."
  - "Anticoagulated patient + groin/flank pain + knee buckling → **iliopsoas hematoma** compressing the femoral nerve."
  - "Weak adduction too → think **L3–L4 root**/plexus, not isolated femoral nerve."
  - "NAVEL — lateral to medial in the femoral triangle."
imaging:
  ct: CT abdomen/pelvis for retroperitoneal or iliopsoas hematoma.
  mri: Lumbosacral plexus/MR neurography; lumbar spine MRI to exclude L3/L4 radiculopathy.
  ultrasound: Identifies the nerve lateral to the femoral artery for blocks; shows groin hematomas and pseudoaneurysms after catheterization.
exam:
  landmarks: Just below the midpoint of the inguinal ligament, about 1 cm lateral to the femoral pulse.
  testing: Resisted knee extension, hip flexion; patellar reflex (L4); sensation over the anterior thigh and medial leg (saphenous). Compare hip adduction (obturator) to localize.
  specialTests:
    - name: Femoral nerve stretch test (reverse straight-leg raise)
      technique: Prone; flex the knee and extend the hip.
      positive: Pain in the anterior thigh.
      significance: Suggests upper lumbar (L2–L4) root irritation.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 66-year-old man on warfarin with an INR of 5.8 has right groin and flank pain. His right knee gives way when
      walking, the right patellar reflex is absent, and he has numbness over the anterior thigh. Hip adduction is
      normal. What is the most likely cause?
    choices:
      - L5 radiculopathy
      - Iliopsoas hematoma compressing the femoral nerve
      - Obturator nerve injury
      - Meralgia paresthetica
      - Common fibular nerve palsy
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Supratherapeutic anticoagulation can cause a **retroperitoneal/iliopsoas hematoma** that compresses the
      **femoral nerve** — quadriceps weakness, lost patellar reflex and anterior thigh numbness, with **normal
      adduction** (obturator nerve spared). Confirm with CT.
  - stem: Which structure in the femoral triangle lies outside the femoral sheath?
    choices:
      - Femoral artery
      - Femoral vein
      - Femoral nerve
      - Deep inguinal lymph nodes
      - Femoral branch of the genitofemoral nerve
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **femoral nerve** lies lateral to and **outside** the femoral sheath, which contains the femoral artery, vein
      and canal (lymphatics); the femoral branch of the genitofemoral nerve does pierce the sheath's lateral part.
