name: Popliteal fossa
type: space

subregion: Knee & popliteal fossa
taName: Fossa poplitea
summary: Diamond-shaped space behind the knee containing the popliteal vessels, the tibial and common fibular nerves and lymph nodes. Baker cysts, popliteal aneurysms and knee-dislocation vascular injury arise here.
tags: [knee, vascular]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Posterior to the [[knee-joint]].
  boundaries:
    superolateral: Biceps femoris ([[hamstrings]])
    superomedial: Semimembranosus and semitendinosus
    inferolateral: Lateral head of gastrocnemius (and plantaris)
    inferomedial: Medial head of gastrocnemius
    roof: Skin, superficial fascia (with the small saphenous vein) and the popliteal fascia
    floor: Popliteal surface of the femur, the posterior knee capsule (oblique popliteal ligament) and popliteus
  contents:
    - "**Superficial to deep**: [[tibial-nerve]] → **popliteal vein** → [[popliteal-artery]] (deepest)"
    - "[[common-fibular-nerve]] along the lateral border (medial edge of biceps femoris)"
    - "Termination of the **small saphenous vein** into the popliteal vein"
    - "Popliteal lymph nodes"
    - "Fat and bursae (e.g. semimembranosus–gastrocnemius bursa → Baker cyst)"
  communications: Above with the adductor canal (via the adductor hiatus); below with the posterior compartment of the leg (under the soleal arch).
clinical:
  - title: Popliteal (Baker) cyst
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Distension of the gastrocnemius–semimembranosus bursa by knee effusions (OA, RA, meniscal tears).
    presentation: Posteromedial swelling behind the knee, more prominent with the knee extended; **rupture mimics DVT** (calf pain, swelling) — ultrasound distinguishes them.
  - title: Popliteal artery aneurysm and injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: See [[popliteal-artery]] — atherosclerotic aneurysm or tethered-vessel injury in knee dislocation.
    presentation: Pulsatile mass or distal ischemia; check the ABI after knee dislocation.
  - title: Popliteal fossa masses
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Cysts, aneurysms, lymphadenopathy (foot/heel infections), nerve sheath tumors, sarcomas.
    presentation: A non-pulsatile firm mass that isn't a simple cyst on ultrasound needs further imaging (MRI).
pance:
  - "Popliteal fossa superficial → deep: **nerve, vein, artery**."
  - "Posteromedial swelling behind the arthritic knee → **Baker cyst**; rupture mimics DVT."
  - "Pulsatile popliteal mass → **aneurysm** (check the aorta and other knee)."
imaging:
  ultrasound: First-line for popliteal masses (cyst vs aneurysm vs DVT).
  mri: Defines masses, cysts and their communication with the joint.
  keyViews:
    - "Ultrasound of the popliteal fossa"
exam:
  landmarks: With the knee slightly flexed, the hamstring tendons form the upper borders (biceps laterally; semitendinosus medially) and the gastrocnemius heads the lower.
  palpation: Palpate with the knee flexed about 30° (relaxed fascia) for the popliteal pulse and masses; a Baker cyst is firmer in extension and softer in flexion (Foucher sign).
quiz:
  - stem: Which structure is the deepest in the popliteal fossa?
    choices:
      - Tibial nerve
      - Popliteal vein
      - Popliteal artery
      - Common fibular nerve
      - Small saphenous vein
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      From superficial to deep: **tibial nerve, popliteal vein, popliteal artery**. The artery lies against the femur and
      knee capsule, which is why it is injured in supracondylar fractures and knee dislocations.
  - stem: Which muscle forms the superolateral border of the popliteal fossa?
    choices:
      - Semimembranosus
      - Biceps femoris
      - Lateral head of gastrocnemius
      - Popliteus
      - Sartorius
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Biceps femoris** forms the superolateral border; semimembranosus and semitendinosus the superomedial; the two
      heads of gastrocnemius the inferior borders.
