name: Hamstrings
type: muscle
region: lower-limb
subregion: Thigh
taName: Musculus biceps femoris; Musculus semitendinosus; Musculus semimembranosus
aka: [Hamstring muscles]
summary: Posterior thigh muscles — biceps femoris, semitendinosus and semimembranosus — that extend the hip and flex the knee. Hamstring strains are common in sprinting sports.
tags: [thigh, sports-medicine, sciatic-nerve]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Posterior compartment of the thigh.
  group: Posterior compartment of the thigh
  origin: >-
    **Ischial tuberosity** of the [[hip-bone]] (long head of biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus). Short
    head of biceps femoris: lateral lip of the linea aspera of the [[femur]] (so not a "true" hamstring).
  insertion: >-
    **Biceps femoris**: head of the [[fibula]]. **Semitendinosus**: upper medial tibia as part of the **pes
    anserinus** (with sartorius and gracilis). **Semimembranosus**: posterior medial tibial condyle.
  innervation: "[[sciatic-nerve]]: **tibial division** (L5–S2) for the long head of biceps, semitendinosus and semimembranosus; **common fibular division** for the short head of biceps femoris"
  bloodSupply: Perforating branches of the deep femoral artery, and the inferior gluteal artery.
  action:
    - "**Extension of the hip** (except the short head of biceps) — important in walking"
    - "**Flexion of the knee**"
    - "With the knee flexed: biceps femoris rotates the leg laterally; semitendinosus and semimembranosus rotate it medially"
  relations: The sciatic nerve runs deep to the long head of biceps femoris. At the knee, the hamstring tendons form the upper borders of the [[popliteal-fossa]] (biceps laterally; semitendinosus and semimembranosus medially).
clinical:
  - title: Hamstring strain
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Eccentric overload during **sprinting** or kicking, usually at the musculotendinous junction of biceps femoris.
    presentation: Sudden posterior thigh pain ("pulled hamstring") during running, bruising, pain with resisted knee flexion and stretch. Most heal with rehabilitation (eccentric strengthening).
  - title: Proximal hamstring avulsion
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Forceful hip flexion with the knee extended (e.g. water-skiing, slipping into the splits); in adolescents, avulsion of the ischial tuberosity apophysis.
    presentation: A pop, large posterior thigh bruise, and weakness; complete avulsions with retraction usually need surgical repair.
pance:
  - "Sprinter with sudden posterior thigh pain → **hamstring strain** (biceps femoris)."
  - "Hamstrings = **tibial division** of the sciatic nerve (short head of biceps = common fibular)."
  - "Pes anserinus: **Sartorius, Gracilis, semiTendinosus** (\"Say Grace before Tea\")."
imaging:
  mri: Grades strains and detects proximal avulsions (retraction distance guides surgery).
  ultrasound: Hematoma and tendon continuity.
  xray: Ischial tuberosity avulsion fragments in adolescents.
exam:
  landmarks: The tendons are palpable behind the knee with resisted flexion — biceps femoris laterally, semitendinosus (cord-like) and semimembranosus medially.
  palpation: Localize tenderness along the posterior thigh and at the ischial tuberosity.
  testing: Resisted knee flexion prone; passive straight-leg raise (hamstring tightness vs neural tension).
quiz:
  - stem: A sprinter feels sudden sharp pain in the back of her thigh during a race. Which muscle is most commonly injured?
    choices:
      - Rectus femoris
      - Biceps femoris
      - Adductor longus
      - Gastrocnemius
      - Sartorius
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Biceps femoris** (long head) is the most commonly strained hamstring, typically at the musculotendinous junction
      during high-speed running.
  - stem: Which part of the hamstring group is supplied by the common fibular division of the sciatic nerve?
    choices:
      - Semitendinosus
      - Semimembranosus
      - Long head of biceps femoris
      - Short head of biceps femoris
      - Hamstring part of adductor magnus
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      The **short head of biceps femoris** is supplied by the **common fibular** division; all the other hamstrings
      (and the hamstring part of adductor magnus) are supplied by the tibial division.
  - stem: >-
      A water-skier's hip is forced into flexion with the knee extended. He feels a pop and develops a large bruise on
      the back of the thigh. From which bony attachment have the tendons most likely avulsed?
    choices:
      - Anterior superior iliac spine
      - Anterior inferior iliac spine
      - Ischial tuberosity
      - Greater trochanter
      - Lesser trochanter
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The hamstrings arise from the **ischial tuberosity**. Forceful hip flexion with the knee extended can cause a
      **proximal hamstring avulsion** (or, in adolescents, avulsion of the ischial apophysis). The ASIS and AIIS give
      origin to sartorius and rectus femoris.
  - stem: Which hamstring muscle inserts on the tibia as part of the pes anserinus?
    choices:
      - Semimembranosus
      - Biceps femoris (long head)
      - Biceps femoris (short head)
      - Semitendinosus
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      The pes anserinus is formed by **sartorius, gracilis and semitendinosus** ("Say Grace before Tea") on the upper
      medial tibia. Semimembranosus inserts on the posterior medial tibial condyle, and biceps femoris on the fibular
      head.
  - stem: With the knee flexed, which hamstring rotates the leg laterally?
    choices:
      - Biceps femoris
      - Semitendinosus
      - Semimembranosus
      - Gracilis
      - Sartorius
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      **Biceps femoris** (inserting on the fibular head) laterally rotates the flexed knee. Semitendinosus and
      semimembranosus, inserting medially, rotate it medially.
