---
name: Great saphenous vein
type: vein
region: lower-limb
subregion: Thigh
taName: Vena saphena magna
aka: [Long saphenous vein, GSV]
summary: The longest vein in the body, running superficially from the front of the medial malleolus to the groin. It is the usual source of varicose veins and a common graft for coronary bypass.
tags: [vascular, venous]
systems: [cardiovascular]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Superficial fascia of the medial leg and thigh.
  formation: From the **medial end of the dorsal venous arch** of the foot.
  course: >-
    Passes **anterior to the medial malleolus** (reliably, for cutdown access), ascends along the medial leg with the
    saphenous nerve, passes a hand's breadth behind the medial border of the patella, up the medial thigh, and through
    the **saphenous opening** in the fascia lata to join the femoral vein about 3–4 cm below and lateral to the pubic
    tubercle (the **saphenofemoral junction**).
  tributaries:
    - "Superficial veins of the medial leg and thigh, including accessory saphenous veins"
    - "Near the junction: superficial epigastric, superficial circumflex iliac and superficial external pudendal veins"
    - "Communications with the **small saphenous vein** and, through **perforating veins** with one-way valves, with the deep veins"
  drainage: Femoral vein at the saphenofemoral junction (see [[deep-veins-of-lower-limb]]).
  relations: The **saphenous nerve** runs with it below the knee (injury causes medial leg numbness). The small saphenous vein runs up the back of the calf to the popliteal vein.
clinical:
  - title: Varicose veins and chronic venous insufficiency
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Valve incompetence (often at the saphenofemoral junction) with reflux and venous hypertension; risk factors — age, female sex, pregnancy, obesity, prolonged standing, prior DVT.
    presentation: >-
      Dilated tortuous veins, aching and heaviness worse at the end of the day, ankle edema; advanced disease causes
      **hyperpigmentation, lipodermatosclerosis** and **venous ulcers above the medial malleolus**. Treat with
      compression and leg elevation; endovenous ablation or sclerotherapy for symptomatic reflux.
  - title: Superficial thrombophlebitis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Thrombosis of a superficial vein (varicosities, IV cannulas, pregnancy, malignancy).
    presentation: >-
      A tender, red, **palpable cord**. Ultrasound because about a quarter have associated DVT; thrombus **within about
      3 cm of the saphenofemoral junction** (or long segments) is treated with anticoagulation. {{verify: thresholds for anticoagulating superficial vein thrombosis}}
  - title: Saphenous vein graft
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Harvested for coronary artery bypass grafting and peripheral bypass (reversed so its valves don't block flow).
    presentation: Donor-leg complications include wound infection and saphenous nerve numbness.
pance:
  - "Venous ulcer: **above the medial malleolus**, shallow, irregular, with hyperpigmentation → compression."
  - "Arterial ulcer: distal (toes, lateral malleolus), punched out, painful."
  - "Superficial thrombophlebitis → ultrasound (DVT coexists); anticoagulate if near the saphenofemoral junction."
  - "GSV lies **anterior to the medial malleolus**."
imaging:
  ultrasound: '**Venous duplex ultrasound** with reflux testing (standing) maps incompetent valves and excludes DVT.'
  keyViews:
    - "Venous reflux duplex ultrasound (standing)"
exam:
  landmarks: Visible or palpable just in front of the medial malleolus; the saphenofemoral junction lies about 3–4 cm inferolateral to the pubic tubercle.
  palpation: Palpate for cords, tenderness and a cough impulse at the saphenofemoral junction (reflux).
  testing: Inspect the standing patient for varicosities, edema, skin changes and ulcers; compare with arterial pulses before compression therapy (check ABI).
  specialTests:
    - name: Trendelenburg (Brodie–Trendelenburg) test
      technique: Elevate the leg to empty the veins, apply a tourniquet around the upper thigh (or press on the saphenofemoral junction), then have the patient stand.
      positive: Rapid filling of varicosities from above when the tourniquet is released (saphenofemoral incompetence); filling with the tourniquet in place implies incompetent perforators below.
      significance: Localizes valvular incompetence (largely replaced by duplex ultrasound).
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 62-year-old woman who works as a cashier has a shallow, irregular ulcer above her left medial malleolus with
      surrounding brown pigmentation and ankle edema. Pedal pulses are normal. What is the first-line treatment?
    choices:
      - Surgical debridement and skin graft only
      - Compression therapy and leg elevation
      - Systemic antibiotics
      - Revascularization
      - Topical corticosteroids
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      This is a **venous stasis ulcer** from chronic venous insufficiency. **Compression** (after confirming adequate
      arterial flow) and elevation are the cornerstone; ablation of refluxing veins reduces recurrence.
  - stem: Where does the great saphenous vein end?
    choices:
      - Popliteal vein
      - Femoral vein at the saphenous opening
      - External iliac vein
      - Internal iliac vein
      - Small saphenous vein
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The great saphenous vein passes through the **saphenous opening** in the fascia lata to join the **femoral vein**
      in the femoral triangle. The small saphenous vein drains to the popliteal vein.
