aka: [Kneecap]
summary: The largest sesamoid bone, embedded in the quadriceps tendon in front of the knee. It increases the leverage of the quadriceps and is involved in patellofemoral pain, dislocation and fractures.
tags: [knee, sports-medicine]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: In front of the distal [[femur]], within the quadriceps tendon; articulates with the patellar surface of the femur.
  classification: Sesamoid bone (the largest in the body)
  features:
    - "**Base** (superior) receives the quadriceps tendon; **apex** (inferior) gives origin to the **patellar ligament** (to the tibial tuberosity)"
    - "**Articular (posterior) surface**: medial and lateral facets, the lateral larger; thick cartilage"
    - "Medial and lateral **retinacula** (expansions of the vasti) and the **medial patellofemoral ligament** stabilize it"
  ossification: Several centers appear at about 3–6 years and fuse into one bone; failure of fusion leaves a bipartite patella (usually superolateral), which can mimic a fracture.
  relations: The prepatellar bursa lies between the skin and the patella; the infrapatellar fat pad lies behind the patellar ligament.
  notes: >-
    The patella increases the moment arm of the [[quadriceps-femoris]]. The **Q angle** (ASIS → center of patella →
    tibial tuberosity) is larger in women; a large Q angle and a shallow trochlear groove predispose to **lateral**
    patellar dislocation.
clinical:
  - title: Patellofemoral pain syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Overload of the patellofemoral joint, often with maltracking, quadriceps/hip weakness; the most common cause of anterior knee pain in young athletes (especially women).
    presentation: Diffuse anterior knee pain worse with **stairs, squatting, running** and prolonged sitting ("theater sign"); treat with physical therapy (quadriceps and hip strengthening).
  - title: Lateral patellar dislocation
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Twisting on a planted foot with the knee slightly flexed; the patella dislocates **laterally**, often tearing the medial patellofemoral ligament.
    presentation: Obvious lateral deformity (often reduced on extension); afterwards, effusion (hemarthrosis), medial tenderness and a positive apprehension test.
  - title: Patellar fracture
    highYield: false
    mechanism: A direct blow (fall onto the knee) or forceful quadriceps contraction.
    presentation: Painful swelling and a palpable gap; **inability to perform a straight-leg raise** means the extensor mechanism is disrupted and usually needs surgery.
  - title: Prepatellar bursitis
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Repeated kneeling ("housemaid's knee") or infection (usually *Staphylococcus aureus*).
    presentation: Swelling in front of the patella with preserved knee motion; aspirate if septic bursitis is suspected.
pance:
  - "Anterior knee pain with stairs and prolonged sitting → **patellofemoral pain syndrome** → PT."
  - "Patella dislocates **laterally**; positive apprehension test."
  - "Can't do a straight-leg raise after knee injury → **extensor mechanism** disruption (patellar fracture or tendon rupture)."
imaging:
  xray: AP, lateral and **sunrise (Merchant) view** of the knee — fractures, patellar height (alta/baja) and tilt/subluxation. A bipartite patella has smooth, rounded margins.
  mri: Shows MPFL tears, osteochondral fragments and the bone bruise pattern of a transient lateral dislocation (lateral femoral condyle and medial patella).
  keyViews:
    - "Knee AP, lateral and sunrise (Merchant) views"
exam:
  landmarks: The patella, patellar ligament and tibial tuberosity are palpable in front of the knee.
  palpation: Tenderness at the facets, poles (patellar or quadriceps tendinopathy) or medial retinaculum; check tracking during active flexion–extension.
  testing: Straight-leg raise (integrity of the extensor mechanism).
  specialTests:
    - name: Patellar apprehension test
      technique: With the knee in about 20–30° of flexion, the examiner pushes the patella laterally.
      positive: The patient becomes apprehensive or contracts the quadriceps to stop the movement.
      significance: Patellar instability or recent lateral dislocation.
    - name: Patellar grind (Clarke) test
      technique: The examiner presses the patella down into the trochlea while the patient contracts the quadriceps.
      positive: Pain.
      significance: Suggests patellofemoral pain, but many normal people are positive — low specificity.
