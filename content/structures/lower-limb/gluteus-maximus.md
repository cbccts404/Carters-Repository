name: Gluteus maximus
type: muscle
region: lower-limb
subregion: Gluteal region
taName: Musculus gluteus maximus
summary: The largest and most superficial gluteal muscle, the main extensor of the hip for rising, climbing and running. Weakness from an inferior gluteal nerve lesion causes a backward-lurching gait.
tags: [gluteal, hip]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Forms the bulk of the buttock, superficial to the other gluteal muscles and the [[sciatic-nerve]].
  group: Gluteal region, superficial layer
  origin: Posterior gluteal line and adjacent ilium of the [[hip-bone]], the dorsal surface of the [[sacrum]] and coccyx, the thoracolumbar fascia and the [[sacrotuberous-and-sacrospinous-ligaments|sacrotuberous ligament]].
  insertion: Most fibers into the **iliotibial tract** (and through it to the lateral tibial condyle); deep lower fibers into the **gluteal tuberosity** of the [[femur]].
  innervation: "**Inferior gluteal nerve** (L5–S2), from the [[sacral-plexus]]"
  bloodSupply: Inferior and superior gluteal arteries (from the [[internal-iliac-artery]]).
  action:
    - "Powerful **extension** of the hip (rising from sitting, climbing stairs, running) — little used in level walking"
    - "Lateral rotation of the hip"
    - "Through the iliotibial tract, helps stabilize the extended knee"
  relations: >-
    Deep to it: piriformis, the short lateral rotators, the sciatic nerve, the gluteal vessels and nerves, and the
    ischial tuberosity (covered when standing, uncovered when sitting). The trochanteric bursa lies between it and the
    greater trochanter.
clinical:
  - title: Inferior gluteal nerve injury
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Posterior hip surgery, pelvic fracture or intramuscular injection.
    presentation: Difficulty rising from a chair or climbing stairs; a **gluteus maximus (backward-lurching) gait** — the trunk is thrown backward at heel strike to keep the hip extended.
  - title: Greater trochanteric pain syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Tendinopathy of gluteus medius/minimus and trochanteric bursitis under gluteus maximus and the iliotibial tract; common in middle-aged women.
    presentation: Lateral hip pain, **tenderness over the greater trochanter**, pain lying on that side; normal hip range of motion.
  - title: Safe intramuscular injection site
    highYield: true
    mechanism: The sciatic nerve runs deep to the lower medial part of the buttock.
    presentation: Give dorsogluteal injections in the **upper outer quadrant**, or use the ventrogluteal site (gluteus medius, between the ASIS and iliac tubercle).
pance:
  - "Gluteus maximus = main hip **extensor** (stairs, rising) — inferior gluteal nerve."
  - "Lateral hip pain with trochanteric tenderness → **greater trochanteric pain syndrome**."
  - "Buttock IM injection → **upper outer quadrant**."
imaging:
  mri: Tendon tears, muscle injury, trochanteric bursitis.
  ultrasound: Guides trochanteric bursa injections and shows gluteal tendinopathy.
exam:
  landmarks: The buttock; the gluteal fold is formed by fat below the lower border of gluteus maximus (not the muscle border itself).
  palpation: Palpate the greater trochanter and bursa.
  testing: Resisted hip extension with the patient prone and knee flexed (isolates gluteus maximus from the hamstrings); rising from a chair without using the arms.
quiz:
  - stem: A patient has difficulty climbing stairs and rising from a chair, but walks on level ground almost normally. Which nerve is most likely injured?
    choices:
      - Superior gluteal nerve
      - Inferior gluteal nerve
      - Femoral nerve
      - Obturator nerve
      - Tibial nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Gluteus maximus** (inferior gluteal nerve) is the powerful hip extensor for climbing and rising but is little
      used in level walking. Superior gluteal nerve injury causes a Trendelenburg gait.
  - stem: How is the hip extended most selectively when testing gluteus maximus strength?
    choices:
      - Prone with the knee flexed
      - Supine with the knee extended
      - Standing on one leg
      - Side-lying with the hip abducted
      - Seated with the hip flexed
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      Flexing the knee shortens the **hamstrings**, reducing their contribution, so resisted hip extension in the prone
      position with the knee bent mainly tests gluteus maximus.
  - stem: >-
      A 52-year-old woman has lateral hip pain that is worse lying on that side. The greater trochanter is tender and
      hip range of motion is normal. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Greater trochanteric pain syndrome
      - Hip osteoarthritis
      - Avascular necrosis of the femoral head
      - Piriformis syndrome
      - L4 radiculopathy
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      **Greater trochanteric pain syndrome** (gluteal tendinopathy and trochanteric bursitis beneath gluteus maximus and
      the iliotibial tract) causes lateral hip pain with **trochanteric tenderness** and normal hip range of motion. Hip
      osteoarthritis limits motion, especially internal rotation.
