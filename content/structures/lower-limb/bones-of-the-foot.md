taName: Ossa pedis
aka: [Tarsals, metatarsals and phalanges]
summary: Seven tarsals, five metatarsals and fourteen phalanges forming the arches of the foot. Fifth metatarsal fractures, calcaneal fractures, stress fractures and plantar fasciitis are the common problems.
tags: [foot, fracture, sports-medicine]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Below the [[ankle-joint]]; the talus articulates with the [[tibia]] and [[fibula]].
  classification: Short bones (tarsals) and long bones (metatarsals, phalanges)
  features:
    - "**Talus**: body, neck and head; no muscle attachments; its blood supply enters mainly through the neck, so neck fractures risk **avascular necrosis**"
    - "**Calcaneus**: the largest tarsal (heel); the **sustentaculum tali** supports the talus; the calcaneal tuberosity receives the **Achilles tendon** and gives origin to the plantar fascia"
    - "**Navicular** (with a tuberosity for tibialis posterior), **cuboid** (groove for fibularis longus) and three **cuneiforms**"
    - "**Metatarsals**: the **5th metatarsal tuberosity** receives fibularis brevis; the 1st metatarsal head has two sesamoids in the flexor hallucis brevis tendons"
    - "**Phalanges**: two in the great toe, three in each of the others"
    - "**Joints**: subtalar (inversion/eversion), transverse tarsal (Chopart), tarsometatarsal (**Lisfranc**)"
    - "**Arches**: medial longitudinal (highest; supported by tibialis posterior, the spring ligament and plantar fascia), lateral longitudinal and transverse"
  ossification: The calcaneus and talus ossify before birth; the navicular is the last tarsal to ossify (about 3 years), which is relevant to Köhler disease (AVN of the navicular in children).
  relations: The [[tibial-nerve]] and posterior tibial artery pass behind the medial malleolus through the tarsal tunnel to the sole; the dorsalis pedis artery lies on the dorsum between the extensor hallucis longus and extensor digitorum longus tendons.
clinical:
  - title: Fifth metatarsal fractures
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Avulsion (pseudo-Jones) fracture** of the tuberosity from an inversion injury (fibularis brevis pull). **Jones fracture**: at the metaphyseal–diaphyseal junction, in a watershed area.'
    presentation: Lateral midfoot pain after an inversion injury. Avulsions heal well in a walking boot; **Jones fractures have a high risk of nonunion** (non-weight-bearing cast or surgical fixation in athletes).
  - title: Plantar fasciitis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Degenerative overload of the plantar fascia at its origin on the medial calcaneal tuberosity (running, obesity, prolonged standing, flat or high-arched feet).
    presentation: Sharp heel pain with the **first steps in the morning** or after rest, improving with activity; tenderness at the medial calcaneal tubercle. Treat with stretching, supportive footwear, NSAIDs and night splints.
  - title: Calcaneal fracture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Axial load — a **fall from height** onto the heels.
    presentation: Heel pain and swelling, inability to bear weight; look for associated **lumbar spine** compression fractures and a contralateral calcaneal fracture. Böhler angle is reduced on the lateral X-ray.
  - title: Lisfranc injury
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Disruption of the tarsometatarsal joints (the Lisfranc ligament runs from the medial cuneiform to the 2nd metatarsal base) from an axial load on a plantarflexed foot.
    presentation: Midfoot pain and swelling, **plantar ecchymosis**; easily missed — weight-bearing X-rays show widening between the 1st and 2nd metatarsal bases.
  - title: Metatarsal stress fracture ("march fracture")
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Repetitive loading, usually the 2nd (or 3rd) metatarsal shaft, in runners and recruits; consider the female athlete triad.
    presentation: Gradual forefoot pain with focal tenderness; X-rays may be normal for 2–3 weeks — MRI if needed.
pance:
  - "Inversion injury + base of 5th metatarsal: avulsion heals well; **Jones fracture** → nonunion risk."
  - "Heel pain with the **first morning steps** → plantar fasciitis."
  - "Fall from height with calcaneal fracture → check the **lumbar spine**."
  - "Midfoot injury with **plantar bruising** → Lisfranc injury → weight-bearing films."
  - "2nd metatarsal stress fracture = march fracture."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    Foot AP, oblique and lateral views; **Ottawa foot rules** decide who needs X-rays (tenderness at the navicular or
    base of the 5th metatarsal, or inability to bear weight). Weight-bearing views for Lisfranc injury.
  ct: Calcaneal and complex midfoot fractures.
  mri: Stress fractures, AVN of the talus or navicular, occult fractures.
  ultrasound: "Thickened plantar fascia (over about 4 mm) in plantar fasciitis. {{verify: thickness threshold}}"
  keyViews:
    - "Foot AP, oblique and lateral (weight-bearing when possible)"
    - "Harris (axial) view of the calcaneus"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    Base of the 5th metatarsal (lateral midfoot), navicular tuberosity (medial midfoot), medial calcaneal tubercle
    (plantar heel), sustentaculum tali (below the medial malleolus), head of the talus (in front of the ankle on
    eversion/inversion).
  palpation: Apply the **Ottawa foot rules** after trauma; palpate the plantar fascia origin, metatarsal shafts and the Lisfranc joint.
  testing: Inspect the arches standing (pes planus/cavus); single-leg heel raise tests tibialis posterior function.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 45-year-old woman has sharp pain at the bottom of her right heel that is worst with her first steps in the
      morning and improves after walking for a few minutes. There is tenderness at the medial calcaneal tubercle. What
      is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Achilles tendinopathy
      - Plantar fasciitis
      - Calcaneal stress fracture
      - Tarsal tunnel syndrome
      - Gout
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Plantar fasciitis** causes heel pain with the first steps after rest, with tenderness at the fascia's origin on
      the medial calcaneal tubercle. Treatment is conservative (stretching, footwear, NSAIDs).
  - stem: A college basketball player has a fracture at the metaphyseal–diaphyseal junction of the 5th metatarsal. What is the main concern?
    choices:
      - Compartment syndrome
      - Nonunion because of poor blood supply
      - Injury to the sural artery
      - Plantar fasciitis
      - Tarsal coalition
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **Jones fracture** occurs in a vascular watershed area and has a high rate of **nonunion**; athletes often have
      surgical fixation. Tuberosity avulsion fractures heal well with a boot.
  - stem: A worker falls 4 m and lands on his feet. X-rays show a comminuted calcaneal fracture. Which other area should be imaged?
    choices:
      - Cervical spine only
      - Lumbar spine
      - Skull
      - Shoulders
      - Wrists
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Axial load through the heels is transmitted up the skeleton; calcaneal fractures are commonly associated with
      **lumbar (thoracolumbar) compression fractures** and fractures of the opposite calcaneus.
