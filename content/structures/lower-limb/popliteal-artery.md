---
name: Popliteal artery
type: artery
region: lower-limb
subregion: Knee & popliteal fossa
taName: Arteria poplitea
summary: Continuation of the femoral artery through the popliteal fossa, the deepest structure there. It is tethered and easily injured in knee dislocations and is the most common site of peripheral aneurysms.
tags: [knee, vascular, trauma]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: '[[popliteal-fossa]], lying deepest (against the femur and knee capsule).'
  origin: Continuation of the [[femoral-artery]] at the **adductor hiatus**.
  course: Descends through the popliteal fossa, deep to the popliteal vein and [[tibial-nerve]], and ends at the lower border of popliteus by dividing into the anterior and posterior tibial arteries.
  branches:
    - "**Genicular arteries** (superior medial and lateral, middle, inferior medial and lateral) forming the genicular anastomosis"
    - "**Sural arteries** to gastrocnemius and soleus"
    - "**Anterior tibial artery**: through the interosseous membrane into the anterior compartment; continues on the dorsum of the foot as the **dorsalis pedis artery**"
    - "**Posterior tibial artery** (tibioperoneal trunk): deep posterior compartment, gives the **fibular (peroneal) artery**; passes behind the medial malleolus and divides into the medial and lateral plantar arteries"
  supplies: The knee joint and all of the leg and foot.
  anastomoses: The genicular anastomosis around the knee connects with the descending genicular (femoral) and circumflex femoral arteries — insufficient to keep the leg alive after acute occlusion.
  termination: Divides into the anterior tibial and posterior tibial arteries at the lower border of popliteus.
clinical:
  - title: Popliteal artery injury in knee dislocation
    highYield: true
    mechanism: The artery is **tethered** above (adductor hiatus) and below (soleus arch), so **knee dislocation** (tibiofemoral) stretches or tears it — often an intimal tear with thrombosis.
    presentation: >-
      Pulses may be present initially. Check pulses and the **ankle–brachial index** in every knee dislocation (ABI below
      0.9 → CT angiography); ischemia beyond about 6–8 hours often leads to amputation. The common fibular nerve is
      often injured too.
  - title: Popliteal artery aneurysm
    highYield: true
    mechanism: The **most common peripheral aneurysm**, usually atherosclerotic in older men; frequently **bilateral** and associated with **abdominal aortic aneurysm**.
    presentation: >-
      A **pulsatile mass** behind the knee; complications are **thrombosis or distal embolism** causing acute limb
      ischemia (rupture is rare). Image the other knee and the aorta.
  - title: Popliteal artery entrapment syndrome
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Abnormal relationship with the medial head of gastrocnemius compressing the artery; young athletes.
    presentation: Exertional calf claudication in a young person; pulses diminish with active plantarflexion.
pance:
  - "Knee dislocation → check pulses and **ABI** → CTA if abnormal (popliteal artery injury)."
  - "Pulsatile popliteal mass → **popliteal aneurysm** → check the other side and the **aorta**."
  - "Dorsalis pedis = continuation of the **anterior tibial**; posterior tibial pulse behind the medial malleolus."
  - "Deepest structure in the popliteal fossa = **popliteal artery**."
imaging:
  ultrasound: Duplex ultrasound for aneurysm size and thrombus, and entrapment (with provocative maneuvers).
  ct: CT angiography after knee dislocation with abnormal ABI or pulses, and for aneurysm planning.
  keyViews:
    - "Ankle–brachial index (screening after knee dislocation)"
    - "CT angiography of the lower extremity"
exam:
  landmarks: The popliteal pulse is felt deep in the popliteal fossa with the knee flexed about 30° and the muscles relaxed (press against the back of the tibia/femur).
  palpation: An easily palpable, broad popliteal pulse suggests an aneurysm. **Dorsalis pedis** lateral to the EHL tendon on the dorsum; **posterior tibial** behind the medial malleolus.
  testing: ABI; distal capillary refill; motor/sensory exam of the foot.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 25-year-old man has a knee dislocation after a motorcycle crash; it is reduced in the ED. His dorsalis pedis
      pulse is palpable but weaker than on the left. What is the most appropriate next step?
    choices:
      - Discharge with a knee immobilizer
      - Measure the ankle–brachial index and obtain CT angiography if it is abnormal
      - MRI of the knee next week
      - Repeat pulse exam in 48 hours
      - Start aspirin only
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **popliteal artery** is tethered and often injured in knee dislocations; a palpable pulse doesn't exclude an
      intimal tear. An **ABI below 0.9** mandates **CT angiography** (or surgical exploration if the limb is ischemic).
  - stem: A 72-year-old man has a pulsatile mass behind his right knee. What other study is most important?
    choices:
      - Brain MRI
      - Ultrasound of the abdominal aorta and the opposite popliteal artery
      - Echocardiogram only
      - Venous duplex for DVT only
      - Lumbar spine X-ray
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Popliteal aneurysms** are often **bilateral** and frequently associated with **abdominal aortic aneurysm**, so
      both should be screened.
