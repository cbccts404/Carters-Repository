---
name: Obturator nerve
type: nerve
region: lower-limb
subregion: Thigh
taName: Nervus obturatorius
summary: Lumbar plexus branch (L2–L4) that runs along the lateral pelvic wall through the obturator canal to supply the hip adductors and a patch of medial thigh skin. It is injured in pelvic surgery and childbirth.
tags: [thigh, lumbar-plexus, nerve-injury]
systems: [neurologic, musculoskeletal]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Lateral pelvic wall, then the medial compartment of the thigh.
  roots: Anterior divisions of the ventral rami of **L2–L4**.
  origin: "[[lumbar-plexus]], emerging from the medial border of [[psoas-major]]"
  course: >-
    Crosses the pelvic brim behind the common iliac vessels, runs along the lateral pelvic wall (in the floor of the
    ovarian fossa in females) and leaves through the **obturator canal** into the thigh, dividing into anterior and
    posterior branches around adductor brevis.
  branches:
    - "**Anterior branch**: adductor longus, adductor brevis, gracilis (± pectineus); skin of the medial thigh; branch to the hip joint"
    - "**Posterior branch**: obturator externus and the adductor part of adductor magnus; branch to the knee joint"
  motor: The [[adductor-muscles]] (except pectineus and the hamstring part of adductor magnus) and obturator externus.
  sensory: A variable area of skin on the **medial thigh**.
  relations: Accompanied by the obturator artery and vein in the obturator canal. Hip pain may be referred to the medial knee along its articular branches.
clinical:
  - title: Obturator nerve injury
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Pelvic surgery (lymph node dissection, hysterectomy), pelvic fractures, compression by the fetal head during labor, pelvic tumors, obturator hernia.
    presentation: Weak **hip adduction**, a wide-based (circumducting) gait, and numbness of the medial thigh.
  - title: Obturator hernia and Howship–Romberg sign
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Herniation through the obturator canal, typically in **thin elderly women**.
    presentation: Small-bowel obstruction with medial thigh/knee pain that worsens with hip extension, abduction or medial rotation (**Howship–Romberg sign**).
  - title: Referred pain from ovary and hip
    highYield: false
    mechanism: The nerve lies against the ovarian fossa and supplies the hip joint.
    presentation: Ovarian or pelvic disease can cause medial thigh pain; hip disease can present as medial knee pain.
pance:
  - "Obturator nerve: **L2–L4**, hip **adduction**, medial thigh skin."
  - "Thin elderly woman with bowel obstruction + medial thigh pain → **obturator hernia** (Howship–Romberg)."
  - "Hip pain may be felt at the **knee** (obturator/femoral articular branches)."
imaging:
  ct: Shows obturator hernias (bowel between pectineus and obturator externus) and pelvic masses.
  mri: Lumbosacral plexus and pelvic wall lesions.
exam:
  testing: Resisted hip adduction (squeeze a fist between the knees); sensation over the medial thigh. Adductor reflex (L2–L4) is sometimes tested.
quiz:

    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **obturator nerve** runs along the lateral pelvic wall next to the pelvic lymph nodes. Injury weakens the
      **adductors** (crossing the legs) and numbs a patch of medial thigh skin.
  - stem: Through which opening does the obturator nerve reach the thigh?
    choices:
      - Greater sciatic foramen
      - Lesser sciatic foramen
      - Obturator canal
      - Femoral ring
      - Adductor hiatus
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The obturator nerve and vessels leave the pelvis through the **obturator canal**, a gap at the top of the
      obturator membrane.
  - stem: >-
      A thin 82-year-old woman has small-bowel obstruction and pain down the inner thigh to the knee that worsens when
      her hip is extended and internally rotated. What is the most likely cause?
    choices:
      - Femoral hernia
      - Obturator hernia
      - Indirect inguinal hernia
      - Spigelian hernia
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      An **obturator hernia** passes through the obturator canal, typically in **thin elderly women**, and compresses
      the obturator nerve: medial thigh and knee pain worsened by hip extension, abduction or medial rotation is the
      **Howship–Romberg sign**.
