---
name: Quadriceps femoris
type: muscle
region: lower-limb
subregion: Thigh
taName: Musculus quadriceps femoris
aka: [Quadriceps, Quads]
summary: Four-headed extensor of the knee in the anterior thigh, supplied by the femoral nerve. It tests the L4 reflex, and its tendon, patella and patellar ligament form the knee's extensor mechanism.
tags: [thigh, knee, femoral-nerve]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Anterior compartment of the thigh.
  group: Anterior compartment of the thigh
  origin: >-
    **Rectus femoris**: straight head from the **AIIS** of the [[hip-bone]]; reflected head from above the acetabulum.
    **Vastus lateralis**: greater trochanter and lateral lip of the linea aspera of the [[femur]]. **Vastus
    medialis**: intertrochanteric line and medial lip of the linea aspera. **Vastus intermedius**: anterior and lateral
    surfaces of the femoral shaft.
  insertion: The four heads form the **quadriceps tendon** to the base of the [[patella]], continuing as the **patellar ligament** to the **tibial tuberosity** of the [[tibia]].
  innervation: "[[femoral-nerve]] (L2–L4)"
  bloodSupply: Lateral circumflex femoral and deep femoral arteries, and the [[femoral-artery]].
  action:
    - "**Extension of the knee**"
    - "Rectus femoris also **flexes the hip** (crosses both joints)"
    - "Vastus medialis (oblique fibers) helps keep the patella tracking medially"
  relations: Sartorius (also femoral nerve) crosses the thigh obliquely in front; the femoral vessels run medially in the femoral triangle and adductor canal.
clinical:
  - title: Quadriceps tendon or patellar ligament rupture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Quadriceps tendon**: older adults (over 40) with eccentric load; risk with diabetes, CKD, steroids, **fluoroquinolones**. **Patellar ligament**: younger athletes.'
    presentation: A palpable gap above (quadriceps) or below (patellar ligament) the patella, **inability to do a straight-leg raise**, patella baja or alta on X-ray; needs early surgical repair.
  - title: Quadriceps contusion and myositis ossificans
    highYield: false
    mechanism: A direct blow to the thigh ("charley horse"); heterotopic bone may form in the hematoma.
    presentation: Painful swelling, reduced knee flexion; a firm mass with calcification on X-ray weeks later. Early gentle knee flexion helps prevent it.
  - title: Quadriceps weakness in femoral neuropathy and L4 radiculopathy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Femoral nerve lesions (diabetic amyotrophy, iliopsoas hematoma) or L3/L4 root compression.
    presentation: Knee buckling, trouble with stairs, and a **reduced patellar reflex (L4)**. Hip adduction weakness points to a root/plexus problem rather than a femoral nerve lesion.
pance:
  - "Patellar (knee-jerk) reflex = **L4** (femoral nerve)."
  - "Can't straight-leg raise after injury → extensor mechanism rupture."
  - "Fluoroquinolones increase tendon rupture risk."
  - "Rectus femoris crosses two joints: hip flexion + knee extension."
imaging:
  xray: Patella alta (patellar ligament rupture) or baja (quadriceps tendon rupture); avulsion fragments; myositis ossificans.
  ultrasound: Rapid confirmation of tendon rupture and hematoma.
  mri: Partial vs complete tendon tears, muscle strains.
exam:
  landmarks: The vastus medialis bulge above the medial knee; the rectus femoris in the midline of the thigh; the quadriceps tendon above and patellar ligament below the patella.
  palpation: Palpate for a gap in the tendon; assess muscle bulk and wasting (vastus medialis wastes early after knee injury).
  testing: Resisted knee extension; straight-leg raise; patellar reflex (L4); sit-to-stand.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 58-year-old man on levofloxacin for pneumonia stumbles on a stair and feels a pop above his knee. He cannot
      raise his straightened leg off the bed, and there is a gap just above the patella. What is the most likely
      injury?
    choices:
      - Patellar ligament rupture
      - Quadriceps tendon rupture
      - ACL tear
      - Medial meniscal tear
      - Patellar dislocation
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A gap **above** the patella with loss of active knee extension indicates **quadriceps tendon rupture**, typical
      in men over 40 and more likely with fluoroquinolones, steroids, diabetes and CKD. It needs early surgical repair.
  - stem: Which part of the quadriceps also flexes the hip?
    choices:
      - Vastus lateralis
      - Vastus medialis
      - Vastus intermedius
      - Rectus femoris
      - All four heads equally
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      **Rectus femoris** arises from the pelvis (AIIS), so it crosses the hip as well as the knee; the vasti arise from
      the femur and act only on the knee.
