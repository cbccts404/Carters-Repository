name: Ankle joint
type: joint
region: lower-limb
subregion: Ankle & foot
taName: Articulatio talocruralis
aka: [Talocrural joint]
summary: Hinge joint in which the talus sits in a mortise formed by the tibia and fibula. Sprains of the lateral ligaments and malleolar fractures are extremely common; the Ottawa rules decide who needs an X-ray.
tags: [ankle, sports-medicine, fracture]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Between the leg and the foot; the malleoli are palpable on each side.
  classification: Synovial hinge joint
  bones: The distal [[tibia]] (plafond and medial malleolus), the [[fibula]] (lateral malleolus) and the trochlea of the talus ([[bones-of-the-foot]])
  capsule: Thin anteriorly and posteriorly, reinforced on each side by the collateral ligaments.
  ligaments:
    - "**Medial (deltoid) ligament**: strong, fan-shaped, from the medial malleolus to the navicular, talus and calcaneus; resists eversion"
    - "[[lateral-ankle-ligaments]]: anterior talofibular (ATFL), calcaneofibular (CFL) and posterior talofibular (PTFL); resist inversion"
    - "**Distal tibiofibular syndesmosis**: anterior and posterior inferior tibiofibular ligaments and the interosseous ligament, holding the mortise together"
  movements:
    - "Dorsiflexion (tibialis anterior, extensor digitorum and hallucis longus, fibularis tertius) — about 20°"
    - "Plantarflexion ([[triceps-surae]], tibialis posterior, flexor hallucis and digitorum longus, fibularis longus and brevis) — about 50°"
    - "Inversion and eversion occur mainly at the subtalar and transverse tarsal joints, not the ankle"
  stability: >-
    The talus is wider anteriorly, so the joint is **most stable in dorsiflexion** and **least stable in
    plantarflexion** — which is why most sprains happen with the foot plantarflexed and inverted.
  innervation: Deep fibular and tibial nerves (branches to the joint).
  bloodSupply: Malleolar branches of the anterior tibial, posterior tibial and fibular arteries.
  relations: >-
    **Behind the medial malleolus** (tarsal tunnel, front to back — "Tom, Dick And Very Nervous Harry"): **T**ibialis
    posterior, flexor **D**igitorum longus, posterior tibial **A**rtery and **V**ein, tibial **N**erve, flexor
    **H**allucis longus. **Behind the lateral malleolus**: fibularis longus and brevis tendons, sural nerve and small
    saphenous vein. **In front**: tibialis anterior, EHL, the dorsalis pedis artery (anterior tibial) and deep fibular
    nerve, EDL.
clinical:
  - title: Ankle sprain
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Inversion of the plantarflexed foot — the **ATFL** tears first (see [[lateral-ankle-ligaments]]).
    presentation: Lateral swelling, bruising and tenderness over the ATFL; able to bear weight in most. Managed with protection, early mobilization and balance training.
  - title: Ankle fracture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Twisting, most often supination–external rotation; unimalleolar, bimalleolar or trimalleolar (with the posterior malleolus).
    presentation: >-
      Bony tenderness at the malleoli (**Ottawa ankle rules**), deformity. Unstable fractures (bimalleolar, widened
      **medial clear space** or syndesmotic disruption) need open reduction and internal fixation. A dislocated ankle
      with skin tenting or neurovascular compromise needs **immediate reduction**.
  - title: High ankle (syndesmotic) sprain
    highYield: false
    mechanism: External rotation of the foot, common in contact sports.
    presentation: Pain above the ankle over the anterior tibiofibular ligament, positive squeeze and external-rotation tests; slower recovery than a lateral sprain.
  - title: Tarsal tunnel syndrome
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Compression of the [[tibial-nerve]] behind the medial malleolus under the flexor retinaculum.
    presentation: Burning pain and numbness of the sole and toes, worse with standing or at night; positive Tinel sign behind the medial malleolus.
pance:
  - "**Ottawa ankle rules**: X-ray if malleolar pain plus tenderness at the **posterior edge or tip of either malleolus** (distal 6 cm) or inability to bear weight 4 steps."
  - "Most common sprain: **ATFL** (plantarflexion + inversion)."
  - "Behind the medial malleolus: **Tom, Dick And Very Nervous Harry**."
  - "Ankle most stable in **dorsiflexion**."
  - "Deltoid ligament is strong — medial malleolus often fractures instead."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    **AP, lateral and mortise** views: malleolar fractures, talar shift, medial clear space (widened over about 4 mm
    suggests deltoid/syndesmotic injury) and tibiofibular overlap. {{verify: medial clear space threshold}} Stress
    views for instability.
  ct: Complex (trimalleolar, pilon) fractures.
  mri: Ligament and osteochondral lesions of the talus when pain persists after a "sprain".
  keyViews:
    - "Ankle AP, lateral and mortise"
exam:
  landmarks: Medial and lateral malleoli (the lateral lies lower and more posterior), the ATFL just in front of the lateral malleolus, the base of the 5th metatarsal, the navicular, the Achilles tendon.
  palpation: Apply the Ottawa ankle and foot rules; palpate the ATFL, CFL, deltoid ligament, syndesmosis, proximal fibula and Achilles tendon.
  testing: Check dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial pulses; range of motion; single-leg stance.
  specialTests:
    - name: Anterior drawer test of the ankle
      technique: Stabilize the tibia and pull the heel forward with the ankle in slight (about 10–20°) plantarflexion.
      positive: Excess anterior translation of the talus compared with the other side.
      significance: ATFL tear.
    - name: Talar tilt test
      technique: Invert the calcaneus/talus with the ankle in neutral.
      positive: Excess tilt compared with the other side.
      significance: CFL (with ATFL) injury.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 25-year-old woman rolled her ankle stepping off a curb. She has lateral swelling and walked into the clinic.
      There is tenderness just anterior to the lateral malleolus but none at the posterior edge or tip of either
      malleolus, the navicular or the base of the 5th metatarsal. What is the most appropriate management?
    choices:
      - Ankle X-rays
      - MRI
      - Rest, ice, compression, elevation and early mobilization without imaging
      - Short leg cast for 6 weeks
      - Orthopedic surgery referral
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      She does not meet the **Ottawa ankle rules** (no bony tenderness at the specified sites and able to bear weight),
      so X-rays aren't needed. The tenderness over the ATFL indicates a **lateral ankle sprain** managed conservatively.
  - stem: Which structure lies between the flexor digitorum longus tendon and the tibial nerve behind the medial malleolus?
    choices:
      - Tibialis posterior tendon
      - Posterior tibial artery and veins
      - Flexor hallucis longus tendon
      - Great saphenous vein
      - Sural nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      From anterior to posterior: **Tibialis posterior, flexor Digitorum longus, posterior tibial Artery and Vein, tibial
      Nerve, flexor Hallucis longus** — the pulse is felt between the tendons in front and the nerve behind.
