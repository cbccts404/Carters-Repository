name: Femoral triangle
type: space
region: lower-limb
subregion: Thigh
taName: Trigonum femorale
aka: [Scarpa triangle]
summary: Triangular region of the upper anterior thigh containing the femoral nerve, artery, vein and canal. It is the site of the femoral pulse, arterial and venous access, and femoral hernias.
tags: [thigh, groin, vascular-access, hernia]
systems: [cardiovascular, musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Upper anterior thigh, just below the inguinal ligament.
  boundaries:
    superior (base): Inguinal ligament
    lateral: Medial border of sartorius
    medial: Medial border of adductor longus
    floor: Iliopsoas (laterally) and pectineus (medially), with adductor longus
    roof: Fascia lata with the saphenous opening (cribriform fascia)
    apex: Where sartorius crosses adductor longus, continuing as the adductor canal
  contents:
    - "**Lateral to medial — NAVEL**: [[femoral-nerve]] (outside the sheath), [[femoral-artery]], **femoral vein**, empty space/**femoral canal**, **lymphatics** (deep inguinal nodes, node of Cloquet)"
    - "**Femoral sheath**: a fascial sleeve (from transversalis and iliac fascia) around the artery, vein and canal — not the nerve"
    - "Deep femoral artery and vein; the termination of the [[great-saphenous-vein]] through the saphenous opening"
    - "Femoral branch of the genitofemoral nerve"
  communications: >-
    Above: the **femoral ring** (the mouth of the femoral canal) opens into the abdomen — bounded by the inguinal
    ligament (anterior), pectineal ligament (posterior), **lacunar ligament (medial)** and femoral vein (lateral).
    Below: continuous with the adductor canal.
clinical:
  - title: Femoral hernia
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Herniation through the femoral ring into the femoral canal, **below the inguinal ligament** and **medial to the femoral vein**; more common in **women**.
    presentation: A small groin bulge below and lateral to the pubic tubercle; **highest risk of incarceration and strangulation** of groin hernias (narrow, rigid ring), so repair is recommended. See [[inguinal-canal]].
  - title: Femoral vascular access
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Arterial puncture for angiography/PCI; central venous access into the femoral vein.
    presentation: Palpate the arterial pulse below the mid-inguinal point; the **vein is just medial**. Puncture **below** the inguinal ligament — a high puncture can cause retroperitoneal hemorrhage.
  - title: Inguinal lymphadenopathy
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Drainage of the lower limb, perineum, lower abdominal wall and anal canal below the pectinate line (see [[pelvic-lymph-nodes]]).
    presentation: Enlarged nodes in the triangle; examine the whole drainage area.
pance:
  - "**NAVEL** lateral → medial: Nerve, Artery, Vein, Empty space, Lymphatics."
  - "Femoral hernia: **women**, below the inguinal ligament, medial to the femoral vein, **highest strangulation risk**."
  - "Femoral venous line: vein is **medial** to the pulse."
  - "The femoral nerve lies **outside** the femoral sheath."
imaging:
  ultrasound: Guides femoral arterial and venous access and nerve blocks; distinguishes hernia, lymph node, pseudoaneurysm and saphena varix.
  ct: Femoral hernias with bowel obstruction; hematomas after catheterization.
exam:
  landmarks: 'Inguinal ligament from the ASIS to the pubic tubercle; the femoral pulse just below its midpoint (mid-inguinal point).'
  palpation: >-
    Femoral pulse; lymph nodes; groin masses (a femoral hernia lies below and lateral to the pubic tubercle; an
    inguinal hernia above and medial). A saphena varix has a cough thrill and disappears on lying down.
  testing: Cough impulse over any groin mass.
quiz:
  - stem: Which structure lies immediately medial to the femoral vein in the femoral triangle?
    choices:
      - Femoral artery
      - Femoral nerve
      - Femoral canal
      - Sartorius
      - Inguinal ligament
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      Lateral to medial: nerve, artery, vein, **femoral canal (empty space)**, lymphatics. Femoral hernias pass
      through the canal, just medial to the vein.
  - stem: >-
      A 70-year-old woman has vomiting and a tender, irreducible lump in the groin below the inguinal ligament and lateral
      to the pubic tubercle. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Direct inguinal hernia
      - Indirect inguinal hernia
      - Incarcerated femoral hernia
      - Psoas abscess
      - Saphena varix
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      A lump **below the inguinal ligament** and **inferolateral to the pubic tubercle** in an older woman with bowel
      obstruction is an **incarcerated femoral hernia** — the groin hernia most prone to strangulation, needing urgent
      surgery.
