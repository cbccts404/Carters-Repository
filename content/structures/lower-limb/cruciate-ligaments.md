---
name: Cruciate ligaments
type: ligament
region: lower-limb
subregion: Knee & popliteal fossa
taName: Ligamentum cruciatum anterius; Ligamentum cruciatum posterius
aka: [ACL, PCL, Anterior cruciate ligament, Posterior cruciate ligament]
summary: Crossed intra-articular ligaments of the knee that prevent the tibia sliding forward (ACL) or backward (PCL) on the femur. ACL tears from non-contact pivoting are among the most common serious sports injuries.
tags: [knee, sports-medicine]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Inside the capsule of the [[knee-joint]] but outside its synovial cavity, in the intercondylar fossa, crossing like an X.
  attachments: >-
    **ACL**: from the **anterior** intercondylar area of the [[tibia]] upward, backward and laterally to the medial
    side of the **lateral femoral condyle**. **PCL**: from the **posterior** intercondylar area of the tibia upward,
    forward and medially to the lateral side of the **medial femoral condyle**. (Named for their tibial attachments.)
  function: >-
    **ACL**: prevents **anterior translation of the tibia** on the femur (posterior displacement of the femur on the
    tibia) and limits hyperextension and rotation; it is the weaker of the two. **PCL**: prevents **posterior
    translation of the tibia**; the stronger, main stabilizer when weight-bearing on a flexed knee (walking downhill).
  relations: Supplied by the middle genicular artery (from the [[popliteal-artery]]). The ACL is often torn together with the MCL and medial meniscus.
clinical:
  - title: ACL tear
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Usually **non-contact**: sudden deceleration, cutting or pivoting, or landing from a jump with the knee near
      extension and in valgus. More common in female athletes. Contact valgus blows can tear the **"unhappy triad"**
      (ACL, MCL and medial meniscus; the lateral meniscus is often involved in practice).
    presentation: >-
      A **"pop"**, immediate inability to continue, and **rapid hemarthrosis** (within hours); later instability
      ("giving way") with pivoting. Positive **Lachman** (most sensitive), anterior drawer and pivot-shift tests.
    notes: Confirm with MRI. Young active patients usually have **ACL reconstruction**; others may do well with rehabilitation alone.
  - title: PCL tear
    highYield: true
    mechanism: A posteriorly directed force on the proximal tibia with the knee flexed — **dashboard injury** or a fall onto the flexed knee.
    presentation: Posterior sag of the tibia, positive **posterior drawer** test; often less disabling than ACL tears and frequently managed non-operatively when isolated.
pance:
  - "Non-contact pivot + pop + rapid swelling → **ACL** tear → **Lachman** (most sensitive)."
  - "Dashboard injury to the flexed knee → **PCL** tear (posterior drawer, sag sign)."
  - "Unhappy triad: **ACL + MCL + medial meniscus**."
  - "ACL prevents anterior tibial translation; PCL prevents posterior."
imaging:
  xray: Usually normal; a **Segond fracture** (avulsion of the lateral tibial plateau) is strongly associated with ACL tears. Rule out fractures (Ottawa knee rules).
  mri: '**MRI** confirms the tear and shows associated injuries — the "kissing" bone bruises of the lateral femoral condyle and posterolateral tibial plateau, meniscal and MCL tears.'
  keyViews:
    - "MRI knee (sagittal proton-density)"
exam:
  landmarks: Not palpable; tested by stress maneuvers comparing both knees.
  testing: Examine soon after injury (before guarding) or after the swelling settles; always compare with the uninjured side.
  specialTests:
    - name: Lachman test
      technique: Knee flexed 20–30°; stabilize the distal femur with one hand and pull the proximal tibia forward with the other.
      positive: Increased anterior translation with a soft or absent end point compared with the other side.
      significance: The **most sensitive** test for ACL tear.
    - name: Anterior drawer test
      technique: Knee flexed 90° with the foot stabilized (sit on it); pull the proximal tibia forward.
      positive: Excess anterior translation.
      significance: ACL tear; less sensitive than Lachman in acute injuries (hamstring guarding).
    - name: Pivot-shift test
      technique: With the knee extended, apply valgus and internal rotation, then flex the knee.
      positive: A clunk as the subluxed lateral tibial plateau reduces at about 20–40° of flexion.
      significance: The most **specific** test for ACL insufficiency, but hard to do in an awake patient.
    - name: Posterior drawer test and sag sign
      technique: 'Knee at 90°: push the proximal tibia backward. Sag sign: with hips and knees flexed to 90°, look from the side.'
      positive: Excess posterior translation; the tibia sags backward under gravity.
      significance: PCL tear.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 19-year-old female soccer player plants her foot and pivots, feels a pop, and falls. Her knee swells within 2
      hours. Which exam test is most sensitive for the most likely injury?
    choices:
      - McMurray test
      - Lachman test
      - Valgus stress test
      - Posterior drawer test
      - Apley compression test
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A non-contact pivoting injury with a pop and rapid hemarthrosis suggests an **ACL tear**. The **Lachman test** at
      20–30° of flexion is the most sensitive exam maneuver.
  - stem: A driver hits his flexed knee on the dashboard. The tibia sags backward when both knees are flexed to 90°. Which structure is torn?
    choices:
      - Anterior cruciate ligament
      - Posterior cruciate ligament
      - Medial collateral ligament
      - Lateral meniscus
      - Patellar ligament
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **dashboard injury** drives the tibia backward, tearing the **PCL**; the posterior sag sign and posterior drawer
      test are positive.
  - stem: The ACL attaches to the femur on which surface?
    choices:
      - Lateral surface of the medial condyle
      - Medial surface of the lateral condyle
      - Anterior surface of the medial condyle
      - Adductor tubercle
      - Patellar surface
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The ACL runs from the anterior tibial intercondylar area to the **medial side of the lateral femoral condyle**;
      the PCL runs to the lateral side of the medial condyle.
  - stem: Which ligament is the main stabilizer when weight-bearing on a flexed knee, as when walking downhill?
    choices:
      - Posterior cruciate ligament
      - Anterior cruciate ligament
      - Medial collateral ligament
      - Lateral collateral ligament
      - Patellar ligament
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      The **PCL** is the stronger cruciate and prevents posterior translation of the tibia (anterior sliding of the
      femur on the tibia), which matters most when bearing weight on a flexed knee. The ACL prevents anterior tibial
      translation.
  - stem: >-
      MRI confirms a complete ACL tear in a 20-year-old competitive soccer player who wants to return to pivoting
      sports. What is the usual management?
    choices:
      - Long leg cast for 6 weeks
      - Corticosteroid injection
      - Total knee arthroplasty
      - Observation without rehabilitation
      - ACL reconstruction
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      Young, active patients who pivot usually have **ACL reconstruction**; less active patients may do well with
      rehabilitation alone. Immobilization and injection don't restore stability.
