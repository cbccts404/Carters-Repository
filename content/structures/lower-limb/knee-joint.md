name: Knee joint
type: joint
region: lower-limb
subregion: Knee & popliteal fossa
taName: Articulatio genus
summary: The largest synovial joint — a modified hinge between the femur, tibia and patella, stabilized mainly by ligaments and the menisci. Ligament and meniscal injuries, effusions and osteoarthritis make it one of the most examined joints.
tags: [knee, sports-medicine, arthritis]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Between the distal [[femur]], the proximal [[tibia]] and the [[patella]]; the [[popliteal-fossa]] lies behind it.
  classification: Synovial modified hinge (bicondylar) joint, with a patellofemoral (plane/saddle) component
  bones: "[[femur]] (condyles), [[tibia]] (plateau) and [[patella]]; the fibula is not part of the joint"
  capsule: >-
    Thin and incomplete anteriorly, where the quadriceps tendon, patella and patellar ligament take its place. The
    synovial membrane forms the **suprapatellar bursa**, which communicates with the joint (why effusions fill above
    the patella).
  ligaments:
    - "[[cruciate-ligaments]] (ACL and PCL): intra-articular, extrasynovial; anteroposterior stability"
    - "[[collateral-ligaments-of-knee]] (MCL and LCL): varus/valgus stability"
    - "**Patellar ligament**: continuation of the quadriceps tendon to the tibial tuberosity"
    - "**Oblique and arcuate popliteal ligaments** (posterior capsule)"
    - "[[menisci]]: medial (C-shaped, attached to the MCL) and lateral (O-shaped, more mobile)"
  movements:
    - "Flexion ([[hamstrings]], gastrocnemius, popliteus)"
    - "Extension ([[quadriceps-femoris]])"
    - "Slight medial/lateral rotation of the flexed knee"
    - "**Screw-home mechanism**: the femur rotates medially on the tibia in the last degrees of extension, locking the knee; **popliteus** unlocks it by rotating the femur laterally"
  stability: Mostly ligamentous and muscular (quadriceps), since the bony surfaces are poorly congruent.
  innervation: Branches of the [[femoral-nerve]], [[obturator-nerve]], [[tibial-nerve]] and [[common-fibular-nerve]] (genicular nerves).
  bloodSupply: Genicular anastomosis — genicular branches of the [[popliteal-artery]], the descending genicular branch of the [[femoral-artery]], the descending branch of the lateral circumflex femoral, and the anterior tibial recurrent artery.
  relations: Bursae around the knee include prepatellar, infrapatellar, pes anserine (medial tibia) and semimembranosus bursae; a **popliteal (Baker) cyst** is a distended gastrocnemius–semimembranosus bursa.
clinical:
  - title: Knee effusion and hemarthrosis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Acute hemarthrosis** within hours of injury suggests **ACL tear** (most common), patellar dislocation or intra-articular fracture. Non-bloody effusions: OA, inflammatory or crystal arthritis, infection.'
    presentation: Swelling filling the suprapatellar pouch and obscuring the "dimples" beside the patella. **Aspirate** an unexplained effusion (cell count, Gram stain, culture, crystals).
  - title: Knee osteoarthritis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Degenerative cartilage loss, most often in the **medial compartment** (varus deformity).
    presentation: Activity-related pain, short morning stiffness, crepitus, bony enlargement; X-ray with joint space narrowing and osteophytes. Treat with exercise, weight loss, topical then oral NSAIDs, intra-articular steroids, then total knee arthroplasty.
  - title: Septic arthritis and crystal arthritis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: The knee is the most commonly affected joint in both septic arthritis (*S. aureus*; gonococcus in young sexually active adults) and **pseudogout (CPPD)**.
    presentation: Acute hot, swollen knee — aspirate. Synovial WBC over about 50,000/µL suggests infection; **rhomboid, positively birefringent** crystals = CPPD; needle-shaped negatively birefringent = gout.
  - title: Popliteal (Baker) cyst
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Fluid from the joint (OA, meniscal tears, RA) distends the gastrocnemius–semimembranosus bursa.
    presentation: Posterior knee swelling; **rupture** causes calf pain and swelling that mimics DVT (crescent sign) — Doppler ultrasound distinguishes them.
pance:
  - "Rapid hemarthrosis after a pivoting injury → **ACL tear**."
  - "Hot swollen knee → **aspirate** (septic vs crystals)."
  - "Rhomboid, positively birefringent crystals = **pseudogout**; knee is the most common site."
  - "Ruptured Baker cyst mimics **DVT** → ultrasound."
  - "Popliteus unlocks the knee; the femur rotates medially to lock it (screw-home)."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    AP (weight-bearing for OA), lateral, and sunrise views. **Ottawa knee rules** decide who needs films after trauma.
    Chondrocalcinosis in CPPD; fat–fluid level in intra-articular fractures; Segond fracture (lateral tibial avulsion)
    implies an ACL tear.
  mri: The test of choice for ligament, meniscal and cartilage injuries and bone bruises.
  ultrasound: Effusions, Baker cysts, tendon injuries; guides aspiration.
  keyViews:
    - "Weight-bearing AP (standing) for joint-space narrowing"
    - "MRI knee without contrast"
exam:
  landmarks: The joint line is palpable on each side of the patellar ligament with the knee flexed; the tibial tuberosity, fibular head, femoral epicondyles and pes anserinus below the medial joint line.
  palpation: >-
    Joint-line tenderness (meniscus), MCL/LCL tenderness, effusion: **bulge (wipe) test** for small effusions and the
    **patellar tap (ballottement)** for larger ones.
  testing: >-
    Inspect alignment (varus/valgus), swelling and quadriceps bulk; range of motion (full extension 0°, flexion about
    135°); gait. Ligament and meniscal tests — see [[cruciate-ligaments]], [[collateral-ligaments-of-knee]] and
    [[menisci]]. Patellar reflex (L4).
  specialTests:
    - name: Bulge (wipe) test
      technique: Stroke fluid up out of the medial recess into the suprapatellar pouch, then stroke down the lateral side.
      positive: A bulge of fluid reappears on the medial side.
      significance: Detects small effusions.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 72-year-old woman has a hot, swollen right knee. Aspiration shows 25,000 WBC/µL and rhomboid-shaped, weakly
      positively birefringent crystals. X-ray shows linear calcification of the menisci. What is the diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Gout
      - Pseudogout (CPPD)
      - Septic arthritis
      - Rheumatoid arthritis
      - Osteoarthritis flare only
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Rhomboid, positively birefringent** crystals with **chondrocalcinosis** indicate calcium pyrophosphate
      deposition (pseudogout), which most often affects the knee. Gout crystals are needle-shaped and negatively
      birefringent.
  - stem: Which muscle "unlocks" the fully extended knee at the start of flexion?
    choices:
      - Gastrocnemius
      - Popliteus
      - Biceps femoris
      - Sartorius
      - Vastus medialis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Popliteus** rotates the femur laterally on the fixed tibia (or the tibia medially when the foot is free),
      reversing the screw-home locking so flexion can begin.
  - stem: >-
      A 55-year-old man with knee OA has sudden calf pain and swelling after gardening. Homans sign is equivocal. What
      is the best initial test?
    choices:
      - D-dimer only
      - Compression Doppler ultrasound of the leg
      - Knee MRI
      - Venography
      - Plain X-ray of the knee
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A ruptured **Baker cyst** and **DVT** look alike. **Compression (duplex) ultrasound** excludes DVT and can show the
      cyst. Treat for DVT only if a thrombus is seen.
