---
name: Common fibular nerve
type: nerve
region: lower-limb
subregion: Knee & popliteal fossa
taName: Nervus fibularis communis
aka: [Common peroneal nerve, Peroneal nerve]
summary: Lateral terminal branch of the sciatic nerve that winds around the fibular neck — the most commonly injured nerve of the lower limb. Injury causes foot drop and numbness of the dorsum of the foot.
tags: [knee, leg, nerve-injury, foot-drop]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Lateral side of the [[popliteal-fossa]], then around the neck of the [[fibula]] into the leg.
  roots: L4–S2.
  origin: "[[sciatic-nerve]], at the apex of the popliteal fossa"
  course: >-
    Follows the medial border of biceps femoris to the lateral side of the popliteal fossa, passes behind the fibular
    head, then **winds around the fibular neck** (superficial, against bone) and divides deep to fibularis longus into
    the superficial and deep fibular nerves.
  branches:
    - "**Superficial fibular nerve**: lateral compartment (fibularis longus and brevis → **eversion**); skin of the lower lateral leg and most of the **dorsum of the foot**"
    - "**Deep fibular nerve**: anterior compartment (tibialis anterior, extensor digitorum longus, extensor hallucis longus, fibularis tertius → **dorsiflexion and toe extension**) and extensor digitorum brevis; skin of the **first web space**"
    - "**Lateral sural cutaneous nerve** and the sural communicating branch; articular branches to the knee"
  motor: The anterior and lateral compartments of the leg (dorsiflexion, toe extension, eversion) and short head of biceps femoris (via the sciatic division).
  sensory: Lateral leg, dorsum of the foot, and the first web space.
  relations: Palpable (and vulnerable) as it crosses the fibular neck just below the fibular head.
clinical:
  - title: Common fibular neuropathy (foot drop)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Compression or trauma at the fibular neck** — habitual leg crossing, prolonged squatting or kneeling, weight loss, tight casts or braces, bed rest; fibular neck fracture; knee dislocation or LCL injury.'
    presentation: >-
      **Foot drop** (weak dorsiflexion and toe extension) with **weak eversion**, a **high-stepping (steppage) gait**,
      and numbness of the lateral leg and dorsum of the foot. **Inversion (tibial nerve) and the ankle reflex are
      normal** — which distinguishes it from an **L5 radiculopathy** (inversion and hip abduction also weak).
  - title: Anterior compartment syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Raised pressure in the anterior compartment compresses the deep fibular nerve (see [[leg-compartments]]).
    presentation: Severe pain, pain on passive toe flexion, and early numbness of the **first web space**.
pance:
  - "Foot drop + weak eversion + dorsal foot numbness + **normal inversion** → common fibular nerve at the **fibular neck**."
  - "Foot drop + weak **inversion** and hip abduction → **L5 radiculopathy**."
  - "First web-space numbness = **deep** fibular nerve."
  - "Leg crossing, tight cast or fibular neck fracture → fibular palsy."
imaging:
  xray: Fibular neck fracture, knee dislocation.
  mri: Intraneural ganglion cysts from the proximal tibiofibular joint, masses; lumbar MRI if L5 radiculopathy is suspected.
  ultrasound: Shows compression or ganglion cysts at the fibular neck.
exam:
  landmarks: Palpable as a cord behind the fibular head and around the fibular neck.
  palpation: Tinel sign at the fibular neck.
  testing: >-
    Dorsiflexion and great toe extension (deep fibular), eversion (superficial fibular), **inversion (tibial —
    preserved)**, hip abduction (L5 — preserved), ankle reflex (preserved). Sensation of the first web space and
    dorsum of the foot. Observe for steppage gait. EMG/nerve conduction studies localize the lesion.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      After losing 20 kg, a woman who habitually sits with her legs crossed develops a right foot drop. Eversion is
      weak, inversion is normal, and the ankle reflex is intact. Sensation is reduced over the dorsum of the foot.
      Where is the lesion?
    choices:
      - L5 nerve root
      - Sciatic nerve in the buttock
      - Common fibular nerve at the fibular neck
      - Tibial nerve in the tarsal tunnel
      - Femoral nerve
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      Weight loss (less fat padding) and leg crossing compress the **common fibular nerve at the fibular neck**. Weak
      dorsiflexion and eversion with **preserved inversion** (tibial nerve) and a normal ankle reflex localize the lesion
      there rather than to L5.
  - stem: Sensory loss limited to the first web space between the great and second toes indicates injury to which nerve?
    choices:
      - Superficial fibular nerve
      - Deep fibular nerve
      - Sural nerve
      - Saphenous nerve
      - Medial plantar nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **deep fibular nerve** supplies only the skin of the **first web space**; the superficial fibular nerve supplies
      most of the dorsum of the foot.
