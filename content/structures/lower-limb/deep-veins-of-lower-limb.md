---
name: Deep veins of the lower limb
type: vein
region: lower-limb
subregion: Leg
taName: Venae profundae membri inferioris
aka: [Femoral vein, Popliteal vein, Tibial veins, Soleal veins]
summary: Paired deep veins that accompany the leg arteries and join to form the popliteal and femoral veins. They carry most venous return, helped by the calf muscle pump, and are where DVT forms.
tags: [vascular, venous, thrombosis]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Deep to the deep fascia, accompanying the arteries of the leg and thigh.
  formation: >-
    **Venae comitantes** (paired) of the anterior tibial, posterior tibial and fibular arteries, plus the **soleal and
    gastrocnemius venous sinuses** within the calf muscles.
  course: >-
    The tibial and fibular veins join near the popliteus to form the **popliteal vein**, which runs superficial to the
    [[popliteal-artery]], passes through the adductor hiatus to become the **femoral vein** (medial to the
    [[femoral-artery]] in the [[femoral-triangle]]), receives the deep femoral vein and the
    [[great-saphenous-vein]], and becomes the **external iliac vein** at the inguinal ligament.
  tributaries:
    - "Anterior and posterior tibial veins, fibular veins, soleal and gastrocnemius sinuses"
    - "Small saphenous vein (into the popliteal vein)"
    - "Deep femoral vein and great saphenous vein (into the femoral vein)"
    - "Perforating veins from the superficial system (flow normally superficial → deep)"
  drainage: External iliac vein → common iliac vein → [[inferior-vena-cava]].
  relations: The **left common iliac vein** is crossed by the right common iliac artery (May–Thurner compression → left-sided DVT). Valves are numerous below the knee and absent in the iliac veins.
  notes: '**Calf muscle pump**: contraction of [[triceps-surae]] squeezes the deep veins; valves prevent backflow. Immobility abolishes the pump — one limb of **Virchow triad** (stasis, endothelial injury, hypercoagulability).'
clinical:
  - title: Deep vein thrombosis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Virchow triad** — stasis (immobility, surgery, travel), endothelial injury (trauma, surgery), hypercoagulability (cancer, pregnancy, estrogen, thrombophilia). Usually starts in the calf (soleal sinuses) and propagates proximally.'
    presentation: >-
      Unilateral calf or leg swelling (calf circumference > 3 cm larger), pain, warmth, pitting edema, dilated
      superficial veins. Estimate probability with the **Wells score**: low probability → **D-dimer** (normal rules out
      DVT); high probability or positive D-dimer → **compression ultrasound**. Proximal DVT is treated with
      anticoagulation (a DOAC for most) for at least 3 months.
  - title: Pulmonary embolism
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Most PEs arise from **proximal** (popliteal and above) leg DVTs.
    presentation: Sudden dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain, tachycardia, hypoxia; sinus tachycardia is the most common ECG finding. CT pulmonary angiography for diagnosis.
  - title: Phlegmasia cerulea dolens
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Massive iliofemoral DVT obstructing nearly all venous outflow.
    presentation: A massively swollen, **blue**, painful leg that can progress to venous gangrene — an emergency (anticoagulation, catheter-directed thrombolysis or thrombectomy).
pance:
  - "**Virchow triad**: stasis, endothelial injury, hypercoagulability."
  - "Low pretest probability → **D-dimer**; high → **compression ultrasound**."
  - "Proximal DVT → anticoagulate ≥ 3 months; most PEs come from proximal DVT."
  - "Left-sided DVT in a young woman → consider **May–Thurner** (left common iliac vein compression)."
  - "Homans sign is unreliable."
imaging:
  ultrasound: '**Compression duplex ultrasound** — a non-compressible vein with echogenic thrombus is diagnostic; whole-leg or two-point (femoral and popliteal) protocols.'
  ct: CT venography for iliac/IVC thrombus; CT pulmonary angiography for PE.
  mri: MR venography for pelvic veins (pregnancy, May–Thurner).
  keyViews:
    - "Compression ultrasound (femoral and popliteal veins)"
exam:
  landmarks: The femoral vein lies just **medial** to the femoral pulse below the inguinal ligament (femoral venous access site); the popliteal vein is superficial to the artery in the popliteal fossa.
  palpation: Calf tenderness along the deep veins, pitting edema, warmth.
  testing: Measure calf circumference 10 cm below the tibial tuberosity on both legs; apply the Wells score.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 45-year-old woman returns from a 14-hour flight with a swollen, tender left calf that is 4 cm larger than the
      right. Her Wells score indicates DVT is likely. What is the best next step?
    choices:
      - D-dimer and discharge if normal
      - Compression duplex ultrasound of the left leg
      - CT pulmonary angiography
      - Venography as the first test
      - Aspirin and follow-up in 2 weeks
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      When DVT is **likely** by Wells score, go straight to **compression ultrasound** (a normal D-dimer alone is not
      enough to rule it out in high-probability patients). D-dimer is used first when probability is low.
  - stem: Which is NOT a component of Virchow triad?
    choices:
      - Venous stasis
      - Endothelial injury
      - Hypercoagulability
      - Arterial hypertension
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      **Virchow triad** is venous **stasis**, **endothelial injury** and **hypercoagulability**; systemic arterial
      hypertension is not part of it.
