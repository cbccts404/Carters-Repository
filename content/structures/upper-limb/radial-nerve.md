---
name: Radial nerve
type: nerve
region: upper-limb
subregion: Arm
taName: Nervus radialis
aka: [Musculospiral nerve]
summary: Largest branch of the brachial plexus (posterior cord). It supplies all extensor muscles of the arm and forearm and winds around the humerus in the radial groove.
tags: [brachial-plexus]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Posterior compartment of the arm → lateral elbow → posterior compartment of the forearm (via its deep branch) and dorsum of the hand (via its superficial branch).
  roots: C5, C6, C7, C8, T1
  origin: Terminal branch of the posterior cord of the [[brachial-plexus]] (its largest branch).
  course: >-
    Leaves the [[axilla]] posterior to the [[axillary-artery]] and passes through the **triangular interval** (below
    teres major, between the long head of [[triceps-brachii]] and the humerus) with the **profunda brachii (deep
    brachial) artery**. It then spirals around the posterior humerus in the **radial groove** (spiral groove) between
    the lateral and medial heads of triceps. On the lateral side of the arm it pierces the lateral intermuscular septum
    and descends anterior to the lateral epicondyle between brachialis and brachioradialis. In the [[cubital-fossa]] it
    divides into:

    - **Deep branch**: motor. It passes between the two layers of **supinator** (entering under its proximal edge, the
      arcade of Frohse) and continues as the **posterior interosseous nerve (PIN)**.
    - **Superficial branch**: sensory. It runs under brachioradialis and emerges in the distal forearm to supply the
      dorsum of the hand.
  branches:
    - Posterior cutaneous nerve of the arm; inferior lateral cutaneous nerve of the arm; posterior cutaneous nerve of the forearm
    - Muscular branches to triceps and anconeus (mostly arising **proximal** to the radial groove)
    - Muscular branches to brachioradialis and extensor carpi radialis longus (above the elbow)
    - "**Deep branch / posterior interosseous nerve**: extensor carpi radialis brevis, supinator, and the posterior forearm extensors"
    - "**Superficial branch**: sensory to the lateral dorsum of the hand"
  motor: >-
    **Arm:** [[triceps-brachii]], anconeus. **Forearm (radial nerve proper):** brachioradialis, extensor carpi radialis
    longus. **Deep branch/PIN:** extensor carpi radialis brevis, supinator, extensor digitorum, extensor digiti minimi,
    extensor carpi ulnaris, abductor pollicis longus, extensor pollicis brevis, extensor pollicis longus, extensor
    indicis. {{verify: whether ECRB is supplied by the deep branch or the superficial branch varies between sources}}
  sensory: >-
    Posterior arm and forearm, the inferolateral arm, and the lateral two-thirds of the dorsum of the hand with the dorsum
    of the lateral 3½ digits proximal to the nail beds. The most reliable test site is the dorsal **first web space**.
clinical:
  - title: Midshaft humerus fracture (radial groove)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Fracture of the humeral shaft injures the nerve where it lies against the bone in the radial groove. A spiral
      fracture of the distal third of the shaft (Holstein–Lewis fracture) is classically associated with radial nerve palsy.
    presentation: >-
      **Wrist drop**: the wrist and the MCP joints can't be extended. Sensory loss on the dorsum of the hand (first
      web space). **Triceps is usually spared** because its branches arise proximal to the groove.
  - title: Compression in the axilla or arm ("crutch palsy", "Saturday night palsy")
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Axillary compression from improperly used crutches, or compression against the humerus when the arm hangs over a
      chair back during deep sleep or intoxication.
    presentation: >-
      Wrist drop and dorsal hand numbness. With **axillary** compression, **triceps is also weak** and the triceps reflex
      is reduced, because the lesion is proximal to the triceps branches.
  - title: Posterior interosseous nerve syndrome
    highYield: false
    mechanism: >-
      Compression of the deep branch at the arcade of Frohse/supinator (repetitive pronation–supination, masses such as
      lipoma or ganglion, radial head fracture or dislocation).
    presentation: >-
      **Finger drop without wrist drop**: MCP extension of the fingers and thumb is weak. The wrist extends with
      **radial deviation** because ECRL (supplied above the elbow) is preserved while ECU is paralyzed. **No sensory
      loss.**
  - title: Superficial radial neuropathy (Wartenberg syndrome)
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Compression of the superficial branch at the distal forearm by tight watchbands, handcuffs or casts.
    presentation: Numbness or paresthesias over the dorsoradial hand and thumb with no weakness.
pance:
  - "**Midshaft humerus fracture → radial nerve → wrist drop**; triceps usually spared."
  - "Crutch palsy (axilla) → wrist drop **plus** triceps weakness."
  - "PIN syndrome = finger drop, wrist extends with radial deviation, **no sensory loss**."
  - "Radial nerve territory in the hand: dorsal first web space."
  - "Reflexes carried by the radial nerve: triceps (C7) and brachioradialis (C6)."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    The nerve is not visible. AP and lateral humerus radiographs identify shaft fractures, particularly distal-third
    spiral fractures, which carry the highest association with radial nerve palsy.
  mri: >-
    Denervation edema of supinator and the forearm extensors in PIN syndrome. MRI can show a compressive mass
    (ganglion, lipoma) at the arcade of Frohse.
  ultrasound: >-
    High-resolution ultrasound can follow the nerve along the **spiral groove** after humeral fracture to check
    continuity or entrapment by fragments, and can image the deep branch entering supinator.
  keyViews:
    - "Humerus AP and lateral (include shoulder and elbow)"
    - "Ultrasound along the posterolateral humerus, spiral groove"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    Crosses the posterior humerus roughly at mid-shaft. The superficial branch can be rolled over the distal radius
    just proximal to the anatomical snuffbox.
  testing: >-
    Wrist extension, finger extension at the MCP joints, and thumb extension against resistance. Triceps strength
    (elbow extension). Triceps (C7) and brachioradialis (C6) reflexes. Sensation over the dorsal first web space.
    **Localize the lesion:** triceps weak → axilla; triceps spared with wrist drop → radial groove; wrist extends with
    radial deviation but fingers drop → PIN.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 34-year-old man sustains a midshaft fracture of the right humerus. He cannot extend his wrist or fingers and
      has decreased sensation over the dorsum of the first web space. Elbow extension is normal. Where is the nerve
      injured?
    choices:
      - In the axilla
      - In the radial groove of the humerus
      - At the arcade of Frohse
      - In the anatomical snuffbox
      - At the medial epicondyle
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Wrist drop with dorsal-hand sensory loss but **preserved triceps** localizes to the **radial groove**, because
      the triceps branches arise proximally. An axillary lesion would weaken triceps too. A lesion at the arcade of
      Frohse (PIN) causes no sensory loss and spares wrist extension through ECRL.
  - stem: >-
      A 45-year-old carpenter cannot extend his fingers at the MCP joints. When he extends the wrist, it deviates
      radially. Sensation is normal throughout the hand. Which nerve is most likely compressed?
    choices:
      - Superficial branch of the radial nerve
      - Radial nerve in the spiral groove
      - Anterior interosseous nerve
      - Posterior interosseous nerve
      - Ulnar nerve at Guyon's canal
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      **PIN syndrome**: the deep branch is compressed at the supinator. ECRL (supplied before the division) still
      extends the wrist, but ECU is paralyzed, so the wrist deviates radially. The PIN carries no cutaneous fibres,
      so sensation is normal.
  - stem: >-
      A patient who has been using axillary crutches incorrectly develops wrist drop. Which additional finding would
      confirm that the lesion is in the axilla rather than the arm?
    choices:
      - Loss of sensation over the palmar thumb
      - Weakness of elbow extension
      - Weakness of thumb opposition
      - Clawing of the fourth and fifth digits
      - Weak shoulder abduction
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The branches to **triceps** leave the radial nerve in the axilla/proximal arm. A lesion there weakens elbow
      extension. Palmar thumb sensation and opposition are median; clawing is ulnar; shoulder abduction is axillary.
  - stem: Which artery accompanies the radial nerve in the radial groove?
    choices:
      - Superior ulnar collateral artery
      - Posterior circumflex humeral artery
      - Profunda brachii artery
      - Radial recurrent artery
      - Brachial artery
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **profunda brachii (deep brachial) artery**, a branch of the [[brachial-artery]], accompanies the radial
      nerve through the triangular interval and along the radial groove.
