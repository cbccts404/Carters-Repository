---
name: Subscapularis
type: muscle
region: upper-limb
subregion: Pectoral & scapular region
taName: Musculus subscapularis
summary: Large rotator cuff muscle on the costal surface of the scapula. It is the main medial rotator of the arm and the only cuff muscle inserting on the lesser tubercle.
tags: [rotator-cuff, shoulder]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Subscapular fossa, forming most of the posterior wall of the [[axilla]].
  group: Rotator cuff (SITS); scapulohumeral muscles.
  origin: Subscapular fossa (costal/anterior surface) of the [[scapula]].
  insertion: Lesser tubercle of the [[humerus]].
  innervation: "Upper and lower subscapular nerves from the posterior cord (C5, C6) {{verify: some texts list C5–C7}}"
  bloodSupply: "Subscapular artery and its branches {{verify}}"
  action:
    - Medially (internally) rotates and adducts the arm
    - With the other rotator cuff muscles, holds the humeral head in the glenoid cavity (anterior stabilizer)
  relations: >-
    The tendon passes anterior to the [[glenohumeral-joint]] capsule, separated from the scapular neck by the
    subtendinous bursa of subscapularis, which usually communicates with the joint cavity. It forms part of the
    stabilizing sling for the long head of [[biceps-brachii]] at the entrance of the intertubercular groove.
clinical:
  - title: Subscapularis tear
    highYield: false
    mechanism: >-
      Forced external rotation or hyperextension, or **anterior dislocation** (especially in patients over ~40). Can
      also be degenerative.
    presentation: >-
      Anterior shoulder pain and weak internal rotation. Increased passive external rotation compared with the other
      side. Positive lift-off and belly-press tests. May be associated with **medial subluxation/dislocation of the
      long head of biceps** tendon.
pance:
  - "Subscapularis = main **internal rotator**; inserts on the **lesser tubercle**."
  - "Tests: **lift-off (Gerber)**, **belly-press**, bear-hug."
  - "Consider a subscapularis tear in an older patient with persistent weakness after an anterior dislocation."
imaging:
  xray: Usually normal.
  mri: >-
    **Axial** images are best for the subscapularis tendon and lesser tuberosity insertion. Look for an empty or
    medially displaced biceps tendon from its groove when the subscapularis tendon is torn.
  ultrasound: >-
    Scanned anteriorly with the arm externally rotated. The tendon is seen in long axis inserting on the lesser
    tuberosity. Dynamic rotation can show medial subluxation of the biceps tendon.
  keyViews:
    - "MRI axial proton density fat-sat through the lesser tuberosity"
exam:
  landmarks: The muscle is largely inaccessible. The tendon insertion can be palpated at the lesser tubercle, medial to the intertubercular groove, with the arm externally rotated.
  testing: Resisted internal rotation with the elbow at the side.
  specialTests:
    - name: Lift-off test (Gerber)
      technique: >-
        The patient places the dorsum of the hand on the lower back (lumbar region) and tries to lift the hand away from
        the back.
      positive: Unable to lift the hand off the back, or does so only by extending the elbow or shoulder.
      significance: Subscapularis tear or dysfunction. Requires enough internal rotation to reach the position.
    - name: Belly-press test
      technique: >-
        The patient presses the palm against the abdomen with the wrist straight, bringing the elbow forward in front
        of the trunk.
      positive: The elbow drops back behind the trunk, or the wrist flexes to generate pressure.
      significance: Subscapularis tear. Useful when the patient can't reach behind the back.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 62-year-old man dislocated his shoulder anteriorly 6 weeks ago. He still has anterior shoulder pain. When he
      places the back of his hand against his lower back, he can't lift it away. Which tendon is most likely torn?
    choices:
      - Supraspinatus
      - Infraspinatus
      - Teres minor
      - Subscapularis
      - Long head of biceps brachii
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      A positive **lift-off test** indicates **subscapularis** weakness. Subscapularis is an anterior stabilizer and can
      tear with anterior dislocation, especially in older patients.
  - stem: Subscapularis inserts onto which structure?
    choices:
      - Greater tubercle
      - Lesser tubercle
      - Intertubercular groove floor
      - Deltoid tuberosity
      - Coracoid process
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Subscapularis is the only rotator cuff muscle that inserts on the **lesser tubercle**. The other three insert on the
      greater tubercle.
  - stem: Which nerves supply subscapularis?
    choices:
      - Suprascapular nerve
      - Upper and lower subscapular nerves
      - Thoracodorsal nerve
      - Axillary nerve
      - Medial pectoral nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **upper and lower subscapular nerves** (posterior cord) supply subscapularis. The lower subscapular nerve also
      supplies teres major, and the thoracodorsal nerve supplies latissimus dorsi.
