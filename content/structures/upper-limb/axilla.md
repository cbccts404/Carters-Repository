name: Axilla
type: space
region: upper-limb
subregion: Axilla
taName: Fossa axillaris
aka: [Armpit, Axillary fossa]
summary: Pyramidal space between the arm and the thoracic wall. It is the passageway for the neurovascular bundle of the upper limb and contains the axillary lymph nodes, which drain most of the breast.
tags: [axilla, breast]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Inferior to the glenohumeral joint, between the upper arm and the lateral thoracic wall.
  boundaries:
    apex: "Cervicoaxillary canal: clavicle (anterior), first rib (medial), superior border of the scapula (posterior)"
    base: Axillary fascia and skin of the armpit
    anterior wall: Pectoralis major and pectoralis minor (with subclavius and the clavipectoral fascia)
    posterior wall: "[[subscapularis]] (mainly), teres major and latissimus dorsi"
    medial wall: "Thoracic wall (ribs 1–4 and intercostal muscles) covered by [[serratus-anterior]]"
    lateral wall: Intertubercular groove of the humerus
  contents:
    - "[[axillary-artery]] and its branches"
    - Axillary vein and its tributaries
    - "Cords and branches of the [[brachial-plexus]]"
    - "Axillary lymph nodes (five groups: pectoral/anterior, subscapular/posterior, humeral/lateral, central, apical)"
    - "[[long-thoracic-nerve]] on the medial wall; thoracodorsal nerve on the posterior wall; intercostobrachial nerve crossing the space"
    - Axillary fat and the axillary sheath (surrounding the artery and plexus)
  communications: >-
    With the posterior triangle of the neck through the apex (cervicoaxillary canal); with the arm; and with the scapular
    region through the quadrangular space, triangular space and triangular interval.
  notes: >-
    **Surgical levels of axillary nodes** (by their relation to pectoralis minor): **Level I** lateral/inferior,
    **Level II** deep (posterior), **Level III** medial/superior (apical). Most lymph from the breast (about 75%) drains
    to the axillary nodes, first to the pectoral (anterior) group.
clinical:
  - title: Axillary lymphadenopathy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Drainage of the upper limb, breast and lateral trunk. Causes include infection (hand or arm cellulitis, cat-scratch disease), **breast cancer metastasis**, lymphoma and other malignancy.
    presentation: >-
      Palpable nodes. Hard, fixed or matted nodes raise concern for malignancy. In breast cancer, axillary status
      determines staging; sentinel lymph node biopsy identifies the first node(s) draining the tumor.
  - title: Complications of axillary lymph node dissection
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Surgical injury to nerves on the walls of the axilla and disruption of lymphatic drainage.
    presentation: >-
      **Long thoracic nerve** → winged scapula. **Thoracodorsal nerve** → weak latissimus dorsi (extension, adduction,
      medial rotation). **Intercostobrachial nerve** → numbness of the medial arm and axilla. **Lymphedema** of the
      upper limb.
  - title: Hidradenitis suppurativa
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Chronic inflammatory disease of follicles in intertriginous skin (axillae, groin).
    presentation: Recurrent painful nodules, abscesses, sinus tracts and scarring in the axilla.
pance:
  - "Most breast lymph → **axillary nodes** (pectoral group first); level I–III defined by **pectoralis minor**."
  - "Axillary dissection injuries: **long thoracic** (winging), **thoracodorsal** (latissimus), **intercostobrachial** (medial arm numbness); **lymphedema**."
  - "Walls: anterior (pectorals), posterior (subscapularis, teres major, latissimus), medial (serratus anterior), lateral (intertubercular groove)."
imaging:
  xray: Soft tissue only. Calcified nodes may be seen.
  ct: >-
    Contrast CT of the chest shows axillary adenopathy (size, necrosis) for lymphoma or cancer staging and axillary
    vascular injury.
  mri: Breast MRI includes the axilla. MR neurography for the plexus.
  ultrasound: >-
    First-line for axillary nodes. **Normal nodes** are oval with a thin cortex and a fatty echogenic hilum.
    Suspicious features are **cortical thickening, loss of the fatty hilum**, a round shape, and non-hilar blood flow.
    It guides core biopsy. The axillary artery is the landmark for axillary brachial plexus block.
  keyViews:
    - "Mammography MLO view includes the low axilla"
    - "Axillary ultrasound ± core biopsy"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    **Anterior axillary fold** (lower border of pectoralis major) and **posterior axillary fold** (latissimus dorsi and
    teres major). The humeral head and axillary artery pulse can be felt in the lateral axilla.
  palpation: >-
    With the patient's arm relaxed and supported by the examiner, reach high into the apex with cupped fingers and press
    against the chest wall. Then examine each group: **central** (against the chest wall), **pectoral** (inside the
    anterior fold), **subscapular** (inside the posterior fold), and **lateral** (along the upper humerus). Also check
    the infraclavicular and supraclavicular nodes.
  testing: After axillary surgery, test serratus anterior (wall push), latissimus dorsi (resisted extension/adduction) and medial arm sensation.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      Two months after a left modified radical mastectomy with axillary dissection, a patient has numbness over the
      medial upper arm and axilla. Which nerve was most likely injured?
    choices:
      - Long thoracic nerve
      - Thoracodorsal nerve
      - Intercostobrachial nerve
      - Axillary nerve
      - Medial pectoral nerve
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **intercostobrachial nerve** (lateral cutaneous branch of T2) crosses the axilla to supply the skin of the
      medial arm and axilla. It is often injured in axillary dissection. The long thoracic and thoracodorsal nerves
      are motor nerves.
  - stem: Axillary lymph node levels are defined by their relationship to which muscle?
    choices:
      - Pectoralis major
      - Pectoralis minor
      - Latissimus dorsi
      - Serratus anterior
      - Subclavius
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Level I**: lateral/inferior to pectoralis minor. **Level II**: deep to it. **Level III**: medial/superior to it
      (apical).
  - stem: Which muscle forms most of the posterior wall of the axilla?
    choices:
      - Serratus anterior
      - Pectoralis minor
      - Subscapularis
      - Coracobrachialis
      - Trapezius
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **posterior wall** is formed mainly by **subscapularis**, with teres major and latissimus dorsi inferiorly.
      Serratus anterior forms the medial wall.
  - stem: On ultrasound, which feature of an axillary lymph node is most suggestive of malignancy?
    choices:
      - Oval shape with a fatty echogenic hilum
      - Thin, uniform cortex
      - Cortical thickening with loss of the fatty hilum
      - Size of 5 mm with preserved hilum
      - Hilar blood flow on Doppler
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Cortical thickening and loss of the fatty hilum** (plus a round shape and non-hilar vascularity) suggest
      metastatic involvement. The other options describe normal-appearing nodes.
