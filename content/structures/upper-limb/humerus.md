summary: Long bone of the arm. It articulates with the scapula at the shoulder and with the radius and ulna at the elbow, and four major nerves lie against it at predictable fracture sites.
tags: [shoulder, elbow]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Arm, from the glenohumeral joint to the elbow.
  classification: Long bone.
  features:
    - "**Head**: articulates with the glenoid cavity of the [[scapula]]"
    - "**Anatomical neck**: groove just beyond the head; attachment of the joint capsule"
    - "**Surgical neck**: narrowing distal to the tubercles; a common fracture site ([[axillary-nerve]], posterior circumflex humeral artery)"
    - "**Greater tubercle** (supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor) and **lesser tubercle** (subscapularis)"
    - "**Intertubercular (bicipital) groove**: long head of biceps tendon"
    - "**Deltoid tuberosity**: insertion of [[deltoid]]"
    - "**Radial (spiral) groove**: posterior shaft; [[radial-nerve]] and profunda brachii artery"
    - "**Medial and lateral epicondyles**: common flexor and common extensor origins; the [[ulnar-nerve]] passes behind the medial epicondyle"
    - "**Capitulum** (articulates with the radial head) and **trochlea** (articulates with the ulna)"
    - "**Olecranon fossa** (posterior), **coronoid fossa** and **radial fossa** (anterior)"
    - "Medial and lateral supracondylar ridges"
  relations: >-
    **Nerves in direct contact with the humerus:** axillary nerve (surgical neck), radial nerve (radial groove/shaft),
    median nerve and brachial artery (anterior to the supracondylar region), ulnar nerve (posterior to the medial
    epicondyle).
clinical:
  - title: Proximal humerus (surgical neck) fracture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Fall on an outstretched hand or onto the shoulder, especially in **older women with osteoporosis**.
    presentation: Pain, swelling and extensive bruising of the arm and chest wall. Check axillary nerve function (deltoid, regimental badge sensation).
  - title: Humeral shaft fracture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Direct blow, fall, or twisting (spiral) injury.
    presentation: Deformity of the arm. **Radial nerve palsy (wrist drop)** is the classic associated injury, especially with distal-third spiral fractures.
  - title: Supracondylar fracture (pediatric)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Most common elbow fracture in children, usually a **fall on an outstretched hand with the elbow hyperextended**
      (extension type, distal fragment displaced posteriorly).
    presentation: >-
      Elbow pain, swelling and deformity. Assess the **brachial artery** (radial pulse, perfusion) and the **median
      nerve/AIN**, radial and ulnar nerves. Watch for **forearm compartment syndrome**; untreated ischemia leads to
      **Volkmann ischemic contracture**.
  - title: Medial epicondyle fracture
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Valgus stress or avulsion by the flexor–pronator mass, often in adolescents (e.g. with elbow dislocation).
    presentation: Medial elbow pain and swelling; the [[ulnar-nerve]] is at risk.
pance:
  - "Fracture sites → nerve at risk: **surgical neck → axillary**, **midshaft → radial**, **supracondylar → median (AIN) + brachial artery**, **medial epicondyle → ulnar**."
  - "Supracondylar fracture in a child: check pulses, watch for compartment syndrome (pain with passive stretch) → Volkmann contracture."
  - "**Posterior fat pad sign** on a lateral elbow film = effusion; in a child with trauma, suspect an occult supracondylar fracture."
  - "Proximal humerus fracture in an older adult is a fragility fracture; consider osteoporosis evaluation."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    **Shoulder:** AP, scapular Y and axillary views for proximal fractures. **Shaft:** AP and lateral humerus including
    both joints. **Elbow (pediatric):** on a true lateral view, the **anterior humeral line** should pass through the
    **middle third of the capitellum**. In extension-type supracondylar fractures it passes anterior to it. A visible
    **posterior fat pad** or elevated anterior fat pad ("sail sign") indicates an effusion. Know the ossification
    center sequence (CRITOE) so normal centers aren't mistaken for fractures.
  ct: Used for complex proximal humerus or intra-articular distal humerus fractures (surgical planning).
  mri: Occult fractures, bone bruises, osteonecrosis of the humeral head, and tumors.
  keyViews:
    - "Shoulder trauma series (AP, scapular Y, axillary)"
    - "Humerus AP and lateral"
    - "Elbow AP and true lateral at 90° (anterior humeral line, fat pads)"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    The greater tubercle is the most lateral bony point of the shoulder, felt below the acromion. The medial and
    lateral epicondyles are subcutaneous at the elbow. With the elbow **extended**, the two epicondyles and the tip of
    the olecranon lie in a **straight line**; with the elbow **flexed to 90°** they form an **isosceles triangle**.
    Elbow dislocation disrupts this relationship; a supracondylar fracture typically preserves it.
  palpation: Palpate the greater tubercle, bicipital groove, shaft (through the muscles) and both epicondyles. Palpate the ulnar nerve behind the medial epicondyle.
  testing: >-
    In humeral injuries test the axillary nerve (deltoid, lateral shoulder sensation), radial nerve (wrist and finger
    extension, dorsal first web space), median/AIN ("OK" sign, index pad sensation), ulnar nerve (finger abduction,
    little finger sensation), and the radial pulse and capillary refill.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 6-year-old girl falls from a trampoline onto her outstretched hand. Lateral radiograph of the elbow shows a
      posterior fat pad, and the anterior humeral line passes anterior to the capitellum. Which structure must be
      assessed most urgently?
    choices:
      - Axillary nerve
      - Brachial artery
      - Long thoracic nerve
      - Ulnar collateral ligament
      - Suprascapular nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      These findings indicate an **extension-type supracondylar fracture**. The **brachial artery** (and median
      nerve/AIN) lies directly anterior to the distal humerus and can be injured or kinked. Check the radial pulse
      and perfusion, and watch for compartment syndrome.
  - stem: Which nerve lies in direct contact with the humerus at the midshaft?
    choices:
      - Axillary nerve
      - Median nerve
      - Radial nerve
      - Ulnar nerve
      - Musculocutaneous nerve
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **radial nerve** lies in the radial (spiral) groove on the posterior midshaft. The axillary nerve is at the
      surgical neck and the ulnar nerve behind the medial epicondyle.
  - stem: >-
      A 78-year-old woman with osteoporosis falls onto her outstretched arm. Which fracture of the upper limb is she at
      particular risk of, and which nerve would that fracture endanger?
    choices:
      - Surgical neck fracture — axillary nerve
      - Supracondylar fracture — ulnar nerve
      - Midshaft fracture — musculocutaneous nerve
      - Medial epicondyle fracture — median nerve
      - Anatomical neck fracture — suprascapular nerve
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      **Proximal humerus (surgical neck) fractures** are common fragility fractures in older adults. The axillary nerve
      and posterior circumflex humeral artery wrap around the surgical neck.
  - stem: With the elbow flexed to 90°, the medial epicondyle, lateral epicondyle and olecranon normally form which configuration?
    choices:
      - A straight line
      - An isosceles triangle
      - A right angle with the olecranon at the apex
      - A square
      - An irregular shape that varies between individuals
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      In flexion the three points form an **isosceles triangle**; in extension they lie in a **straight line**. A
      posterior elbow dislocation disrupts this relationship.
