---
name: Radial artery
type: artery
region: upper-limb
subregion: Forearm
taName: Arteria radialis
summary: Smaller terminal branch of the brachial artery. It is palpated at the wrist, used for ABGs, arterial lines and cardiac catheterization, and forms most of the deep palmar arch.
tags: [forearm, wrist, hand]
systems: [cardiovascular]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Lateral anterior forearm → wrist (pulse point) → anatomical snuffbox → deep palm.
  origin: Terminal branch of the [[brachial-artery]] in the [[cubital-fossa]], at about the level of the neck of the radius.
  course: >-
    Descends in the lateral forearm, **deep to brachioradialis** proximally, then superficial in the distal forearm,
    where it lies **lateral to the flexor carpi radialis tendon** over the distal radius (pulse point). At the wrist it
    curves dorsally around the lateral carpus, crosses the floor of the **anatomical snuffbox** (over the [[scaphoid]]
    and trapezium), then passes between the two heads of the first dorsal interosseous muscle into the palm and between
    the heads of adductor pollicis to form the **deep palmar arch**.
  branches:
    - Radial recurrent artery (elbow anastomosis)
    - Muscular branches
    - Palmar carpal branch and superficial palmar branch (joins the superficial palmar arch)
    - Dorsal carpal branch (dorsal carpal arch)
    - Princeps pollicis artery and radialis indicis artery (thumb and index finger)
    - Deep palmar arch (with the deep palmar branch of the ulnar artery)
  supplies: Lateral forearm muscles, radius, carpal bones (including the retrograde supply of the scaphoid), thumb and index finger, deep palm.
  anastomoses: >-
    The **superficial palmar arch** (mainly from the ulnar artery) and the **deep palmar arch** (mainly from the radial
    artery) connect the two arteries, so either can usually supply the hand if the other is occluded. This dual supply
    is what the Allen test checks before radial artery procedures.
  termination: Deep palmar arch.
clinical:
  - title: Arterial puncture and cannulation
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Its superficial position over the distal radius and the hand's dual blood supply make it the preferred site for
      **arterial blood gas sampling** and **arterial lines**. It's also used for **transradial cardiac
      catheterization**.
    presentation: >-
      Complications include hematoma, thrombosis or occlusion, pseudoaneurysm, and rarely hand ischemia. Assess
      collateral (ulnar) flow beforehand.
  - title: Radial artery harvest (CABG conduit)
    highYield: false
    mechanism: The radial artery is used as a conduit in coronary artery bypass grafting.
    presentation: Adequate ulnar collateral flow to the hand must be confirmed before harvest.
pance:
  - "Radial pulse: **lateral to the FCR tendon** at the distal radius."
  - "Perform an **Allen test** (or modified Allen) before radial artery puncture or cannulation."
  - "Radial artery → mainly the **deep** palmar arch; ulnar artery → mainly the **superficial** arch."
  - "Radial artery crosses the **anatomical snuffbox**, over the scaphoid and trapezium."
imaging:
  xray: Not visible, though calcification may be seen in diabetes or end-stage kidney disease (medial arterial calcification).
  ct: CT angiography for trauma or ischemia of the hand.
  ultrasound: >-
    **Ultrasound-guided radial arterial line placement** improves first-pass success. Duplex shows patency, diameter
    (before catheterization or harvest) and complications such as thrombosis or pseudoaneurysm.
  keyViews:
    - "Transverse ultrasound at the distal radius, lateral to FCR"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    **Wrist:** pulse palpated against the distal radius, lateral to the FCR tendon, just proximal to the wrist crease.
    **Snuffbox:** pulse palpable in the floor of the anatomical snuffbox.
  palpation: Use the pads of the index and middle fingers. Compare both sides for rate, rhythm and volume.
  specialTests:
    - name: Allen test (modified)
      technique: >-
        The patient makes a tight fist. The examiner occludes both the radial and ulnar arteries at the wrist. The
        patient opens the hand (now blanched), and the examiner releases pressure on the **ulnar** artery only.
      positive: >-
        (Abnormal) Color takes too long to return to the palm. Thresholds of roughly 5–10 seconds are quoted.
        {{verify: normal-return threshold varies between sources}}
      significance: >-
        A delayed return suggests inadequate ulnar collateral flow, which argues against cannulating or harvesting the
        radial artery on that side. Repeating the test with radial release assesses the radial contribution.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      Before placing a radial arterial line, a PA occludes both the radial and ulnar arteries while the patient clenches
      the fist, then releases the ulnar artery. The palm stays pale for 15 seconds. What does this indicate?
    choices:
      - Normal result; proceed with radial cannulation
      - Inadequate ulnar collateral circulation; avoid cannulating this radial artery
      - Radial artery occlusion
      - Carpal tunnel syndrome
      - Raynaud phenomenon
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A delayed return of color after releasing the **ulnar** artery suggests poor ulnar collateral flow through the
      palmar arches. If the radial artery were then occluded by cannulation, the hand could become ischemic.
  - stem: The radial pulse at the wrist is palpated immediately lateral to which tendon?
    choices:
      - Palmaris longus
      - Flexor carpi radialis
      - Flexor carpi ulnaris
      - Abductor pollicis longus
      - Flexor pollicis longus
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The radial artery lies **lateral to the flexor carpi radialis** tendon, over the distal radius. The median nerve
      lies deep to palmaris longus, between FCR and FDS.
  - stem: The radial artery contributes mainly to which structure in the hand?
    choices:
      - Superficial palmar arch
      - Deep palmar arch
      - Dorsal venous network
      - Common palmar digital arteries of digits 3–5
      - Hypothenar branch
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The radial artery forms most of the **deep palmar arch**, joined by the deep palmar branch of the ulnar artery. The
      superficial arch is formed mainly by the [[ulnar-artery]].
