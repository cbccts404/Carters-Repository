---
name: Serratus anterior
type: muscle
region: upper-limb
subregion: Pectoral & scapular region
taName: Musculus serratus anterior
aka: ["Boxer's muscle"]
summary: Serrated muscle on the lateral thoracic wall that protracts the scapula, rotates it upward, and holds it against the chest wall.
tags: [shoulder]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Lateral thoracic wall, forming the medial wall of the [[axilla]]. Lies deep to the scapula posteriorly.
  group: Anterior axioappendicular muscles.
  origin: >-
    External surfaces of the lateral parts of the upper **8–9 ribs** by fleshy digitations.
    {{verify: textbooks give ribs 1–8 or 1–9}}
  insertion: Anterior (costal) surface of the medial border of the [[scapula]], including the inferior angle.
  innervation: "[[long-thoracic-nerve]] (C5, C6, C7)"
  bloodSupply: "Lateral thoracic and thoracodorsal arteries {{verify}}"
  action:
    - Protracts the scapula (drawing it anterolaterally, as when punching)
    - Holds the scapula against the thoracic wall
    - Rotates the scapula **upward** (inferior angle moves laterally), with trapezius, allowing the arm to be raised above 90°
  relations: >-
    The long thoracic nerve runs on its **superficial** surface. The lower digitations interdigitate with the origin of
    the external oblique.
clinical:
  - title: Winged scapula (serratus anterior paralysis)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Injury to the [[long-thoracic-nerve]]: axillary dissection, mastectomy, chest tubes, blows to the lateral chest,
      heavy shoulder loads.
    presentation: >-
      The **medial border and inferior angle** of the scapula project posteriorly, especially when pushing against a
      wall. The arm is hard to abduct above the horizontal.
pance:
  - "Serratus anterior: **protraction + upward rotation** of the scapula. Long thoracic nerve (C5–C7)."
  - "Medial scapular winging after axillary surgery."
imaging:
  xray: The digitations are not visible. Chest radiographs are used for associated rib pathology.
  ct: Cross-sectional imaging shows a sheet of muscle along the lateral ribs with a "sawtooth" arrangement of digitations.
  mri: Denervation edema or fatty atrophy in long thoracic nerve palsy.
  ultrasound: >-
    Visible superficial to the ribs in the mid-axillary line, deep to latissimus dorsi. This plane is the target of
    the **serratus anterior plane block** used for chest wall analgesia (e.g. rib fractures).
exam:
  landmarks: The digitations are visible and palpable on the lateral chest wall below the axilla in muscular people.
  testing: Resisted protraction (pushing forward against the examiner's hand). Observe the scapula during wall push-ups.
  specialTests:
    - name: Wall push-up test
      technique: The patient pushes against a wall with the arms extended at shoulder height while the examiner watches the scapulae.
      positive: The medial border and inferior angle lift from the chest wall.
      significance: Serratus anterior weakness (long thoracic nerve).
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A patient has difficulty raising the arm above the head after a lateral chest wall injury. Weakness of which
      scapular movement best explains this?
    choices:
      - Retraction
      - Elevation
      - Depression
      - Upward rotation
      - Downward rotation
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      Serratus anterior (with trapezius) **rotates the scapula upward** so the glenoid faces superiorly, which is needed
      for abduction above 90°. The long thoracic nerve on the lateral chest wall is vulnerable to such injuries.
  - stem: Serratus anterior inserts on which part of the scapula?
    choices:
      - Coracoid process
      - Lateral border
      - Anterior surface of the medial border
      - Spine of the scapula
      - Supraglenoid tubercle
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      Serratus anterior runs from the lateral ribs to the **costal (anterior) surface of the medial border** of the
      scapula. That's why its paralysis lets the medial border lift away from the chest wall.
  - stem: Which muscle forms most of the medial wall of the axilla?
    choices:
      - Subscapularis
      - Pectoralis minor
      - Serratus anterior
      - Latissimus dorsi
      - Coracobrachialis
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The medial wall of the [[axilla]] is the thoracic wall covered by **serratus anterior**. Subscapularis forms most
      of the posterior wall and pectoralis major/minor the anterior wall.
