name: Musculocutaneous nerve
type: nerve
region: upper-limb
subregion: Arm
taName: Nervus musculocutaneus
summary: Lateral cord branch that pierces coracobrachialis, supplies the anterior arm muscles, and ends as the lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm.
tags: [brachial-plexus]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Anterior compartment of the arm, between [[biceps-brachii]] and brachialis.
  roots: C5, C6, C7
  origin: Terminal branch of the lateral cord of the [[brachial-plexus]].
  course: >-
    Leaves the [[axilla]] by **piercing coracobrachialis**, then descends between biceps brachii and brachialis,
    supplying all three anterior arm muscles. Lateral to the biceps tendon, just above the elbow, it pierces the deep
    fascia and continues as the **lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm** (lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve).
  branches:
    - Muscular branches to coracobrachialis, [[biceps-brachii]] and brachialis
    - Lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm (terminal sensory branch)
  motor: Coracobrachialis, biceps brachii, brachialis (all flexor muscles of the anterior arm).
  sensory: Skin of the lateral (radial) forearm, via the lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm.
  relations: >-
    In the axilla it lies lateral to the axillary artery and median nerve. In the arm it runs between biceps
    (superficial) and brachialis (deep).
clinical:
  - title: Musculocutaneous nerve injury
    highYield: false
    mechanism: >-
      Uncommon in isolation because the nerve is protected deep in the arm. Causes include penetrating trauma to the
      arm or axilla, shoulder dislocation or surgery, and compression during strenuous exercise or prolonged
      positioning.
    presentation: >-
      Weak elbow flexion and supination. Some flexion remains through brachioradialis (radial nerve) and some
      supination through supinator. Reduced or absent **biceps reflex**. Sensory loss over the lateral forearm.
  - title: Lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm injury
    highYield: false
    mechanism: >-
      Irritation near the cubital fossa, e.g. during venipuncture of the cephalic or median cubital vein, or
      compression by the biceps tendon/aponeurosis.
    presentation: Burning pain or numbness along the lateral forearm, with no motor deficit.
pance:
  - "Pierces **coracobrachialis**; supplies the three anterior-arm flexors (coracobrachialis, biceps, brachialis)."
  - "**Biceps reflex** (C5–C6) tests the musculocutaneous nerve."
  - "Continues as the **lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm**."
imaging:
  xray: Not visible. Radiographs assess associated shoulder or humeral injury.
  mri: MRI shows denervation edema or atrophy of biceps and brachialis in chronic injury.
  ultrasound: >-
    Commonly identified during **axillary brachial plexus block**. At the axilla it often lies separately from the
    other terminal nerves, within or between coracobrachialis and biceps, so it usually needs a separate injection.
  keyViews:
    - "Ultrasound in the axilla, short axis to the axillary artery"
exam:
  testing: >-
    Resisted elbow flexion with the forearm **supinated** (isolates biceps and brachialis from brachioradialis).
    Biceps reflex (C5–C6). Pinprick sensation over the lateral forearm.

    explanation: >-
      The **musculocutaneous nerve** supplies biceps and brachialis and continues as the lateral cutaneous nerve of the
      forearm. Elbow flexion is not lost completely because brachioradialis (radial nerve) still contributes.

    choices:

      - Teres major
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The musculocutaneous nerve typically **pierces coracobrachialis** and then descends between biceps brachii and
      brachialis.
  - stem: A patient has numbness of the lateral forearm after venipuncture at the cubital fossa. Which nerve was most likely irritated?
    choices:
      - Superficial branch of the radial nerve
      - Medial cutaneous nerve of the forearm
      - Lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm
      - Posterior interosseous nerve
      - Anterior interosseous nerve
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm**, the terminal branch of the musculocutaneous nerve, becomes
      superficial lateral to the biceps tendon near the cephalic and median cubital veins. The PIN and AIN are
      deep motor nerves.
