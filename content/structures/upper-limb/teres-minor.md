name: Teres minor
type: muscle
region: upper-limb
subregion: Pectoral & scapular region
taName: Musculus teres minor
summary: Small rotator cuff muscle from the lateral border of the scapula. It laterally rotates the arm and is supplied by the axillary nerve.
tags: [rotator-cuff, shoulder]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Along the lateral border of the scapula, inferior to infraspinatus (often partly blended with it).
  group: Rotator cuff (SITS); scapulohumeral muscles.
  origin: Middle part of the lateral border of the [[scapula]].
  insertion: Inferior facet of the greater tubercle of the [[humerus]].
  innervation: "[[axillary-nerve]] (C5, C6), via its posterior branch"
  bloodSupply: "Circumflex scapular and posterior circumflex humeral arteries {{verify}}"
  action:
    - Laterally (externally) rotates the arm
    - With the other rotator cuff muscles, holds the humeral head in the glenoid cavity
  relations: >-
    Forms the **superior boundary of the quadrangular space** (axillary nerve and posterior circumflex humeral
    artery). Together with teres major and the long head of triceps it also bounds the **triangular space** (circumflex
    scapular artery). It is the only rotator cuff muscle **not** supplied by the suprascapular or subscapular nerves.
clinical:
  - title: Quadrilateral space syndrome
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Compression of the axillary nerve in the quadrangular space, often in overhead athletes.
    presentation: >-
      Posterior shoulder pain and paresthesias. On MRI, **isolated teres minor atrophy/denervation** is a
      characteristic finding.
  - title: Axillary nerve injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Surgical neck fracture of the humerus or anterior shoulder dislocation.
    presentation: Weak lateral rotation (teres minor) together with deltoid weakness and lateral shoulder numbness.
pance:
  - "Teres minor is the only rotator cuff muscle innervated by the **axillary nerve**."
  - "Superior border of the **quadrangular space**."
  - "Hornblower's sign tests teres minor."
imaging:
  xray: Not assessed directly.
  mri: >-
    Sagittal oblique images show muscle bulk. Isolated fatty atrophy or edema of teres minor suggests axillary nerve
    compression (quadrilateral space syndrome). Tendon tears are uncommon and usually part of a massive cuff tear.
  ultrasound: Seen from a posterior approach, inferior to infraspinatus, inserting on the lowest facet of the greater tuberosity.
exam:
  landmarks: Lies along the lateral border of the scapula. Difficult to separate from infraspinatus by palpation.
  testing: Resisted lateral rotation, especially with the arm abducted (teres minor contributes more in abduction).
  specialTests:
    - name: Hornblower's sign
      technique: >-
        The arm is supported at 90° of abduction in the scapular plane with the elbow flexed at 90°. The patient tries
        to externally rotate against resistance (or bring the hand to the mouth).
      positive: >-
        Unable to externally rotate in this position, or has to abduct the arm further (elbow rising like a horn
        player's).
      significance: Teres minor dysfunction, usually with a massive posterosuperior cuff tear.
quiz:
  - stem: Which rotator cuff muscle is innervated by the axillary nerve?
    choices:
      - Supraspinatus
      - Infraspinatus
      - Teres minor
      - Subscapularis
      - Teres major
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Teres minor** is supplied by the posterior branch of the axillary nerve. Supraspinatus and infraspinatus are
      supplied by the suprascapular nerve, subscapularis by the upper and lower subscapular nerves. Teres major is not
      part of the rotator cuff.
  - stem: Teres minor forms which boundary of the quadrangular space?
    choices:
      - Superior
      - Inferior
      - Medial
      - Lateral
      - Anterior
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      Quadrangular space boundaries: **teres minor (superior)**, teres major (inferior), long head of triceps (medial),
      surgical neck of the humerus (lateral).
  - stem: >-
      MRI of a 24-year-old baseball pitcher with posterior shoulder pain shows fatty atrophy limited to teres minor. Which
      structure is most likely compressed?
    choices:
      - Suprascapular nerve at the spinoglenoid notch
      - Axillary nerve in the quadrangular space
      - Long thoracic nerve on serratus anterior
      - Radial nerve in the triangular interval
      - Thoracodorsal nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Isolated teres minor denervation points to the **axillary nerve** in the **quadrangular space** (quadrilateral
      space syndrome). Deltoid may be involved too.
