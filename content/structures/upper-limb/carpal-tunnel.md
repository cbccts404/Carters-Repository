region: upper-limb
subregion: Wrist & hand
taName: Canalis carpi
summary: Osseofibrous tunnel on the palmar wrist, formed by the carpal bones and the flexor retinaculum, that carries the median nerve and nine long flexor tendons.
tags: [wrist, carpal-tunnel]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Palmar aspect of the wrist, deep to the flexor retinaculum.
  boundaries:
    roof: >-
      **Flexor retinaculum** (transverse carpal ligament). Medial attachments: **pisiform** and **hook of hamate**.
      Lateral attachments: **scaphoid tubercle** and **trapezium tubercle**.
    floor and walls: The concave palmar arch formed by the carpal bones
  contents:
    - "[[median-nerve]] (the most superficial structure in the tunnel)"
    - Four tendons of flexor digitorum superficialis
    - Four tendons of flexor digitorum profundus
    - One tendon of flexor pollicis longus
    - "Synovial sheaths: the common flexor sheath (ulnar bursa) for FDS/FDP and a separate sheath (radial bursa) for FPL"
  communications: Leads from the anterior compartment of the forearm to the central compartment of the palm.
  notes: >-
    **Not in the tunnel:** the ulnar nerve and artery (superficial to the retinaculum, in **Guyon's canal**); the
    palmar cutaneous branch of the median nerve and the palmaris longus tendon (superficial to the retinaculum). The
    flexor carpi radialis tendon runs in its own split within the retinaculum (a separate compartment).
    Mnemonic: **9 tendons + 1 nerve**.
clinical:
  - title: Carpal tunnel syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Anything that reduces the space or enlarges the contents compresses the median nerve: **pregnancy** (fluid),
      **hypothyroidism**, **diabetes**, **rheumatoid arthritis** (flexor tenosynovitis), obesity, acromegaly,
      dialysis-related amyloidosis, distal radius fracture, lunate dislocation, and repetitive wrist use.
    presentation: >-
      Nocturnal paresthesias of the thumb, index, middle and radial half of the ring finger. Hand-shaking relief.
      Clumsiness. Late thenar atrophy and weakness of thumb abduction. **Thenar skin sensation is spared.**
  - title: Carpal tunnel release
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Surgical division of the flexor retinaculum (open or endoscopic) for severe or refractory symptoms.
    presentation: >-
      The **recurrent (thenar motor) branch** of the median nerve is at risk, particularly with anatomic variants
      where it passes through the retinaculum. Injury leaves the patient unable to oppose the thumb.
pance:
  - "Carpal tunnel contents: **median nerve + 9 flexor tendons** (4 FDS, 4 FDP, 1 FPL)."
  - "The ulnar nerve and artery are **not** in the carpal tunnel (Guyon's canal)."
  - "CTS associations: pregnancy, hypothyroidism, diabetes, RA, acromegaly, amyloidosis."
  - "Diagnosis is clinical (Tinel, Phalen, Durkan); **nerve conduction studies/EMG** confirm and grade severity."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    The **carpal tunnel view** (axial projection with the wrist dorsiflexed) shows the pisiform, the hook of hamate, and
    the tubercles of the scaphoid and trapezium, the attachments of the retinaculum. It is useful for hook of hamate
    fractures and bony encroachment.
  ct: Hook of hamate fractures and bony masses.
  mri: >-
    Swelling of the median nerve proximally, flattening distally, increased T2 signal, bowing of the flexor
    retinaculum, and tenosynovitis or masses.
  ultrasound: >-
    Shows the median nerve's cross-sectional area at the tunnel inlet, flexor tenosynovitis, anatomic variants
    (bifid median nerve, persistent median artery), and ganglia. Guides corticosteroid injection.
  keyViews:
    - "Carpal tunnel (axial) radiograph"
    - "Ultrasound transverse at the pisiform and hook of hamate levels"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    The **distal wrist crease** roughly marks the proximal edge of the flexor retinaculum, which extends distally into
    the palm. Palpable attachments: **pisiform** (base of the hypothenar eminence), **hook of hamate** (deep, distal and radial to the
    pisiform), **scaphoid tubercle** and **trapezium tubercle** (base of the thenar eminence).
  palpation: Palpate the four retinacular attachments. Look for thenar atrophy (flattening of the lateral eminence).
  testing: Thumb abduction strength (APB). Two-point discrimination on the index pad. Thenar sensation (spared in CTS).
  specialTests:
    - name: Tinel sign
      technique: Tap over the carpal tunnel.
      positive: Tingling in the median-innervated digits.
      significance: Carpal tunnel syndrome.
    - name: Phalen test
      technique: Full wrist flexion for 60 seconds.
      positive: Median-distribution paresthesias.
      significance: Carpal tunnel syndrome.
    - name: Carpal compression (Durkan) test
      technique: Direct pressure over the carpal tunnel for up to 30 seconds.
      positive: Median-distribution paresthesias.
      significance: Carpal tunnel syndrome.

    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The carpal tunnel contains the median nerve and **nine tendons**: 4 FDS, 4 FDP and **FPL**. The ulnar nerve and

      retinaculum.
  - stem: >-
      A 48-year-old woman with fatigue, cold intolerance and weight gain reports bilateral nocturnal hand numbness in

    choices:
      - Serum uric acid
      - TSH
      - ANA
      - Serum calcium
      - HLA-B27
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Her symptoms suggest **hypothyroidism**, a recognized cause of **carpal tunnel syndrome** (myxedematous tissue
      deposition). Check **TSH**. Other associations include pregnancy, diabetes, RA and acromegaly.
  - stem: The medial attachments of the flexor retinaculum are which structures?
    choices:
      - Scaphoid tubercle and trapezium
      - Pisiform and hook of hamate
      - Lunate and triquetrum
      - Capitate and hamate body
      - Ulnar styloid and pisiform
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The flexor retinaculum attaches **medially to the pisiform and hook of hamate** and **laterally to the scaphoid
      tubercle and trapezium**.
  - stem: During carpal tunnel release, injury to which branch would cause loss of thumb opposition?
    choices:
      - Palmar cutaneous branch of the median nerve
      - Recurrent (thenar) branch of the median nerve
      - Deep branch of the ulnar nerve
      - Anterior interosseous nerve
      - Superficial branch of the radial nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **recurrent branch** supplies abductor pollicis brevis, opponens pollicis and the superficial head of FPB.
      Injuring it causes loss of opposition. The palmar cutaneous branch is purely sensory.
