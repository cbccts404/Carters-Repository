name: Clavicle
type: bone
region: upper-limb
subregion: Pectoral & scapular region
taName: Clavicula
aka: [Collarbone]
summary: S-shaped strut connecting the upper limb to the axial skeleton; one of the most commonly fractured bones, usually in its middle third.
tags: [shoulder]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Subcutaneous along its whole length at the root of the neck, from the manubrium to the acromion.
  classification: "Long bone that lies horizontally. It is described as lacking a true medullary cavity (spongy bone within a compact shell). {{verify}}"
  features:
    - "**Sternal (medial) end:** enlarged, articulates with the manubrium at the sternoclavicular joint"
    - "**Acromial (lateral) end:** flat, articulates with the acromion at the acromioclavicular joint"
    - "**Medial two-thirds** convex anteriorly; **lateral third** flattened and concave anteriorly"
    - "**Conoid tubercle** and **trapezoid line** (inferior surface, lateral third): attachments of the coracoclavicular ligament"
    - "Impression for the costoclavicular ligament (inferior surface, medial end)"
    - "Subclavian groove (inferior surface, middle third): attachment of subclavius"
  ossification: >-
    The **first bone to begin ossifying** (around weeks 5–6 of gestation), largely by intramembranous ossification. The
    medial (sternal) epiphysis is among the last to fuse, in the early-to-mid 20s.
    {{verify: exact timing of medial epiphyseal fusion varies between sources}}
  relations: >-
    The subclavian vessels and the trunks and divisions of the [[brachial-plexus]] pass **posterior and inferior to the
    middle third**, protected by subclavius. It acts as a strut holding the limb away from the trunk and transmits
    force from the upper limb to the axial skeleton.
clinical:
  - title: Clavicle fracture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Fall onto the shoulder or on an outstretched hand. The **middle third** is the most common site (roughly
      80%). Also occurs in newborns during difficult deliveries (shoulder dystocia).
    presentation: >-
      Pain, swelling and a visible or palpable deformity. The patient supports the arm. The **medial fragment is pulled
      superiorly by sternocleidomastoid**, and the **lateral fragment is pulled down by the weight of the limb** and
      drawn medially by pectoralis major. Check the skin (tenting) and the neurovascular status of the limb.
  - title: Neonatal clavicle fracture
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Birth trauma, particularly with shoulder dystocia or large birth weight.
    presentation: Crepitus or a palpable lump, decreased movement of the arm, and an asymmetric Moro reflex. Must be distinguished from Erb palsy, which can coexist.
  - title: Cleidocranial dysplasia
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Autosomal dominant disorder (RUNX2) affecting intramembranous ossification.
    presentation: Hypoplastic or absent clavicles (the shoulders can be brought together in front of the chest), delayed closure of the fontanelles, and dental anomalies.
pance:
  - "Most common location of clavicle fracture: **middle third**."
  - "Medial fragment displaced **up** (SCM); lateral fragment displaced **down** (weight of the arm)."
  - "Most midshaft clavicle fractures are managed non-operatively with a sling; open fractures, skin tenting, neurovascular injury or marked displacement/shortening prompt orthopedic referral."
  - "Clavicle fracture in a newborn → asymmetric Moro reflex; check for coexisting brachial plexus injury."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    **AP clavicle** plus an **AP view with 15–30° cephalic tilt**, which projects the clavicle clear of the ribs and
    scapula to show displacement. Assess shortening and displacement. Include the AC and SC joints.
  ct: >-
    CT is preferred for **medial clavicle and sternoclavicular joint** injuries (especially posterior SC dislocation,
    which can compress the mediastinal great vessels or trachea) and for surgical planning.
  ultrasound: Can diagnose neonatal clavicle fractures without radiation.
  keyViews:
    - "AP clavicle"
    - "AP with 15–30° cephalic tilt"
    - "CT for sternoclavicular joint"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    Palpable along its whole length from the jugular notch to the acromion. Its lateral end forms a small step above
    the acromion at the AC joint.
  palpation: Palpate from the SC joint to the AC joint for step-off, crepitus and point tenderness. Check the skin over any deformity.
  testing: Assess distal pulses and brachial plexus function (motor and sensory) in any clavicle injury.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 19-year-old cyclist falls onto his left shoulder. There is a deformity in the middle of the clavicle. Which
      displacement is expected?
    choices:
      - Medial fragment inferiorly, lateral fragment superiorly
      - Medial fragment superiorly, lateral fragment inferiorly
      - Both fragments displaced posteriorly
      - Both fragments displaced superiorly
      - No displacement, because the periosteum is intact in adults
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Sternocleidomastoid** pulls the medial fragment **up**. The **weight of the limb** (and pectoralis major) pulls
      the lateral fragment **down** and medially.
  - stem: Which structures lie immediately posterior/inferior to the middle third of the clavicle and are at risk in a fracture?
    choices:
      - Axillary nerve and posterior circumflex humeral artery
      - Subclavian vessels and brachial plexus
      - Common carotid artery and vagus nerve
      - Long thoracic nerve and lateral thoracic artery
      - Suprascapular nerve and artery
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **subclavian artery and vein** and the trunks and divisions of the **brachial plexus** pass behind the middle
      third of the clavicle, separated from it by subclavius.
  - stem: Which radiographic view best shows displacement of a midshaft clavicle fracture in addition to the standard AP?
    choices:
      - Scapular Y view
      - AP view with cephalic tilt of 15–30°
      - Axillary lateral view
      - West Point view
      - Oblique view with caudal tilt of 45°
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **cephalic-tilt AP view** projects the clavicle above the ribs and scapula, showing superior displacement and
      shortening. The scapular Y and axillary views are for the glenohumeral joint.
