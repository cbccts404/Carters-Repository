name: Axillary nerve
type: nerve
region: upper-limb
subregion: Pectoral & scapular region
taName: Nervus axillaris
aka: [Circumflex nerve]
summary: Branch of the posterior cord that passes through the quadrangular space around the surgical neck of the humerus to supply deltoid and teres minor.
tags: [brachial-plexus]
systems: [neurologic, musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Posterior [[axilla]] → quadrangular space → deep surface of [[deltoid]] around the surgical neck of the [[humerus]].
  roots: C5, C6
  origin: Terminal branch of the posterior cord of the [[brachial-plexus]].
  course: >-
    Passes posteriorly through the **quadrangular space** with the **posterior circumflex humeral artery**. The
    space is bounded by teres minor above, teres major below, the long head of [[triceps-brachii]] medially and the
    surgical neck of the humerus laterally. The nerve then winds around the surgical neck of the humerus, deep to deltoid.
  branches:
    - "**Anterior branch:** winds around the surgical neck deep to deltoid and supplies its anterior and middle parts"
    - "**Posterior branch:** supplies [[teres-minor]] and the posterior part of deltoid, then continues as the **superior lateral cutaneous nerve of the arm**"
    - "Articular branch to the [[glenohumeral-joint]]"
  motor: "[[deltoid]] and [[teres-minor]]."
  sensory: >-
    Skin over the inferior part of deltoid (the **"regimental badge"** area), via the superior lateral cutaneous nerve of the arm.
  relations: The posterior circumflex humeral artery runs with it through the quadrangular space and around the surgical neck.
clinical:
  - title: Fracture of the surgical neck of the humerus
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Usually a fall on an outstretched hand or directly onto the shoulder, commonly in older adults with osteoporosis.
      The nerve and the posterior circumflex humeral artery are in direct contact with the surgical neck.
    presentation: >-
      Weak abduction (deltoid), especially from about 15° to 90°, and weak lateral rotation. Numbness over the
      regimental badge area. Deltoid atrophy develops later.
  - title: Anterior dislocation of the glenohumeral joint
    highYield: true
    mechanism: The dislocated humeral head, displaced anteroinferiorly, stretches or compresses the axillary nerve.
    presentation: >-
      Same deficits as above. **Check and document regimental badge sensation and deltoid function before and after
      reduction.**
  - title: Quadrilateral space syndrome
    highYield: false
    mechanism: >-
      Compression of the axillary nerve (± posterior circumflex humeral artery) in the quadrangular space, e.g. by
      fibrous bands or muscle hypertrophy, typically in overhead athletes.
    presentation: >-
      Poorly localized posterior shoulder pain and paresthesias, worse with abduction and external rotation.
      Teres minor ± deltoid atrophy may be seen on MRI.
pance:
  - "**Surgical neck** fracture or **anterior dislocation** → axillary nerve injury."
  - "Test **regimental badge** sensation (lateral shoulder) before and after reducing a shoulder dislocation."
  - "Quadrangular space contents: axillary nerve + posterior circumflex humeral artery."
  - "Deltoid atrophy → flattened shoulder contour; weak abduction beyond the first ~15°."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    The nerve is not visible. The shoulder trauma series (AP, scapular Y, axillary view) identifies surgical neck
    fractures and dislocations, the injuries that put the nerve at risk.
  mri: >-
    Denervation of **teres minor** (± deltoid) appears as edema acutely and fatty atrophy chronically. Isolated teres
    minor atrophy suggests quadrilateral space syndrome. The neurovascular bundle can be followed through the
    quadrangular space on sagittal oblique images.
  ultrasound: >-
    The nerve and posterior circumflex humeral artery can be identified together in the quadrangular space from a
    posterior approach, using color Doppler to find the artery.
  keyViews:
    - "Shoulder trauma series: AP, scapular Y, axillary lateral"
    - "MRI sagittal oblique through the quadrangular space"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    The nerve lies deep to deltoid about at the level of the surgical neck, roughly 5–6 cm below the acromion.
    {{verify: landmark distance quoted differently between sources}}
  testing: >-
    Resisted abduction with the arm at 90° (middle deltoid). Resisted lateral rotation with the elbow flexed at the side
    (teres minor, with infraspinatus). Sensation over the lateral shoulder (regimental badge).
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 74-year-old woman falls onto her right shoulder. Radiographs show a fracture of the surgical neck of the
      humerus. Which additional finding is most likely?
    choices:
      - Wrist drop
      - Loss of sensation over the lateral shoulder
      - Winging of the scapula
      - Weak elbow flexion with forearm supinated
      - Loss of sensation over the medial arm
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **axillary nerve** wraps around the surgical neck. Injury causes numbness over the inferior deltoid (the
      regimental badge area) and weak deltoid and teres minor. Wrist drop is the radial nerve at the midshaft
      (radial groove). Medial arm sensation is intercostobrachial/medial cutaneous nerve of the arm.
  - stem: Which artery accompanies the axillary nerve through the quadrangular space?
    choices:
      - Circumflex scapular artery
      - Profunda brachii artery
      - Anterior circumflex humeral artery
      - Thoracodorsal artery
      - Posterior circumflex humeral artery
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      The **posterior circumflex humeral artery** (from the third part of the [[axillary-artery]]) travels with the
      axillary nerve. The circumflex scapular artery passes through the triangular space, and the profunda brachii runs
      with the radial nerve through the triangular interval.
  - stem: >-
      The lateral boundary of the quadrangular space is formed by which structure?
    choices:
      - Long head of triceps brachii
      - Teres major
      - Surgical neck of the humerus
      - Lateral border of the scapula
      - Teres minor
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The quadrangular space is bounded by teres minor above, teres major below, the long head of triceps medially and
      the **surgical neck of the humerus** laterally.
  - stem: >-
      A 22-year-old man is brought in with an anterior shoulder dislocation. Before reduction, which bedside test best
      screens for the nerve most commonly injured?
    choices:
      - Pinprick sensation over the lateral shoulder
      - Wrist extension strength
      - Froment sign
      - Biceps reflex
      - Thumb opposition
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      The **axillary nerve** is the nerve most commonly injured in anterior dislocation. Testing sensation over the
      regimental badge area is a quick screen, because deltoid strength is hard to test in a painful dislocated
      shoulder.
