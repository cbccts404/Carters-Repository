region: upper-limb
subregion: Forearm
taName: Musculus pronator teres
summary: Superficial muscle of the anterior forearm. It pronates the forearm, forms the medial border of the cubital fossa, and the median nerve passes between its two heads.
tags: [elbow, forearm]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Proximal anterior forearm, running obliquely from the medial epicondyle to the mid-lateral radius.
  group: Anterior compartment of the forearm, superficial layer (with FCR, palmaris longus and FCU).
  origin: >-
    **Humeral head:** medial epicondyle of the [[humerus]] (common flexor origin). **Ulnar head:** coronoid process of
    the [[ulna]].
  insertion: Middle of the lateral surface of the [[radius]] (pronator tuberosity).
  innervation: "[[median-nerve]] (C6, C7)"
  bloodSupply: "Muscular branches of the [[ulnar-artery]] (including the anterior ulnar recurrent) and [[radial-artery]] {{verify}}"
  action:
    - Pronates the forearm (with pronator quadratus)
    - Assists flexion of the elbow
  relations: >-
    Forms the **medial boundary of the [[cubital-fossa]]**. The [[median-nerve]] enters the forearm between its humeral
    and ulnar heads. The ulnar artery passes deep to the ulnar head.
clinical:
  - title: Pronator syndrome
    highYield: false
    mechanism: >-
      Compression of the median nerve between the two heads of pronator teres (or under the bicipital aponeurosis or FDS
      arch), often with repetitive forceful pronation.
    presentation: >-
      Aching of the proximal volar forearm with median-distribution paresthesias, **including the thenar eminence**.
      Symptoms are provoked by resisted pronation. Nocturnal symptoms are less prominent than in carpal tunnel syndrome.
  - title: Medial epicondylitis (golfer's elbow)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Tendinopathy of the common flexor–pronator origin at the medial epicondyle (**pronator teres** and flexor carpi
      radialis most involved) from repetitive wrist flexion and pronation.
    presentation: Pain and tenderness at or just distal to the medial epicondyle, worse with resisted wrist flexion and resisted pronation.
pance:
  - "Pronator teres = medial border of the cubital fossa; the median nerve passes between its heads."
  - "**Medial epicondylitis**: pain with resisted wrist flexion and pronation."
  - "Pronator syndrome vs carpal tunnel syndrome: thenar sensation is **reduced** in pronator syndrome."
imaging:
  xray: Not visible. Elbow radiographs may show calcification at the medial epicondyle in chronic epicondylitis.
  mri: Medial epicondylitis appears as increased T2 signal and thickening of the common flexor tendon origin, with or without partial tearing.
  ultrasound: Hypoechoic swelling of the common flexor origin in medial epicondylitis. The median nerve can be followed between the two heads.
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    The muscle forms a palpable oblique ridge from the medial epicondyle across the proximal forearm, easiest to see
    during resisted pronation with the elbow flexed.
  testing: Resisted pronation with the elbow extended (pronator teres contributes most in extension; pronator quadratus is isolated with the elbow fully flexed).
  specialTests:
    - name: Pronator compression test
      technique: The examiner presses over pronator teres in the proximal volar forearm for about 30 seconds.
      positive: Paresthesias in the median distribution.
      significance: Suggests pronator syndrome. {{verify}}
    - name: Resisted pronation (medial epicondylitis)
      technique: Elbow extended. The patient pronates the forearm (and flexes the wrist) against resistance.
      positive: Pain at the medial epicondyle.
      significance: Medial epicondylitis.

  - stem: >-
      A 40-year-old recreational golfer has pain at the medial elbow. Resisted wrist flexion and resisted forearm

    choices:
      - Lateral epicondylitis
      - Medial epicondylitis
      - Olecranon bursitis
      - Radial tunnel syndrome
      - Distal biceps rupture
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Medial epicondylitis** involves the common flexor–pronator origin, especially pronator teres and FCR. Lateral
      epicondylitis involves ECRB and is painful with resisted wrist extension.
  - stem: Pronator teres forms which boundary of the cubital fossa?
    choices:
      - Lateral boundary
      - Medial boundary
      - Floor
      - Roof
      - Superior boundary
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The [[cubital-fossa]] is bounded **medially by pronator teres**, laterally by brachioradialis, and superiorly by a
      line joining the humeral epicondyles.
