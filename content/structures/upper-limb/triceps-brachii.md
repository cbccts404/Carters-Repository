name: Triceps brachii
type: muscle
region: upper-limb
subregion: Arm
taName: Musculus triceps brachii
summary: Three-headed muscle of the posterior arm and the chief extensor of the elbow; supplied by the radial nerve.
tags: [elbow]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Posterior compartment of the arm.
  group: Posterior (extensor) compartment of the arm, with anconeus.
  origin: >-
    **Long head:** infraglenoid tubercle of the [[scapula]]. **Lateral head:** posterior surface of the [[humerus]]
    superior to the radial groove. **Medial head:** posterior surface of the humerus inferior to the radial groove.
  insertion: Proximal end of the olecranon of the [[ulna]] and the deep fascia of the forearm.
  innervation: "[[radial-nerve]] (C6, C7, C8)"
  bloodSupply: "Profunda brachii (deep brachial) artery {{verify: additional supply from the posterior circumflex humeral and ulnar collateral arteries}}"
  action:
    - Chief extensor of the elbow
    - The long head also extends and adducts the arm at the shoulder and steadies the humeral head when the arm is adducted
  relations: >-
    The long head passes between teres minor and teres major, dividing the region into the quadrangular space
    (laterally) and the triangular space (medially). The **radial nerve** and profunda brachii artery run in the radial
    groove between the lateral and medial heads.
clinical:
  - title: Triceps weakness from radial nerve injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      High (axillary) radial nerve lesions such as crutch palsy. Midshaft humerus fractures usually spare triceps
      because its branches arise proximal to the radial groove.
    presentation: Weak elbow extension and a reduced triceps reflex together with wrist drop.
  - title: Triceps tendon rupture
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Uncommon. Forced flexion against a contracting triceps (fall on an outstretched hand) or a direct blow. Risk factors include anabolic steroid use.
    presentation: Posterior elbow pain, a palpable gap above the olecranon, and inability to extend against gravity with a complete tear.
pance:
  - "Triceps reflex = **C7** (C6–C7), radial nerve."
  - "Radial nerve injury at the midshaft humerus usually **spares triceps**; an axillary lesion does not."
  - "Long head of triceps = medial border of the quadrangular space."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    A small bony fleck avulsed from the olecranon on the lateral elbow view ("flake sign") suggests triceps tendon
    avulsion.
  mri: Defines partial vs complete tendon tears and retraction.
  ultrasound: Dynamic assessment of the distal tendon at the olecranon. Also shows snapping of the medial head over the medial epicondyle.
exam:
  landmarks: The olecranon and the distal tendon just above it are easily palpable posteriorly.
  testing: Resisted elbow extension. Triceps reflex (C7), tapping the tendon just above the olecranon.
  specialTests:
    - name: Modified Thompson test (triceps squeeze)
      technique: >-
        With the arm supported and the elbow flexed and hanging freely (e.g. patient prone with the arm over the table
        edge), the examiner squeezes the triceps muscle belly.
      positive: No passive elbow extension.
      significance: Suggests complete triceps tendon rupture. {{verify}}
quiz:
  - stem: Which nerve root is primarily tested by the triceps reflex?
    choices:
      - C5
      - C6
      - C7
      - C8
      - T1
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The triceps reflex is mainly **C7** (with C6), carried by the [[radial-nerve]]. The biceps reflex is C5–C6 and the
      brachioradialis reflex C6.
  - stem: The long head of triceps brachii originates from which structure?
    choices:
      - Supraglenoid tubercle
      - Coracoid process
      - Infraglenoid tubercle
      - Posterior humerus above the radial groove
      - Lateral border of the scapula below teres minor
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The long head arises from the **infraglenoid tubercle** of the scapula. The lateral and medial heads arise from
      the posterior humerus above and below the radial groove.
  - stem: >-
      A 30-year-old man has a spiral fracture of the humeral shaft with wrist drop. Elbow extension is strong. What
      explains the preserved triceps function?
    choices:
      - Triceps is innervated by the axillary nerve
      - The branches to triceps arise proximal to the radial groove
      - Triceps is innervated by the musculocutaneous nerve
      - The radial nerve does not pass through the radial groove
      - Anconeus alone extends the elbow
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **triceps branches** of the radial nerve arise in the axilla and proximal arm, **before** the nerve enters the
      radial groove, so they are usually spared in shaft fractures.
