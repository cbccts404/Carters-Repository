---
name: Infraspinatus
type: muscle
region: upper-limb
subregion: Pectoral & scapular region
taName: Musculus infraspinatus
summary: Rotator cuff muscle in the infraspinous fossa and the main lateral (external) rotator of the arm.
tags: [rotator-cuff, shoulder]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Infraspinous fossa of the scapula, below the spine, covered partly by deltoid and trapezius.
  group: Rotator cuff (SITS); scapulohumeral muscles.
  origin: Infraspinous fossa of the [[scapula]] and the deep fascia covering it.
  insertion: Middle facet of the greater tubercle of the [[humerus]].
  innervation: "Suprascapular nerve (C5, C6)"
  bloodSupply: "Suprascapular and circumflex scapular arteries {{verify}}"
  action:
    - Laterally (externally) rotates the arm
    - With the other rotator cuff muscles, holds the humeral head in the glenoid cavity
  relations: >-
    The suprascapular nerve reaches infraspinatus by passing around the lateral border of the scapular spine through
    the **spinoglenoid notch** after supplying supraspinatus.
clinical:
  - title: Suprascapular nerve entrapment
    highYield: false
    mechanism: >-
      Compression at the **suprascapular notch** (affects both supraspinatus and infraspinatus) or at the
      **spinoglenoid notch** (affects **infraspinatus only**). Causes include a paralabral ganglion cyst (often with a
      labral tear), traction in overhead athletes (volleyball, baseball) and fractures.
    presentation: >-
      Deep, poorly localized posterior shoulder pain, weak external rotation, and visible wasting of the infraspinous
      fossa. Isolated infraspinatus atrophy points to the spinoglenoid notch.
  - title: Posterosuperior rotator cuff tear
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Extension of a supraspinatus tear posteriorly, or an isolated tear, usually degenerative.
    presentation: Weak external rotation with a positive external rotation lag sign, and difficulty with overhead and reaching activities.
pance:
  - "Infraspinatus = main **external rotator**; tested by resisted external rotation with the elbow at the side."
  - "Suprascapular nerve supplies **both** supraspinatus and infraspinatus; spinoglenoid notch lesions affect infraspinatus only."
  - "Infraspinous fossa wasting → think suprascapular neuropathy or a chronic posterior cuff tear."
imaging:
  xray: Usually normal. Shoulder radiographs exclude fractures of the scapula or glenoid.
  mri: >-
    Assesses tendon tears, muscle atrophy and fatty infiltration (sagittal oblique). A **paralabral cyst** in the
    spinoglenoid notch with denervation edema of infraspinatus alone suggests suprascapular nerve compression at that
    level.
  ultrasound: >-
    The posterior approach shows the infraspinatus tendon inserting on the greater tuberosity and posterior joint
    recess. Dynamic external/internal rotation helps.
  keyViews:
    - "MRI sagittal oblique (muscle bulk) and axial (spinoglenoid notch)"
exam:
  landmarks: Infraspinous fossa below the palpable spine of the scapula. Atrophy shows as hollowing.
  testing: Resisted external rotation with the arm adducted and the elbow flexed at 90°.
  specialTests:
    - name: External rotation lag sign
      technique: >-
        With the elbow flexed at 90° and the arm at the side (slight abduction), the examiner passively rotates the
        shoulder into near-maximal external rotation and asks the patient to hold the position after release.
      positive: The forearm drifts back toward internal rotation (a lag).
      significance: Suggests a tear of infraspinatus (± supraspinatus).
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 26-year-old volleyball player has vague posterior shoulder pain and weak external rotation. There is visible
      wasting of the infraspinous fossa, but the supraspinous fossa is normal and abduction is strong. MRI shows a
      ganglion cyst. Where is the cyst most likely located?
    choices:
      - Suprascapular notch
      - Spinoglenoid notch
      - Quadrangular space
      - Triangular interval
      - Coracoacromial arch
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      At the **spinoglenoid notch** the suprascapular nerve has already supplied supraspinatus, so only
      **infraspinatus** is affected. A lesion at the suprascapular notch would weaken both muscles. The quadrangular
      space contains the axillary nerve (teres minor and deltoid).
  - stem: Which pair of muscles laterally rotates the arm?
    choices:
      - Subscapularis and teres major
      - Supraspinatus and deltoid
      - Infraspinatus and teres minor
      - Pectoralis major and latissimus dorsi
      - Coracobrachialis and biceps brachii
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Infraspinatus** (suprascapular nerve) and **teres minor** (axillary nerve) are the primary lateral rotators,
      with help from the posterior deltoid. The other pairs are mainly medial rotators, abductors or flexors.
  - stem: The infraspinatus tendon inserts on which part of the humerus?
    choices:
      - Lesser tubercle
      - Superior facet of the greater tubercle
      - Middle facet of the greater tubercle
      - Inferior facet of the greater tubercle
      - Deltoid tuberosity
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      From superior to inferior on the greater tubercle the insertions are supraspinatus (superior facet),
      **infraspinatus (middle facet)** and teres minor (inferior facet). Subscapularis inserts on the lesser tubercle.
