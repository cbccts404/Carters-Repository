---
name: Deltoid
type: muscle
region: upper-limb
subregion: Pectoral & scapular region
taName: Musculus deltoideus
summary: Triangular muscle that forms the rounded contour of the shoulder. It is the main abductor of the arm beyond the first ~15° and is supplied by the axillary nerve.
tags: [shoulder]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Covers the glenohumeral joint anteriorly, laterally and posteriorly, forming the shoulder contour.
  group: Superficial scapulohumeral muscles; three parts (clavicular/anterior, acromial/middle, spinal/posterior).
  origin: Lateral third of the [[clavicle]] (anterior part), acromion (middle part), and spine of the [[scapula]] (posterior part).
  insertion: Deltoid tuberosity of the [[humerus]].
  innervation: "[[axillary-nerve]] (C5, C6)"
  bloodSupply: "Posterior circumflex humeral artery (principal) and the deltoid branch of the thoracoacromial artery {{verify}}"
  action:
    - "**Anterior (clavicular) part:** flexes and medially rotates the arm"
    - "**Middle (acromial) part:** abducts the arm after supraspinatus initiates the movement"
    - "**Posterior (spinal) part:** extends and laterally rotates the arm"
  relations: >-
    The axillary nerve and posterior circumflex humeral artery run on its deep surface around the surgical neck of the
    humerus. The subacromial–subdeltoid bursa lies between deltoid and the rotator cuff. The deltopectoral groove
    (with the cephalic vein) separates its anterior border from pectoralis major.
clinical:
  - title: Deltoid paralysis (axillary nerve injury)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Surgical neck fracture of the humerus, anterior shoulder dislocation, or iatrogenic injury.
    presentation: >-
      Weak abduction beyond the initial range. Over weeks the muscle atrophies, **flattening the rounded contour** of
      the shoulder. Numbness over the regimental badge area.
  - title: Intramuscular injection site
    highYield: false
    mechanism: >-
      Deltoid is a common IM injection site. The axillary nerve runs horizontally on its deep surface around the
      surgical neck, so injections too low or too posterior risk the nerve. Injections placed too high risk the
      subacromial bursa (shoulder injury related to vaccine administration, SIRVA).
    presentation: >-
      Inject into the thickest part of the middle deltoid, about 2–3 fingerbreadths (≈5 cm) below the acromion.
      {{verify: landmark recommendations differ slightly between guidelines}}
pance:
  - "Deltoid atrophy + lateral shoulder numbness → **axillary nerve** (surgical neck fracture, anterior dislocation)."
  - "Supraspinatus initiates abduction (~0–15°); deltoid continues it."
  - "A 'squared-off' shoulder in acute anterior dislocation comes from the displaced humeral head, not deltoid paralysis."
imaging:
  xray: Soft-tissue contour only. Look at the humerus for surgical neck fractures that endanger its nerve supply.
  mri: Denervation edema (acute) or fatty atrophy (chronic) with axillary neuropathy. Rarely, deltoid tears or avulsions.
  ultrasound: >-
    Superficial muscle over the cuff. Ultrasound shows deltoid thickness when guiding IM injections and visualizes the
    subdeltoid bursa beneath it.
exam:
  landmarks: Rounded contour of the shoulder. The deltoid tuberosity is palpable roughly mid-way down the lateral arm.
  palpation: Palpate all three parts. Compare bulk with the other side and look for hollowing below the acromion.
  testing: >-
    Resisted abduction with the arm at about 90° (middle part), resisted flexion (anterior part), and resisted extension
    (posterior part). Check sensation over the lateral shoulder.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      Three months after a proximal humerus fracture, a 70-year-old woman has flattening of the lateral shoulder contour
      and can't abduct her arm beyond about 15°. Which nerve was injured?
    choices:
      - Suprascapular nerve
      - Axillary nerve
      - Long thoracic nerve
      - Radial nerve
      - Musculocutaneous nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **axillary nerve** supplies deltoid and wraps around the surgical neck. Supraspinatus (suprascapular nerve)
      still initiates abduction, but the patient can't continue it without deltoid, and the muscle atrophies.
  - stem: Which part of the deltoid laterally rotates the arm?
    choices:
      - Clavicular part
      - Acromial part
      - Spinal (posterior) part
      - All parts equally
      - None of its parts
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **spinal (posterior) part** extends and laterally rotates. The clavicular (anterior) part flexes and medially
      rotates. The acromial (middle) part abducts.
  - stem: The deltoid inserts on which structure?
    choices:
      - Greater tubercle
      - Intertubercular groove
      - Deltoid tuberosity of the humerus
      - Radial tuberosity
      - Coracoid process
    answer: C
    explanation: All three parts of deltoid converge on the **deltoid tuberosity** on the lateral surface of the humeral shaft.
