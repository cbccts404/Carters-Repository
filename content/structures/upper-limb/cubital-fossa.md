name: Cubital fossa
type: space
region: upper-limb
subregion: Elbow & cubital fossa
taName: Fossa cubitalis
aka: [Antecubital fossa]
summary: Triangular depression on the anterior elbow containing the biceps tendon, brachial artery and median nerve. It is the usual site for venipuncture and blood pressure auscultation.
tags: [elbow]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Anterior aspect of the elbow.
  boundaries:
    superior: Imaginary line joining the medial and lateral epicondyles of the [[humerus|humerus]]
    medial: "[[pronator-teres]]"
    lateral: Brachioradialis
    apex: Where the medial and lateral boundaries meet, distally
    floor: Brachialis (proximally) and supinator (distally)
    roof: Skin, subcutaneous tissue (with the superficial veins and cutaneous nerves), deep fascia, and the **bicipital aponeurosis**
  contents:
    - "[[biceps-brachii]] tendon"
    - "[[brachial-artery]], dividing at the apex into the [[radial-artery]] and [[ulnar-artery]]"
    - "[[median-nerve]] (most medial)"
    - "[[radial-nerve]]: deep, at the lateral edge between brachialis and brachioradialis, dividing into superficial and deep branches"
  communications: Continuous with the anterior compartment of the arm above and the anterior compartment of the forearm below.
  notes: >-
    Main contents from **lateral to medial** are remembered as **TAN**: biceps **T**endon, brachial **A**rtery, median
    **N**erve.


    **Superficial veins in the roof:** the cephalic vein (lateral) and basilic vein (medial) are connected by the
    **median cubital vein**, which crosses superficial to the bicipital aponeurosis. The aponeurosis separates the vein
    from the brachial artery and median nerve. The superficial vein pattern varies considerably between people.
clinical:
  - title: Venipuncture and IV access
    highYield: true
    mechanism: The **median cubital vein** is the preferred site because it is superficial, large and anchored.
    presentation: >-
      The bicipital aponeurosis provides some protection to the brachial artery and median nerve beneath it. Risks
      include hematoma, puncture of the brachial artery (bright red, pulsatile return), and injury to cutaneous nerves
      (lateral or medial cutaneous nerves of the forearm).
  - title: Supracondylar fracture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Displacement of the distal humerus fragments into the fossa.
    presentation: Risk to the **brachial artery** and **median nerve/AIN** with swelling that may progress to compartment syndrome.
  - title: Distal biceps tendon rupture
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Eccentric loading of the flexed elbow.
    presentation: Loss of the palpable tendon in the fossa (positive hook test), bruising and weak supination.
pance:
  - "Cubital fossa contents, lateral → medial: **biceps Tendon, brachial Artery, median Nerve** (TAN); radial nerve deep at the lateral edge."
  - "Median cubital vein → venipuncture; superficial to the bicipital aponeurosis."
  - "Brachial pulse / BP auscultation: **medial to the biceps tendon**."
imaging:
  xray: The fossa is a soft-tissue region. Elbow radiographs show fat pads and fractures.
  mri: Distal biceps tendon (FABS view), brachialis injury, and masses.
  ultrasound: >-
    Ultrasound-guided IV access in difficult patients (identify the vein and avoid the artery and nerve). Assessment of
    the distal biceps tendon and the brachial artery bifurcation.
  keyViews:
    - "Transverse ultrasound across the fossa showing biceps tendon → artery → median nerve (lateral to medial)"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    The biceps tendon is felt as a firm cord when the patient flexes the supinated forearm against resistance. The
    brachial pulse is just medial to it. The superficial veins become visible with a tourniquet.
  palpation: Palpate the biceps tendon, the brachial pulse and the medial and lateral borders (pronator teres, brachioradialis).
  testing: Hook test for distal biceps integrity. BP auscultation over the brachial artery.
