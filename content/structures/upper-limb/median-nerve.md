name: Median nerve
type: nerve
region: upper-limb
subregion: Forearm
taName: Nervus medianus
summary: Formed by roots from the lateral and medial cords. It supplies most of the anterior forearm and the thenar muscles, and passes through the carpal tunnel.
tags: [brachial-plexus, carpal-tunnel]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Arm (medial) → [[cubital-fossa]] → anterior forearm (between the flexor layers) → [[carpal-tunnel]] → palm.
  roots: "C6, C7, C8, T1 (± C5) {{verify: some texts include C5 via the lateral root. Check which your atlas uses.}}"
  origin: >-
    **Lateral root** from the lateral cord and **medial root** from the medial cord of the [[brachial-plexus]]. The
    roots unite anterior to the third part of the [[axillary-artery]].
  course: >-
    **Arm:** descends alongside the [[brachial-artery]], lateral to it proximally, then crosses (usually anteriorly) to
    lie **medial** to it at the elbow. It gives no named muscular branches in the arm. **Cubital fossa:** the most
    medial of the main contents, medial to the brachial artery and deep to the bicipital aponeurosis. **Forearm:**
    enters between the two heads of [[pronator-teres]] and gives off the **anterior interosseous nerve (AIN)**. It then
    runs between flexor digitorum superficialis (FDS) and flexor digitorum profundus (FDP), adherent to the deep surface
    of FDS. **Wrist:** becomes superficial between the tendons of FCR and FDS, deep to palmaris longus. The **palmar
    cutaneous branch** arises proximal to the flexor retinaculum and passes **superficial** to it. The nerve then passes
    through the **carpal tunnel** deep to the flexor retinaculum and divides into the **recurrent (thenar motor) branch**
    and common palmar digital nerves.
  branches:
    - "**Muscular (forearm):** [[pronator-teres]], flexor carpi radialis, palmaris longus, flexor digitorum superficialis"
    - "**Anterior interosseous nerve:** flexor pollicis longus, lateral half of FDP (digits 2–3), pronator quadratus (no cutaneous branch)"
    - "**Palmar cutaneous branch:** lateral palm; arises proximal to and passes superficial to the flexor retinaculum"
    - "**Recurrent (thenar) branch:** abductor pollicis brevis, opponens pollicis, superficial head of flexor pollicis brevis"
    - "**Common/proper palmar digital nerves:** lateral two lumbricals; skin of the lateral 3½ digits"
  motor: >-
    Most of the anterior forearm flexors except FCU and the medial half of FDP (ulnar). In the hand: **"LOAF"**, the
    lateral two **L**umbricals, **O**pponens pollicis, **A**bductor pollicis brevis and **F**lexor pollicis brevis
    (superficial head).
  sensory: >-
    Palmar surface of the lateral 3½ digits (thumb, index, middle, lateral half of ring) and the dorsal distal
    phalanges/nail beds of those digits. Lateral palm via the palmar cutaneous branch. Most reliable test site: the
    **pad of the index finger**.
  relations: >-
    Crosses the brachial artery in the arm. In the carpal tunnel it is the most superficial structure, lying with the
    nine long flexor tendons.
clinical:
  - title: Carpal tunnel syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Compression of the median nerve in the [[carpal-tunnel]]. It is the most common entrapment neuropathy.
      Associations: pregnancy, hypothyroidism, diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, obesity, acromegaly, dialysis-related
      amyloidosis, repetitive wrist use, and space-occupying lesions (ganglion, lunate dislocation).
    presentation: >-
      Paresthesias and pain in the lateral 3½ digits, **worse at night**, often relieved by shaking the hand ("flick
      sign"). Later: weakness of thumb abduction/opposition and **thenar atrophy**. **Sensation over the thenar
      eminence is spared** because the palmar cutaneous branch passes superficial to the retinaculum.
  - title: Anterior interosseous nerve palsy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Injury in the proximal forearm. Classically associated with **pediatric supracondylar humerus fractures** (the
      AIN is the nerve most often injured in extension-type fractures), or with compression or neuritis.
      {{verify: some older texts name the radial nerve as most commonly injured in supracondylar fractures}}
    presentation: >-
      Can't make an **"OK" sign**: the thumb IP joint and index DIP joint don't flex (FPL and FDP to the index), so
      the pinch is flat. Weak pronation with the elbow flexed (pronator quadratus). **No sensory loss.**
  - title: Proximal median nerve lesion ("hand of benediction")
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Injury at or above the elbow, e.g. supracondylar fracture or penetrating trauma.
    presentation: >-
      When the patient tries to make a fist, the index and middle fingers stay extended (loss of FDS and the lateral
      FDP) while the ring and little fingers flex (ulnar FDP). This is the **"hand of benediction"**. There is also thenar
      weakness and median sensory loss including the thenar eminence.
  - title: Pronator syndrome
    highYield: false
    mechanism: >-
      Compression near the elbow: between the heads of pronator teres, under the bicipital aponeurosis, or under the
      FDS arch.
    presentation: >-
      Aching of the volar forearm with median-distribution paresthesias. Unlike carpal tunnel syndrome, sensation over
      the **thenar eminence is reduced** (palmar cutaneous branch involved) and night symptoms are less prominent.
  - title: Laceration at the wrist ("ape hand")
    highYield: false
    mechanism: A cut at the wrist crease (e.g. glass or a suicide attempt) divides the nerve distal to its forearm branches.
    presentation: >-
      Thenar paralysis and atrophy leave the thumb lying adducted in the plane of the palm ("ape hand"), unable to
      oppose. Median sensory loss in the digits. Forearm flexors are intact.
pance:
  - "**Carpal tunnel syndrome**: nocturnal paresthesias of the lateral 3½ digits; **Tinel**, **Phalen**, and the carpal compression (Durkan) test; confirm with nerve conduction studies/EMG."
  - "Associations with CTS: **pregnancy, hypothyroidism, diabetes, RA**, acromegaly."
  - "Initial management of mild–moderate CTS commonly includes a wrist splint in neutral (especially at night). Thenar atrophy or severe/progressive deficit prompts surgical referral."
  - "**Supracondylar fracture** (child) + can't make an \"OK\" sign → **AIN** injury."
  - "Thenar sensation spared in CTS (palmar cutaneous branch); reduced in pronator syndrome."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    Not visible. A carpal tunnel (axial) view can show bony causes of compression. At the elbow, radiographs evaluate
    supracondylar fractures: look for the **posterior fat pad sign** and an abnormal anterior humeral line.
  mri: >-
    In carpal tunnel syndrome: enlargement of the nerve at the pisiform level, flattening at the hamate level,
    increased T2 signal, and palmar bowing of the flexor retinaculum. MRI also shows masses and tenosynovitis.
  ultrasound: >-
    First-line imaging adjunct for carpal tunnel syndrome. The nerve appears as a honeycomb (fascicular) oval
    structure beneath the flexor retinaculum. An increased **cross-sectional area** at the tunnel inlet (pisiform
    level) supports the diagnosis. {{verify: diagnostic cut-offs vary between studies, roughly 9–12 mm²}} It also
    detects anatomic variants (bifid median nerve, persistent median artery) and masses.
  keyViews:
    - "Ultrasound transverse at pisiform (inlet) and hook of hamate (outlet) levels"
    - "Elbow lateral radiograph for pediatric supracondylar fracture"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    At the wrist, the nerve lies deep to palmaris longus, between the FCR and FDS tendons, just proximal to the distal
    wrist crease. (Palmaris longus is absent in some people.)
  palpation: The nerve itself is not reliably palpable; percussion over the carpal tunnel is used for the Tinel sign.
  testing: >-
    **Thumb abduction** against resistance (APB), the most reliable median motor test in the hand; opposition.
    **"OK" sign** for the AIN (FPL + FDP index). Fist making: look for a hand of benediction. Two-point discrimination or
    light touch on the **pad of the index finger**.
  specialTests:
    - name: Tinel sign (wrist)
      technique: Tap over the median nerve at the carpal tunnel.
      positive: Tingling radiating into the median-innervated digits.
      significance: Suggests carpal tunnel syndrome. Sensitivity and specificity are moderate.
    - name: Phalen test
      technique: Hold the wrists in full flexion (dorsal surfaces of the hands pressed together) for 60 seconds.
      positive: Paresthesias in the median distribution.
      significance: Suggests carpal tunnel syndrome.
    - name: Carpal compression test (Durkan)
      technique: The examiner presses with the thumbs directly over the carpal tunnel for up to 30 seconds.
      positive: Paresthesias in the median distribution.
      significance: Suggests carpal tunnel syndrome. Often reported as the most sensitive of the provocative tests.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 29-year-old woman at 32 weeks' gestation reports numbness of the right thumb, index and middle fingers that
      wakes her at night and improves when she shakes her hand. Which finding would suggest the lesion is proximal to
      the carpal tunnel rather than within it?
    choices:
      - Positive Phalen test
      - Weak thumb abduction
      - Decreased sensation over the thenar eminence
      - Tingling with percussion over the volar wrist
      - Thenar muscle atrophy
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **palmar cutaneous branch** arises proximal to the flexor retinaculum and passes superficial to it, so thenar
      skin sensation is **spared in carpal tunnel syndrome**. Thenar sensory loss points to a more proximal lesion
      such as pronator syndrome. The other findings occur in carpal tunnel syndrome.
  - stem: >-
      A 7-year-old boy falls from monkey bars onto an outstretched hand and sustains an extension-type supracondylar
      humerus fracture. After reduction he can't flex the IP joint of the thumb or the DIP joint of the index finger.
      Sensation in the hand is normal. Which nerve is injured?
    choices:
      - Superficial radial nerve
      - Ulnar nerve
      - Recurrent branch of the median nerve
      - Posterior interosseous nerve
      - Anterior interosseous nerve
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      The **AIN** supplies FPL, the lateral FDP and pronator quadratus and has **no cutaneous branch**. The patient
      can't form an "OK" sign. It is the nerve most often injured in extension-type supracondylar fractures.
  - stem: Which intrinsic hand muscle is NOT innervated by the median nerve?
    choices:
      - Abductor pollicis brevis
      - Opponens pollicis
      - Adductor pollicis
      - First lumbrical
      - Second lumbrical
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Adductor pollicis** is supplied by the deep branch of the [[ulnar-nerve]] (that's the basis of the Froment
      sign). The median nerve supplies "LOAF": lateral two lumbricals, opponens pollicis, abductor pollicis brevis and
      the superficial head of flexor pollicis brevis.
  - stem: A patient tries to make a fist, but the index and middle fingers remain extended. Where is the lesion?
    choices:
      - Median nerve at the wrist
      - Median nerve at or above the elbow
      - Ulnar nerve at the elbow
      - Radial nerve in the radial groove
      - Recurrent branch of the median nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **hand of benediction** results from loss of FDS and the lateral half of FDP, which are supplied in the
      forearm. The lesion must therefore be **proximal** to those branches, at or above the elbow. A lesion at the
      wrist spares the long flexors.
