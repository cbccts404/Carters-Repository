name: Brachial plexus
type: nerve
region: upper-limb
subregion: Axilla
taName: Plexus brachialis
summary: Network formed by the ventral rami of C5–T1 that supplies the motor and most of the sensory innervation of the upper limb.
tags: [brachial-plexus]
systems: [neurologic, musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: >-
    Extends from the neck into the axilla. The roots and trunks lie in the lateral cervical region (posterior
    triangle), the divisions lie posterior to the [[clavicle]], and the cords lie in the [[axilla]].
  roots: >-
    Ventral (anterior) rami of **C5, C6, C7, C8 and T1**. A "prefixed" plexus has a large C4 contribution and a
    "postfixed" plexus a large T2 contribution.
  origin: Ventral rami of spinal nerves C5–T1.
  course: >-
    **Roots** emerge between the anterior and middle scalene muscles together with the subclavian artery. They unite
    to form three **trunks**: superior (C5–C6), middle (C7) and inferior (C8–T1). Each trunk splits behind the clavicle
    into an anterior and a posterior **division**. The anterior divisions of the superior and middle trunks form the
    **lateral cord**; the anterior division of the inferior trunk forms the **medial cord**; all three posterior
    divisions form the **posterior cord**. The cords are named by their position relative to the second part of the
    [[axillary-artery]], deep to pectoralis minor. Anterior divisions supply flexor (anterior) compartments; posterior
    divisions supply extensor (posterior) compartments.
  branches:
    - "**From roots:** dorsal scapular nerve (C5); [[long-thoracic-nerve]] (C5–C7)"
    - "**From superior trunk:** suprascapular nerve (C5–C6); nerve to subclavius (C5–C6)"
    - "**From lateral cord:** lateral pectoral nerve; [[musculocutaneous-nerve]]; lateral root of the [[median-nerve]]"
    - "**From medial cord:** medial pectoral nerve; medial cutaneous nerves of the arm and forearm; [[ulnar-nerve]]; medial root of the [[median-nerve]]"
    - "**From posterior cord:** upper and lower subscapular nerves; thoracodorsal nerve (C6–C8); [[axillary-nerve]]; [[radial-nerve]]"
  motor: All muscles of the upper limb except trapezius (spinal accessory nerve, CN XI).
  sensory: >-
    Nearly all skin of the upper limb. Exceptions: the skin over the top of the shoulder (supraclavicular nerves,
    C3–C4, from the cervical plexus) and part of the medial arm near the axilla (intercostobrachial nerve, T2).
  relations: >-
    Roots and trunks accompany the subclavian artery between the scalenes, while the subclavian vein passes anterior to
    anterior scalene. In the axilla the cords and their branches surround the axillary artery within the axillary sheath.
clinical:
  - title: Upper plexus injury (Erb–Duchenne palsy)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Injury to the **C5–C6** roots or superior trunk from forcible widening of the neck–shoulder angle, e.g. shoulder
      dystocia during delivery or a fall onto the shoulder (motorcycle, contact sports).
    presentation: >-
      **"Waiter's tip" posture**: arm adducted and medially rotated, elbow extended, forearm pronated. Loss of
      abduction (deltoid, supraspinatus), lateral rotation (infraspinatus, teres minor), elbow flexion and supination
      (biceps). Sensory loss over the lateral arm. Biceps reflex reduced or absent. In a newborn the Moro reflex is
      asymmetric.
  - title: Lower plexus injury (Klumpke palsy)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Injury to the **C8–T1** roots or inferior trunk from excessive upward traction on the arm, e.g. grabbing a
      support to break a fall, or delivery with the arm abducted overhead.
    presentation: >-
      Weakness of the intrinsic hand muscles producing a **claw hand**, with sensory loss along the medial forearm and
      hand. If the T1 root is avulsed near the spinal cord, sympathetic fibres to the head are interrupted, causing
      ipsilateral **Horner syndrome** (ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis).
  - title: Pancoast (superior sulcus) tumor
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Apical lung carcinoma invading the inferior trunk (C8–T1) and the cervical sympathetic chain/stellate ganglion.
    presentation: >-
      Shoulder pain radiating down the medial arm and forearm, wasting of the intrinsic hand muscles, and ipsilateral
      Horner syndrome, typically in a patient with a smoking history.
  - title: Thoracic outlet syndrome (neurogenic)
    highYield: false
    mechanism: >-
      Compression of the lower plexus (usually C8–T1/inferior trunk) in the interscalene triangle or costoclavicular
      space, e.g. by a cervical rib, fibrous band or scalene hypertrophy. The subclavian vessels may be compressed
      too (vascular TOS).
    presentation: >-
      Pain and paresthesias along the medial forearm and hand, worse with overhead activity; weakness or wasting of
      the intrinsic hand muscles in advanced cases.
pance:
  - "Erb palsy = **C5–C6**, waiter's-tip posture; classic cause is **shoulder dystocia**."
  - "Klumpke palsy = **C8–T1**, claw hand ± **Horner syndrome**."
  - "Apical lung mass + Horner syndrome + ulnar-side arm pain → **Pancoast tumor**."
  - "Cords are named for their relation to the **second part of the axillary artery**."
  - "Mnemonic for the order: **R**oots, **T**runks, **D**ivisions, **C**ords, **B**ranches."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    The plexus itself is not visible. Look for causes or associated injuries: a cervical rib or elongated C7
    transverse process (thoracic outlet syndrome), fractures of the clavicle, first rib or cervical spine, and an
    apical lung mass (Pancoast tumor) on chest X-ray.
  ct: >-
    CT chest characterizes an apical (superior sulcus) mass. CT myelography can show pseudomeningoceles in
    traumatic root avulsion.
  mri: >-
    MRI (including MR neurography) is the main modality for the plexus. It shows nerve thickening and T2/STIR
    hyperintensity after trauma or inflammation, **pseudomeningoceles** in root avulsion, tumor invasion, and
    denervation edema in the affected muscles.
  ultrasound: >-
    Used for regional anesthesia. In the **interscalene groove** the C5–C7 roots/trunks appear as a vertical stack of
    round hypoechoic structures between the anterior and middle scalenes. In the **supraclavicular** region the
    divisions appear as a cluster of hypoechoic nodules lateral to the subclavian artery, above the first rib and pleura.
  keyViews:
    - "Chest X-ray (PA) with attention to the lung apices"
    - "Cervical spine AP to look for a cervical rib"
    - "MRI brachial plexus protocol (coronal and sagittal STIR/T2)"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    Roots and trunks: posterior triangle of the neck, deep to the lateral border of sternocleidomastoid. Cords: deep
    to pectoralis minor, inferomedial to the coracoid process.
  testing: >-
    Localize by root using **myotomes** (ASIA key muscles): C5 elbow flexion; C6 wrist extension; C7 elbow extension;
    C8 finger flexion (FDP of the middle finger); T1 little-finger abduction. Reflexes: biceps (C5–C6),
    brachioradialis (C6), triceps (C7). Dermatomes: see the diagram.
  specialTests:
    - name: Roos test (elevated arm stress test)
      technique: >-
        Arms abducted to 90° and externally rotated with elbows flexed to 90°. The patient slowly opens and closes the
        hands for up to 3 minutes.
      positive: Reproduction of the patient's symptoms (pain, paresthesias, heaviness), or being unable to continue.
      significance: Supports thoracic outlet syndrome. Specificity is limited.
    - name: Adson test
      technique: >-
        The examiner palpates the radial pulse with the arm slightly abducted and extended. The patient extends the
        neck, rotates the head toward the tested side and takes a deep breath.
        {{verify: head rotation direction differs between sources (toward vs away from the tested side)}}
      positive: Diminished or absent radial pulse with reproduction of symptoms.
      significance: Suggests vascular thoracic outlet compression. High false-positive rate in healthy people.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A newborn delivered after a difficult vaginal delivery complicated by shoulder dystocia holds the right arm
      adducted and internally rotated, with the elbow extended and the forearm pronated. The Moro reflex is absent on
      the right. Injury to which spinal nerve roots is most likely?
    choices:
      - C5–C6
      - C7 only
      - C8–T1
      - C5–T1
      - C3–C4
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      This is the "waiter's tip" posture of **Erb–Duchenne palsy** (upper trunk, C5–C6). Deltoid and supraspinatus
      are weak (arm hangs adducted), infraspinatus and teres minor are weak (unopposed medial rotation), and biceps is
      weak (elbow extended, forearm pronated). C8–T1 (Klumpke) would produce a claw hand. Complete C5–T1 injury
      produces a flail arm.
  - stem: >-
      A 61-year-old man with a 50-pack-year smoking history has 3 months of right shoulder pain radiating to the
      medial forearm. Exam shows wasting of the right interossei, right ptosis and a small right pupil. Which part of
      the brachial plexus is most likely involved?
    choices:
      - Superior trunk
      - Middle trunk
      - Lateral cord
      - Inferior trunk
      - Posterior cord
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      A **Pancoast tumor** at the lung apex invades the **inferior trunk (C8–T1)**, producing medial arm and forearm
      pain and intrinsic hand weakness. Involvement of the adjacent cervical sympathetic chain causes **Horner
      syndrome**. The superior trunk (C5–C6) would affect shoulder abduction and elbow flexion instead.
  - stem: Which nerve arises from the posterior cord of the brachial plexus?
    choices:
      - Lateral pectoral nerve
      - Suprascapular nerve
      - Musculocutaneous nerve
      - Thoracodorsal nerve
      - Ulnar nerve
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      The posterior cord gives the upper and lower subscapular, **thoracodorsal**, axillary and radial nerves. The
      lateral pectoral and musculocutaneous nerves come from the lateral cord, the suprascapular nerve from the
      superior trunk, and the ulnar nerve from the medial cord.
  - stem: The cords of the brachial plexus are named according to their relationship to which structure?
    choices:
      - Subclavian artery
      - First part of the axillary artery
      - Second part of the axillary artery
      - Pectoralis major
      - Coracoid process
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The lateral, medial and posterior cords are named for their position around the **second part of the axillary
      artery**, which lies posterior to pectoralis minor.
