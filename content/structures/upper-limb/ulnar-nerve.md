---
name: Ulnar nerve
type: nerve
region: upper-limb
subregion: Forearm
taName: Nervus ulnaris
aka: [Funny bone nerve]
summary: Medial cord branch that passes behind the medial epicondyle and through Guyon's canal. It supplies most intrinsic hand muscles and the medial 1½ digits.
tags: [brachial-plexus]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Medial arm → posterior to the medial epicondyle (cubital tunnel) → medial anterior forearm → Guyon's canal → hand.
  roots: "C8, T1 (often C7)"
  origin: Terminal branch of the medial cord of the [[brachial-plexus]].
  course: >-
    Descends medial to the [[brachial-artery]] in the arm. At mid-arm it pierces the medial intermuscular septum with
    the superior ulnar collateral artery and descends on the medial head of triceps. It passes **posterior to the medial
    epicondyle** of the [[humerus]] in the ulnar groove/**cubital tunnel**, where it is subcutaneous and easily
    compressed (the "funny bone"). It enters the forearm between the two heads of **flexor carpi ulnaris (FCU)**
    and runs between FCU and FDP, with the [[ulnar-artery]] on its lateral side in the distal forearm. The
    **dorsal cutaneous branch** arises in the distal forearm. The nerve enters the hand **superficial to the flexor
    retinaculum** through **Guyon's canal** (the ulnar canal, between the pisiform and the hook of hamate), where it
    divides into superficial (sensory) and deep (motor) branches.
  branches:
    - Muscular branches to flexor carpi ulnaris and the medial half of flexor digitorum profundus (digits 4–5)
    - "**Palmar cutaneous branch:** medial palm"
    - "**Dorsal cutaneous branch:** medial dorsum of the hand and dorsal medial 1½ digits (arises before Guyon's canal)"
    - "**Superficial branch:** palmaris brevis; palmar skin of the medial 1½ digits"
    - "**Deep branch:** hypothenar muscles, medial two lumbricals, all interossei, adductor pollicis, deep head of flexor pollicis brevis"
  motor: >-
    **Forearm:** FCU and the medial half of FDP. **Hand:** most intrinsic muscles, including abductor digiti minimi,
    flexor digiti minimi brevis, opponens digiti minimi, the 3rd and 4th lumbricals, all palmar and dorsal
    interossei, adductor pollicis, the deep head of flexor pollicis brevis, and palmaris brevis.
  sensory: >-
    Palmar and dorsal skin of the medial 1½ digits (little finger and medial half of the ring finger) and the
    corresponding medial palm and dorsum. Most reliable test site: the **pulp of the little finger**.
clinical:
  - title: Cubital tunnel syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Compression or traction at the elbow: prolonged elbow flexion, leaning on the elbows, nerve subluxation, cubitus
      valgus after an old fracture, or fracture of the medial epicondyle. It is the second most common upper-limb
      entrapment neuropathy after carpal tunnel syndrome.
    presentation: >-
      Paresthesias in the little finger and the medial half of the ring finger, **including the dorsal ulnar hand**
      (the dorsal cutaneous branch arises proximal to the wrist). Medial elbow pain and a positive Tinel sign at the
      elbow. Weak grip, pinch and finger abduction; interosseous wasting in advanced cases.
  - title: Guyon's canal (ulnar tunnel) syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Compression at the wrist, e.g. cyclists leaning on handlebars ("handlebar palsy"), fracture of the **hook of
      hamate** (golf, baseball, racquet sports), ganglion cyst, or ulnar artery aneurysm/thrombosis.
    presentation: >-
      Intrinsic hand weakness ± palmar sensory loss in the medial 1½ digits, with **sparing of dorsal hand sensation**.
      FCU and FDP are spared.
  - title: Ulnar claw hand
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Loss of the medial lumbricals and interossei with intact long finger extensors and flexors.
    presentation: >-
      When the patient tries to extend the fingers, the 4th and 5th digits **hyperextend at the MCP and flex at the IP
      joints**. Clawing is **worse with distal (wrist) lesions** than proximal (elbow) lesions, because a proximal
      lesion also paralyzes the medial FDP (the "ulnar paradox").
pance:
  - "Hook of hamate fracture or cycling → ulnar nerve compression at **Guyon's canal**; dorsal hand sensation spared."
  - "**Froment sign** = weak adductor pollicis (thumb IP flexes via FPL to compensate)."
  - "Ulnar claw: 4th and 5th digits clawed; the ulnar paradox makes distal lesions claw more."
  - "Elbow (cubital tunnel) lesion: dorsal ulnar hand numbness + FCU/FDP weakness in addition to intrinsic weakness."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    Not visible. Elbow films show a medial epicondyle fracture, osteophytes or valgus deformity. A **carpal tunnel view**
    (or CT) shows a hook of hamate fracture, which is often missed on routine wrist views.
  ct: CT is sensitive for hook of hamate fractures.
  mri: >-
    Increased T2 signal and enlargement of the nerve in the cubital tunnel. Denervation edema of the intrinsic hand
    muscles. Masses in Guyon's canal.
  ultrasound: >-
    Measures nerve enlargement at the medial epicondyle. **Dynamic ultrasound** during elbow flexion shows subluxation
    or dislocation of the nerve over the medial epicondyle. At the wrist, identifies ganglia and ulnar artery
    thrombosis or aneurysm in Guyon's canal.
  keyViews:
    - "Elbow AP and lateral; carpal tunnel view for the hook of hamate"
    - "Dynamic ultrasound at the cubital tunnel, elbow extended → flexed"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    Palpable in the groove **posterior to the medial epicondyle**. At the wrist it lies lateral to the pisiform and
    medial to the ulnar artery, lateral to the FCU tendon.
  palpation: Roll the nerve in the ulnar groove with the elbow partly flexed. Check for tenderness, thickening, or subluxation with flexion.
  testing: >-
    **Finger abduction** against resistance (first dorsal interosseous). Adduction (card test for palmar interossei).
    FDP to the little finger (DIP flexion). Sensation at the pulp of the little finger, and on the dorsal ulnar hand to
    distinguish elbow from wrist lesions.
  specialTests:
    - name: Froment sign
      technique: >-
        The patient holds a sheet of paper between the thumb and the radial side of the index finger (key pinch) while
        the examiner pulls it away.
      positive: The thumb **IP joint flexes** (FPL, median nerve) to compensate for weak adductor pollicis.
      significance: Ulnar nerve palsy.
    - name: Wartenberg sign
      technique: Ask the patient to hold all fingers extended and adducted.
      positive: The little finger drifts into **abduction** and can't be adducted.
      significance: Weakness of the third palmar interosseous (ulnar nerve), with unopposed pull of extensor digiti minimi.
    - name: Elbow flexion test
      technique: The patient holds the elbow fully flexed with the wrist extended for about 1 minute.
      positive: Paresthesias in the ulnar distribution.
      significance: Suggests cubital tunnel syndrome.
    - name: Tinel sign (elbow)
      technique: Tap over the nerve in the ulnar groove behind the medial epicondyle.
      positive: Tingling radiating to the little and ring fingers.
      significance: Suggests ulnar neuropathy at the elbow.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 38-year-old competitive cyclist has numbness of the palmar little finger and weakness of finger abduction after
      long rides. Sensation over the dorsal ulnar hand is normal, and flexion of the little finger DIP joint is strong.
      Where is the nerve compressed?
    choices:
      - Cubital tunnel
      - Between the heads of flexor carpi ulnaris
      - Guyon's canal
      - Carpal tunnel
      - Medial intermuscular septum
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      Sparing of the **dorsal cutaneous branch** (dorsal hand sensation) and of FDP localizes the lesion **distal** to
      the forearm, at **Guyon's canal**. The median nerve, not the ulnar, runs in the carpal tunnel.
  - stem: >-
      When a patient pinches a piece of paper between the thumb and index finger, the thumb IP joint flexes. Weakness of
      which muscle does this compensate for?
    choices:
      - Flexor pollicis longus
      - Adductor pollicis
      - Opponens pollicis
      - Abductor pollicis brevis
      - First lumbrical
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      This is the **Froment sign**. The patient recruits **FPL** (median nerve) to compensate for a weak **adductor
      pollicis** (deep branch of the ulnar nerve).
  - stem: >-
      Compared with an ulnar nerve lesion at the wrist, why does a lesion at the elbow typically produce less clawing
      of the 4th and 5th digits?
    choices:
      - The lumbricals are spared in elbow lesions
      - The medial half of FDP is also paralyzed
      - The extensor digitorum is also paralyzed
      - The median nerve takes over the interossei
      - The dorsal cutaneous branch is spared
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A proximal lesion paralyzes the **medial FDP**, so there is less unopposed flexion at the IP joints. That's the
      **ulnar paradox**: the more proximal lesion produces less clawing.
  - stem: The ulnar nerve passes into the forearm between the two heads of which muscle?
    choices:
      - Pronator teres
      - Flexor digitorum superficialis
      - Flexor carpi ulnaris
      - Flexor carpi radialis
      - Supinator
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The ulnar nerve enters the forearm between the humeral and ulnar heads of **flexor carpi ulnaris**. The median
      nerve passes between the heads of pronator teres.
