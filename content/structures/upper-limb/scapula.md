anatomy:
  location: "Posterolateral thoracic wall, overlying ribs 2–7. {{verify: some sources give ribs 2–8}}"
  classification: Flat bone.
  features:
    - "**Spine**: ridge on the posterior surface, separating the supraspinous and infraspinous fossae; continues laterally as the **acromion**"
    - "**Acromion**: articulates with the [[clavicle]] at the AC joint; forms the point of the shoulder"
    - "**Coracoid process**: anterior beak-like projection (attachments: pectoralis minor, short head of biceps, coracobrachialis, coracoclavicular and coracoacromial ligaments)"
    - "**Glenoid cavity**: shallow articular fossa for the humeral head"
    - "**Supraglenoid tubercle** (long head of [[biceps-brachii]]) and **infraglenoid tubercle** (long head of [[triceps-brachii]])"
    - "**Suprascapular notch**: bridged by the superior transverse scapular ligament. The suprascapular **nerve passes below** and the **artery above** the ligament"
    - "**Spinoglenoid notch**: between the spine and the glenoid; the suprascapular nerve passes to infraspinatus"
    - "**Fossae**: supraspinous, infraspinous (posterior), subscapular (anterior/costal)"
    - "Superior, inferior and lateral angles; medial, lateral and superior borders"
  relations: >-
    Moves on the thoracic wall at the "scapulothoracic joint" (a physiological, not anatomical, joint), held in place by
    [[serratus-anterior]], trapezius, the rhomboids and levator scapulae. Seventeen muscles attach to it.
    {{verify: the count of attaching muscles varies with how muscles are counted}}
clinical:
  - title: Scapular fracture
    highYield: false
    mechanism: >-
      Uncommon. Requires **high-energy trauma** (motor vehicle collisions, falls from height), because the bone is
      mobile and protected by muscle.
    presentation: >-
      Posterior shoulder pain. The main concern is **associated injuries**: rib fractures, pneumothorax, pulmonary
      contusion, and injuries to the head, spine or great vessels.
  - title: Winged scapula
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      **Medial winging**: serratus anterior paralysis ([[long-thoracic-nerve]]). **Lateral winging**: trapezius
      paralysis (spinal accessory nerve, e.g. after posterior triangle lymph node biopsy) or rhomboid weakness (dorsal
      scapular nerve).
    presentation: The scapular border lifts from the thoracic wall, with shoulder weakness and pain on elevating the arm.
  - title: Glenoid rim fracture (bony Bankart lesion)
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Anterior dislocation of the [[glenohumeral-joint]] shears off the anteroinferior glenoid rim.
    presentation: Recurrent instability. Glenoid bone loss influences surgical management.
pance:
  - "A scapular fracture implies **high-energy trauma**; look for rib fractures, pneumothorax and pulmonary contusion."
  - "**Scapular Y view** judges anterior vs posterior shoulder dislocation."
  - "Suprascapular notch: **artery above**, **nerve below** the superior transverse scapular ligament (\"Army over the bridge, Navy under\")."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    The **scapular Y view** (true lateral scapula) shows the scapula as a "Y": the spine and acromion, the coracoid, and
    the body. The humeral head should be centered over the junction of the Y. It lies anterior (subcoracoid) in anterior
    dislocation and posterior in posterior dislocation. The AP (and Grashey, a true AP of the glenohumeral joint) and
    axillary views complete the shoulder series.
  ct: >-
    CT with 3D reconstruction characterizes scapular body and glenoid fractures and measures glenoid bone loss after
    instability.
  mri: Soft-tissue injuries (labrum, cuff) and paralabral cysts at the spinoglenoid notch.
  keyViews:
    - "Shoulder series: AP/Grashey, scapular Y, axillary"
    - "Chest X-ray in trauma (ribs, pneumothorax)"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    With the arm at the side, the **medial end of the spine** is at about the level of the **T3** spinous process and the
    **inferior angle** at about **T7**. The acromion, spine and inferior angle are subcutaneous. The **coracoid
    process** can be felt deep in the deltopectoral triangle, just inferior to the junction of the middle and lateral
    thirds of the clavicle.
  palpation: Palpate the acromion, spine, medial border, inferior angle and coracoid. Observe scapular position and symmetry at rest and with movement.
  testing: Observe scapulohumeral rhythm during abduction and check for winging (wall push-up, resisted protraction).
quiz:

      A 35-year-old unrestrained driver has a comminuted scapular body fracture on CT. Which associated injury is most

      - Radial head fracture
      - Rotator cuff tendinopathy
      - Adhesive capsulitis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A scapular fracture implies **high-energy trauma** and is associated with rib fractures, **pneumothorax**,
      pulmonary contusion and other thoracic injuries.
  - stem: On a scapular Y view, the humeral head lies anterior to the center of the Y, under the coracoid. What does this indicate?
    choices:
      - Normal alignment
      - Posterior dislocation
      - Anterior dislocation
      - Inferior (luxatio erecta) dislocation
      - Acromioclavicular separation
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      On the **scapular Y view** the humeral head normally overlies the center of the Y. Anterior (subcoracoid)
      displacement indicates **anterior dislocation**.
  - stem: The suprascapular nerve passes through which structure?
    choices:
      - Above the superior transverse scapular ligament
      - Below the superior transverse scapular ligament
      - Through the quadrangular space
      - Through the triangular interval
      - Deep to the coracoacromial ligament
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The suprascapular **nerve** passes **below** the superior transverse scapular ligament through the suprascapular
      notch. The suprascapular **artery** usually passes **above** it.
