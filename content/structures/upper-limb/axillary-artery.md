highYield: true

anatomy:
  location: "[[axilla]], from the lateral border of the first rib to the inferior border of teres major."
  origin: Continuation of the subclavian artery at the **lateral border of the first rib**.
  course: >-
    Descends through the axilla within the **axillary sheath** with the cords of the [[brachial-plexus]]. Divided by
    **pectoralis minor** into three parts:

    - **First part**: medial to pectoralis minor. **One** branch.
    - **Second part**: posterior to pectoralis minor. **Two** branches. The cords are named by their relation to this part.
    - **Third part**: lateral to pectoralis minor. **Three** branches.
  branches:
    - "**1st part:** superior thoracic artery"
    - "**2nd part:** thoracoacromial artery (clavicular, acromial, deltoid, pectoral branches); lateral thoracic artery"
    - "**3rd part:** subscapular artery (→ circumflex scapular and thoracodorsal arteries); anterior circumflex humeral artery; posterior circumflex humeral artery (with the [[axillary-nerve]] through the quadrangular space)"
    - "Mnemonic (in order): **S**crew **T**he **L**awyer **S**ave **A** **P**atient"
  supplies: Axillary walls, pectoral region, lateral thoracic wall (including the lateral breast), scapular muscles, and the shoulder joint and proximal humerus.
  anastomoses: >-
    **Scapular anastomosis** around the scapula: branches of the subclavian artery (suprascapular artery; dorsal
    scapular artery or deep branch of the transverse cervical artery) connect with branches of the axillary artery
    (circumflex scapular, thoracodorsal). If the axillary artery is occluded **between the thyrocervical trunk and the
    subscapular artery**, flow can reverse through this network to reach the third part. Around the surgical neck, the
    anterior and posterior circumflex humeral arteries anastomose.
  termination: Becomes the [[brachial-artery]] at the **inferior border of teres major**.
  relations: >-
    The **axillary vein** lies anteromedial to the artery. The cords of the brachial plexus surround the second part
    (lateral, medial and posterior to it), and their branches surround the third part. The median nerve forms anterior
    to the third part.
clinical:
  - title: Axillary artery injury
    highYield: false
    mechanism: >-
      Penetrating trauma, or blunt injury with anterior shoulder dislocation or proximal humerus fracture (older
      patients with atherosclerotic, less compliant vessels are at higher risk).
    presentation: >-
      Expanding axillary hematoma, diminished or absent distal pulses, and signs of limb ischemia. Associated
      brachial plexus injury is common because of their close relationship.
  - title: Collateral circulation after proximal occlusion
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Slow occlusion or ligation of the axillary artery between the thyrocervical trunk and subscapular artery.
    presentation: The limb may remain viable through the **scapular anastomosis**. Occlusion **distal** to the subscapular artery has much poorer collateral flow.
  - title: Posterior circumflex humeral artery injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Surgical neck fracture of the humerus (it runs with the axillary nerve around the surgical neck).
    presentation: Bleeding or hematoma with a proximal humerus fracture; accompanies axillary nerve injury.
pance:
  - "Pectoralis minor divides the axillary artery into 3 parts with **1, 2 and 3 branches**."
  - "Axillary artery becomes brachial at the **inferior border of teres major**."
  - "**Scapular anastomosis** protects the limb if the artery is ligated proximal to the subscapular artery."
  - "Posterior circumflex humeral artery + axillary nerve → quadrangular space and surgical neck."
imaging:
  xray: Not visible. Radiographs show dislocations or fractures that put it at risk.
  ct: >-
    **CT angiography** is the main modality in trauma or suspected occlusion. It shows extravasation, pseudoaneurysm,
    dissection or occlusion and the collateral network.
  mri: MR angiography is an alternative for non-emergent vascular evaluation.
  ultrasound: >-
    Duplex ultrasound in the axilla shows flow, stenosis, thrombosis and pseudoaneurysm. The artery is the landmark for
    ultrasound-guided **axillary brachial plexus block**, with the terminal nerves arranged around it.
  keyViews:
    - "CT angiography of the upper extremity"
    - "Duplex ultrasound, axillary approach"
exam:
  landmarks: Pulse in the axilla against the humerus along the lateral wall of the axilla, with the arm abducted.
  palpation: Palpate high in the axilla against the humerus, lateral to the axillary hair-bearing area, with the arm abducted about 90°.
  testing: >-
    In shoulder trauma, compare radial and brachial pulses and capillary refill with the other side. Check the brachial
    plexus as well.
