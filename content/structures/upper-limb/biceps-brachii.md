---
name: Biceps brachii
type: muscle
region: upper-limb
subregion: Arm
taName: Musculus biceps brachii
summary: Two-headed muscle of the anterior arm and the most powerful supinator of the forearm; it also flexes the elbow.
tags: [shoulder, elbow]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Anterior compartment of the arm, crossing both the shoulder and elbow joints.
  group: Anterior (flexor) compartment of the arm, with brachialis and coracobrachialis.
  origin: >-
    **Long head:** supraglenoid tubercle of the [[scapula]] (and the superior glenoid labrum). **Short head:** tip of
    the coracoid process of the scapula.
  insertion: >-
    Radial tuberosity of the [[radius]], and the **bicipital aponeurosis** (lacertus fibrosus) into the deep fascia of
    the medial forearm.
  innervation: "[[musculocutaneous-nerve]] (C5, C6)"
  bloodSupply: "Muscular branches of the [[brachial-artery]]"
  action:
    - Supinates the forearm (most powerful when the elbow is flexed)
    - Flexes the elbow (most effective with the forearm supinated)
    - The short head helps resist shoulder dislocation; the long head assists weak shoulder flexion
  relations: >-
    The **long head tendon** runs inside the [[glenohumeral-joint]] capsule (intracapsular but extrasynovial), then
    descends in the **intertubercular (bicipital) groove** held by the transverse humeral ligament. In the
    [[cubital-fossa]] the tendon lies **lateral** to the [[brachial-artery]] and [[median-nerve]]; the bicipital
    aponeurosis passes over both. The median cubital vein lies superficial to the aponeurosis.
clinical:
  - title: Proximal (long head) biceps tendon rupture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Usually degenerative, in older adults with rotator cuff disease or subacromial impingement. May follow a minor
      lifting effort.
    presentation: >-
      A sudden pop and anterior shoulder pain, then bruising and a distal bulge of the muscle belly (**"Popeye" sign**)
      on elbow flexion. Functional loss is usually minimal because the short head and brachialis remain.
  - title: Distal biceps tendon rupture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Usually middle-aged men, from an **eccentric load**: a heavy weight forcing a flexed elbow into extension.
    presentation: >-
      A pop at the antecubital fossa, then bruising, proximal retraction of the belly ("reverse Popeye"), and weakness of
      **supination** (more than flexion). The **hook test** is positive. Referred early for orthopedic evaluation, since
      repair is time-sensitive in active patients.
  - title: Bicipital tendinopathy / tenosynovitis
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Overuse, often coexisting with impingement or rotator cuff tendinopathy.
    presentation: Anterior shoulder pain localized to the bicipital groove, worse with lifting and overhead activity. Positive Speed and Yergason tests.
  - title: SLAP lesion
    highYield: false
    mechanism: >-
      Tear of the **superior labrum from anterior to posterior** at the long head's attachment, e.g. in throwing
      athletes or after a fall on an outstretched arm.
    presentation: Deep shoulder pain with overhead activity, clicking or catching. O'Brien (active compression) test may be positive.
pance:
  - "**Popeye sign** = long head rupture (older patient, often with cuff disease)."
  - "Distal rupture: eccentric load, weak **supination**, positive **hook test**."
  - "Tests: **Speed** (resisted forward flexion), **Yergason** (resisted supination)."
  - "Biceps reflex: **C5–C6**, musculocutaneous nerve."
  - "Biceps is the strongest supinator; brachialis is the strongest pure elbow flexor."
imaging:
  xray: Usually normal. Excludes bony avulsion from the radial tuberosity and other fractures.
  mri: >-
    Distal tendon: tear and retraction are best seen with the **FABS position** (Flexed elbow, ABducted shoulder,
    forearm Supinated), which shows the whole tendon in one plane. Proximal: tendinopathy and subluxation from the
    groove. MR arthrography is used for SLAP lesions.
  ultrasound: >-
    Short axis at the **bicipital groove**: the tendon is an oval hyperechoic structure in the groove. An **empty
    groove** means rupture or medial dislocation. Fluid around the tendon suggests tenosynovitis or a joint effusion.
    Distal tendon: assessed anteriorly and via medial or lateral windows.
  keyViews:
    - "Ultrasound short axis over the intertubercular groove"
    - "MRI elbow FABS view for the distal tendon"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    The muscle belly forms the anterior arm contour. The distal tendon is palpable in the cubital fossa when the
    patient flexes against resistance, with the brachial pulse just medial to it.
  palpation: The long head tendon is palpable in the bicipital groove with the arm slightly internally rotated (about 10°).
  testing: Resisted elbow flexion with the forearm supinated and resisted supination. Biceps reflex at the distal tendon.
  specialTests:
    - name: Speed's test
      technique: Elbow extended, forearm supinated. The patient forward-flexes the shoulder against resistance.
      positive: Pain in the bicipital groove.
      significance: Biceps (long head) tendinopathy or SLAP lesion.
    - name: Yergason's test
      technique: Elbow flexed to 90°, forearm pronated. The patient supinates against resistance.
      positive: Pain in the bicipital groove, or the tendon subluxing from the groove.
      significance: Biceps tendinopathy or instability of the tendon in the groove.
    - name: Hook test
      technique: >-
        Elbow flexed to 90° and forearm supinated. The examiner tries to hook an index finger under the lateral edge of
        the distal biceps tendon in the antecubital fossa.
      positive: No cord-like tendon can be hooked.
      significance: Complete distal biceps tendon rupture.
    - name: O'Brien (active compression) test
      technique: >-
        Shoulder flexed to 90° and adducted about 10–15°, elbow extended. The patient resists downward force first with
        the thumb down (full internal rotation), then with the palm up.
      positive: Pain (deep in the shoulder) with thumb down that is relieved with palm up.
      significance: Suggests a SLAP lesion (deep pain) or AC joint pathology (pain on top of the shoulder).
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 45-year-old man felt a pop in his right antecubital fossa while catching a heavy falling box. He has bruising
      and marked weakness of forearm supination. The examiner can't hook a finger under a tendon in the antecubital
      fossa. Which structure is injured?
    choices:
      - Long head of biceps tendon
      - Distal biceps tendon
      - Brachialis
      - Triceps tendon
      - Pronator teres
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Eccentric load, a pop at the elbow, weak **supination** and a positive **hook test** indicate a **distal biceps
      tendon rupture**. A long head rupture produces a Popeye deformity with little weakness.
  - stem: >-
      A 68-year-old man with chronic shoulder pain notices a new bulge in his lower anterior arm after lifting a
      suitcase. Strength is nearly normal. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Distal biceps rupture
      - Rupture of the long head of biceps
      - Supraspinatus tear
      - Triceps rupture
      - Brachialis strain
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **Popeye sign** comes from a **long head of biceps** rupture, typically degenerative with coexisting rotator
      cuff disease. The short head and brachialis keep elbow flexion strength largely intact.
  - stem: Which test is performed by resisting supination with the elbow flexed at 90° and the forearm pronated?
    choices:
      - Speed's test
      - Yergason's test
      - Hawkins–Kennedy test
      - Neer test
      - Hook test
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Yergason's test** stresses the long head of biceps through resisted supination. Speed's test uses resisted
      forward flexion with the elbow extended.
  - stem: The long head of biceps brachii originates from which structure?
    choices:
      - Coracoid process
      - Infraglenoid tubercle
      - Supraglenoid tubercle
      - Lesser tubercle
      - Acromion
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The long head arises from the **supraglenoid tubercle** and superior labrum. The short head arises from the
      coracoid process. The infraglenoid tubercle is the origin of the long head of triceps.
