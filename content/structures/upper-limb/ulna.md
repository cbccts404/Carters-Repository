taName: Ulna
summary: Medial, stabilizing bone of the forearm. Its trochlear notch forms the hinge of the elbow with the humerus.
tags: [forearm, elbow]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Medial (little finger side) of the forearm; large proximally, small distally.
  classification: Long bone.
  features:
    - "**Olecranon**: posterior projection, the point of the elbow; insertion of [[triceps-brachii]]"
    - "**Coronoid process**: anterior projection; origin of the ulnar head of [[pronator-teres]]"
    - "**Trochlear notch**: articulates with the trochlea of the [[humerus]]"
    - "**Radial notch**: articulates with the head of the [[radius]] (proximal radioulnar joint)"
    - "**Tuberosity of the ulna**: insertion of brachialis"
    - "**Supinator crest**: origin of part of supinator"
    - "**Head** (distal) and **ulnar styloid process**; separated from the carpals by the articular disc of the TFCC"
    - "Subcutaneous **posterior border** palpable along its whole length"
  relations: >-
    The [[ulnar-nerve]] runs along its medial side, deep to flexor carpi ulnaris. The interosseous membrane links it to
    the radius.
clinical:
  - title: Monteggia fracture-dislocation
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Fracture of the **proximal third of the ulna** with **dislocation of the radial head**, e.g. a fall with forced pronation or a direct blow.
    presentation: >-
      Forearm deformity and elbow pain. The radial head dislocation is easily **missed** unless the elbow is imaged.
      The posterior interosseous nerve may be injured.
  - title: Nightstick fracture
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Isolated fracture of the ulnar shaft from a direct blow, classically when raising the forearm to protect the face.
    presentation: Localized tenderness and swelling over the subcutaneous border of the ulna.
  - title: Olecranon fracture
    highYield: false
    mechanism: A fall directly onto the point of the elbow, or avulsion by triceps.
    presentation: Posterior elbow pain and swelling. **Displacement by triceps** may prevent active extension against gravity.
  - title: Olecranon bursitis
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Inflammation of the subcutaneous olecranon bursa from repetitive pressure ("student's elbow"), trauma, gout, or infection.
    presentation: Fluctuant swelling over the olecranon. Erythema, warmth and fever suggest **septic bursitis**, which needs aspiration.
pance:
  - "**MUGR**: **M**onteggia = **U**lnar fracture + radial head dislocation."
  - "Forearm fractures: always image the **elbow and the wrist** (joint above and below)."
  - "Nightstick fracture = isolated ulnar shaft fracture from a direct blow."
  - "Olecranon bursitis: aspirate if septic bursitis is suspected (fever, erythema, warmth)."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    Forearm AP and lateral including the elbow and wrist. In Monteggia, the **radiocapitellar line** does not pass
    through the capitellum. Elbow lateral view for olecranon fractures.
  ct: Comminuted or intra-articular proximal ulna and coronoid fractures.
  ultrasound: Characterizes olecranon bursal fluid and guides aspiration.
  keyViews:
    - "Forearm AP and lateral with the elbow and wrist included"
    - "Elbow lateral (olecranon, coronoid)"
exam:
  landmarks: The olecranon, the entire posterior border, the ulnar head (dorsomedial wrist) and the ulnar styloid are subcutaneous and palpable.
  palpation: Run a finger along the posterior border from the olecranon to the styloid, feeling for step-off or tenderness.
  testing: Elbow flexion and extension, forearm rotation, and ulnar nerve function.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 10-year-old boy has a displaced fracture of the proximal ulnar shaft after a fall. What associated injury must be
      excluded?
    choices:
      - Distal radioulnar joint dislocation
      - Radial head dislocation
      - Scaphoid fracture
      - Clavicle fracture
      - Lunate dislocation
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Monteggia fracture-dislocation**: proximal ulnar fracture with **radial head dislocation**. Check the
      radiocapitellar line. Galeazzi involves the DRUJ with a radial fracture.
  - stem: Which muscle inserts on the olecranon?
    choices:
      - Brachialis
      - Biceps brachii
      - Triceps brachii
      - Brachioradialis
      - Pronator teres
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Triceps brachii** inserts on the olecranon. Brachialis inserts on the ulnar tuberosity and coronoid process;
      biceps inserts on the radial tuberosity.
  - stem: A police officer raised his forearm to block a baton strike and has an isolated fracture of the ulnar shaft. What is this called?
    choices:
      - Monteggia fracture
      - Galeazzi fracture
      - Nightstick fracture
      - Smith fracture
      - Boxer's fracture
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      An isolated ulnar shaft fracture from a direct blow is a **nightstick fracture**. Monteggia and Galeazzi involve
      a joint dislocation.
