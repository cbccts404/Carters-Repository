name: Scaphoid
type: bone
region: upper-limb
subregion: Wrist & hand
taName: Os scaphoideum
aka: [Navicular of the hand]
summary: Boat-shaped bone of the proximal carpal row. It is the most commonly fractured carpal bone and its proximal pole is prone to avascular necrosis.
tags: [wrist]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Lateral part of the proximal carpal row, forming the floor of the anatomical snuffbox.
  classification: Short bone (carpal).
  features:
    - "**Proximal pole**, **waist**, **distal pole**"
    - "**Tubercle**: palpable at the base of the thenar eminence; attachment of the flexor retinaculum"
    - "Articulates with the [[radius]] proximally; with the lunate, capitate, trapezium and trapezoid"
  relations: >-
    Forms the floor of the **anatomical snuffbox**, with the [[radial-artery]] crossing over it. It links the proximal
    and distal carpal rows, which is part of why it is so often fractured.
  notes: >-
    **Blood supply is retrograde**: the main vessels (from the [[radial-artery]], mainly dorsal carpal branches) enter
    at the waist and distal pole and supply the proximal pole by flowing backward. A fracture through the waist can cut
    off the proximal pole, causing **avascular necrosis** and **nonunion**.
clinical:
  - title: Scaphoid fracture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Fall on an outstretched hand with the wrist dorsiflexed and radially deviated. The waist is the most common site. Typically young adults.
    presentation: >-
      Radial-sided wrist pain with **anatomical snuffbox tenderness**, scaphoid tubercle tenderness, and pain with axial
      loading of the thumb. **Initial radiographs can be normal.** With clinical suspicion, immobilize in a **thumb
      spica** splint and repeat imaging in 10–14 days, or obtain MRI (or CT) early.
  - title: Avascular necrosis and nonunion
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Interrupted retrograde blood flow to the proximal pole, especially in proximal pole or displaced fractures, or from delayed diagnosis.
    presentation: Persistent wrist pain. Radiographs show sclerosis and collapse of the proximal pole. Long-term, the result is scaphoid nonunion advanced collapse (SNAC) arthritis.
  - title: Scapholunate ligament injury
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Fall on an outstretched hand. The most common carpal instability.
    presentation: >-
      Dorsal wrist pain and weakness. On PA radiograph, a **widened scapholunate gap** ("Terry Thomas" sign).
      {{verify: >3 mm is the commonly quoted threshold}} Positive Watson scaphoid shift test.
pance:
  - "**Most commonly fractured carpal bone.** Snuffbox tenderness + normal X-ray → **thumb spica splint** and repeat imaging (or MRI)."
  - "Retrograde blood supply → **proximal pole AVN** and **nonunion**."
  - "Anatomical snuffbox: floor = scaphoid (and trapezium); contains the radial artery."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    PA, lateral, oblique, plus a dedicated **scaphoid view** (PA with ulnar deviation), which elongates the scaphoid
    and shows the waist. Fractures can be **radiographically occult** at first. Late signs: sclerosis of the proximal
    pole (AVN), nonunion, collapse.
  ct: Shows fracture displacement, union and nonunion. Useful for surgical planning.
  mri: The **most sensitive** modality for occult scaphoid fracture (marrow edema, fracture line). Also assesses proximal pole viability.
  keyViews:
    - "Scaphoid series: PA, lateral, oblique, PA in ulnar deviation"
    - "MRI wrist if radiographs are negative and suspicion persists"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    **Anatomical snuffbox:** a depression on the dorsolateral wrist when the thumb is extended, bounded by the EPL
    tendon (medial/ulnar) and the APL and EPB tendons (lateral/radial). The scaphoid lies in its floor. The **scaphoid
    tubercle** is palpable on the volar wrist at the distal wrist crease, at the base of the thenar eminence.
  palpation: Palpate the snuffbox and scaphoid tubercle for tenderness; compare with the other side.
  specialTests:
    - name: Scaphoid compression (axial load) test
      technique: The examiner applies axial compression along the long axis of the thumb metacarpal.
      positive: Pain at the radial wrist.
      significance: Suggests scaphoid fracture. Sensitivity is higher when combined with snuffbox and tubercle tenderness.
    - name: Watson scaphoid shift test
      technique: >-
        The examiner presses the scaphoid tubercle volarly with a thumb while moving the wrist from ulnar to radial
        deviation.
      positive: Pain and/or a clunk as the scaphoid subluxes dorsally and then reduces.
      significance: Scapholunate instability.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 22-year-old man fell on his outstretched hand while skateboarding. He has tenderness in the anatomical
      snuffbox. Wrist radiographs, including a scaphoid view, show no fracture. What is the most appropriate next step?
    choices:
      - Reassure and return to full activity
      - Thumb spica immobilization and repeat imaging in 10–14 days (or early MRI)
      - Immediate open reduction and internal fixation
      - Corticosteroid injection
      - Ulnar gutter splint
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Scaphoid fractures are often **occult** on initial films. With snuffbox tenderness, **immobilize in a thumb
      spica** and re-image, or get an MRI, to avoid missing a fracture that could progress to nonunion or AVN.
  - stem: Which portion of the scaphoid is most at risk of avascular necrosis after a waist fracture?
    choices:
      - Distal pole
      - Tubercle
      - Proximal pole
      - Waist
      - None; the scaphoid has a robust dual blood supply
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      Blood enters **distally** and flows **retrograde**, so a waist fracture can leave the **proximal pole** without
      a blood supply.
  - stem: Which tendons form the boundaries of the anatomical snuffbox?
    choices:
      - FCR and palmaris longus
      - EPL medially; APL and EPB laterally
      - EDC and EIP
      - ECRL and ECRB
      - FPL and FDS
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The snuffbox is bounded by **EPL** on the ulnar (medial) side and **APL/EPB** on the radial (lateral) side. The
      scaphoid and trapezium form the floor, and the radial artery crosses it.
