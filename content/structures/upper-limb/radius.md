summary: Lateral bone of the forearm. It rotates around the ulna in pronation and supination, and its distal end is one of the most commonly fractured sites in the body.

anatomy:
  location: Lateral (thumb side) of the forearm; narrow proximally, broad distally.
  classification: Long bone.
  features:
    - "**Head**: disc-shaped; articulates with the capitulum of the [[humerus]] and the radial notch of the [[ulna]]; encircled by the anular ligament"
    - "**Neck** and **radial tuberosity** (insertion of [[biceps-brachii]])"
    - "**Pronator tuberosity**: roughened area mid-lateral shaft (insertion of [[pronator-teres]])"
    - "**Interosseous border**: attachment of the interosseous membrane"
    - "**Ulnar notch** (distal radioulnar joint) and **carpal articular surface** (scaphoid and lunate)"
    - "**Radial styloid process**: extends about 1 cm further distally than the ulnar styloid"
    - "**Dorsal (Lister) tubercle**: the extensor pollicis longus tendon turns around it"
  relations: >-
    Proximal and distal radioulnar joints allow the radius to rotate around the ulna. The [[radial-artery]] is palpable
    anterior to its distal end, lateral to the FCR tendon.
clinical:
  - title: Distal radius fracture (Colles and Smith)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      **Colles:** fall on an outstretched hand with the wrist extended, typically an older woman with osteoporosis. The
      distal fragment is displaced and angulated **dorsally**, often with an ulnar styloid fracture. **Smith (reverse
      Colles):** fall on a flexed wrist; the distal fragment is displaced **volarly**.
    presentation: >-
      **Colles:** "dinner fork" deformity. **Smith:** "garden spade" deformity. Check median nerve function (acute carpal
      tunnel syndrome can develop). A late complication is **rupture of the EPL tendon** at Lister's tubercle.
  - title: Radial head fracture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Fall on an outstretched hand. The most common elbow fracture in adults.
    presentation: >-
      Lateral elbow pain and tenderness over the radial head, painful or blocked pronation and supination, and limited
      extension. Radiographs may show only a **fat pad sign**.
  - title: Radial head subluxation (nursemaid's elbow)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Sudden longitudinal traction on a young child's extended, pronated forearm (lifting or swinging by the hand). The
      anular ligament slips over the radial head. Typically ages 1–4. {{verify: quoted peak age range varies}}
    presentation: >-
      The child refuses to use the arm, holding it slightly flexed and pronated, without swelling or deformity. Reduced
      by **hyperpronation** or **supination with flexion**; X-rays aren't needed with a classic history.
  - title: Galeazzi fracture
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Fracture of the distal third of the radial shaft with dislocation of the **distal radioulnar joint**.
    presentation: Forearm deformity with a prominent ulnar head at the wrist. Imaging must include the wrist.
pance:
  - "**Colles** = dorsal displacement (dinner fork); **Smith** = volar displacement (garden spade)."
  - "Nursemaid's elbow: child pulled by the arm, holds it pronated; reduce by hyperpronation or supination–flexion."
  - "Adult FOOSH + fat pad sign + tender radial head → occult **radial head fracture**."
  - "**MUGR**: **M**onteggia = **U**lnar fracture + radial head dislocation; **G**aleazzi = **R**adial fracture + DRUJ dislocation."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    **Wrist PA and lateral.** Normal parameters are roughly: radial inclination ~22°, radial height ~11–12 mm and
    **volar tilt ~11°**. {{verify: normal values differ slightly between sources}} Loss of volar tilt (dorsal tilt)
    indicates a Colles-type fracture. **Elbow:** the **radiocapitellar line** (along the radial shaft/neck) should
    pass through the capitellum in every view. Its disruption indicates radial head dislocation (e.g. Monteggia).
  ct: Intra-articular distal radius fractures (step-off, comminution) and surgical planning.
  mri: Occult fractures and associated ligament injuries (scapholunate, TFCC).
  keyViews:
    - "Wrist PA and lateral (± oblique)"
    - "Elbow AP and lateral: radiocapitellar line and fat pads"
    - "Forearm AP/lateral including both the elbow and the wrist"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    The radial head is palpable in the depression just distal to the lateral epicondyle, where it can be felt rotating
    during pronation and supination. The radial styloid is palpable at the lateral wrist, and Lister's tubercle on the
    dorsal distal radius.
  palpation: Palpate the radial head, the shaft (distal half), Lister's tubercle and the styloid for tenderness.
  testing: Active and passive pronation/supination. Check median nerve function and the radial pulse after distal radius fractures.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 72-year-old woman falls on her outstretched hand. The wrist has a "dinner fork" deformity. What displacement of
      the distal radial fragment is expected on the lateral radiograph?
    choices:
      - Volar displacement and angulation
      - Dorsal displacement and angulation
      - No displacement with an intra-articular fracture line
      - Proximal migration of the radial head
      - Dislocation of the lunate
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **Colles fracture** has **dorsal** displacement and angulation of the distal fragment, producing the dinner-fork
      deformity. A Smith fracture has volar displacement.
  - stem: >-
      A 3-year-old boy won't use his left arm after his father lifted him by the hand onto a curb. The arm is held
      slightly flexed and pronated with no swelling. What is the next step?
    choices:
      - MRI of the elbow
      - Long arm cast
      - Reduction by hyperpronation or supination with flexion
      - Orthopedic surgery referral
      - Aspiration of the elbow joint
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      This is **radial head subluxation (nursemaid's elbow)**. With a classic history and exam, **closed reduction**
      is performed without imaging. The child typically resumes using the arm within minutes.
  - stem: Which fracture pattern consists of a fracture of the distal third of the radius with dislocation of the distal radioulnar joint?
    choices:
      - Monteggia fracture
      - Galeazzi fracture
      - Colles fracture
      - Barton fracture
      - Nightstick fracture
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Galeazzi** = radial shaft fracture + DRUJ dislocation. **Monteggia** = proximal ulnar fracture + radial head
      dislocation. A nightstick fracture is an isolated ulnar shaft fracture.
  - stem: >-
      A 35-year-old man falls onto an outstretched hand. He has lateral elbow tenderness and painful supination.
      Radiographs show an elevated anterior fat pad and a visible posterior fat pad, with no fracture line. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Normal elbow
      - Occult radial head fracture
      - Olecranon bursitis
      - Lateral epicondylitis
      - Posterior elbow dislocation
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **posterior fat pad** always indicates an effusion. In an adult after a fall on an outstretched hand with radial
      head tenderness, this implies an **occult radial head fracture**.
