aka: [Radiocarpal joint]
summary: Condyloid joint between the distal radius (and articular disc) and the proximal carpal row. It's a common site of fractures, instability and tenosynovitis.
tags: [wrist]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Between the distal forearm and the carpus.
  classification: Synovial **condyloid (ellipsoid)**, biaxial joint.
  bones: >-
    Distal [[radius]] and the **articular disc of the triangular fibrocartilage complex (TFCC)** proximally, with the
    **[[scaphoid]], lunate and triquetrum** distally. The [[ulna]] does not articulate directly with the carpus; the
    articular disc separates it.
  capsule: Fibrous capsule reinforced by the palmar and dorsal radiocarpal ligaments and the ulnar and radial collateral ligaments.
  ligaments:
    - "Palmar radiocarpal and palmar ulnocarpal ligaments (strong)"
    - "Dorsal radiocarpal ligament"
    - "Radial and ulnar collateral ligaments of the wrist"
    - "**Scapholunate** and lunotriquetral interosseous ligaments (key to carpal stability)"
    - "**TFCC**: articular disc, radioulnar ligaments and ulnocarpal ligaments; stabilizes the DRUJ and cushions the ulnar carpus"
  movements:
    - Flexion and extension (shared with the midcarpal joint)
    - Abduction (radial deviation) and adduction (ulnar deviation)
    - Circumduction
  stability: Carpal ligaments, the TFCC and the tendons crossing the wrist.
  innervation: "Anterior interosseous branch of the [[median-nerve]], posterior interosseous branch of the [[radial-nerve]], and dorsal and deep branches of the [[ulnar-nerve]]"
  bloodSupply: "Dorsal and palmar carpal arches from the [[radial-artery]] and [[ulnar-artery]]"
  relations: >-
    Volar: the [[carpal-tunnel]] (median nerve and flexor tendons) and Guyon's canal (ulnar nerve and artery). Dorsal:
    the extensor tendons in six compartments; the first compartment (APL, EPB) is the site of de Quervain
    tenosynovitis. Radial: the anatomical snuffbox with the radial artery.
clinical:
  - title: De Quervain tenosynovitis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Stenosing tenosynovitis of the **first dorsal compartment** (**abductor pollicis longus** and **extensor pollicis
      brevis**) from repetitive thumb and wrist motion, e.g. **new parents lifting infants**.
    presentation: Radial-sided wrist pain and tenderness over the radial styloid, with a positive Finkelstein test.
  - title: Ganglion cyst
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Mucin-filled cyst arising from a joint capsule or tendon sheath. It is the most common soft-tissue mass of the hand
      and wrist, most often dorsal over the scapholunate region.
    presentation: Smooth, firm, often transilluminating mass that may change in size. Usually painless or mildly painful.
  - title: Lunate and perilunate dislocation
    highYield: false
    mechanism: High-energy hyperextension (fall from height, motor vehicle collision).
    presentation: >-
      Wrist pain and swelling. The volarly displaced lunate may compress the median nerve (**acute carpal tunnel
      syndrome**). Easily missed without a true lateral radiograph.
  - title: Kienböck disease
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Avascular necrosis of the **lunate**, associated with negative ulnar variance (a short ulna).
    presentation: Dorsal wrist pain and stiffness. Progressive sclerosis and collapse of the lunate.
  - title: TFCC injury
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Fall on an outstretched pronated hand, or degenerative (positive ulnar variance).
    presentation: Ulnar-sided wrist pain, clicking, worse with ulnar deviation and rotation.
pance:
  - "**De Quervain**: APL + EPB (first dorsal compartment); **Finkelstein** positive; new parents."
  - "**Ganglion cyst**: most common hand/wrist mass; dorsal; transilluminates."
  - "Lunate dislocation → acute median nerve compression; **\"spilled teacup\"** on lateral X-ray."
  - "Proximal row (lateral to medial): scaphoid, lunate, triquetrum, pisiform. Distal row: trapezium, trapezoid, capitate, hamate."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    **PA, lateral and oblique.** PA: **Gilula arcs** (three smooth arcs along the carpal rows) should be unbroken;
    scapholunate gap (widening suggests scapholunate dissociation); ulnar variance. Lateral: the radius, lunate and
    capitate should be **colinear**. In **lunate dislocation**, the lunate tips volarly ("**spilled teacup**") and
    looks triangular on PA ("piece of pie").
  ct: Occult carpal fractures and fracture characterization.
  mri: TFCC tears (MR arthrography improves sensitivity), ligament injuries, Kienböck disease, occult scaphoid fracture.
  ultrasound: Ganglion cysts (anechoic, well defined), de Quervain tenosynovitis (thickened first-compartment tendons and sheath), and guidance for injections.
  keyViews:
    - "Wrist PA, lateral and oblique"
    - "Scaphoid view (PA ulnar deviation)"
    - "MR arthrogram for TFCC"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    Radial styloid, **Lister's tubercle**, ulnar head and styloid, **anatomical snuffbox** (scaphoid), pisiform (base of
    the hypothenar eminence), hook of hamate (in the hypothenar eminence, distal and radial to the pisiform), and
    the scaphoid tubercle (base of the thenar eminence).
  palpation: Palpate systematically radial → dorsal → ulnar → volar, including the snuffbox, first dorsal compartment, TFCC (ulnar fovea) and pisiform.
  testing: Active and passive flexion, extension, and radial and ulnar deviation. Grip strength.
  specialTests:
    - name: Finkelstein test
      technique: >-
        The patient flexes the thumb into the palm and closes the fingers over it, then the examiner ulnarly deviates
        the wrist. (This commonly taught form is technically the Eichhoff maneuver; in Finkelstein's original, the
        examiner grasps the thumb and deviates the wrist.)
      positive: Sharp pain over the radial styloid (first dorsal compartment).
      significance: De Quervain tenosynovitis. The commonly taught form produces false positives in normal wrists.
    - name: Ulnar fovea sign
      technique: Press into the soft spot between the ulnar styloid and FCU tendon, between the pisiform and the ulnar head.
      positive: Tenderness reproducing the patient's pain.
      significance: Suggests a TFCC foveal tear or ulnotriquetral ligament injury. {{verify}}
    - name: Watson scaphoid shift test
      technique: The examiner presses on the scaphoid tubercle while moving the wrist from ulnar to radial deviation.
      positive: Pain and/or a clunk.
      significance: Scapholunate instability.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 31-year-old woman, 6 weeks postpartum, has pain over the radial side of her wrist when lifting her baby. Pain
      is reproduced when she makes a fist around her thumb and ulnarly deviates the wrist. Which tendons are involved?
    choices:
      - Extensor carpi radialis longus and brevis
      - Abductor pollicis longus and extensor pollicis brevis
      - Extensor pollicis longus
      - Flexor carpi radialis
      - Extensor digitorum
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **De Quervain tenosynovitis** involves the **first dorsal compartment**: **APL and EPB**. EPL is in the third
      compartment, where it turns around Lister's tubercle.
  - stem: Which bones form the distal articular surface of the radiocarpal joint?
    choices:
      - Scaphoid, lunate and triquetrum
      - Scaphoid, trapezium and trapezoid
      - Lunate, capitate and hamate
      - Pisiform, triquetrum and hamate
      - All eight carpal bones
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      The radius and articular disc articulate with the **scaphoid, lunate and triquetrum**. The pisiform sits on the
      triquetrum and isn't part of the radiocarpal joint.
  - stem: >-
      After a fall from a ladder, a patient has wrist pain and numbness of the thumb, index and middle fingers. The
      lateral radiograph shows the lunate tipped volarly, like a spilled teacup. Which nerve is at risk?
    choices:
      - Ulnar nerve
      - Median nerve
      - Superficial radial nerve
      - Posterior interosseous nerve
      - Musculocutaneous nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A volarly dislocated **lunate** pushes into the carpal tunnel and compresses the **median nerve** (acute carpal
      tunnel syndrome). This is an orthopedic emergency.
