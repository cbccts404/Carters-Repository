name: Brachial artery
type: artery
region: upper-limb
subregion: Arm
taName: Arteria brachialis
summary: Main artery of the arm, continuing from the axillary artery. It gives the profunda brachii, then divides in the cubital fossa into the radial and ulnar arteries; it's the site for blood pressure measurement.
tags: [elbow]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Medial arm, then the [[cubital-fossa]].
  origin: Continuation of the [[axillary-artery]] at the **inferior border of teres major**.
  course: >-
    Descends on the medial side of the arm, anterior to triceps and then brachialis, in the medial bicipital groove.
    The [[median-nerve]] lies lateral to it proximally, crosses it (usually anteriorly) at mid-arm, and lies medial to
    it at the elbow. In the [[cubital-fossa]] it lies **medial to the biceps tendon** and deep to the **bicipital
    aponeurosis**.
  branches:
    - "**Profunda brachii (deep artery of the arm):** accompanies the [[radial-nerve]] in the radial groove; supplies the posterior arm and ends in collateral branches around the elbow"
    - "**Superior ulnar collateral artery:** accompanies the [[ulnar-nerve]] behind the medial epicondyle"
    - "**Inferior ulnar collateral artery:** above the medial epicondyle"
    - "Nutrient artery of the humerus; muscular branches"
    - "**Terminal branches:** [[radial-artery]] and [[ulnar-artery]]"
  supplies: Anterior arm muscles, humerus; posterior arm via the profunda brachii.
  anastomoses: >-
    **Periarticular arterial anastomosis of the elbow**: the collateral arteries (from the brachial and profunda
    brachii) connect with the recurrent arteries (from the radial, ulnar and interosseous arteries). This network can
    maintain flow to the forearm if the brachial artery is slowly occluded distal to the profunda brachii.
  termination: >-
    Divides into the **radial and ulnar arteries** in the cubital fossa, at about the level of the **neck of the
    radius**. A **high division** occurs as a variant.
  relations: >-
    Superficial and palpable along much of its course. Accompanied by paired venae comitantes. The median cubital vein
    lies superficial to it, separated by the bicipital aponeurosis.
clinical:
  - title: Supracondylar fracture with arterial injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Displaced extension-type supracondylar humerus fracture in a child; the proximal fragment can tent, kink or tear the artery.
    presentation: >-
      A **pulseless hand** that may be pink (collateral-perfused) or white (ischemic). Forearm **compartment
      syndrome** (pain out of proportion, pain with passive finger extension) can lead to **Volkmann ischemic
      contracture** if unrecognized.
  - title: Blood pressure measurement
    highYield: true
    mechanism: The cuff occludes the brachial artery against the humerus.
    presentation: >-
      Korotkoff sounds are auscultated over the artery in the cubital fossa, **medial to the biceps tendon**. The cuff
      should fit the arm, with its bladder centered over the artery.
  - title: Proximal hemorrhage control
    highYield: false
    mechanism: The artery lies superficially along the medial arm and can be compressed against the humerus.
    presentation: Manual compression at mid-arm, medial to the humerus, controls distal bleeding temporarily.

imaging:
  xray: Not visible. Assess fractures (supracondylar) that threaten it.
  ct: CT angiography in trauma (e.g. a pulseless supracondylar fracture that stays pulseless after reduction) or suspected occlusion.
  ultrasound: >-
    Duplex ultrasound shows flow, stenosis and pseudoaneurysm (e.g. after catheterization or arterial puncture). The
    artery is a landmark for ultrasound-guided access to the brachial veins.
  keyViews:
    - "Duplex ultrasound of the brachial artery"
    - "CT angiography of the upper extremity"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    Pulse palpable in the medial bicipital groove of the arm, and in the cubital fossa **medial to the biceps tendon**
    (feel the tendon while the patient flexes against resistance, then move medially).
  palpation: Palpate with the elbow slightly flexed and relaxed.
  testing: >-
    In elbow trauma, assess the radial pulse, capillary refill, color, temperature, and pain on passive finger extension
    (compartment syndrome).

    explanation: >-

      contracture**.
