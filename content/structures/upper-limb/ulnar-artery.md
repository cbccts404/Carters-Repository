name: Ulnar artery
type: artery
region: upper-limb
subregion: Forearm
taName: Arteria ulnaris
summary: Larger terminal branch of the brachial artery. It gives the common interosseous artery, enters the hand through Guyon's canal, and forms most of the superficial palmar arch.
tags: [forearm, wrist, hand]
systems: [cardiovascular]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Medial anterior forearm → Guyon's canal → superficial palm.
  origin: Terminal branch of the [[brachial-artery]] in the [[cubital-fossa]].
  course: >-
    Passes deep to the ulnar head of [[pronator-teres]] and descends deep to flexor digitorum superficialis, then between
    flexor carpi ulnaris and flexor digitorum profundus. In the distal forearm the [[ulnar-nerve]] lies **medial** to
    it. At the wrist it passes **superficial to the flexor retinaculum**, lateral to the pisiform, through **Guyon's
    canal** with the ulnar nerve. It ends as the **superficial palmar arch**.
  branches:
    - Anterior and posterior ulnar recurrent arteries (elbow anastomosis)
    - "**Common interosseous artery** → anterior and posterior interosseous arteries (deep forearm)"
    - Palmar and dorsal carpal branches
    - Deep palmar branch (joins the deep palmar arch)
    - Superficial palmar arch → common palmar digital arteries
  supplies: Medial forearm muscles, deep forearm (via the interosseous arteries), medial carpus, and most of the fingers (via the superficial palmar arch).
  anastomoses: >-
    Superficial and deep palmar arches with the [[radial-artery]]. The recurrent branches join the periarticular
    anastomosis of the elbow.
  termination: >-
    **Superficial palmar arch**, completed by the superficial palmar branch of the radial artery. The arch lies at about
    the level of the distal border of the fully extended thumb. {{verify}}
clinical:
  - title: Hypothenar hammer syndrome
    highYield: false
    mechanism: >-
      Repetitive blunt trauma to the hypothenar eminence (using the palm as a hammer) damages the superficial ulnar
      artery where it crosses the **hook of hamate**, causing aneurysm or thrombosis with distal emboli.
    presentation: >-
      Pain, pallor, coldness and Raynaud-like changes of the **ulnar-side fingers** (typically 4th and 5th) in a manual
      worker. The Allen test shows poor ulnar filling. May compress the ulnar nerve in Guyon's canal.
  - title: Ulnar artery injury at the wrist
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Lacerations at the medial wrist; the ulnar nerve is often cut at the same time.
    presentation: Bleeding, with numbness of the little finger and intrinsic weakness if the nerve is involved.
pance:
  - "Ulnar artery → mainly the **superficial** palmar arch."
  - "Travels with the ulnar nerve through **Guyon's canal** (superficial to the flexor retinaculum)."
  - "Hypothenar hammer syndrome: manual worker, digital ischemia of the ulnar fingers."
imaging:
  ct: CT angiography shows aneurysm or occlusion in hypothenar hammer syndrome and in trauma.
  ultrasound: Duplex ultrasound for patency, aneurysm or thrombosis at the hook of hamate, and flow assessment before radial artery procedures.
  keyViews:
    - "Duplex at the wrist and hypothenar region"
    - "Catheter or CT angiography of the hand (tortuous \"corkscrew\" ulnar artery is described in hypothenar hammer syndrome) {{verify}}"
exam:
  landmarks: Pulse palpable at the medial wrist, **lateral to the FCU tendon** and just proximal to the pisiform. It is fainter than the radial pulse.
  palpation: Palpate lateral to the FCU tendon, proximal to the pisiform.
  specialTests:
    - name: Allen test
      technique: See [[radial-artery]]. Release each artery in turn after occluding both.
      positive: Delayed palmar flushing when the ulnar artery is released indicates poor ulnar flow.
      significance: Assesses the contribution of each artery to the palmar arches.

    explanation: >-

  - stem: Which branch of the ulnar artery supplies the deep compartments of the forearm?
    choices:
      - Anterior ulnar recurrent artery
      - Common interosseous artery
      - Superficial palmar branch
      - Palmar carpal branch
      - Princeps pollicis artery
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **common interosseous artery** divides into the anterior and posterior interosseous arteries, which run on
      either side of the interosseous membrane.
  - stem: The ulnar artery enters the hand through which structure?
    choices:
      - Carpal tunnel
      - Guyon's canal
      - Anatomical snuffbox
      - First dorsal interosseous muscle
      - Quadrangular space
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The ulnar artery and [[ulnar-nerve]] pass through **Guyon's canal**, superficial to the flexor retinaculum. The
      carpal tunnel contains the median nerve and the long flexor tendons.
