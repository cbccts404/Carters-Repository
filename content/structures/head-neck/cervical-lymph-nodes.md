name: Cervical lymph nodes
type: lymphatic
region: head-neck
subregion: Neck
taName: Nodi lymphoidei cervicales
aka: [Neck lymph nodes, Deep cervical nodes, Virchow node, Jugulodigastric node]
summary: Superficial and deep nodes of the head and neck, grouped clinically into levels I–VI. Lymphadenopathy is common, and a persistent neck mass in an adult is cancer until proven otherwise.
tags: [lymphatic, neck, oncology]
systems: [hematologic, eent]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: >-
    **Superficial ring** at the junction of head and neck (occipital, mastoid, preauricular/parotid, submandibular,
    submental) and **superficial cervical** nodes along the external jugular vein; the **deep cervical chain** along the
    [[internal-jugular-vein]] under [[sternocleidomastoid]]. Key named nodes — **jugulodigastric** (tonsil, below the
    angle of the jaw) and **jugulo-omohyoid** (tongue).
  drains: >-
    All lymph from the head and neck ultimately reaches the deep cervical chain. Tip of tongue and lower lip →
    submental. Face, lateral tongue → submandibular. Scalp, ear → occipital/mastoid/preauricular. Tonsil, pharynx →
    jugulodigastric. Thyroid and larynx → central compartment and deep nodes.
  drainsTo: >-
    Deep cervical nodes → **jugular trunk** → on the left the **[[thoracic-duct]]**, on the right the right lymphatic
    duct → the junction of the internal jugular and subclavian veins.
  relations: >-
    Clinical **levels**: **I** submental/submandibular; **II** upper jugular (skull base to hyoid); **III** middle
    jugular (hyoid to cricoid); **IV** lower jugular (cricoid to clavicle); **V** posterior triangle; **VI** central
    compartment (pretracheal, paratracheal, prelaryngeal). **VII** superior mediastinum in some systems. The spinal
    accessory nerve runs through level V (see [[triangles-of-the-neck]]).
  notes: The **left supraclavicular node (Virchow)** receives lymph from the thoracic duct region and can enlarge with abdominal or thoracic cancers (**Troisier sign**).
clinical:
  - title: Cervical lymphadenopathy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Usually **reactive** (viral URI, strep pharyngitis, dental infection); also EBV/CMV, cat-scratch disease (*Bartonella*), TB (scrofula), lymphoma and metastatic cancer.
    presentation: >-
      Reactive nodes are tender, soft, mobile and under about 1 cm. **Concerning**: **over 1.5–2 cm**, **hard, fixed,
      non-tender**, **supraclavicular location**, persistent over 2–4 weeks, weight loss/night sweats/fever, age over 40,
      smoking or alcohol history. **Posterior cervical** nodes suggest mononucleosis; **anterior** nodes, strep.
  - title: Adult neck mass
    highYield: true
    mechanism: In adults a persistent lateral neck mass is **metastatic squamous cell carcinoma** (oral cavity, oropharynx — often HPV, larynx) or **lymphoma** until proven otherwise.
    presentation: 'Workup: full head and neck exam with **laryngoscopy**, **contrast CT of the neck**, and **fine-needle aspiration** (avoid open excision first). HPV-related oropharyngeal cancer often presents as a **cystic** node.'
  - title: Virchow node
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Metastasis to the **left supraclavicular** node via the thoracic duct.
    presentation: A hard left supraclavicular node → look for **gastric** and other abdominal or thoracic cancers. Right supraclavicular nodes drain the mediastinum, lungs and esophagus.
  - title: Lymphoma
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Hodgkin (Reed–Sternberg cells; young adults; contiguous spread) or non-Hodgkin lymphoma.
    presentation: Painless, **rubbery** cervical or supraclavicular nodes ± B symptoms; Hodgkin nodes may hurt after alcohol. Diagnosis requires **excisional biopsy** (architecture), unlike SCC.
pance:
  - "Hard, fixed, painless, > 1.5 cm or supraclavicular node → **biopsy/FNA**."
  - "**Left supraclavicular (Virchow) node** → gastric/abdominal cancer."
  - "Adult with persistent neck mass → **head and neck SCC** until proven otherwise → CT + FNA + scope."
  - "Posterior cervical nodes → **mono**; anterior → **strep**."
  - "Suspected lymphoma → **excisional** biopsy (FNA is insufficient)."
imaging:
  ultrasound: First-line for superficial nodes in children and to guide FNA; features of malignancy — round shape, loss of the fatty hilum, necrosis.
  ct: '**Contrast CT of the neck** for adult neck masses and cancer staging; necrotic nodes suggest SCC or TB.'
  mri: Complementary for staging; **PET-CT** for staging lymphoma and head and neck cancers or finding an unknown primary.
  keyViews:
    - "Contrast CT neck"
    - "Ultrasound-guided FNA"
exam:
  landmarks: Levels I–VI as above; the supraclavicular fossa behind the clavicle.
  palpation: >-
    Use a systematic sequence with the pads of the fingers, patient's neck slightly flexed: preauricular, posterior
    auricular, occipital, tonsillar (jugulodigastric), submandibular, submental, superficial cervical, **deep cervical
    chain** (hook around SCM), posterior cervical, **supraclavicular** (have the patient shrug or perform Valsalva).
    Note size, consistency, tenderness, mobility and matting.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 58-year-old man who smokes has a painless, firm 3-cm mass in the left upper neck for 6 weeks. There are no
      infectious symptoms. What is the most appropriate next step?
    choices:
      - Two more weeks of antibiotics
      - Excisional biopsy of the node first
      - Complete head and neck exam with laryngoscopy, contrast CT of the neck and FNA
      - Reassurance and follow-up in 6 months
      - Monospot test only
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      A persistent neck mass in an adult smoker is **metastatic SCC** until proven otherwise. Look for the primary
      (endoscopy), image (CT) and obtain cytology (**FNA**). Open excisional biopsy first can seed the neck and
      compromise treatment.
  - stem: A hard left supraclavicular lymph node is most classically associated with which cancer?
    choices:
      - Papillary thyroid carcinoma
      - Gastric adenocarcinoma
      - Basal cell carcinoma of the scalp
      - Laryngeal carcinoma
      - Parotid tumor
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Virchow node** (left supraclavicular) receives lymph near the termination of the **thoracic duct** and is a
      classic sign of **gastric** (and other abdominal) cancer.
