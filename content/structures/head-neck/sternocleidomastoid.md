---
name: Sternocleidomastoid
type: muscle
region: head-neck
subregion: Neck
taName: Musculus sternocleidomastoideus
aka: [SCM, Sternomastoid]
summary: Prominent two-headed neck muscle that divides the neck into anterior and posterior triangles. It is supplied by the accessory nerve and is shortened in congenital muscular torticollis.
tags: [neck, cranial-nerves]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  group: Superficial lateral neck muscle, enclosed in the investing layer of deep cervical fascia (with trapezius)
  origin: '**Sternal head** — anterior surface of the manubrium; **clavicular head** — superior surface of the medial third of the clavicle (see [[sternum]], [[clavicle]]).'
  insertion: Lateral surface of the **mastoid process** and the lateral half of the superior nuchal line (see [[skull]]).
  innervation: '**Spinal accessory nerve (CN XI)** (motor); C2–C3 ventral rami (proprioception, via the [[cervical-plexus]]).'
  bloodSupply: Occipital and superior thyroid arteries (branches of the [[external-carotid-artery]]) and the suprascapular artery.
  action:
    - "**One side**: tilts the head to the same side and **rotates the face to the opposite side**"
    - "**Both sides**: flex the neck (and extend the head at the atlanto-occipital joint)"
    - "Accessory muscle of inspiration when the head is fixed (raises the sternum)"
  relations: >-
    Divides the neck into the **anterior and posterior triangles** (see [[triangles-of-the-neck]]). Deep to it lies the
    **carotid sheath** ([[carotid-arteries]], [[internal-jugular-vein]], vagus). The **nerve point of the neck**
    (Erb point), where the cutaneous branches of the cervical plexus emerge, is at the midpoint of its posterior border.
    The external jugular vein crosses it superficially.
  notes: The small gap between the sternal and clavicular heads (lesser supraclavicular fossa) overlies the internal jugular vein — a landmark for central venous access.
clinical:
  - title: Congenital muscular torticollis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Fibrosis and shortening of one SCM, associated with intrauterine positioning or birth trauma (breech, difficult delivery).
    presentation: Infant with the **head tilted toward the affected side and chin rotated to the opposite side**; sometimes a palpable nontender mass ("SCM tumor") in the first weeks; plagiocephaly. Most respond to **stretching/physical therapy**. Check the hips (associated developmental dysplasia).
  - title: Spinal accessory nerve injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Most commonly **iatrogenic in the posterior triangle** (lymph node biopsy, neck dissection), where the nerve is superficial.
    presentation: 'Posterior triangle injury usually spares SCM (its branch arises higher) but paralyzes **trapezius** → shoulder droop, lateral scapular winging, weak abduction above 90°. A proximal lesion also weakens SCM → weak head turning to the **opposite** side.'
  - title: Cervical dystonia (spasmodic torticollis)
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Focal dystonia in adults with involuntary contraction of neck muscles including SCM.
    presentation: Involuntary head turning or tilting, often with pain; treated with botulinum toxin injections.
pance:
  - "Infant head tilted toward one side, chin to the other → **congenital muscular torticollis** → stretching; check the hips."
  - "SCM and trapezius are supplied by **CN XI**."
  - "Right SCM turns the face to the **left**."
  - "Posterior triangle node biopsy → CN XI injury → **trapezius** weakness (shoulder droop)."
imaging:
  ultrasound: Confirms fibromatosis colli (SCM thickening) in infants; avoids radiation.
  ct: Neck masses deep to SCM (e.g. branchial cleft cyst along its anterior border, carotid body tumor).
exam:
  landmarks: Visible and palpable from the mastoid to the sternum and clavicle, especially when the head is turned to the opposite side; the suprasternal notch lies between the two sternal heads.
  palpation: Palpate along the anterior border for lymph nodes (deep cervical chain) and masses.
  testing: '**CN XI (SCM)**: ask the patient to turn the head against your hand — the **opposite** SCM contracts (turning to the left tests the right SCM). Compare both sides; test trapezius with a shoulder shrug.'

      - Physical therapy with passive stretching and repositioning

    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Congenital muscular torticollis** from a shortened **right SCM** (tilt to the right, chin to the left) usually
      resolves with **stretching and positioning**. Surgery is reserved for persistent cases; evaluate the hips for
      developmental dysplasia.

      A patient is asked to turn his head to the left against resistance. Which muscle is mainly being tested?

      - Left scalene muscles
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Each SCM rotates the face to the **opposite** side, so turning the head to the **left** tests the **right** SCM
      (CN XI).
