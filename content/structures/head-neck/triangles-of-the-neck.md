name: Triangles of the neck
type: space
region: head-neck
subregion: Neck
taName: Regio cervicalis anterior; Regio cervicalis lateralis
aka: [Anterior triangle, Posterior triangle, Carotid triangle, Submandibular triangle, Lateral cervical region]
summary: Sternocleidomastoid divides each side of the neck into anterior and posterior triangles, and each is subdivided further. Knowing their contents helps localize neck masses and avoid injuring the accessory nerve.
tags: [neck, surface-anatomy]
systems: [eent]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Each side of the neck, divided by [[sternocleidomastoid]].
  boundaries:
    anterior triangle: Midline of the neck (front), anterior border of SCM (back), inferior border of the [[mandible]] (top); apex at the suprasternal notch
    posterior triangle: Posterior border of SCM (front), anterior border of [[trapezius]] (back), middle third of the [[clavicle]] (base); apex at the occipital bone; roof — investing fascia; floor — prevertebral fascia over splenius, levator scapulae and the scalenes
    submandibular (digastric) triangle: Mandible and the two bellies of digastric
    carotid triangle: Superior belly of omohyoid, posterior belly of digastric, anterior border of SCM
    muscular triangle: Superior belly of omohyoid, anterior border of SCM, midline of the neck
    submental triangle: Anterior bellies of both digastrics and the hyoid (unpaired)
    occipital and omoclavicular triangles: Parts of the posterior triangle above and below the inferior belly of omohyoid
  contents:
    - "**Submandibular triangle**: [[submandibular-gland]], submandibular nodes, facial artery and vein, hypoglossal nerve"
    - "**Carotid triangle**: **carotid bifurcation**, carotid sinus and body ([[carotid-arteries]], [[external-carotid-artery]]), [[internal-jugular-vein]], vagus and hypoglossal nerves, ansa cervicalis, deep cervical nodes"
    - "**Muscular triangle**: infrahyoid (strap) muscles, [[thyroid-gland]], [[parathyroid-glands]], [[larynx]], [[trachea]], [[esophagus]]"
    - "**Submental triangle**: submental nodes, small veins forming the anterior jugular vein"
    - "**Posterior triangle**: **spinal accessory nerve (CN XI)** crossing superficially from SCM to trapezius, cutaneous branches of the [[cervical-plexus]], external jugular vein, **trunks of the [[brachial-plexus]]**, subclavian artery (third part), occipital and supraclavicular nodes"
  communications: >-
    The posterior triangle leads down behind the clavicle into the **axilla** (cervicoaxillary canal) with the brachial
    plexus and subclavian vessels (see [[axilla]]).
  notes: The **hyoid bone** (C3) lies at the junction of the submandibular, carotid and muscular triangles.
clinical:
  - title: Neck masses by location
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Congenital lesions follow embryology; location narrows the differential.
    presentation: >-
      **Midline**: thyroglossal duct cyst (moves with tongue protrusion), dermoid cyst, thyroid isthmus. **Anterior
      triangle (lateral)**: **branchial cleft cyst** (2nd arch — along the anterior border of SCM, often after a URI),
      carotid body tumor (pulsatile, moves side to side but not vertically — **Fontaine sign**), deep cervical nodes,
      submandibular gland. **Posterior triangle**: **cystic hygroma** (lymphatic malformation, infants,
      transilluminates), nodes (mono, lymphoma, metastases, TB).
  - title: Spinal accessory nerve injury in the posterior triangle
    highYield: true
    mechanism: The nerve lies **superficially** on levator scapulae in the posterior triangle, so it is vulnerable during **lymph node biopsy** and neck dissection.
    presentation: '**Trapezius** paralysis → shoulder droop, difficulty abducting above 90°, lateral scapular winging; SCM usually spared.'
  - title: Brachial plexus blocks
    highYield: false
    mechanism: The trunks of the brachial plexus pass through the lower posterior triangle between the anterior and middle scalenes.
    presentation: '**Interscalene** and **supraclavicular** blocks are performed here (with ultrasound); nearly all interscalene blocks paralyze the ipsilateral phrenic nerve.'
pance:
  - "SCM divides anterior from posterior triangle."
  - "Posterior triangle node biopsy → **CN XI** injury → trapezius weakness."
  - "Lateral neck cyst along the anterior border of SCM → **branchial cleft cyst**; midline → **thyroglossal duct cyst**."
  - "Transilluminating posterior triangle mass in an infant → **cystic hygroma** (Turner syndrome)."
  - "Carotid triangle → **carotid bifurcation** (pulse, bruits, carotid body tumor)."
imaging:
  ultrasound: First-line for neck masses in children and for thyroid and nodes; guides FNA.
  ct: Contrast CT of the neck for adult neck masses and deep infections.
  mri: Vascular and soft-tissue tumors (e.g. paraganglioma, lymphatic malformation extent).
exam:
  landmarks: 'Turn the head to the opposite side to outline SCM. Hyoid (C3), thyroid cartilage (C4–C5), cricoid (C6); the clavicle forms the base of the posterior triangle.'
  palpation: Palpate each triangle systematically with the lymph node sequence; describe masses by triangle, size, consistency, mobility, pulsatility and relation to swallowing and tongue protrusion.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 24-year-old has a smooth, fluctuant, nontender mass along the anterior border of the left sternocleidomastoid
      that enlarged after a cold. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Thyroglossal duct cyst
      - Branchial cleft cyst
      - Cystic hygroma
      - Parathyroid adenoma
      - Carotid body tumor
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **lateral** cystic mass along the **anterior border of SCM** that swells with URIs is a **second branchial cleft
      cyst**. A thyroglossal cyst is midline; a carotid body tumor is firm and pulsatile at the bifurcation.
  - stem: >-
      After an excisional biopsy of a node in the posterior triangle, a patient cannot shrug her right shoulder fully and
      has difficulty lifting her arm above her head. Which nerve was injured?
    choices:
      - Long thoracic nerve
      - Spinal accessory nerve
      - Suprascapular nerve
      - Phrenic nerve
      - Great auricular nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **spinal accessory nerve (CN XI)** crosses the posterior triangle superficially and supplies **trapezius**.
      Injury causes shoulder droop and weak abduction above 90°.
  - stem: Which structure is found in the carotid triangle?
    choices:
      - Trunks of the brachial plexus
      - Bifurcation of the common carotid artery
      - Submandibular gland
      - Spinal accessory nerve crossing to trapezius
      - Thyroid isthmus
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **carotid triangle** (omohyoid, posterior digastric, SCM) contains the **carotid bifurcation**, carotid sinus
      and body, internal jugular vein, and the vagus and hypoglossal nerves.
  - stem: >-
      A newborn girl with Turner syndrome has a soft, transilluminating mass in the posterior triangle of the neck. What
      is it most likely to be?
    choices:
      - Branchial cleft cyst
      - Thyroglossal duct cyst
      - Cystic hygroma (lymphatic malformation)
      - Carotid body tumor
      - Lymphoma
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      A **cystic hygroma** is a lymphatic malformation, typically a soft, **transilluminating posterior triangle** mass
      in an infant, associated with **Turner syndrome**. Branchial cleft cysts lie along the anterior border of SCM.
  - stem: >-
      A painless, pulsatile mass at the carotid bifurcation moves side to side but not up and down. What is the most
      likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Carotid body tumor (paraganglioma)
      - Branchial cleft cyst
      - Thyroid nodule
      - Reactive lymph node
      - Thyroglossal duct cyst
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      A **carotid body tumor** lies at the bifurcation in the **carotid triangle**; it is pulsatile and moves
      **horizontally but not vertically** (Fontaine sign). Thyroid and thyroglossal lesions move with swallowing.
