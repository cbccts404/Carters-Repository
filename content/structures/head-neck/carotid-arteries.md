name: Common and internal carotid arteries
type: artery
region: head-neck
subregion: Neck
taName: Arteria carotis communis; Arteria carotis interna
aka: [Carotid artery, Common carotid artery, Internal carotid artery, ICA, CCA]
summary: Main arteries of the head. Each common carotid divides at about C4 into external and internal carotids, and atherosclerosis at the bifurcation is a major cause of stroke and TIA.
tags: [neck, stroke, vascular]
systems: [cardiovascular, neurologic]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  origin: '**Right common carotid**: from the brachiocephalic trunk behind the right sternoclavicular joint. **Left common carotid**: directly from the **arch of the aorta** (see [[thoracic-aorta]]).'
  course: >-
    Ascends in the **carotid sheath** with the [[internal-jugular-vein]] (lateral) and **vagus nerve** (posterior,
    between them), under cover of [[sternocleidomastoid]]. It **bifurcates at the upper border of the thyroid
    cartilage (about C3–C4)** in the carotid triangle (see [[triangles-of-the-neck]]). The **internal carotid** has **no
    branches in the neck**; it enters the skull through the carotid canal, runs through the **cavernous sinus**, and
    ends by dividing into the anterior and middle cerebral arteries.
  branches:
    - "**Common carotid**: no branches in the neck apart from its terminal division"
    - "**[[external-carotid-artery]]** — face, scalp, neck, dura"
    - "**Internal carotid** — no neck branches; intracranially the **ophthalmic artery** (see [[orbit]]), posterior communicating, anterior choroidal, then **anterior and middle cerebral arteries**"
  supplies: Internal carotid — the brain (anterior circulation), eye and orbit, forehead.
  anastomoses: >-
    The **circle of Willis** links both internal carotids with the vertebrobasilar system (see [[vertebral-artery]]).
    Ophthalmic branches anastomose with facial branches of the external carotid — a collateral path if the internal
    carotid is occluded.
  relations: >-
    The **carotid sinus** (baroreceptor, CN IX) is a dilation at the origin of the internal carotid; the **carotid body**
    (chemoreceptor) lies at the bifurcation. The hypoglossal nerve crosses both carotids above the bifurcation.
  notes: The carotid pulse is palpated at the anterior border of sternocleidomastoid at the level of the thyroid cartilage.
clinical:
  - title: Carotid artery stenosis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Atherosclerotic plaque at the **bifurcation/proximal ICA**; emboli or low flow cause ischemia in the ICA territory.
    presentation: >-
      **TIA or stroke** — contralateral weakness or numbness, aphasia (dominant hemisphere), or **amaurosis fugax**
      (transient monocular blindness, "curtain coming down" — ophthalmic artery emboli); a **carotid bruit** may be
      heard. **Symptomatic stenosis of 70–99%** → **carotid endarterectomy** (or stenting) within about 2 weeks, plus
      antiplatelet therapy and statin; 50–69% symptomatic → consider surgery. Asymptomatic stenosis — medical therapy,
      with selective intervention.
  - title: Carotid artery dissection
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Intimal tear, spontaneous or after neck trauma or manipulation; a common cause of **stroke in young adults**.
    presentation: 'Unilateral **neck, face or head pain**, ipsilateral **Horner syndrome** (partial — ptosis and miosis), and TIA/stroke; sometimes pulsatile tinnitus or lower cranial nerve palsies. CTA/MRA diagnoses it.'
  - title: Carotid sinus hypersensitivity
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Exaggerated baroreceptor reflex in older adults.
    presentation: Syncope with head turning, tight collars or shaving. Carotid sinus massage (a vagal maneuver) can slow SVT; avoid it if there is a carotid bruit or recent stroke.
pance:
  - "**Amaurosis fugax** (curtain over one eye) → ICA emboli → **carotid duplex**."
  - "**Symptomatic** 70–99% stenosis → **carotid endarterectomy** (within 2 weeks)."
  - "Young adult with neck pain + **Horner syndrome** + stroke → **carotid dissection**."
  - "Common carotid bifurcates at the **upper border of the thyroid cartilage (C4)**."
  - "Carotid **sinus** = baroreceptor; carotid **body** = chemoreceptor (both CN IX)."
imaging:
  ultrasound: '**Carotid duplex ultrasound** — first-line to detect and grade stenosis.'
  ct: '**CT angiography** of the head and neck — stenosis, dissection and planning for intervention.'
  mri: MRA (with or without contrast) as an alternative; MRI with fat-saturated sequences shows intramural hematoma in dissection.
  keyViews:
    - "Carotid duplex (peak systolic velocity grades stenosis)"
    - "CTA head and neck"
exam:
  landmarks: Anterior border of sternocleidomastoid, level of the thyroid cartilage.
  palpation: Palpate **one carotid at a time** (never both together) at the thyroid cartilage level, low in the neck to avoid the carotid sinus. Assess rate, amplitude and upstroke (slow-rising in aortic stenosis).
  testing: '**Auscultate for bruits** with the bell while the patient holds a breath. Absence of a bruit does not exclude severe stenosis.'

      A 68-year-old man had 10 minutes of painless vision loss in his right eye "like a curtain coming down," which has
      resolved. What is the most appropriate next test?
    choices:
      - Temporal artery biopsy
      - Carotid duplex ultrasound
      - Echocardiogram only
      - MRI of the lumbar spine
      - Tonometry
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Amaurosis fugax** is a TIA of the retina, usually from emboli from the ipsilateral **internal carotid**
      (ophthalmic artery). Urgent **carotid imaging (duplex)** is needed; consider giant cell arteritis in older
      patients with headache or jaw claudication.
  - stem: >-
      A 38-year-old woman has sudden left-sided neck pain after a chiropractic adjustment, followed by right arm weakness.
      Her left pupil is small and her left eyelid droops. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Left internal carotid artery dissection
      - Right middle cerebral artery embolism from atrial fibrillation
      - Migraine with aura
      - Left Pancoast tumor
      - Bell palsy
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      **Neck pain + ipsilateral Horner syndrome** (sympathetic fibers run on the ICA) **+ contralateral weakness** after
      neck manipulation indicates **left internal carotid dissection**.
  - stem: Where does the common carotid artery normally bifurcate?
    choices:
      - At the level of the cricoid cartilage (C6)
      - At the upper border of the thyroid cartilage (about C3–C4)
      - At the angle of the mandible (C2)
      - At the sternoclavicular joint
      - Within the carotid canal
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The bifurcation is usually at the **upper border of the thyroid cartilage (C3–C4)**, in the carotid triangle,
      although it varies.
  - stem: Which cranial nerve carries afferent signals from the carotid sinus baroreceptor?
    choices:
      - Facial nerve (CN VII)
      - Vagus nerve (CN X)
      - Glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX)
      - Hypoglossal nerve (CN XII)
      - Accessory nerve (CN XI)
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **carotid sinus** (baroreceptor, at the origin of the ICA) and the **carotid body** (chemoreceptor, at the
      bifurcation) are both supplied by the **glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX)**.
  - stem: >-
      A 70-year-old had a right-hand weakness TIA 5 days ago. Carotid duplex shows 80% stenosis of the left internal
      carotid artery. What is the most appropriate management?
    choices:
      - Aspirin alone, with repeat ultrasound in 1 year
      - Warfarin
      - Observation only
      - Thrombolysis
      - Carotid endarterectomy within about 2 weeks, plus antiplatelet and statin therapy
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      **Symptomatic 70–99% ICA stenosis** is treated with **carotid endarterectomy** (or stenting), ideally **within
      about 2 weeks**, along with antiplatelet therapy and a statin. A left ICA lesion causes right-sided symptoms.
