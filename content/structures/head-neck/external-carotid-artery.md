---
name: External carotid artery
type: artery
region: head-neck
subregion: Neck
taName: Arteria carotis externa
aka: [ECA]
summary: Main artery of the face, scalp and neck outside the skull. It has eight branches (SALFOPSM) and ends in the parotid gland as the superficial temporal and maxillary arteries.
tags: [neck, face, vascular]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  origin: Terminal branch of the **[[carotid-arteries|common carotid artery]]** at the upper border of the thyroid cartilage (about C3–C4).
  course: >-
    Starts **anteromedial** to the internal carotid in the carotid triangle, ascends deep to the posterior belly of
    digastric and stylohyoid, and enters the [[parotid-gland]], where it ends behind the neck of the [[mandible]].
  branches:
    - "**S**uperior thyroid — thyroid, larynx (superior laryngeal artery), SCM (see [[thyroid-gland]])"
    - "**A**scending pharyngeal — pharynx, tonsil, meninges (see [[pharynx]])"
    - "**L**ingual — tongue, floor of mouth (see [[tongue]])"
    - "**F**acial — hooks over the mandible at the anterior border of masseter; face, tonsil, submandibular gland; ends as the angular artery"
    - "**O**ccipital — back of the scalp"
    - "**P**osterior auricular — scalp behind the ear, auricle"
    - "**S**uperficial temporal (terminal) — temple and scalp (see [[scalp]])"
    - "**M**axillary (terminal) — deep face: **[[middle-meningeal-artery]]**, inferior alveolar, muscles of mastication, **sphenopalatine** (nasal cavity), infraorbital, palatine"
  supplies: The face, scalp, thyroid, tongue, pharynx, nasal cavity, teeth, muscles of mastication and much of the dura.
  anastomoses: Rich anastomoses across the midline and with the internal carotid system (facial ↔ ophthalmic at the angle of the eye) — so facial and scalp wounds bleed heavily, and the ECA is a collateral path when the ICA is occluded.
  termination: Divides into the **superficial temporal** and **maxillary** arteries within the parotid gland.
  relations: The glossopharyngeal nerve and stylopharyngeus pass between the external and internal carotids; the hypoglossal nerve crosses superficially to both.
  notes: Mnemonic for the branches in order — "**S**ome **A**natomists **L**ike **F**reaking **O**ut **P**oor **S**tudents in **M**edicine" (Superior thyroid, Ascending pharyngeal, Lingual, Facial, Occipital, Posterior auricular, Superficial temporal, Maxillary).
clinical:
  - title: Giant cell (temporal) arteritis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Granulomatous large-vessel vasculitis affecting the **superficial temporal artery** and other ECA/ICA branches (ophthalmic, posterior ciliary); age over 50; linked to polymyalgia rheumatica.
    presentation: >-
      New **temporal headache**, scalp tenderness, **jaw claudication**, visual loss, a tender, nodular or pulseless
      temporal artery, **very high ESR/CRP**. Start **high-dose glucocorticoids immediately** (before biopsy) to prevent
      blindness; confirm with **temporal artery biopsy** or ultrasound.
  - title: Epidural hematoma and posterior epistaxis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Maxillary artery branches — **[[middle-meningeal-artery]]** (skull fracture at the pterion) and **sphenopalatine artery** (posterior nosebleeds).
    presentation: See [[middle-meningeal-artery]] and [[nasal-cavity]]. The sphenopalatine artery can be ligated or embolized for refractory posterior epistaxis.
  - title: Facial and scalp bleeding
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Rich blood supply and anastomoses.
    presentation: Facial and scalp lacerations bleed briskly but heal well; the facial artery pulse is felt where it crosses the mandible.
pance:
  - "Branches = **SALFOPSM**; ends as superficial temporal + maxillary in the parotid."
  - "Age > 50, headache, **jaw claudication**, high ESR → **giant cell arteritis** → **steroids first**, then temporal artery biopsy."
  - "Maxillary artery → **middle meningeal** (epidural hematoma) and **sphenopalatine** (posterior epistaxis)."
  - "The ECA has branches in the neck; the ICA does not."
imaging:
  ultrasound: Duplex distinguishes the ECA (branches in the neck, higher-resistance waveform) from the ICA; temporal artery ultrasound shows the "halo sign" in giant cell arteritis.
  ct: CTA of the head and neck.
  keyViews:
    - "Catheter angiography for embolization (epistaxis, tumors)"
exam:
  landmarks: '**Facial artery** pulse at the lower border of the mandible at the anterior edge of masseter; **superficial temporal** pulse just in front of the tragus.'
  palpation: Palpate the temporal arteries for tenderness, nodularity and reduced pulse in suspected giant cell arteritis.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 72-year-old woman has a new headache over the right temple, pain in her jaw when chewing, and blurred vision.
      ESR is 95 mm/h. What is the most appropriate next step?
    choices:
      - Schedule temporal artery biopsy and start steroids only if positive
      - Start high-dose glucocorticoids immediately
      - MRI brain and outpatient follow-up
      - NSAIDs and reassess in 2 weeks
      - Lumbar puncture
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      This is **giant cell arteritis** with visual symptoms. **Start high-dose glucocorticoids immediately** to prevent
      permanent blindness; the biopsy remains diagnostic for 1–2 weeks after starting steroids.
  - stem: Which artery is a terminal branch of the external carotid artery?
    choices:
      - Facial artery
      - Lingual artery
      - Maxillary artery
      - Ophthalmic artery
      - Superior thyroid artery
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The ECA ends in the parotid gland by dividing into the **maxillary** and **superficial temporal** arteries. The
      ophthalmic artery is a branch of the **internal** carotid.
