summary: Cartilaginous airway organ from C3 to C6 that protects the airway and produces voice. Epiglottitis, croup, vocal cord paralysis from recurrent laryngeal nerve injury and emergency cricothyrotomy are the key topics.
tags: [airway, voice, emergency]
systems: [eent, pulmonary]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Anterior neck at **C3–C6**, below the hyoid bone and above the [[trachea]], in front of the laryngopharynx (see [[pharynx]]).
  parts:
    - "**Cartilages**: thyroid (laryngeal prominence, \"Adam's apple\"), **cricoid** (the only complete ring, at C6), paired arytenoids, epiglottis, corniculate and cuneiform"
    - "**Membranes**: thyrohyoid membrane (pierced by the internal laryngeal nerve and superior laryngeal artery); **cricothyroid membrane** (between thyroid and cricoid — the emergency airway site)"
    - "**Vestibular (false) folds** above and **vocal (true) folds** below; the space between the vocal folds is the **rima glottidis**, the **narrowest part of the adult airway**. In infants the narrowest part is the subglottis at the cricoid level. {{verify: pediatric narrowest point is debated}}"
    - "**Divisions**: supraglottis, glottis, subglottis"
  bloodSupply: Superior laryngeal artery (from the superior thyroid artery, a branch of the [[external-carotid-artery]]) and inferior laryngeal artery (from the inferior thyroid artery).
  venousDrainage: Superior and inferior laryngeal veins → thyroid veins.
  lymphatics: Supraglottis → deep cervical nodes (rich drainage); glottis — very sparse lymphatics (early vocal cord cancers rarely spread); subglottis → pretracheal/paratracheal nodes.
  innervation: >-
    All from the **[[vagus-nerve|vagus (CN X)]]**. **Superior laryngeal nerve**: internal branch — sensation above the vocal folds (cough
    reflex); external branch — **cricothyroid** (tenses the cords; pitch). **[[recurrent-laryngeal-nerve]]** — all other
    intrinsic muscles and sensation below the vocal folds. **Posterior cricoarytenoid** is the **only abductor**
    (opens the glottis).
  relations: The [[thyroid-gland]] lies on the front and sides of the lower larynx and upper trachea; the carotid sheaths lie laterally.
clinical:
  - title: Epiglottitis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Bacterial cellulitis of the epiglottis and supraglottis — classically *H. influenzae* type b (now rare after vaccination); now also streptococci and *S. aureus*, and adults.
    presentation: >-
      Rapid onset of high fever, severe sore throat, **drooling, dysphagia, muffled voice, tripod positioning**, toxic
      appearance; inspiratory stridor is late. **Keep the patient calm; don't examine the throat with a tongue
      depressor**; secure the airway in the OR; IV ceftriaxone. Lateral neck X-ray shows the **thumbprint sign**.
  - title: Croup (laryngotracheobronchitis)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Parainfluenza virus** causing subglottic edema; ages 6 months–3 years.'
    presentation: '**Barking (seal-like) cough**, hoarseness, inspiratory stridor, worse at night; **steeple sign** on AP neck film. Treat with **dexamethasone**; add **nebulized racemic epinephrine** for stridor at rest.'
  - title: Laryngeal cancer and hoarseness
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Squamous cell carcinoma — **smoking and alcohol**; glottic tumors cause early hoarseness.
    presentation: '**Hoarseness lasting over about 4 weeks** (especially in a smoker) needs **laryngoscopy**. Other causes: laryngitis, vocal nodules (voice overuse), reflux, recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy.'
  - title: Emergency cricothyrotomy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '"Can''t intubate, can''t oxygenate" situations.'
    presentation: "Incision through the **cricothyroid membrane**, which lies just below the thyroid cartilage and is relatively avascular and superficial. Needle cricothyrotomy is preferred in young children (under about 10–12 years). {{verify: pediatric age cutoff varies by source}}"
pance:
  - "Drooling, tripod position, muffled voice, toxic → **epiglottitis** (thumbprint sign) → airway in OR; don't use a tongue depressor."
  - "Barking cough, steeple sign → **croup** (parainfluenza) → **dexamethasone** ± racemic epinephrine."
  - "Hoarseness > 4 weeks in a smoker → **laryngoscopy** (SCC)."
  - "Only laryngeal abductor = **posterior cricoarytenoid**."
  - "Cricothyroid muscle = **external laryngeal nerve**; everything else = recurrent laryngeal."
imaging:
  xray: 'Lateral neck: **thumbprint sign** (epiglottitis). AP neck: **steeple sign** (croup). Airway imaging must not delay airway management.'
  ct: Contrast CT of the neck for laryngeal cancer staging and trauma.
  mri: Cartilage invasion and staging of laryngeal cancer.
  keyViews:
    - "Soft-tissue lateral and AP neck"
    - "Flexible laryngoscopy (not imaging, but the key examination)"
exam:
  landmarks: 'From top down: hyoid bone (C3), thyroid notch and laryngeal prominence (C4–C5), **cricothyroid membrane** (a soft dip), cricoid cartilage (C6), trachea.'
  palpation: Palpate the thyroid and cricoid cartilages and the cricothyroid membrane (practice this landmark); the larynx and thyroid move up with swallowing.
  testing: Listen to the voice (hoarseness, breathiness), cough strength and stridor (inspiratory = supraglottic/glottic; biphasic = subglottic/tracheal); indirect mirror or flexible laryngoscopy for cord mobility.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 4-year-old unvaccinated child has a 6-hour history of high fever, drooling, muffled voice and sits leaning forward
      with the neck extended. What is the most appropriate next step?
    choices:
      - Examine the throat with a tongue depressor
      - Keep the child calm and arrange airway management in the operating room
      - Oral dexamethasone and discharge
      - Nebulized albuterol
      - Throat culture in the waiting room
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      This is **epiglottitis** (likely *H. influenzae* type b in an unvaccinated child). Agitating the child or using a
      tongue depressor can precipitate complete obstruction. **Secure the airway** in a controlled setting, then give IV
      antibiotics.
  - stem: Which intrinsic laryngeal muscle is the only abductor of the vocal cords?
    choices:
      - Lateral cricoarytenoid
      - Posterior cricoarytenoid
      - Cricothyroid
      - Transverse arytenoid
      - Thyroarytenoid
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **posterior cricoarytenoid** rotates the arytenoids to **open** the glottis; bilateral paralysis can cause
      airway obstruction. The cricothyroid tenses the cords.
  - stem: A 2-year-old has a barking cough and inspiratory stridor at rest after a cold. What is the best initial treatment?
    choices:
      - Amoxicillin
      - Dexamethasone and nebulized racemic epinephrine
      - Immediate intubation
      - Albuterol only
      - Chest physiotherapy
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Moderate-to-severe **croup** (stridor at rest) is treated with **corticosteroids (dexamethasone)** plus **nebulized
      racemic epinephrine**, observing for rebound. It is viral, so antibiotics don't help.
  - stem: Which nerve supplies the cricothyroid muscle?
    choices:
      - Recurrent laryngeal nerve
      - Internal laryngeal nerve
      - Hypoglossal nerve
      - External laryngeal nerve (branch of the superior laryngeal)
      - Glossopharyngeal nerve
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      **Cricothyroid** (tenses the cords; pitch) is the only intrinsic laryngeal muscle supplied by the **external
      laryngeal nerve**. All others, including the posterior cricoarytenoid, are supplied by the recurrent laryngeal
      nerve.
  - stem: On an AP neck radiograph, what sign is typical of croup?
    choices:
      - Thumbprint sign
      - Steeple sign
      - Double bubble sign
      - Sail sign
      - Widened prevertebral soft tissue
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Croup** (parainfluenza) causes subglottic narrowing, the **steeple sign** on an AP film. **Epiglottitis** shows
      the **thumbprint sign** on a lateral film; retropharyngeal abscess widens the prevertebral soft tissue.
