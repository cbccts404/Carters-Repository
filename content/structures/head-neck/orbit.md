summary: Pyramidal bony cavity holding the eyeball, extraocular muscles, nerves and vessels. Its thin floor and medial wall break in blowout fractures, and infection behind the orbital septum is an emergency.

highYield: true

anatomy:
  location: Paired cavities in the upper face, apex pointing posteromedially toward the optic canal.
  boundaries:
    roof: Orbital plate of the frontal bone and lesser wing of the sphenoid (anterior cranial fossa above)
    floor: Maxilla (with zygomatic and palatine bones) — thin; the **maxillary sinus** lies below; infraorbital nerve in its groove
    medial wall: Ethmoid (lamina papyracea — paper-thin), lacrimal, maxilla, sphenoid; ethmoid sinuses medially
    lateral wall: Zygomatic bone and greater wing of the sphenoid — the thickest wall
    apex: Optic canal and superior orbital fissure
  contents:
    - "[[eyeball]] and [[optic-nerve|optic nerve (CN II)]]"
    - "[[extraocular-muscles]] and levator palpebrae superioris"
    - "Nerves: [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]], [[trochlear-nerve|IV]], [[abducens-nerve|VI]], [[trigeminal-nerve|V1]] branches (frontal, lacrimal, nasociliary), ciliary ganglion"
    - "Ophthalmic artery (from the internal carotid) and superior/inferior ophthalmic veins"
    - "Lacrimal gland (superolateral) and orbital fat"
  communications: >-
    **Optic canal** → middle cranial fossa (CN II, ophthalmic artery). **Superior orbital fissure** (CN III, IV, V1,
    VI, superior ophthalmic vein) → cavernous sinus (see [[dural-venous-sinuses]]). **Inferior orbital fissure** → pterygopalatine and infratemporal
    fossae. Ophthalmic veins are **valveless** and drain to the **cavernous sinus** — a route for spreading infection.
  notes: The **orbital septum**, a fascial sheet from the orbital rim to the tarsal plates, separates the eyelids (preseptal) from the orbit proper (postseptal).
clinical:
  - title: Orbital floor ("blowout") fracture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: A blunt object larger than the orbit (fist, ball) raises intraorbital pressure, fracturing the thin **floor** (or medial wall).
    presentation: >-
      **Diplopia on upward gaze** (entrapment of **inferior rectus**), enophthalmos, **numbness of the cheek and upper
      lip** (infraorbital nerve), periorbital emphysema after nose blowing. Entrapment in children can trigger the
      oculocardiac reflex (bradycardia, nausea) — urgent repair.
  - title: Orbital vs preseptal (periorbital) cellulitis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Orbital cellulitis** — infection behind the septum, usually spread from **ethmoid sinusitis**. **Preseptal** — eyelid infection in front of the septum.'
    presentation: >-
      Both cause eyelid redness and swelling. **Red flags for orbital cellulitis**: **pain with eye movements,
      ophthalmoplegia, proptosis, decreased vision**, fever. Orbital cellulitis needs **CT orbits**, IV antibiotics and
      ophthalmology; complications include abscess and **cavernous sinus thrombosis**.
  - title: Retrobulbar hemorrhage (orbital compartment syndrome)
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Bleeding behind the eye after trauma or surgery compresses the optic nerve and its blood supply.
    presentation: Painful proptosis, tense orbit, reduced vision, relative afferent pupillary defect — emergency **lateral canthotomy** and cantholysis.
pance:
  - "Blowout fracture: diplopia on **upgaze** (inferior rectus entrapment), cheek numbness (infraorbital nerve)."
  - "Pain with eye movement, proptosis, ophthalmoplegia → **orbital cellulitis** → CT + IV antibiotics."
  - "Orbital cellulitis usually spreads from **ethmoid sinusitis**."
  - "Ophthalmic veins → cavernous sinus (valveless) → cavernous sinus thrombosis risk."
imaging:
  xray: Waters view may show the "teardrop sign" of a blowout fracture, but CT has replaced it.
  ct: '**CT of the orbits** (thin coronal slices) for fractures, muscle entrapment, orbital cellulitis and abscess.'
  mri: Optic nerve and orbital apex lesions, orbital tumors.
  ultrasound: Ocular ultrasound for retrobulbar hematoma and globe injury (not if globe rupture is suspected with pressure).
  keyViews:
    - "CT orbits with coronal reconstructions"
exam:
  landmarks: Orbital rim (supraorbital notch, infraorbital foramen about 1 cm below the rim), medial and lateral canthi.
  palpation: Palpate the rim for step-offs and crepitus; test sensation of the cheek and upper lip (infraorbital nerve).
  testing: Visual acuity, pupils (relative afferent pupillary defect), extraocular movements and diplopia, proptosis, intraocular pressure.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 22-year-old is hit in the eye by a baseball. He has double vision when looking up and numbness of the left cheek
      and upper lip. Which structures are most likely injured?
    choices:
      - Superior rectus and supraorbital nerve
      - Inferior rectus and infraorbital nerve
      - Lateral rectus and zygomatic nerve
      - Medial rectus and anterior ethmoidal nerve
      - Superior oblique and trochlear nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      An **orbital floor blowout fracture** can trap the **inferior rectus** (restricting upgaze) and injure the
      **infraorbital nerve**, which runs in the floor, numbing the cheek and upper lip.
  - stem: >-
      A 7-year-old with a week of sinusitis has a red, swollen left eyelid, fever, pain when moving the eye, and
      limited upward gaze. What is the most appropriate next step?
    choices:
      - Oral amoxicillin and follow-up in 1 week
      - Warm compresses only
      - CT of the orbits and IV antibiotics
      - Topical antibiotic drops
      - Incision of the eyelid in clinic
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Pain with eye movement and ophthalmoplegia** indicate **orbital (postseptal) cellulitis**, usually from ethmoid
      sinusitis. It needs **CT orbits** (to exclude abscess), **IV antibiotics** and ophthalmology/ENT involvement.
