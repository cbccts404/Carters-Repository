name: Submandibular gland
type: organ
region: head-neck
subregion: Oral & nasal cavities
taName: Glandula submandibularis
summary: Salivary gland under the body of the mandible that produces most resting saliva. It is the most common site of salivary stones because its duct runs uphill and its saliva is thick.
tags: [salivary, oral]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: In the submandibular triangle (see [[triangles-of-the-neck]]), wrapped around the posterior border of mylohyoid, with a large superficial part and a small deep part.
  parts:
    - "**Superficial part**: below mylohyoid, in the submandibular triangle"
    - "**Deep part**: above mylohyoid, in the floor of the mouth"
    - "**Submandibular (Wharton) duct**: runs forward in the floor of the mouth to open at the **sublingual papilla** beside the frenulum of the tongue; the **lingual nerve** loops under it"
  bloodSupply: Facial artery (from the [[external-carotid-artery]]), which grooves the gland.
  venousDrainage: Facial vein.
  lymphatics: Submandibular nodes (see [[cervical-lymph-nodes]]).
  innervation: '**Parasympathetic secretomotor: facial nerve (CN VII)** — superior salivatory nucleus → chorda tympani (joins the lingual nerve) → submandibular ganglion.'
  relations: The marginal mandibular branch of the facial nerve, the lingual nerve and the hypoglossal nerve are all close — at risk in submandibular gland excision.
clinical:
  - title: Sialolithiasis (salivary stones)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: About 80–90% of salivary stones occur in the submandibular gland — its **saliva is more mucous and alkaline** and its duct is **long and runs upward**. {{verify}}
    presentation: '**Painful swelling under the jaw at mealtimes** that subsides afterwards; a stone may be palpable in the floor of the mouth. Treat with hydration, sialagogues (lemon drops), massage and warm compresses; removal or sialendoscopy if persistent.'
  - title: Sialadenitis
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Bacterial infection behind an obstructing stone.
    presentation: Painful, red, tender swelling with pus from the duct; antibiotics and treat the stone.
  - title: Ludwig angina
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Rapidly spreading cellulitis of the submandibular and sublingual spaces, usually from a lower molar infection.
    presentation: Brawny swelling of the floor of the mouth, tongue elevation, drooling and **airway compromise** — secure the airway and give IV antibiotics. See [[deep-neck-spaces]].
pance:
  - "Swelling under the jaw at **mealtimes** → submandibular **stone** (most common site)."
  - "Wharton duct opens at the sublingual papilla; Stensen duct opposite the upper 2nd molar."
  - "Submandibular secretomotor = **CN VII** (chorda tympani)."
imaging:
  ultrasound: First-line for stones and gland swelling.
  ct: Non-contrast CT detects stones; contrast CT for abscess and Ludwig angina.
exam:
  landmarks: Below the body of the mandible, in front of the angle.
  palpation: '**Bimanual palpation** — one finger in the floor of the mouth, the other under the jaw — feels the gland and stones along the duct; inspect the duct openings for pus.'

      The submandibular gland produces **more mucous, alkaline, calcium-rich saliva**, and its long **Wharton duct runs

  - stem: A 40-year-old man has swelling and pain under the left side of his jaw every time he eats, which resolves an hour later. What is the most likely diagnosis?

    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Meal-related swelling and pain** that subsides is classic for a stone obstructing the duct (**sialolithiasis**),
      most often in the submandibular gland.
