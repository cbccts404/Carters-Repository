name: Parathyroid glands
type: organ
region: head-neck
subregion: Neck
taName: Glandulae parathyroideae
summary: Usually four small endocrine glands on the back of the thyroid that secrete parathyroid hormone. They are important in hypercalcemia (primary hyperparathyroidism) and in hypocalcemia after thyroid surgery.
tags: [endocrine, neck, calcium]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: On the posterior surface of the thyroid lobes, usually two on each side (superior and inferior); each is about 5 mm, yellow-brown.
  parts:
    - "**Superior parathyroids**: from the **4th pharyngeal pouch**; fairly constant position near the cricothyroid junction"
    - "**Inferior parathyroids**: from the **3rd pharyngeal pouch** (with the thymus); descend further and are **more variable** — may be in the thymus or anterior mediastinum (see [[thymus]])"
    - "**Chief cells** secrete **parathyroid hormone (PTH)** — raises serum calcium (bone resorption, renal calcium reabsorption and phosphate excretion, activation of vitamin D)"
    - "Number varies — supernumerary or fewer than four glands in a minority of people"
  bloodSupply: Mainly the **inferior thyroid artery** (both superior and inferior glands in most people).
  venousDrainage: Thyroid venous plexus.
  lymphatics: With the thyroid (see [[cervical-lymph-nodes]]).
  innervation: Sympathetic vasomotor fibers; secretion is controlled by serum ionized calcium via the calcium-sensing receptor.
  relations: Close to the [[recurrent-laryngeal-nerve]] and the inferior thyroid artery behind the [[thyroid-gland]].
clinical:
  - title: Primary hyperparathyroidism
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Single parathyroid adenoma** (about 80–85%), four-gland hyperplasia (MEN 1, MEN 2A), rarely carcinoma.'
    presentation: >-
      Often **asymptomatic hypercalcemia** on routine labs; otherwise "**stones, bones, groans, thrones and psychiatric
      overtones**" — kidney stones, osteoporosis, abdominal pain/peptic ulcer/pancreatitis, polyuria, depression.
      Labs: **high calcium with high or inappropriately normal PTH**, low phosphate. Treatment: **parathyroidectomy**
      when indicated.
  - title: Secondary and tertiary hyperparathyroidism
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Secondary — a physiological response to low calcium, most often **chronic kidney disease** or vitamin D deficiency. Tertiary — autonomous PTH secretion after long-standing secondary disease.
    presentation: 'Secondary: **high PTH, low or normal calcium**, high phosphate in CKD. Tertiary: high PTH with **high calcium**.'
  - title: Hypoparathyroidism
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Most often **after thyroid or parathyroid surgery** (gland removal or devascularization); also autoimmune, DiGeorge syndrome (absent 3rd/4th pouches), hypomagnesemia.
    presentation: '**Hypocalcemia**: perioral and fingertip tingling, cramps, **Chvostek sign** (facial twitch on tapping the facial nerve), **Trousseau sign** (carpal spasm with a BP cuff), prolonged QT, seizures, laryngospasm. Treat symptomatic cases with **IV calcium gluconate**.'
pance:
  - "High calcium + high/normal PTH → **primary hyperparathyroidism** (usually a single adenoma)."
  - "\"Stones, bones, groans, thrones, psychiatric overtones.\""
  - "Tingling and carpal spasm after thyroidectomy → **hypocalcemia** → Chvostek/Trousseau → IV calcium gluconate."
  - "CKD → **secondary hyperparathyroidism** (high PTH, high phosphate)."
  - "Inferior parathyroids = 3rd pouch (with thymus); superior = 4th pouch."
imaging:
  ultrasound: Neck ultrasound to localize an adenoma (after the biochemical diagnosis).
  ct: '**4D-CT** of the neck for localization before minimally invasive parathyroidectomy.'
  keyViews:
    - "**Sestamibi (Tc-99m) scan** — adenoma retains tracer"
    - "Imaging localizes; it does not diagnose hyperparathyroidism"
exam:
  landmarks: Not palpable normally — a palpable neck mass with hypercalcemia raises concern for parathyroid carcinoma.
  testing: '**Chvostek sign**: tap the facial nerve in front of the ear → twitch of the upper lip. **Trousseau sign**: inflate a BP cuff above systolic for 3 minutes → carpal spasm (more specific).'
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 58-year-old woman has a calcium of 11.2 mg/dL on routine labs, a low phosphate and an elevated PTH. She has had
      a kidney stone. What is the most likely cause?
    choices:
      - Humoral hypercalcemia of malignancy
      - Parathyroid adenoma
      - Vitamin D toxicity
      - Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia (confirmed)
      - Secondary hyperparathyroidism from CKD
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **High calcium with elevated PTH** means PTH-dependent hypercalcemia; most cases are **primary
      hyperparathyroidism from a single adenoma**. Malignancy (PTHrP) suppresses PTH. Check urine calcium to exclude
      familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia.
  - stem: >-
      Two days after total thyroidectomy, a patient reports tingling around the mouth. Tapping in front of the ear causes
      the upper lip to twitch. What is the best treatment?
    choices:
      - Levothyroxine
      - IV calcium gluconate
      - Furosemide
      - Calcitonin
      - Bisphosphonate
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Perioral tingling and a positive **Chvostek sign** indicate **hypocalcemia** from parathyroid injury or removal.
      Symptomatic hypocalcemia is treated with **IV calcium gluconate**, then oral calcium and calcitriol.
