name: Nasal cavity
type: space
region: head-neck
subregion: Oral & nasal cavities
taName: Cavitas nasi
summary: Paired air passages from the nostrils to the choanae, divided by the septum and lined by the conchae that warm and humidify air. Anterior epistaxis from Kiesselbach plexus is the classic problem.
tags: [nose, ent, epistaxis]
systems: [eent]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Above the hard palate, between the orbits, opening in front through the nares and behind through the choanae into the nasopharynx (see [[pharynx]]).
  boundaries:
    roof: Nasal bones, **cribriform plate of the ethmoid** (olfactory nerve fibers), body of the sphenoid
    floor: Hard palate (maxilla and palatine bones)
    medial wall: Nasal septum — septal cartilage, perpendicular plate of the ethmoid, vomer
    lateral wall: Superior, middle and inferior **conchae (turbinates)** with the meatuses beneath them
  contents:
    - "**Olfactory epithelium** in the roof (CN I)"
    - "Respiratory mucosa with a rich venous plexus (warms and humidifies air)"
    - "**Kiesselbach plexus (Little area)** on the anterior septum — anastomosis of the anterior ethmoidal, sphenopalatine, greater palatine and superior labial (facial) arteries"
    - "Openings of the [[paranasal-sinuses]] and the nasolacrimal duct"
  communications: >-
    **Sphenoethmoidal recess** → sphenoid sinus. **Superior meatus** → posterior ethmoid cells. **Middle meatus** →
    frontal, maxillary and anterior ethmoid sinuses (the **ostiomeatal complex**). **Inferior meatus** → nasolacrimal
    duct (tears). Choanae → nasopharynx.
clinical:
  - title: Epistaxis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Anterior (about 90%)** — Kiesselbach plexus (digital trauma, dry air, rhinitis, anticoagulants). **Posterior** — sphenopalatine artery branches; older patients with hypertension, atherosclerosis or anticoagulation, heavier bleeding.'
    presentation: >-
      Anterior: lean **forward** and pinch the soft part of the nose for 10–15 minutes; topical vasoconstrictor
      (oxymetazoline), then cautery (silver nitrate, one side at a time) or anterior packing. Posterior: bleeding into the
      pharynx despite anterior packing → posterior packing/balloon, admission, ENT (sphenopalatine ligation or
      embolization).
  - title: Septal hematoma
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Nasal trauma causing blood to collect between the septal cartilage and its perichondrium.
    presentation: A bulging, fluctuant, bluish swelling of the septum after trauma; must be **drained promptly** to prevent cartilage necrosis (saddle nose) or abscess.
  - title: Rhinitis and nasal polyps
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Allergic rhinitis (IgE-mediated), nonallergic/vasomotor rhinitis, rhinitis medicamentosa (rebound from overuse of topical decongestants); nasal polyps with chronic rhinosinusitis, asthma and aspirin sensitivity (Samter triad).
    presentation: Allergic — pale boggy turbinates, clear discharge, allergic shiners; first-line **intranasal corticosteroid**. Polyps in a child → test for cystic fibrosis.
  - title: Nasal foreign body
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Common in young children.
    presentation: '**Unilateral foul-smelling purulent discharge** in a child.'
pance:
  - "Most nosebleeds are anterior from **Kiesselbach plexus** → lean forward, pinch 10–15 min."
  - "Bleeding into the throat despite anterior packing → **posterior** (sphenopalatine) epistaxis."
  - "Nasal trauma + fluctuant septal swelling → **septal hematoma** → drain now."
  - "Unilateral foul discharge in a child → **foreign body**."
  - "Nasal polyps in a child → cystic fibrosis; asthma + aspirin sensitivity + polyps → Samter triad."
imaging:
  ct: CT of the sinuses/face for fractures, chronic rhinosinusitis, tumors and before sinus surgery (not needed for simple epistaxis).
  mri: Tumors of the nasal cavity and skull base.
  xray: Nasal bone films are rarely useful; nasal fractures are a clinical diagnosis.
exam:
  landmarks: Nasal bones (upper third), septal cartilage, alar cartilages.
  palpation: Palpate the nasal bones for deformity or crepitus after trauma.
  testing: '**Anterior rhinoscopy** with a nasal speculum (or otoscope with a large speculum): septum (deviation, hematoma, bleeding point), turbinates (color, swelling), polyps, discharge. Check patency of each nostril.'
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 10-year-old has a nosebleed from the left nostril that started while picking his nose. What is the most
      appropriate first step?
    choices:
      - Tilt the head back and apply ice to the neck
      - Lean forward and apply firm pressure to the soft part of the nose for 10–15 minutes
      - Posterior nasal packing
      - Emergency CT angiography
      - Start oral antibiotics
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Most pediatric nosebleeds are **anterior** from **Kiesselbach plexus**. **Leaning forward** (to avoid swallowing
      blood) and **continuous firm pinching** for 10–15 minutes stops most; cautery or packing is next if needed.
  - stem: Which structure drains into the inferior meatus?
    choices:
      - Maxillary sinus
      - Frontal sinus
      - Nasolacrimal duct
      - Sphenoid sinus
      - Posterior ethmoid cells
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **nasolacrimal duct** opens into the **inferior meatus**, which is why crying makes the nose run. The frontal,
      maxillary and anterior ethmoid sinuses drain into the middle meatus.
  - stem: Which artery is the main source of posterior epistaxis?
    choices:
      - Anterior ethmoidal artery
      - Superior labial artery
      - Greater palatine artery
      - Facial artery
      - Sphenopalatine artery
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      **Posterior** nosebleeds arise from **sphenopalatine artery** branches; they are heavier and more common in older
      patients with hypertension or anticoagulation. About 90% of nosebleeds are anterior, from **Kiesselbach plexus**.
  - stem: >-
      After a punch to the nose, a patient has a fluctuant, bluish swelling of the nasal septum. What is the most
      appropriate management?
    choices:
      - Observation; it will resorb
      - Prompt incision and drainage
      - Nasal decongestant spray only
      - Oral antibiotics alone
      - Delayed rhinoplasty in 6 weeks
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **septal hematoma** must be **drained promptly** to prevent necrosis of the septal cartilage (saddle nose
      deformity) or abscess.
  - stem: A 3-year-old has a unilateral, foul-smelling purulent nasal discharge. What is the most likely cause?
    choices:
      - Allergic rhinitis
      - Viral URI
      - Nasal polyps
      - Nasal foreign body
      - Choanal atresia
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      **Unilateral foul-smelling discharge** in a young child suggests a **nasal foreign body**. Nasal polyps in a child
      should prompt testing for cystic fibrosis.
