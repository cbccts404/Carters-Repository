name: Tongue
type: organ
region: head-neck
subregion: Oral & nasal cavities
taName: Lingua
summary: Muscular organ of taste, speech and swallowing. Its mixed embryologic origin gives it four different nerve supplies, and hypoglossal lesions make it deviate toward the weak side.
tags: [oral, cranial-nerves]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Floor of the mouth (oral part, anterior two-thirds) and front wall of the oropharynx (pharyngeal part, posterior third), divided by the V-shaped **sulcus terminalis** with the **foramen cecum** at its apex.
  parts:
    - "**Anterior two-thirds** (oral part): papillae — filiform (no taste buds), fungiform, **circumvallate** (in a V in front of the sulcus terminalis), foliate"
    - "**Posterior third** (pharyngeal part): lingual tonsil"
    - "**Intrinsic muscles** (change shape): superior and inferior longitudinal, transverse, vertical"
    - "**Extrinsic muscles** (change position): **genioglossus** (protrudes), hyoglossus (depresses), styloglossus (retracts and elevates), palatoglossus (elevates the back)"
    - "**Foramen cecum**: origin of the thyroid diverticulum (thyroglossal duct)"
  bloodSupply: Lingual artery (from the [[external-carotid-artery]]).
  venousDrainage: Lingual veins → internal jugular vein.
  lymphatics: Tip → submental nodes; lateral anterior two-thirds → submandibular nodes; posterior third → deep cervical (jugulodigastric) nodes, often **bilaterally** (see [[cervical-lymph-nodes]]).
  innervation: >-
    **Motor**: all muscles by the **hypoglossal nerve (CN XII)** except **palatoglossus (vagus, CN X)**. **Anterior
    two-thirds**: general sensation — lingual nerve (**V3**); taste — chorda tympani (**CN VII**). **Posterior
    third**: general sensation and taste — **CN IX**. Epiglottic region/root: **CN X** (internal laryngeal).
  relations: The lingual nerve loops under the submandibular duct in the floor of the mouth; the hypoglossal nerve lies on hyoglossus.
clinical:
  - title: Hypoglossal nerve (CN XII) lesion
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Lower motor neuron lesion (skull base tumors, carotid dissection or surgery, medullary stroke).
    presentation: On protrusion the tongue **deviates toward the weak (lesioned) side** (the intact genioglossus pushes it over), with ipsilateral atrophy and fasciculations. An upper motor neuron lesion causes deviation away from the cortical lesion without atrophy.
  - title: Oral cavity cancer
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Squamous cell carcinoma; risk factors — **tobacco, alcohol**, betel quid; HPV for oropharyngeal (base of tongue, tonsil) cancers.
    presentation: A non-healing ulcer or indurated mass on the **lateral border** of the tongue, leukoplakia/erythroplakia, referred otalgia, neck nodes. Biopsy any lesion persisting over 2–3 weeks.
  - title: Ankyloglossia and glossitis
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Short lingual frenulum (tongue-tie); glossitis from B12, folate or iron deficiency.
    presentation: Tongue-tie — feeding difficulty in infants (frenotomy); atrophic glossitis — smooth red sore tongue.
pance:
  - "Tongue deviates **toward** the side of a **CN XII LMN** lesion."
  - "Taste: anterior 2/3 = **CN VII** (chorda tympani); posterior 1/3 = **CN IX**; sensation anterior 2/3 = **V3**."
  - "All tongue muscles = CN XII **except palatoglossus (CN X)**."
  - "Non-healing lateral tongue ulcer in a smoker/drinker → **SCC** → biopsy."
  - "Smooth red tongue → B12/folate/iron deficiency."
imaging:
  ct: Contrast CT of the neck for staging oral cancers and nodes.
  mri: Best for tongue tumor depth and extent.
  keyViews:
    - "MRI oral cavity/neck"
exam:
  landmarks: Sulcus terminalis and circumvallate papillae, frenulum, openings of the submandibular ducts beside the frenulum.
  palpation: '**Bimanual palpation** of the tongue and floor of the mouth (gloved), especially the lateral borders and base; palpate the neck nodes.'
  testing: '**CN XII**: inspect the tongue at rest (atrophy, fasciculations), then protrusion (deviation) and side-to-side movement against the cheek. Taste testing is rarely done.'
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      After a right carotid endarterectomy, a patient's tongue deviates to the right when protruded, and the right side
      of the tongue later shows atrophy. Which nerve was injured?
    choices:
      - Left hypoglossal nerve
      - Right hypoglossal nerve
      - Right lingual nerve
      - Right glossopharyngeal nerve
      - Right facial nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A lower motor neuron lesion of the **right hypoglossal nerve** weakens the right genioglossus, so the intact left
      genioglossus pushes the tongue **toward the right (weak) side**, with right-sided atrophy. The hypoglossal nerve
      crosses the carotid arteries in the neck.
  - stem: Taste from the anterior two-thirds of the tongue is carried by which nerve?
    choices:
      - Lingual nerve (V3)
      - Chorda tympani (CN VII)
      - Glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX)
      - Vagus nerve (CN X)
      - Hypoglossal nerve (CN XII)
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Taste** from the anterior two-thirds travels in the **chorda tympani (CN VII)**, which joins the lingual nerve.
      The lingual nerve (V3) carries general sensation, and CN IX carries both from the posterior third.

  - front: Direction of tongue deviation in a CN XII LMN lesion?
    back: Toward the lesion (weak side)
