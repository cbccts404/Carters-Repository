---
name: Internal jugular vein
type: vein
region: head-neck
subregion: Neck
taName: Vena jugularis interna
aka: [IJ, IJV]
summary: Main venous drainage of the brain, face and neck. It runs in the carotid sheath and is the source of the jugular venous pressure (JVP), a common central line site, and the vein thrombosed in Lemierre syndrome.
tags: [neck, vascular, procedures]
systems: [cardiovascular]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  formation: Continuation of the **sigmoid sinus** at the **jugular foramen** (with CN IX, X and XI), dilated at its origin as the superior bulb.
  course: >-
    Descends in the **carotid sheath**, lateral to the internal and then common carotid ([[carotid-arteries]]), with the
    vagus posterior between them, under cover of [[sternocleidomastoid]]. It lies deep to the gap between the sternal
    and clavicular heads of SCM low in the neck and has an **inferior bulb** with a valve just above its termination.
  tributaries:
    - "Inferior petrosal sinus"
    - "Facial vein (via the common facial vein)"
    - "Lingual and pharyngeal veins"
    - "Superior and middle thyroid veins (see [[thyroid-gland]])"
  drainage: Joins the **subclavian vein behind the sternoclavicular joint** to form the **brachiocephalic vein** → [[superior-vena-cava]]. The **thoracic duct** enters at the left venous angle (see [[thoracic-duct]]).
  relations: Deep cervical lymph nodes lie along the vein (see [[cervical-lymph-nodes]]); the right IJ runs in a nearly straight line to the right atrium.
  notes: The right IJ is usually larger than the left and is preferred for central lines and for assessing the JVP.
clinical:
  - title: Jugular venous pressure (JVP)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: The right IJ runs in a straight line through the brachiocephalic vein and SVC to the right atrium, so its pulsation reflects **right atrial pressure**.
    presentation: >-
      Measure the top of the IJ pulsation with the patient at **30–45°**; normal is **≤ about 3–4 cm above the sternal
      angle** (about 8 cm H₂O). **Elevated** in **right heart failure**, fluid overload, tricuspid regurgitation, cardiac
      tamponade, constrictive pericarditis and SVC obstruction (non-pulsatile). **Kussmaul sign** — JVP rises with
      inspiration (constrictive pericarditis). **Hepatojugular reflux**. {{verify: normal JVP cutoff varies by source (3 vs 4 cm)}}
  - title: Central venous catheterization
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Ultrasound-guided** cannulation, usually of the right IJ, at the apex of the triangle formed by the two heads of SCM and the clavicle, lateral to the carotid pulse.'
    presentation: Complications — **carotid puncture**, **pneumothorax** (less than the subclavian approach), air embolism (Trendelenburg position helps prevent it), infection, thrombosis. Confirm tip position with a chest X-ray (cavoatrial junction).
  - title: Lemierre syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Septic thrombophlebitis of the IJ** after pharyngitis or peritonsillar abscess, classically *Fusobacterium necrophorum*; adolescents and young adults.'
    presentation: A recent sore throat, then high fever, **lateral neck pain and swelling** along SCM, and **septic pulmonary emboli** (cavitating nodules). Treat with IV antibiotics (e.g. ampicillin-sulbactam or a carbapenem).
pance:
  - "JVP is read on the **right IJ** at 30–45°; above ~3–4 cm over the sternal angle → elevated (right HF, tamponade, fluid overload)."
  - "**Kussmaul sign** → constrictive pericarditis (also restrictive cardiomyopathy, right ventricular infarction; classically absent in tamponade)."
  - "Pharyngitis → neck swelling → septic lung emboli → **Lemierre syndrome** (*Fusobacterium*)."
  - "Central line → **ultrasound guidance**, then **CXR** to confirm tip and exclude pneumothorax."
imaging:
  ultrasound: Compressible, larger and lateral to the carotid; it distends with Valsalva. Non-compressible with echogenic contents → **thrombosis**.
  ct: Contrast CT of the neck and chest shows IJ thrombosis (filling defect) and septic emboli in Lemierre syndrome.
  keyViews:
    - "Point-of-care ultrasound for line placement"
exam:
  landmarks: 'Between the sternal and clavicular heads of SCM low in the neck; the **sternal angle** is the reference point for the JVP.'
  palpation: The IJ pulsation is **not palpable** (unlike the carotid) and is **obliterated by light pressure** above the clavicle.
  testing: 'JVP: patient at 30–45°, head turned slightly left, tangential light; find the highest point of IJ pulsation and measure vertically from the sternal angle. **Hepatojugular reflux**: pressure over the liver for 10 seconds → a sustained rise of over 3 cm suggests right heart failure. The waveform is biphasic (a and v waves) and changes with position and respiration.'
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      Which feature best distinguishes the internal jugular venous pulsation from the carotid pulse on physical exam?
    choices:
      - It is easily palpable
      - It is obliterated by light pressure above the clavicle
      - It is unaffected by position
      - It has a single outward component
      - It does not change with respiration
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **jugular venous pulsation** is not palpable, is **obliterated by gentle pressure**, has a **biphasic**
      waveform, falls with inspiration and changes with position. The carotid pulse is palpable and unaffected by these.
  - stem: >-
      A 19-year-old had a sore throat 1 week ago. He now has high fever, pain and swelling along the left
      sternocleidomastoid, and chest CT shows multiple cavitating nodules. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Ludwig angina
      - Lemierre syndrome
      - Tuberculosis
      - Infectious mononucleosis
      - Granulomatosis with polyangiitis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Lemierre syndrome** — septic thrombophlebitis of the **internal jugular vein** after pharyngitis (classically
      *Fusobacterium necrophorum*) with **septic pulmonary emboli**.
