---
name: Paranasal sinuses
type: space
region: head-neck
subregion: Oral & nasal cavities
taName: Sinus paranasales
aka: [Maxillary sinus, Frontal sinus, Ethmoid sinuses, Sphenoid sinus]
summary: Four paired air-filled cavities — frontal, ethmoid, sphenoid and maxillary — draining into the nasal cavity. Acute sinusitis is usually viral; ethmoid and frontal infections can spread to the orbit and brain.
tags: [nose, sinus, ent]
systems: [eent, infectious]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Within the frontal, ethmoid, sphenoid and maxillary bones around the [[nasal-cavity]].
  boundaries:
    frontal sinus: In the frontal bone above the orbits; the anterior cranial fossa lies behind its posterior wall
    ethmoid cells: Between the orbits (lamina papyracea laterally — paper-thin) and the nasal cavity
    sphenoid sinus: In the sphenoid body below the pituitary (sella turcica); cavernous sinuses and internal carotid arteries lie laterally
    maxillary sinus: The largest; in the maxilla below the orbit (orbital floor above), roots of the upper molars below
  contents:
    - "Air and a thin mucoperiosteal lining with cilia that sweep mucus toward the ostia"
  communications: >-
    **Middle meatus (ostiomeatal complex)**: frontal, maxillary and anterior ethmoid. **Superior meatus**: posterior
    ethmoid. **Sphenoethmoidal recess**: sphenoid. The **maxillary ostium is high on its medial wall**, so drainage
    depends on cilia rather than gravity.
  notes: "At birth only the maxillary and ethmoid sinuses are present; the sphenoid develops in childhood and the **frontal sinus appears around age 5–7** and develops through adolescence. {{verify: ages of sinus development}}"
clinical:
  - title: Acute rhinosinusitis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Usually **viral** (URI); bacterial (*S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae*, *M. catarrhalis*) in a minority.
    presentation: >-
      Nasal congestion, purulent discharge, facial pain/pressure (worse bending forward), maxillary toothache.
      Suspect **bacterial** if symptoms last **≥10 days without improvement**, are **severe** (fever ≥39 °C with purulent
      discharge for 3–4 days), or **"double-worsen"** after initial improvement → **amoxicillin-clavulanate**. Otherwise
      symptomatic care (saline irrigation, intranasal steroids).
  - title: Complications of sinusitis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Spread through thin bone and valveless veins.
    presentation: '**Orbital cellulitis** (ethmoid — see [[orbit]]), **Pott puffy tumor** (frontal bone osteomyelitis with forehead swelling), meningitis, epidural/brain abscess, **cavernous sinus thrombosis** (sphenoid/ethmoid — ophthalmoplegia, proptosis, fever).'
  - title: Chronic rhinosinusitis
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Inflammation lasting over 12 weeks, with or without nasal polyps.
    presentation: Congestion, discharge, facial pressure, reduced smell; confirmed with nasal endoscopy or CT; intranasal steroids and saline irrigation, surgery if refractory.
pance:
  - "Bacterial sinusitis if **≥10 days**, **severe**, or **double-worsening** → amoxicillin-clavulanate."
  - "Most acute sinusitis is **viral** — no antibiotics, no imaging."
  - "Ethmoid sinusitis → **orbital cellulitis** (children)."
  - "Frontal sinusitis + forehead swelling → **Pott puffy tumor**."
  - "Maxillary sinusitis can cause upper toothache (and vice versa)."
imaging:
  xray: Sinus X-rays are not recommended.
  ct: '**CT of the sinuses without contrast** for chronic or recurrent sinusitis or before surgery; **contrast CT (± MRI)** when complications are suspected. Not for uncomplicated acute sinusitis.'
  mri: Intracranial and orbital complications, fungal sinusitis, tumors.
  keyViews:
    - "Non-contrast CT sinuses (coronal)"
exam:
  landmarks: Frontal sinus above the medial eyebrow; maxillary sinus over the cheek below the orbit.
  palpation: Percussion or palpation tenderness over the sinuses (limited accuracy).
  testing: Anterior rhinoscopy for purulent discharge in the middle meatus; check eye movements and vision if orbital spread is possible; transillumination is rarely used.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 34-year-old has had nasal congestion, purulent discharge and maxillary pain for 5 days. She is afebrile and
      improving slightly. What is the most appropriate management?
    choices:
      - Amoxicillin-clavulanate
      - CT of the sinuses
      - Symptomatic treatment (saline irrigation, intranasal steroid, analgesics)
      - Referral for sinus surgery
      - Azithromycin
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      At 5 days and improving, this is most likely **viral rhinosinusitis**. Antibiotics are for symptoms lasting **10
      days or more**, severe illness, or double-worsening. Imaging is not needed for uncomplicated sinusitis.
  - stem: Which sinus is most often the source of orbital cellulitis in children?
    choices:
      - Frontal
      - Maxillary
      - Ethmoid
      - Sphenoid
      - Mastoid air cells
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **ethmoid cells** are separated from the orbit only by the paper-thin **lamina papyracea**, so ethmoid
      sinusitis is the usual source of orbital cellulitis.
