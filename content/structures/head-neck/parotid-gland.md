name: Parotid gland
type: organ
region: head-neck
subregion: Face & scalp
taName: Glandula parotidea
summary: The largest salivary gland, in front of the ear, with the facial nerve branching through it. Mumps, bacterial parotitis, stones and tumors (most often pleomorphic adenoma) are its key problems.
tags: [salivary, face, oncology]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Wrapped around the ramus of the [[mandible]], in front of the ear and below the zygomatic arch, extending behind the mandible toward the mastoid; enclosed in a tough parotid capsule (investing fascia).
  parts:
    - "**Superficial and deep lobes** (surgical division by the plane of the facial nerve)"
    - "**Parotid (Stensen) duct**: crosses the masseter, pierces buccinator and opens opposite the **upper second molar**"
    - "Structures within the gland, superficial to deep: **facial nerve** (and its five branches), **retromandibular vein**, **external carotid artery** (dividing into maxillary and superficial temporal arteries)"
  bloodSupply: Branches of the [[external-carotid-artery]] (transverse facial, superficial temporal).
  venousDrainage: Retromandibular vein.
  lymphatics: Parotid (preauricular) nodes → deep cervical nodes (see [[cervical-lymph-nodes]]).
  innervation: >-
    **Secretomotor: parasympathetic from the [[glossopharyngeal-nerve|glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX)]]** — inferior salivatory nucleus → lesser
    petrosal nerve → otic ganglion → auriculotemporal nerve (V3). Sensory: auriculotemporal and great auricular
    nerves. The facial nerve passes through the gland but does **not** supply it.
  relations: The [[temporomandibular-joint]] and external acoustic meatus lie behind; masseter in front; the styloid process and internal jugular vein deep.
clinical:
  - title: Parotid tumors
    highYield: true
    mechanism: About 80% of salivary gland tumors arise in the parotid, and most parotid tumors are benign — **pleomorphic adenoma** (most common) and Warthin tumor (smokers, often bilateral). Mucoepidermoid carcinoma is the most common malignant salivary tumor.
    presentation: >-
      A slow-growing, painless, mobile mass in front of or below the ear. **Facial nerve weakness, pain, rapid growth or
      fixation suggest malignancy.** Evaluate with ultrasound/MRI and fine-needle aspiration; treatment is
      superficial parotidectomy with facial nerve preservation.
  - title: Mumps and bacterial parotitis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Mumps** (paramyxovirus) — usually bilateral in unvaccinated patients. **Acute suppurative parotitis** (*S. aureus*) — dehydrated, elderly or postoperative patients with reduced salivary flow.'
    presentation: Painful swelling obscuring the angle of the jaw, raised ear lobe. Mumps — fever, bilateral swelling, possible orchitis. Bacterial — unilateral, red, tender, with **pus from Stensen duct** on massage; treat with hydration, sialagogues and anti-staphylococcal antibiotics.
  - title: Frey syndrome (gustatory sweating)
    highYield: false
    mechanism: After parotidectomy, regenerating parasympathetic fibers (auriculotemporal nerve) innervate sweat glands of the overlying skin.
    presentation: Sweating and flushing over the parotid area when eating.
  - title: Sjögren syndrome
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Autoimmune lymphocytic infiltration of salivary and lacrimal glands.
    presentation: Dry mouth and dry eyes, bilateral parotid enlargement; increased risk of lymphoma.
pance:
  - "Most common salivary tumor = **pleomorphic adenoma** (parotid)."
  - "Parotid mass + **facial nerve palsy** → malignancy until proven otherwise."
  - "Stensen duct opens opposite the **upper 2nd molar**."
  - "Parotid secretomotor supply = **CN IX** (otic ganglion); the facial nerve only passes through."
  - "Postoperative elderly dehydrated patient with a painful parotid → bacterial parotitis (*S. aureus*)."
imaging:
  ultrasound: First-line for parotid swelling and superficial masses; guides fine-needle aspiration.
  ct: Abscess, stones (sialolithiasis) and staging.
  mri: Best characterization of tumors, deep-lobe extension and perineural spread along the facial nerve.
  keyViews:
    - "Ultrasound with FNA"
    - "MRI of the parotid"
exam:
  landmarks: In front of the tragus, over the ramus and angle of the mandible; the duct can be rolled over the anterior border of the clenched masseter, about a finger's breadth below the zygomatic arch.
  palpation: Bimanual palpation (one finger inside the cheek); inspect and massage toward the duct opening opposite the upper 2nd molar for pus or saliva.
  testing: Always test facial nerve function (raise eyebrows, close eyes, smile) in any parotid mass.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 45-year-old woman has a painless, mobile mass in front of her right ear that has grown slowly over 3 years.
      Facial movement is normal. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Mucoepidermoid carcinoma
      - Pleomorphic adenoma
      - Acute suppurative parotitis
      - Mumps
      - Lymphoma
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A slow-growing, painless, mobile parotid mass with normal facial nerve function is most likely a **pleomorphic
      adenoma**, the most common salivary gland tumor. Facial nerve weakness would suggest malignancy.
  - stem: Which cranial nerve provides secretomotor (parasympathetic) fibers to the parotid gland?
    choices:
      - Facial nerve (CN VII)
      - Glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX)
      - Vagus nerve (CN X)
      - Trigeminal nerve (CN V)
      - Hypoglossal nerve (CN XII)
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Parasympathetic fibers from **CN IX** travel in the lesser petrosal nerve to the otic ganglion, then hitch a ride on
      the auriculotemporal nerve (V3) to the parotid. The facial nerve passes through the gland without supplying it.
