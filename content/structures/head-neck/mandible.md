name: Mandible
type: bone
region: head-neck
subregion: Skull
taName: Mandibula
aka: [Lower jaw]
summary: The lower jaw — the largest facial bone and the only movable bone of the skull. As a ring with the skull base, it often fractures in two places, and the inferior alveolar nerve runs through it.
tags: [face, trauma, dental]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Lower face, articulating with the temporal bones at the [[temporomandibular-joint|temporomandibular joints]].
  classification: Irregular bone (body and two rami)
  features:
    - "**Body**: alveolar process holding the lower teeth; **mental foramen** (below the 2nd premolar) transmitting the mental nerve and vessels; symphysis in the midline"
    - "**Ramus**: vertical plate with the **angle** at its lower back corner; the **mandibular foramen** on its inner surface (inferior alveolar nerve and vessels), guarded by the lingula"
    - "**Condylar process**: head (condyle) and neck; articulates with the mandibular fossa of the temporal bone"
    - "**Coronoid process**: insertion of temporalis"
    - "Attachments: masseter and medial pterygoid at the angle; lateral pterygoid at the neck of the condyle; digastric, geniohyoid and genioglossus near the symphysis"
  ossification: Mostly intramembranous (around Meckel cartilage of the first pharyngeal arch); the two halves fuse at the symphysis in the first year.
  relations: The inferior alveolar nerve (from V3) runs in the mandibular canal to the teeth and exits as the mental nerve to the lower lip and chin.
clinical:
  - title: Mandibular fractures
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Blunt trauma (assault, falls, motor vehicle collisions). Because the mandible and skull base form a ring, **fractures are often bilateral** (e.g. body or angle on one side and condyle on the other). Most common sites include the **condyle, angle and body**.
    presentation: >-
      Malocclusion, pain and trismus, step deformity, sublingual hematoma (highly suggestive), and **numbness of the lower
      lip and chin** (inferior alveolar/mental nerve injury). Fractures through tooth sockets are open fractures →
      antibiotics.
  - title: Inferior alveolar nerve block
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Local anesthetic near the mandibular foramen on the inner ramus.
    presentation: Anesthesia of the lower teeth, lower lip and chin on that side (the lingual nerve is often blocked too — numb tongue).
  - title: Medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Associated with antiresorptive drugs (bisphosphonates, denosumab), especially IV/high-dose, and dental extractions.
    presentation: Exposed necrotic bone in the mouth for over 8 weeks; mandible more than maxilla.
pance:
  - "Mandible fractures are often **bilateral** (ring) — look for a second fracture."
  - "Sublingual hematoma after facial trauma → mandibular fracture."
  - "Lower lip/chin numbness = inferior alveolar/mental nerve (V3)."
  - "Bisphosphonates + dental extraction → osteonecrosis of the jaw."
imaging:
  xray: A **panoramic radiograph (Panorex)** shows the whole mandible; supplemented by PA mandible views.
  ct: '**CT of the face** (maxillofacial CT) is the standard for mandibular and midface fractures and surgical planning.'
  keyViews:
    - "Panorex"
    - "Maxillofacial CT with 3-D reconstruction"
exam:
  landmarks: Angle of the mandible, mental protuberance, condyle palpable in front of the tragus.
  palpation: Palpate along the inferior border for steps; check occlusion ("does your bite feel normal?"), mouth opening and sensation of the lower lip and chin.
  specialTests:
    - name: Tongue blade test
      technique: The patient bites down on a tongue blade while the examiner twists it.
      positive: The patient can't hold the blade and it can't be broken (inability to grip).
      significance: Suggests mandibular fracture; being able to break the blade makes a fracture less likely.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      After a punch to the left jaw, a patient has malocclusion, a step in the left mandibular body and pain in front of
      the right ear. What is the most likely explanation for the right-sided pain?
    choices:
      - Referred pain from the left fracture
      - A second fracture of the right mandibular condyle
      - Right TMJ dislocation only
      - Right otitis media
      - Right parotitis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The mandible and skull base form a ring, so fractures are often **bilateral** — a body fracture on the struck side
      with a **contralateral condylar fracture** is a classic combination.
  - stem: Which nerve passes through the mental foramen?
    choices:
      - Lingual nerve
      - Mental nerve (branch of the inferior alveolar nerve)
      - Buccal nerve
      - Facial nerve
      - Hypoglossal nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **inferior alveolar nerve** (V3) runs through the mandibular canal and exits at the **mental foramen** as the
      **mental nerve**, supplying the skin of the lower lip and chin.
