name: Muscles of mastication
type: muscle
region: head-neck
subregion: Face & scalp
taName: Musculi masticatorii
aka: [Masseter, Temporalis, Medial pterygoid, Lateral pterygoid]
summary: Four muscles derived from the first pharyngeal arch and supplied by the mandibular nerve (V3) — masseter, temporalis and medial pterygoid close the jaw; lateral pterygoid opens and protrudes it.
tags: [face, trigeminal]
systems: [musculoskeletal, eent]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Around the ramus of the [[mandible]] and the temporal fossa.
  group: First pharyngeal arch derivatives
  origin: >-
    **Masseter**: zygomatic arch. **Temporalis**: temporal fossa and fascia. **Medial pterygoid**: medial surface of
    the lateral pterygoid plate (deep head) and maxillary tuberosity. **Lateral pterygoid**: greater wing of the sphenoid
    (upper head) and lateral surface of the lateral pterygoid plate (lower head).
  insertion: >-
    **Masseter**: lateral surface of the ramus and angle. **Temporalis**: coronoid process and anterior ramus.
    **Medial pterygoid**: medial surface of the ramus and angle. **Lateral pterygoid**: neck of the condyle, the
    articular disc and capsule of the [[temporomandibular-joint]].
  innervation: "**Mandibular division of the [[trigeminal-nerve|trigeminal nerve]] (V3)** — motor root"
  bloodSupply: Branches of the maxillary artery (from the [[external-carotid-artery]]).
  action:
    - "**Elevation (closing)**: masseter, temporalis, medial pterygoid"
    - "**Depression (opening)**: **lateral pterygoid** (with gravity and the suprahyoid muscles)"
    - "**Protrusion**: lateral and medial pterygoids"
    - "**Retraction**: posterior fibers of temporalis"
    - "Side-to-side grinding: alternating pterygoids"
  relations: >-
    Mnemonic: **"M's close the Mouth"** (Masseter, teMporalis, Medial pterygoid); the **Lateral** pterygoid **Lowers**
    (opens). The parotid gland and duct lie over the masseter.
clinical:
  - title: Trismus
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Spasm or inflammation of the masticatory muscles — dental infection (pericoronitis), peritonsillar or deep neck abscess, **tetanus** (lockjaw), radiation.
    presentation: Restricted mouth opening; trismus with fever and neck swelling suggests a deep space infection.
  - title: Trigeminal (V3) motor lesion
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Lesion of the motor root of V3 (skull base tumors, trauma).
    presentation: Weak clenching and wasting of temporalis and masseter on one side; the **jaw deviates toward the weak side on opening** (the intact opposite lateral pterygoid pushes it over).
  - title: Masseteric hypertrophy and bruxism
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Chronic clenching or grinding.
    presentation: Enlarged angle of the jaw, tooth wear, TMJ pain.
pance:
  - "All muscles of mastication = **V3**."
  - "Lateral pterygoid **opens** the jaw; the others close it."
  - "Jaw deviates **toward** the side of a V3 motor lesion on opening."
  - "Lockjaw (trismus) → consider tetanus or deep space infection."
imaging:
  ct: Deep neck/masticator space infections and tumors.
  mri: Masticator space masses; denervation atrophy after V3 injury.
exam:
  landmarks: Masseter is felt at the angle of the jaw and temporalis above the zygomatic arch when the patient clenches.
  palpation: Ask the patient to clench the teeth and palpate both masseters and temporalis muscles for bulk and symmetry.
  testing: CN V motor exam — clenching strength, jaw opening against resistance, jaw deviation on opening; jaw-jerk reflex (brisk in upper motor neuron lesions above the pons).
quiz:
  - stem: Which muscle of mastication opens the mouth?
    choices:
      - Masseter
      - Temporalis
      - Medial pterygoid
      - Lateral pterygoid
      - Buccinator
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      The **lateral pterygoid** pulls the condyle and disc forward, depressing and protruding the jaw. Masseter,
      temporalis and medial pterygoid elevate it.
  - stem: A patient's jaw deviates to the right when she opens her mouth. Which nerve is most likely injured?
    choices:
      - Left mandibular nerve (V3)
      - Right mandibular nerve (V3)
      - Right facial nerve
      - Left hypoglossal nerve
      - Right maxillary nerve (V2)
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The jaw deviates **toward the weak side** because the intact left lateral pterygoid pushes it across. So the
      **right V3** motor supply is affected. (The tongue also deviates toward the weak side in a hypoglossal lesion.)
