anatomy:
  location: In the anterior [[orbit]], cushioned by fat and suspended by fascia (Tenon capsule) and the suspensory ligament of Lockwood.
  parts:
    - "**Fibrous coat**: **sclera** (white, posterior 5/6) and **cornea** (transparent, avascular, anterior; most of the eye's refractive power; supplied by V1)"
    - "**Vascular coat (uvea)**: **choroid**, **ciliary body** (ciliary muscle for accommodation; ciliary processes secrete aqueous humor) and **iris** (sphincter pupillae — parasympathetic CN III; dilator pupillae — sympathetic)"
    - "**Neural coat**: **retina**, with the **macula** and **fovea** (central vision, cones) and the **optic disc** (blind spot)"
    - "**Lens**: suspended by zonular fibers; changes shape for near vision"
    - "**Chambers**: anterior and posterior chambers (aqueous humor) and vitreous body"
    - "**Aqueous flow**: ciliary body → posterior chamber → through the pupil → anterior chamber → **trabecular meshwork** at the iridocorneal angle → canal of Schlemm → episcleral veins"
  bloodSupply: '**Ophthalmic artery** (first branch of the internal carotid; see [[carotid-arteries]]) → **central retinal artery** (inner retina — an end artery) and ciliary arteries (choroid, which supplies the outer retina).'
  venousDrainage: Central retinal vein and vortex veins → ophthalmic veins → cavernous sinus.
  lymphatics: None within the globe.
  innervation: Sensory from V1 (long and short ciliary nerves). Parasympathetic ([[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]] → ciliary ganglion) — pupillary constriction and accommodation. Sympathetic (superior cervical ganglion) — pupillary dilation.
  relations: Moved by the [[extraocular-muscles]]; the optic nerve leaves posteriorly slightly medial to the posterior pole.
clinical:
  - title: Acute angle-closure glaucoma
    highYield: true
    mechanism: The iris blocks the iridocorneal angle, suddenly obstructing aqueous outflow; triggered by pupil dilation (dim light, anticholinergics, mydriatics) in hyperopic older patients.
    presentation: >-
      Sudden severe eye pain, headache, nausea/vomiting, blurred vision with halos; red eye, **fixed mid-dilated pupil**,
      **cloudy (steamy) cornea**, rock-hard eye (IOP often over 40 mm Hg). **Emergency** — topical timolol, pilocarpine,
      apraclonidine, IV/oral acetazolamide, then laser iridotomy. {{verify: IOP value}}
  - title: Open-angle glaucoma
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Chronic optic neuropathy with increased cup-to-disc ratio, often with raised IOP; painless.
    presentation: Gradual **peripheral vision loss**; treat with prostaglandin analog drops (latanoprost) or beta-blocker drops, laser or surgery.
  - title: Retinal detachment
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Separation of the neural retina from the pigment epithelium, usually from a retinal tear (myopia, trauma, older age).
    presentation: '**Flashes (photopsia), new floaters and a "curtain" or shadow** over the visual field — urgent ophthalmology.'
  - title: Central retinal artery occlusion
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Embolus (carotid plaque, cardiac) or giant cell arteritis blocking the end artery to the inner retina.
    presentation: Sudden **painless monocular vision loss**; **cherry-red spot** with a pale retina on fundoscopy. Evaluate as a stroke equivalent (carotid imaging, ESR/CRP for GCA).
  - title: The red eye
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Conjunctivitis (viral, bacterial, allergic), subconjunctival hemorrhage, keratitis, uveitis, acute angle closure, scleritis.
    presentation: '**Red flags**: pain, reduced vision, photophobia, abnormal pupil, corneal opacity, contact lens wear → same-day ophthalmology. Conjunctivitis has normal vision and no pain beyond grittiness.'
pance:
  - "Red painful eye, halos, **mid-dilated fixed pupil**, hard eye → **acute angle-closure glaucoma** (emergency)."
  - "Flashes + floaters + curtain → **retinal detachment**."
  - "Sudden painless vision loss + **cherry-red spot** → CRAO (stroke equivalent)."
  - "Open-angle glaucoma: painless peripheral loss, increased cup-to-disc ratio."
  - "Anticholinergic or mydriatic drops can precipitate angle closure."
imaging:
  ultrasound: '**Ocular (B-scan) ultrasound** detects retinal detachment, vitreous hemorrhage and foreign bodies when the fundus can''t be seen; optic nerve sheath diameter as a marker of raised ICP.'
  ct: Orbital CT for trauma and intraocular foreign bodies (MRI is contraindicated with metallic foreign bodies).
  mri: Optic nerve and orbital lesions.
  keyViews:
    - "Bedside ocular ultrasound"
exam:
  landmarks: The corneoscleral junction (limbus); the optic disc lies slightly nasal to the center of the fundus.
  palpation: Digital palpation of IOP is crude (a rock-hard eye in angle closure); tonometry measures it (normal about 10–21 mm Hg).
  testing: >-
    **Visual acuity first** (Snellen), pupils (direct, consensual, swinging-flashlight for RAPD), visual fields by
    confrontation, extraocular movements, fundoscopy (red reflex, disc, vessels, macula), fluorescein staining for
    corneal abrasions.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 65-year-old woman develops severe right eye pain, blurred vision with halos around lights, and vomiting after
      watching a movie in a dark theater. The right eye is red with a hazy cornea and a mid-dilated pupil that does not
      react. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Acute bacterial conjunctivitis
      - Acute angle-closure glaucoma
      - Anterior uveitis
      - Central retinal artery occlusion
      - Migraine with aura
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Dim light dilates the pupil, which can close a narrow iridocorneal angle. **Acute angle-closure glaucoma** causes a
      painful red eye, halos, a **steamy cornea and fixed mid-dilated pupil** — an emergency to lower IOP.
  - stem: >-
      A 70-year-old man with atrial fibrillation suddenly loses vision in his left eye without pain. Fundoscopy shows a
      pale retina with a cherry-red spot at the macula. Which vessel is occluded?
    choices:
      - Central retinal vein
      - Central retinal artery
      - Posterior cerebral artery
      - Ophthalmic vein
      - Anterior ciliary artery
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Central retinal artery occlusion** makes the inner retina pale and ischemic; the thin fovea lets the underlying
      choroidal circulation show through as a **cherry-red spot**. It is a stroke equivalent.
  - stem: >-
      A 58-year-old with high myopia sees sudden flashes of light, many new floaters, and a shadow spreading across part
      of the visual field. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Retinal detachment
      - Acute angle-closure glaucoma
      - Central retinal artery occlusion
      - Migraine aura
      - Open-angle glaucoma
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      **Flashes (photopsia), new floaters and a curtain or shadow** over the field indicate **retinal detachment** (risk
      factors: myopia, trauma, older age), which needs urgent ophthalmology.
  - stem: Through which structure does aqueous humor normally leave the anterior chamber?
    choices:
      - Ciliary processes
      - Vitreous body
      - Trabecular meshwork and canal of Schlemm
      - Central retinal vein
      - Nasolacrimal duct
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      Aqueous is secreted by the **ciliary processes** into the posterior chamber, flows through the pupil into the
      anterior chamber, and drains through the **trabecular meshwork** at the iridocorneal angle into the **canal of
      Schlemm**. Blocking the angle causes angle-closure glaucoma.
  - stem: What is a common first-line medical treatment for primary open-angle glaucoma?
    choices:
      - Oral acetazolamide long-term
      - Mydriatic drops
      - Topical corticosteroid drops
      - Prostaglandin analog eye drops (e.g. latanoprost)
      - Oral antibiotics
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      Open-angle glaucoma is a painless, chronic optic neuropathy with peripheral field loss. It is commonly treated
      with **prostaglandin analog** or beta-blocker drops, then laser or surgery. Mydriatics can precipitate angle
      closure.
