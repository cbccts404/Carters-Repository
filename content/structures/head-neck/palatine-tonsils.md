---
name: Palatine tonsils
type: lymphatic
region: head-neck
subregion: Pharynx & larynx
taName: Tonsilla palatina
aka: [Tonsils]
summary: Paired masses of lymphoid tissue in the tonsillar fossae of the oropharynx, part of Waldeyer ring. Streptococcal pharyngitis, peritonsillar abscess and infectious mononucleosis are the key problems.
tags: [pharynx, ent, infection]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: In the **tonsillar fossa** between the palatoglossal (anterior) and palatopharyngeal (posterior) arches of the oropharynx (see [[pharynx]]).
  drains: The oral cavity and oropharynx; they sample antigens entering through the mouth and nose (with the pharyngeal tonsil — adenoids — and lingual tonsil, forming **Waldeyer ring**).
  drainsTo: '**Jugulodigastric (tonsillar) node** of the deep cervical chain, just below the angle of the mandible (see [[cervical-lymph-nodes]]).'
  relations: >-
    Lateral: the tonsillar bed (superior constrictor) with the **glossopharyngeal nerve**; the **internal carotid
    artery** lies about 2–2.5 cm posterolateral. Blood supply mainly from the tonsillar branch of the facial artery;
    the **external palatine (paratonsillar) vein** is the usual source of post-tonsillectomy bleeding.
  notes: Tonsils are large in childhood and shrink after puberty. Pain after tonsillectomy is often referred to the ear via CN IX.
clinical:
  - title: Group A streptococcal pharyngitis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '*Streptococcus pyogenes* infection of the tonsils and pharynx; peaks at ages 5–15.'
    presentation: >-
      Sudden sore throat, fever, **tonsillar exudates**, **tender anterior cervical nodes**, **absence of cough**
      (**Centor/McIsaac** criteria). Confirm with a rapid antigen test (± culture in children); treat with **penicillin
      V or amoxicillin** for 10 days to prevent **rheumatic fever**. Viral pharyngitis has cough, rhinorrhea,
      conjunctivitis.
  - title: Peritonsillar abscess (quinsy)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Spread of tonsillitis into the space between the tonsillar capsule and the superior constrictor; adolescents and young adults.
    presentation: Severe unilateral throat pain, **"hot potato" voice**, **trismus**, drooling, **uvula deviated away** from the affected side, bulging soft palate. Treat with **needle aspiration or incision and drainage** plus antibiotics.
  - title: Infectious mononucleosis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Epstein–Barr virus infection of B cells.
    presentation: "Exudative tonsillitis, fever, **posterior cervical lymphadenopathy**, fatigue, splenomegaly, atypical lymphocytes, positive heterophile (Monospot) test. **Amoxicillin causes a rash**; avoid contact sports for at least 3–4 weeks (splenic rupture risk). {{verify: return-to-play timing}}"
pance:
  - "Centor: **fever, exudates, tender anterior nodes, no cough** → test for GAS → **penicillin/amoxicillin**."
  - "Treat strep throat to prevent **rheumatic fever** (not post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis)."
  - "Hot potato voice, trismus, **uvula deviated away** → **peritonsillar abscess** → drain."
  - "Exudative pharyngitis + posterior nodes + splenomegaly → **mono**; amoxicillin rash; no contact sports."
imaging:
  ct: Contrast CT of the neck if the diagnosis is unclear or a deeper (parapharyngeal/retropharyngeal) infection is suspected.
  ultrasound: Intraoral or transcervical ultrasound helps confirm a peritonsillar abscess and guide aspiration.
exam:
  landmarks: Between the anterior and posterior tonsillar pillars; the uvula in the midline.
  palpation: Palpate the jugulodigastric node below the angle of the jaw and the anterior and posterior cervical chains.
  testing: 'Inspect with a tongue depressor and light: tonsil size (grade 0–4), exudate, symmetry, uvula position, palatal bulging; assess trismus and voice.'
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 19-year-old has 4 days of severe left throat pain, fever, muffled voice and difficulty opening his mouth. The
      left soft palate bulges and the uvula is deviated to the right. What is the most appropriate treatment?
    choices:
      - Oral penicillin alone and follow-up in a week
      - Needle aspiration or incision and drainage plus antibiotics
      - Emergency tracheostomy
      - Tonsillectomy the same day in all cases
      - Oral steroids only
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A bulging palate with **uvula deviated away**, trismus and a "hot potato" voice indicate a **peritonsillar
      abscess**, treated with **drainage** (aspiration or I&D) and antibiotics.
  - stem: >-
      An 18-year-old has sore throat, fever and fatigue. She has tonsillar exudates, posterior cervical
      lymphadenopathy and a palpable spleen. She developed a rash after amoxicillin. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Group A streptococcal pharyngitis
      - Infectious mononucleosis
      - Diphtheria
      - Herpangina
      - Peritonsillar abscess
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **EBV mononucleosis**: exudative tonsillitis with **posterior** cervical nodes, **splenomegaly**, and the classic
      **rash after amoxicillin**. Advise avoiding contact sports because of splenic rupture risk.
