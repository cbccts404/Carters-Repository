---
name: Temporomandibular joint
type: joint
region: head-neck
subregion: Skull
taName: Articulatio temporomandibularis
aka: [TMJ, Jaw joint]
summary: Synovial joint between the mandibular condyle and the temporal bone, divided by an articular disc into two compartments that allow hinging and gliding. TMJ disorders and anterior dislocation are common.
tags: [face, dental, pain]
systems: [musculoskeletal, eent]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Just in front of the tragus of the ear.
  classification: Synovial joint (modified hinge with gliding), divided by an **articular disc** into upper and lower compartments
  bones: Condyle of the [[mandible]] and the mandibular fossa and articular tubercle of the temporal bone ([[skull]])
  capsule: Loose; the articular surfaces are covered by fibrocartilage (not hyaline).
  ligaments:
    - "**Lateral (temporomandibular) ligament**: main stabilizer; prevents posterior displacement"
    - "**Sphenomandibular** and **stylomandibular** ligaments (accessory)"
    - "**Articular disc**: fibrocartilage, attached to the capsule and to the lateral pterygoid anteriorly"
  movements:
    - "**Lower compartment**: hinge (rotation) — first part of mouth opening"
    - "**Upper compartment**: gliding (translation) of condyle and disc forward onto the articular tubercle — wide opening, protrusion"
    - "Side-to-side (grinding) movements"
    - "Muscles: see [[muscles-of-mastication]]"
  stability: Most stable with the teeth together (occlusion). Wide opening moves the condyle onto the articular tubercle, the position from which it can dislocate.
  innervation: Auriculotemporal and masseteric branches of the mandibular nerve (V3; [[trigeminal-nerve]]).
  bloodSupply: Superficial temporal and maxillary arteries (branches of the [[external-carotid-artery]]).
  relations: The parotid gland lies behind and lateral to the joint; the external acoustic meatus lies directly behind the condyle, so TMJ pain is often felt as ear pain.
clinical:
  - title: Temporomandibular disorders
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Myofascial pain of the masticatory muscles, disc displacement or degenerative joint disease; associated with bruxism, clenching and stress.
    presentation: Jaw or preauricular pain (often felt as **ear pain with a normal otoscopic exam**), clicking, limited opening, headache; worse with chewing. Managed conservatively (soft diet, NSAIDs, night guard, physical therapy).
  - title: TMJ dislocation
    highYield: false
    mechanism: '**Anterior** dislocation during wide yawning or opening; the condyle locks in front of the articular tubercle, held by muscle spasm.'
    presentation: Open-locked jaw, unable to close, prominent chin. Reduced by pressing down and back on the lower molars (protect the thumbs).
pance:
  - "Ear pain with a normal ear exam, jaw clicking, worse with chewing → **TMJ disorder**."
  - "Jaw stuck open after a yawn → anterior TMJ dislocation."
  - "TMJ has an **articular disc** and fibrocartilage surfaces."
imaging:
  xray: Panoramic radiograph for gross bony changes.
  ct: Bony anatomy, fractures and ankylosis.
  mri: '**MRI** (open and closed mouth) shows the **articular disc** position and effusions — the test for internal derangement.'
exam:
  landmarks: The condyle is palpable just in front of the tragus, moving forward as the mouth opens.
  palpation: Palpate the joint (in front of the tragus or via the external ear canal) during opening and closing for tenderness, clicking or crepitus; palpate masseter and temporalis.
  testing: Measure maximal interincisal opening (normal about 40–50 mm, roughly three fingers' breadth); observe deviation of the jaw on opening (toward the affected side). {{verify}}
quiz:

      her teeth at night. Otoscopy is normal. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Otitis externa
      - Temporomandibular disorder
      - Trigeminal neuralgia
      - Parotitis
      - Giant cell arteritis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Temporomandibular disorder** commonly causes ear/preauricular pain with a normal ear exam, clicking and pain on
      chewing, often with bruxism. Trigeminal neuralgia causes brief electric paroxysms.
  - stem: Which structure divides the TMJ into upper and lower compartments?
    choices:
      - Lateral ligament
      - Articular disc
      - Stylomandibular ligament
      - Coronoid process
      - Lateral pterygoid plate
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The fibrocartilaginous **articular disc** divides the joint into an upper (gliding) and lower (hinge) compartment.
