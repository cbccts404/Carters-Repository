---
name: Recurrent laryngeal nerve
type: nerve
region: head-neck
subregion: Pharynx & larynx
taName: Nervus laryngeus recurrens
aka: [Inferior laryngeal nerve, RLN]
summary: Branch of the vagus that loops under the subclavian artery on the right and the aortic arch on the left, then climbs in the tracheoesophageal groove to supply the larynx. Injury causes hoarseness, a classic thyroidectomy complication.
tags: [larynx, vagus, thyroid-surgery]
systems: [eent, neurologic]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  roots: Vagus nerve (CN X) — motor fibers from the nucleus ambiguus
  origin: Branch of the **[[vagus-nerve|vagus nerve (CN X)]]** in the root of the neck (right) or the thorax (left).
  course: >-
    **Right**: hooks under the **right subclavian artery**. **Left**: hooks under the **aortic arch** just lateral to the
    ligamentum arteriosum (see [[thoracic-aorta]]), so it is longer and has a thoracic course. Both ascend in the
    **tracheoesophageal groove**, pass close to the **inferior thyroid artery** behind the [[thyroid-gland]], and enter
    the [[larynx]] behind the cricothyroid joint (under the inferior constrictor), where the terminal part is called
    the inferior laryngeal nerve.
  branches:
    - "Cardiac, tracheal and esophageal branches"
    - "Inferior laryngeal nerve — motor to all intrinsic laryngeal muscles except cricothyroid"
  motor: '**All intrinsic muscles of the larynx except cricothyroid** — including the **posterior cricoarytenoid**, the only abductor of the vocal folds.'
  sensory: Laryngeal mucosa **below the vocal folds**.
  relations: Close to the inferior thyroid artery and the posterior thyroid capsule (ligament of Berry); the left nerve is related to the aortic arch, left main bronchus, left atrium and aortopulmonary window lymph nodes.
  notes: "A **non-recurrent** right laryngeal nerve occurs in a small minority of people, usually with an aberrant right subclavian artery. {{verify: prevalence ~0.5–1%}}"
clinical:
  - title: Recurrent laryngeal nerve injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Thyroidectomy/parathyroidectomy** (most common iatrogenic cause), anterior cervical spine surgery, and tumors. The **left** nerve is also at risk in the chest — **lung cancer (apex/hilum), aortic arch aneurysm**, mediastinal nodes, and even a massively enlarged left atrium (**Ortner syndrome**, e.g. mitral stenosis).'
    presentation: >-
      **Unilateral**: **hoarse, breathy voice**, weak cough, possible aspiration; the paralyzed cord sits near the
      midline (paramedian). **Bilateral**: both cords paramedian → **stridor and airway obstruction**, often needing
      tracheostomy, with a relatively normal voice.
  - title: External (superior) laryngeal nerve injury
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Injured while ligating the superior thyroid vessels (runs close to them).
    presentation: Paralysis of **cricothyroid** → inability to tense the cord → **loss of high pitch** and voice fatigue (e.g. singers) rather than hoarseness.
pance:
  - "New **hoarseness after thyroidectomy** → recurrent laryngeal nerve injury."
  - "The **left** RLN loops under the aortic arch → hoarseness can be the first sign of **lung cancer, aortic aneurysm** or mitral stenosis (Ortner)."
  - "Bilateral RLN palsy → **stridor/airway compromise**."
  - "External laryngeal nerve injury → loss of **high-pitched voice** (cricothyroid)."
imaging:
  xray: A chest X-ray is part of the workup of unexplained vocal cord paralysis (apical or hilar mass, mediastinal widening).
  ct: '**Contrast CT from the skull base to the aortopulmonary window** (neck and chest) to search along the whole course of the nerve for unexplained vocal cord paralysis.'
exam:
  landmarks: The nerve cannot be palpated; it lies in the tracheoesophageal groove behind the thyroid lobes.
  testing: Voice quality, cough strength, stridor; **laryngoscopy** shows an immobile cord. Many surgeons document cord function before and after thyroid surgery and use intraoperative nerve monitoring.
quiz:

      A 64-year-old man with a 50-pack-year smoking history has had hoarseness for 6 weeks. Laryngoscopy shows a
      paralyzed left vocal cord with no mass. Where is the most likely lesion?
    choices:
      - Right lung apex
      - Aortopulmonary window/left hilum
      - Left parotid gland
      - Left jugular foramen only
      - Cricothyroid muscle
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **left recurrent laryngeal nerve** loops under the aortic arch, so a **left hilar lung cancer or
      aortopulmonary window nodes** can compress it. CT from the skull base to the mediastinum is indicated.
  - stem: >-
      The day after a total thyroidectomy, a patient has inspiratory stridor and increasing respiratory distress but a
      nearly normal voice. What is the most likely cause?
    choices:
      - Unilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve injury
      - Bilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve injury
      - External laryngeal nerve injury
      - Hypocalcemia from parathyroid removal only
      - Postoperative hypothyroidism
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Bilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve injury** leaves both cords near the midline, narrowing the airway and causing
      **stridor**, while the voice may be relatively preserved. Neck hematoma and hypocalcemic laryngospasm are other
      causes of post-thyroidectomy airway compromise to consider.
  - stem: The left recurrent laryngeal nerve hooks under which structure?
    choices:
      - Aortic arch, just lateral to the ligamentum arteriosum
      - Left subclavian artery
      - Left main bronchus only
      - Left common carotid artery
      - Pulmonary trunk
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      The **left** RLN hooks under the **aortic arch** lateral to the ligamentum arteriosum, giving it a thoracic
      course; the **right** hooks under the right subclavian artery. Thoracic lesions (lung cancer, aneurysm) can
      therefore cause left-sided hoarseness.
  - stem: >-
      After a thyroidectomy, a professional singer can speak normally but can no longer reach high notes. Which nerve
      was most likely injured?
    choices:
      - Recurrent laryngeal nerve
      - Internal laryngeal nerve
      - Hypoglossal nerve
      - External laryngeal nerve
      - Ansa cervicalis
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      The **external laryngeal nerve** runs near the **superior thyroid vessels** and supplies **cricothyroid**, which
      tenses the cords. Injury causes loss of **high pitch** and voice fatigue rather than hoarseness.
  - stem: A patient with severe mitral stenosis develops hoarseness. What is the most likely mechanism?
    choices:
      - Laryngeal edema from heart failure
      - Vocal cord nodules
      - An enlarged left atrium compressing the left recurrent laryngeal nerve
      - Aspiration pneumonia
      - Hypocalcemia
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      In **Ortner syndrome**, a massively enlarged **left atrium** (e.g. mitral stenosis) compresses the **left
      recurrent laryngeal nerve**, which runs near the left atrium and aortopulmonary window.
