summary: The bones of the head — the neurocranium enclosing the brain and the viscerocranium forming the face. Pterion fractures, basilar skull fractures and infant fontanelles are the clinically important features.
tags: [skull, trauma, pediatrics]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Sits on the atlas ([[atlas-and-axis]]) at the atlanto-occipital joints.
  classification: 'Flat and irregular bones joined mainly by **sutures** (fibrous joints); the mandible is the only freely movable bone (see [[temporomandibular-joint]]).'
  features:
    - "**Neurocranium (8 bones)**: frontal, 2 parietal, 2 temporal, occipital, sphenoid, ethmoid"
    - "**Viscerocranium (facial skeleton)**: 2 nasal, 2 maxillae, 2 zygomatic, 2 lacrimal, 2 palatine, 2 inferior nasal conchae, vomer and the [[mandible]]"
    - "**Sutures**: coronal (frontal–parietal), sagittal (between the parietals), lambdoid (parietal–occipital), squamous (temporal–parietal)"
    - "**Pterion**: H-shaped junction of the frontal, parietal, temporal and greater wing of the sphenoid, about 3–4 cm above the zygomatic arch; the thinnest part of the lateral skull, overlying the **anterior branch of the [[middle-meningeal-artery]]**"
    - "**Cranial fossae**: anterior (frontal lobes; cribriform plate of the ethmoid), middle (temporal lobes, pituitary in the sella turcica; foramina rotundum, ovale, spinosum), posterior (cerebellum, brainstem; foramen magnum, internal acoustic meatus, jugular foramen, hypoglossal canal)"
    - "**Key foramina**: optic canal (CN II, ophthalmic artery); superior orbital fissure (CN III, IV, V1, VI); rotundum (V2); ovale (V3); spinosum (middle meningeal artery); jugular foramen (CN IX, X, XI, internal jugular vein); hypoglossal canal (CN XII); foramen magnum (medulla, vertebral arteries)"
  ossification: >-
    The vault forms by **intramembranous** ossification, the base by endochondral ossification. At birth the vault bones
    are separated by sutures and **fontanelles**: the **anterior fontanelle** (diamond-shaped) usually closes by about
    **18–24 months**, the **posterior fontanelle** (triangular) by about **2 months**. {{verify: typical fontanelle closure ages}}
  relations: The dural venous sinuses groove the inner surface of the vault; the middle meningeal vessels groove the inner surface of the temporal and parietal bones.
clinical:
  - title: Epidural hematoma from pterion fracture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: A blow to the side of the head fractures the thin pterion and tears the **[[middle-meningeal-artery]]**.
    presentation: Brief loss of consciousness, a **lucid interval**, then rapid deterioration (headache, vomiting, ipsilateral blown pupil, contralateral hemiparesis). CT shows a **biconvex (lens-shaped)** hematoma that does not cross sutures. Neurosurgical emergency.
  - title: Basilar skull fracture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Fracture through the skull base (often the petrous temporal bone or anterior cranial fossa) from significant head trauma.
    presentation: >-
      **Raccoon eyes** (periorbital ecchymosis), **Battle sign** (mastoid ecchymosis), **hemotympanum**, and **CSF
      rhinorrhea or otorrhea**; possible cranial nerve palsies (VII, VIII). Avoid nasogastric tubes and nasotracheal
      intubation (risk of intracranial passage).
  - title: Craniosynostosis
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Premature fusion of one or more sutures; sagittal most common.
    presentation: Abnormal head shape (e.g. long narrow scaphocephaly with sagittal synostosis); may raise intracranial pressure. Distinguish from positional plagiocephaly.
  - title: Fontanelle as a clinical sign
    highYield: true
    mechanism: The open anterior fontanelle reflects intracranial pressure and hydration in infants.
    presentation: '**Bulging** → raised ICP (meningitis, hydrocephalus); **sunken** → dehydration. Delayed closure: hypothyroidism, rickets, raised ICP; early closure: craniosynostosis.'
pance:
  - "Lucid interval + biconvex bleed → **epidural hematoma** (middle meningeal artery at the pterion)."
  - "Raccoon eyes, Battle sign, hemotympanum, CSF leak → **basilar skull fracture** — no NG tube."
  - "Bulging fontanelle = raised ICP; sunken = dehydration."
  - "Anterior fontanelle closes by about 18–24 months; posterior by about 2 months."
imaging:
  xray: Skull films are largely replaced by CT; still used in skeletal surveys for suspected child abuse.
  ct: '**Non-contrast head CT** is the first test in head trauma (fractures with bone windows, epidural/subdural hemorrhage). CT with 3-D reconstruction for craniosynostosis and facial fractures.'
  mri: Brain parenchyma, skull base tumors and CSF leak localization (with CT cisternography).
  ultrasound: Cranial ultrasound through the open anterior fontanelle in neonates (intraventricular hemorrhage).
  keyViews:
    - "Non-contrast head CT (brain and bone windows)"
exam:
  landmarks: External occipital protuberance (inion), mastoid process, zygomatic arch, pterion (about two finger-breadths above the zygomatic arch and a thumb's breadth behind the frontal process of the zygomatic bone), nasion.
  palpation: In infants, palpate the anterior fontanelle (size, tension) with the baby upright and calm; palpate sutures for ridging. In trauma, palpate for steps and bogginess.
  testing: Inspect for raccoon eyes, Battle sign, CSF leak (clear fluid; "halo sign" on gauze) and hemotympanum on otoscopy.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 19-year-old is hit on the side of the head with a baseball. He is briefly unconscious, then talks normally for
      2 hours before becoming drowsy with a dilated right pupil. CT shows a biconvex hyperdense collection. Which vessel
      is most likely injured?
    choices:
      - Bridging veins
      - Middle meningeal artery
      - Middle cerebral artery
      - Superior sagittal sinus
      - Anterior communicating artery aneurysm
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A lucid interval with a **biconvex (lentiform)** extradural collection is an **epidural hematoma**, classically from
      the **middle meningeal artery** under the thin **pterion**. Subdural hematomas (crescent-shaped) come from bridging
      veins.
  - stem: After a car crash, a patient has bruising around both eyes and behind the ear, and clear fluid from the nose. What should be avoided?
    choices:
      - Head CT
      - Cervical spine immobilization
      - Nasogastric tube insertion
      - Neurosurgical consultation
      - Otoscopic exam
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      Raccoon eyes, Battle sign and CSF rhinorrhea indicate a **basilar skull fracture**. A **nasogastric tube** (or
      nasotracheal intubation) can pass through the fractured cribriform plate into the cranium; use the orogastric route.
  - stem: Which structure passes through the foramen spinosum?
    choices:
      - Mandibular nerve (V3)
      - Maxillary nerve (V2)
      - Middle meningeal artery
      - Internal carotid artery
      - Hypoglossal nerve
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **middle meningeal artery** enters the skull through the **foramen spinosum**. V3 passes through the foramen
      ovale, V2 through the foramen rotundum.
