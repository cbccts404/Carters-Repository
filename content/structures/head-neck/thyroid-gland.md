name: Thyroid gland
type: organ
region: head-neck
subregion: Neck
taName: Glandula thyroidea
summary: Butterfly-shaped endocrine gland wrapped around the upper trachea at C5–T1. It moves with swallowing, lies next to the recurrent laryngeal nerves and parathyroids, and thyroid nodules are a common workup.
tags: [endocrine, neck, thyroid-surgery]
systems: [endocrine]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Anterior neck, deep to the infrahyoid (strap) muscles, at about **C5–T1**; the isthmus lies over the **2nd–3rd (or 2nd–4th) tracheal rings**.
  parts:
    - "**Right and left lobes** on the sides of the [[larynx]] and [[trachea]]"
    - "**Isthmus** over the upper tracheal rings"
    - "**Pyramidal lobe** (variable) rising from the isthmus — remnant of the thyroglossal duct"
    - "Enclosed in the **pretracheal layer of deep cervical fascia**, which attaches it to the larynx, so it **moves up on swallowing**"
    - "**Follicular cells** (T3/T4) and **parafollicular C cells** (calcitonin)"
  bloodSupply: >-
    **Superior thyroid artery** (first branch of the [[external-carotid-artery]]) and **inferior thyroid artery** (from
    the thyrocervical trunk of the subclavian); occasionally a **thyroid ima artery** from the brachiocephalic trunk or
    arch.
  venousDrainage: Superior and middle thyroid veins → [[internal-jugular-vein]]; inferior thyroid veins → brachiocephalic veins.
  lymphatics: Prelaryngeal (Delphian), pretracheal and paratracheal (central compartment, level VI) nodes, then deep cervical nodes (see [[cervical-lymph-nodes]]).
  innervation: Sympathetic fibers from the cervical ganglia (vasomotor); secretion is controlled hormonally by TSH.
  relations: >-
    Posteromedially: the [[recurrent-laryngeal-nerve]] in the tracheoesophageal groove, close to the inferior thyroid
    artery. Posteriorly: the [[parathyroid-glands]]. The external laryngeal nerve runs near the superior thyroid
    vessels. Posterolaterally: the carotid sheath ([[carotid-arteries]], [[internal-jugular-vein]], vagus).
  notes: Develops from the **foramen cecum** of the [[tongue]] and descends along the **thyroglossal duct**; persistence gives a thyroglossal duct cyst.
clinical:
  - title: Thyroid nodule
    highYield: true
    mechanism: "Very common; most are benign (colloid nodules, adenomas); about 5–10% are malignant (papillary carcinoma most common). {{verify: malignancy rate}}"
    presentation: >-
      Workup: **TSH first**. **Low TSH** → radioactive iodine uptake scan (a "hot" nodule is rarely malignant). **Normal or
      high TSH** → **ultrasound** → **fine-needle aspiration** based on size and suspicious features
      (microcalcifications, hypoechoic, irregular margins, taller-than-wide).
  - title: Thyroid cancer
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Papillary** (most common; radiation exposure; psammoma bodies; lymphatic spread), **follicular** (hematogenous spread), **medullary** (C cells; calcitonin; **MEN 2**), **anaplastic** (elderly; rapidly fatal).'
    presentation: A hard, fixed nodule, cervical lymphadenopathy, hoarseness (nerve invasion), rapid growth.
  - title: Thyroglossal duct cyst
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Persistent thyroglossal duct remnant.
    presentation: '**Midline** neck mass near the hyoid that **moves up with tongue protrusion** and swallowing; can become infected. Excise with the middle of the hyoid (Sistrunk procedure) after confirming normal thyroid tissue elsewhere.'
  - title: Goiter and thyroidectomy complications
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Goiter — iodine deficiency, Graves disease, multinodular goiter, Hashimoto thyroiditis. Complications of thyroidectomy — recurrent laryngeal nerve injury, hypoparathyroidism, neck hematoma.
    presentation: '**Neck hematoma** after thyroidectomy can compress the airway — open the wound at the bedside. **Hypocalcemia** (perioral tingling, Chvostek and Trousseau signs) from parathyroid injury. **Hoarseness** from RLN injury. A large goiter can cause **Pemberton sign** (facial plethora on raising the arms).'
pance:
  - "Thyroid nodule → **TSH first**; low → uptake scan; normal/high → **ultrasound ± FNA**."
  - "Papillary = most common, **psammoma bodies**, radiation; medullary = **calcitonin, MEN 2**."
  - "Midline mass that **moves with tongue protrusion** → **thyroglossal duct cyst**."
  - "Post-thyroidectomy: **hoarseness** (RLN), **hypocalcemia** (parathyroids), **hematoma** (airway)."
imaging:
  ultrasound: '**First-line imaging** for nodules and goiter; guides FNA.'
  ct: Retrosternal goiter extent, airway compression, and staging of advanced cancer (avoid iodinated contrast before planned radioiodine therapy when possible).
  xray: Tracheal deviation from a large goiter may be seen on a chest X-ray.
  keyViews:
    - "Thyroid ultrasound"
    - "Radioactive iodine (I-123) uptake scan"
exam:
  landmarks: 'Find the thyroid cartilage, then the cricoid; the **isthmus lies just below the cricoid** and the lobes lie lateral to it, behind the sternocleidomastoids.'
  palpation: >-
    Examine from behind or in front with the neck slightly flexed; have the patient **swallow water** — the thyroid
    rises. Assess size, consistency, nodules, tenderness (subacute thyroiditis) and cervical nodes. Auscultate for a
    **bruit** (Graves disease).
  testing: Look for signs of hypo- or hyperthyroidism (reflexes, tremor, skin, eyes).
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 42-year-old woman has a 2-cm thyroid nodule. TSH is normal. What is the most appropriate next step?
    choices:
      - Radioactive iodine uptake scan
      - Thyroid ultrasound
      - Start levothyroxine suppression
      - Total thyroidectomy
      - Repeat TSH in 1 year only
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      With a **normal TSH**, the next step is a **thyroid ultrasound** to characterize the nodule and decide on
      **FNA**. An uptake scan is for a **low TSH**.
  - stem: >-
      A 7-year-old has a painless midline neck mass at the level of the hyoid that rises when she sticks out her tongue.
      What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Branchial cleft cyst
      - Thyroglossal duct cyst
      - Cystic hygroma
      - Dermoid cyst
      - Reactive lymph node
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **midline** mass that **moves with tongue protrusion** is a **thyroglossal duct cyst**, attached to the hyoid and
      foramen cecum. A branchial cleft cyst lies **laterally**, along the anterior border of sternocleidomastoid.
  - stem: >-
      Six hours after thyroidectomy, a patient has an expanding neck swelling, stridor and difficulty breathing. What is
      the most appropriate immediate action?
    choices:
      - CT scan of the neck
      - IV calcium gluconate
      - Open the incision at the bedside to evacuate the hematoma
      - Laryngoscopy in clinic next week
      - Increase the oxygen and observe
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      An **expanding post-thyroidectomy hematoma** compresses the airway; **open the wound immediately** (skin and strap
      sutures) to relieve pressure, then return to the OR. Imaging would delay life-saving treatment.
  - stem: A patient with a thyroid nodule has a low TSH. What is the most appropriate next step?
    choices:
      - Fine-needle aspiration of the nodule
      - Radioactive iodine uptake scan
      - Total thyroidectomy
      - Serum calcitonin only
      - CT of the neck
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      With a **low TSH**, a **radioactive iodine uptake scan** is next; a **"hot"** (hyperfunctioning) nodule is rarely
      malignant. With a normal or high TSH, ultrasound ± FNA is next.
  - stem: Medullary thyroid carcinoma arises from which cells, and which tumor marker is used?
    choices:
      - Follicular cells; thyroglobulin
      - Follicular cells; TSH
      - Lymphocytes; LDH
      - Squamous cells; SCC antigen
      - Parafollicular C cells; calcitonin
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      **Medullary carcinoma** arises from **parafollicular C cells**, secretes **calcitonin**, and is associated with
      **MEN 2**. Papillary carcinoma (most common) arises from follicular cells.
