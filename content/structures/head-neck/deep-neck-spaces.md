name: Deep neck spaces
type: space
region: head-neck
subregion: Neck
taName: Spatia colli profunda
aka: [Retropharyngeal space, Danger space, Parapharyngeal space, Submandibular space, Ludwig angina, Deep cervical fascia]
summary: Potential spaces between the layers of deep cervical fascia. Infections can track through them from the teeth, tonsils and pharynx into the mediastinum.
tags: [neck, infection, emergency]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Between the layers of the **deep cervical fascia**, from the skull base into the chest.
  boundaries:
    investing layer: Surrounds the neck, enclosing [[sternocleidomastoid]], [[trapezius]], and the submandibular and parotid glands
    pretracheal layer: Encloses the [[thyroid-gland]], [[larynx]], [[trachea]], [[esophagus]] and infrahyoid muscles (visceral layer includes the buccopharyngeal fascia behind the pharynx)
    prevertebral layer: Surrounds the vertebral column and deep muscles; its alar part lies in front
    carotid sheath: Formed from all three layers; contains the common/internal carotid, internal jugular vein and vagus
  contents:
    - "**Retropharyngeal space**: between the buccopharyngeal fascia (in front) and the **alar fascia** (behind), from the skull base to about **T1–T2** (upper mediastinum); contains retropharyngeal nodes (prominent in young children) {{verify: inferior extent T1–T2 vs T4 varies between sources}}"
    - "**Danger space**: between the alar and prevertebral fascia, from the skull base **down to the diaphragm** — infection can spread into the posterior mediastinum"
    - "**Prevertebral space**: deep to the prevertebral fascia along the whole spine (vertebral osteomyelitis, Pott disease)"
    - "**Parapharyngeal (lateral pharyngeal) space**: inverted pyramid beside the pharynx from the skull base to the hyoid; connects with the other spaces; carotid sheath in its post-styloid part"
    - "**Submandibular space**: sublingual (above mylohyoid) and submaxillary (below mylohyoid) parts, connected around the back edge of mylohyoid"
  communications: >-
    The spaces communicate, so infection from **teeth** (submandibular), **tonsils** (peritonsillar → parapharyngeal)
    and the **pharynx** (retropharyngeal) can spread into the **carotid sheath** and down to the **mediastinum**.
  notes: Retropharyngeal nodes usually regress by about age 5–6, which is why retropharyngeal abscess is mainly a disease of young children.
clinical:
  - title: Retropharyngeal abscess
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Suppurative retropharyngeal nodes after URI/pharyngitis in children **under 5**; in adults, penetrating trauma (fish bone, instrumentation).
    presentation: >-
      Fever, sore throat, **neck stiffness or pain on extension** (can mimic meningitis), torticollis, drooling,
      muffled voice, stridor. **Lateral neck X-ray: widened prevertebral soft tissue**; confirm with contrast CT. Treat
      with IV antibiotics ± surgical drainage; risk of **mediastinitis**.
  - title: Ludwig angina
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Rapidly spreading bilateral **cellulitis of the submandibular space**, usually from **infected lower molars** (roots below the mylohyoid line); oral streptococci and anaerobes.
    presentation: '**Brawny, woody submandibular swelling**, **elevated and pushed-back tongue**, drooling, trismus, dysphagia, fever — **airway emergency**. **Secure the airway** (awake fiberoptic intubation or tracheostomy), IV antibiotics, drainage if collection.'
  - title: Descending necrotizing mediastinitis
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Spread of a deep neck infection down the retropharyngeal or danger space.
    presentation: Chest pain, dyspnea and sepsis after a neck infection; widened mediastinum; high mortality — urgent CT and surgical drainage.
pance:
  - "Child < 5 with fever, sore throat and **pain on neck extension** → **retropharyngeal abscess** → widened prevertebral space."
  - "Woody submandibular swelling, raised tongue after a dental infection → **Ludwig angina** → airway first."
  - "**Danger space** runs from skull base to diaphragm → mediastinitis."
  - "Deep neck infections → **contrast CT of the neck** once the airway is secure."
imaging:
  xray: 'Lateral soft-tissue neck in extension: **prevertebral soft tissue wider than about 7 mm at C2** (or more than half the vertebral body width) in children suggests retropharyngeal abscess. {{verify: pediatric cutoff values}}'
  ct: '**Contrast CT of the neck** (± chest) — defines the space involved, distinguishes abscess (rim-enhancing collection) from cellulitis, and detects mediastinal spread.'
  mri: Better soft-tissue detail when needed (e.g. prevertebral/spinal involvement).
  keyViews:
    - "Lateral neck film"
    - "Contrast CT neck and chest"
exam:
  landmarks: Submandibular region below the mandible; posterior pharyngeal wall (visible bulge in some retropharyngeal abscesses).
  palpation: Palpate the floor of the mouth and submandibular region (woody induration in Ludwig angina) and the neck for tenderness and fluctuance.
  testing: Assess the **airway first** (stridor, drooling, voice, positioning); neck range of motion (limited extension in retropharyngeal abscess); trismus; teeth for a source.
