name: Extraocular muscles
type: muscle
region: head-neck
subregion: Orbit
taName: Musculi externi bulbi oculi
aka: [Rectus muscles, Oblique muscles, EOMs]
summary: Six muscles that move each eye — four recti and two obliques — supplied by CN III, IV and VI (LR6 SO4, the rest 3). Testing them in the "H" pattern localizes cranial nerve palsies.
tags: [orbit, eye, cranial-nerves]
systems: [eent, neurologic]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Within the [[orbit]], around the optic nerve.
  group: Extraocular muscles of the orbit
  origin: >-
    The four recti arise from the **common tendinous ring** around the optic canal at the orbital apex. **Superior
    oblique**: body of the sphenoid above the ring, passing through the **trochlea** (a pulley on the superomedial
    orbital wall). **Inferior oblique**: floor of the orbit near the nasolacrimal canal (the only one not from the apex).
  insertion: Recti into the sclera in front of the equator; obliques into the sclera behind the equator (posterolateral), which is why they depress/elevate and rotate.
  innervation: '**LR6 SO4, rest 3**: lateral rectus — [[abducens-nerve|abducens nerve]] (**CN VI**); superior oblique — [[trochlear-nerve|trochlear nerve]] (**CN IV**); superior, inferior and medial recti and inferior oblique — oculomotor nerve (**CN III**, which also supplies levator palpebrae superioris).'
  bloodSupply: Muscular branches of the ophthalmic artery.
  action:
    - "**Medial rectus**: adduction"
    - "**Lateral rectus**: abduction"
    - "**Superior rectus**: elevation (mainly when abducted), intorsion, adduction"
    - "**Inferior rectus**: depression (mainly when abducted), extorsion, adduction"
    - "**Superior oblique**: depression (mainly when **adducted**), intorsion, abduction — looking down and in (reading, going downstairs)"
    - "**Inferior oblique**: elevation (mainly when adducted), extorsion, abduction"
  relations: >-
    **Clinical (H-pattern) testing isolates each muscle**: in abduction, superior rectus elevates and inferior rectus
    depresses; in adduction, inferior oblique elevates and superior oblique depresses. Levator palpebrae superioris (CN
    III, with a smooth-muscle part — superior tarsal muscle — supplied by sympathetics) raises the upper lid.
clinical:
  - title: Oculomotor (CN III) palsy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Compressive** (posterior communicating artery aneurysm, uncal herniation) affects the superficial pupillomotor fibers first → **dilated pupil**. **Ischemic** (diabetes, hypertension) spares the pupil.'
    presentation: Eye **"down and out"**, ptosis, diplopia; a **dilated pupil** means compression until proven otherwise (urgent CTA/MRA).
  - title: Trochlear (CN IV) palsy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Head trauma (longest intracranial course), congenital.
    presentation: Vertical diplopia worse **looking down and in** (stairs, reading); the affected eye drifts up; patient **tilts the head away** from the affected side.
  - title: Abducens (CN VI) palsy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Raised intracranial pressure (long course over the petrous apex — a false localizing sign), cavernous sinus lesions, diabetes.
    presentation: Horizontal diplopia worse on looking toward the affected side; the eye can't abduct and rests turned in (esotropia).
  - title: Internuclear ophthalmoplegia
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Lesion of the medial longitudinal fasciculus (multiple sclerosis in the young, stroke in the old).
    presentation: On lateral gaze, the eye on the side of the lesion fails to adduct and the other eye shows nystagmus; convergence is preserved.
pance:
  - "**LR6 SO4**, rest CN III."
  - "CN III palsy: down and out + ptosis; **dilated pupil = aneurysm (PCOM)**; pupil-sparing = diabetic/ischemic."
  - "CN IV palsy: trouble going downstairs, **head tilt away** from the lesion."
  - "CN VI palsy: can't abduct; may be a false localizing sign of raised ICP."
  - "MS in a young adult + failure of adduction with contralateral nystagmus → INO (MLF)."
imaging:
  ct: CT angiography for suspected posterior communicating aneurysm (CN III palsy with dilated pupil); orbital CT for entrapment.
  mri: MRI brain/orbits for cranial nerve palsies, MS (INO) and thyroid eye disease (enlarged muscle bellies sparing the tendons).
  keyViews:
    - "CTA/MRA head for pupil-involving CN III palsy"
exam:
  landmarks: The trochlea is palpable at the superomedial orbital rim.
  testing: >-
    Hold the head still and trace an **"H"** with the target: ask about diplopia in each position and look for
    misalignment and nystagmus. Cover–uncover test for strabismus. Corneal light reflex (Hirschberg) for alignment in
    children.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 58-year-old man has a sudden severe headache. His right eye is deviated down and out with ptosis and a dilated
      pupil that does not react to light. What is the most likely cause?
    choices:
      - Diabetic microvascular CN III palsy
      - Posterior communicating artery aneurysm
      - Multiple sclerosis
      - Myasthenia gravis
      - Trochlear nerve palsy
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **pupil-involving CN III palsy** suggests compression of the superficial parasympathetic fibers — classically a
      **posterior communicating artery aneurysm**, a neurosurgical emergency. Diabetic (ischemic) palsies usually spare
      the pupil.
  - stem: To test the superior oblique in isolation, the patient should look in which direction?
    choices:
      - Up and out
      - Down and out
      - Down and in
      - Up and in
      - Straight laterally
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      With the eye **adducted**, the superior oblique becomes the main **depressor**, so looking **down and in** (toward
      the nose) tests it — the position used for reading or walking downstairs.
  - stem: Which nerve supplies the lateral rectus?
    choices:
      - Oculomotor nerve
      - Trochlear nerve
      - Abducens nerve
      - Ophthalmic nerve
      - Facial nerve
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **abducens nerve (CN VI)** supplies the lateral rectus (LR6). CN IV supplies the superior oblique; CN III
      supplies the rest.
