anatomy:
  location: '**External ear**: auricle and external acoustic meatus (about 2.5 cm, S-shaped). **Middle ear**: tympanic cavity within the petrous temporal bone, between the tympanic membrane and the [[inner-ear]].'
  parts:
    - "**Tympanic membrane**: pearly gray, cone-shaped; the handle of the malleus is attached (umbo at its tip); the **cone of light** points anteroinferiorly; pars flaccida (above) and pars tensa"
    - "**Ossicles**: malleus → incus → stapes (footplate in the oval window); they amplify sound"
    - "**Muscles**: tensor tympani (V3) and **stapedius (CN VII)** — dampen loud sounds (stapedius paralysis → hyperacusis)"
    - "**Pharyngotympanic (Eustachian) tube**: equalizes pressure with the nasopharynx; shorter, wider and more horizontal in children"
    - "**Mastoid antrum and air cells**: posterior communication — route for spread of infection"
    - "Walls: roof (tegmen tympani; middle cranial fossa above); floor (internal jugular vein); medial wall (promontory, oval and round windows, facial canal); posterior (mastoid)"
  bloodSupply: External canal — posterior auricular and superficial temporal arteries; middle ear — tympanic branches of the maxillary and other [[external-carotid-artery]] branches.
  venousDrainage: Pterygoid plexus and superior petrosal sinus.
  lymphatics: Preauricular, mastoid and deep cervical nodes.
  innervation: >-
    External canal and outer drum — auriculotemporal (V3), vagus (Arnold nerve — cough reflex when the canal is
    touched), facial and great auricular nerves. Middle ear mucosa — tympanic branch of **CN IX**. The **chorda
    tympani** (CN VII, taste) crosses the medial surface of the drum.
  relations: The facial nerve runs in its bony canal in the medial and posterior walls — at risk with middle ear disease and surgery.
clinical:
  - title: Acute otitis media
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Eustachian tube dysfunction after a viral URI; *S. pneumoniae*, nontypeable *H. influenzae*, *M. catarrhalis*. Most common in children (short, horizontal tube).
    presentation: >-
      Ear pain, fever, irritability; **bulging, erythematous, opaque tympanic membrane with reduced mobility** on
      pneumatic otoscopy. First-line antibiotic is **high-dose amoxicillin** (amoxicillin-clavulanate if recent
      amoxicillin or conjunctivitis); watchful waiting is an option for some older children with mild disease.
      Complications: perforation, **mastoiditis**, facial palsy, meningitis.
  - title: Otitis externa ("swimmer's ear")
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Infection of the canal skin, usually *Pseudomonas* or *S. aureus*, after moisture or trauma. **Malignant (necrotizing) otitis externa** — *Pseudomonas* osteomyelitis of the skull base in diabetic or immunocompromised older adults.
    presentation: Ear pain with **pain on pulling the auricle or pressing the tragus**, canal edema and discharge; the drum is normal if seen. Treat with topical antibiotic (fluoroquinolone) drops ± steroid; malignant OE needs IV antipseudomonal antibiotics and imaging.
  - title: Otitis media with effusion and cholesteatoma
    highYield: false
    mechanism: '**Effusion**: fluid behind an intact drum after AOM or with Eustachian tube dysfunction. **Cholesteatoma**: keratinizing squamous epithelium in the middle ear eroding bone.'
    presentation: Effusion — conductive hearing loss, dull retracted drum with air-fluid levels; tympanostomy tubes if persistent with hearing loss. Cholesteatoma — painless foul discharge, white mass (often pars flaccida), hearing loss; surgical removal.
pance:
  - "AOM: **bulging** drum, reduced mobility → **high-dose amoxicillin**."
  - "Pain with **tragal pressure or auricle traction** → otitis externa (*Pseudomonas*) → topical drops."
  - "Elderly diabetic with severe ear pain, granulation tissue in the canal → **malignant OE**."
  - "Middle ear disease → **conductive** loss: Weber lateralizes to the affected ear; Rinne BC > AC."
  - "Post-auricular swelling, protruding ear after AOM → **mastoiditis**."
imaging:
  ct: '**Temporal bone CT** for mastoiditis, cholesteatoma, trauma and malignant otitis externa (bone erosion).'
  mri: Intracranial complications (abscess, venous sinus thrombosis); MRI diffusion-weighted imaging for recurrent cholesteatoma.
  keyViews:
    - "CT temporal bones (thin section)"
exam:
  landmarks: Tragus, mastoid process, external acoustic meatus.
  palpation: Pull the auricle and press the tragus (pain = otitis externa); palpate the mastoid for tenderness and swelling.
  testing: >-
    **Otoscopy**: pull the auricle **up and back** in adults (**down and back** in young children). Look for the cone of
    light, handle of malleus, color, position (bulging/retracted), perforation, and use **pneumatic otoscopy** for
    mobility. Hearing: whispered voice, **Weber and Rinne** tuning-fork tests (512 Hz).
  specialTests:
    - name: Rinne test
      technique: Place a vibrating 512-Hz tuning fork on the mastoid (bone conduction), then beside the ear canal (air conduction); ask which is louder or longer.
      positive: '"Negative" Rinne — bone conduction louder than air conduction.'
      significance: Conductive hearing loss in that ear (normal and sensorineural loss show AC > BC).
    - name: Weber test
      technique: Place the vibrating fork on the midline of the forehead or vertex.
      positive: Sound lateralizes to one ear.
      significance: Lateralizes **toward** the ear with **conductive** loss, or **away from** the ear with **sensorineural** loss.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 2-year-old has fever and pulls at his right ear after a cold. Otoscopy shows a bulging, red, opaque tympanic
      membrane that does not move with insufflation. He has no penicillin allergy and no recent antibiotics. What is the
      best treatment?
    choices:
      - High-dose amoxicillin
      - Ciprofloxacin ear drops
      - Azithromycin
      - Tympanostomy tubes now
      - Oral prednisone
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      A **bulging, immobile drum** confirms **acute otitis media**. First-line therapy is **high-dose amoxicillin**.
      Topical fluoroquinolones treat otitis externa; tubes are for recurrent AOM or persistent effusion.
  - stem: >-
      A patient has left-sided hearing loss. Weber lateralizes to the left, and Rinne on the left shows bone conduction
      louder than air conduction. What type of hearing loss is present?
    choices:
      - Left sensorineural
      - Left conductive
      - Right sensorineural
      - Right conductive
      - Normal hearing
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      In **conductive** loss, Weber lateralizes **to** the affected ear and Rinne shows **BC > AC** on that side (e.g.
      cerumen, effusion, otosclerosis). In sensorineural loss, Weber lateralizes to the good ear and AC > BC.
