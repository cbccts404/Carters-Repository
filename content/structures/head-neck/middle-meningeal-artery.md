---
name: Middle meningeal artery
type: artery
region: head-neck
subregion: Skull
taName: Arteria meningea media
summary: Branch of the maxillary artery that enters the skull through the foramen spinosum to supply the dura and skull. Tearing of its anterior branch under the pterion causes an epidural hematoma.
tags: [skull, trauma, emergency]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Between the dura and the inner surface of the skull (extradural), in grooves on the temporal and parietal bones.
  origin: First part of the **maxillary artery** (a terminal branch of the [[external-carotid-artery]]), in the infratemporal fossa.
  course: >-
    Passes between the two roots of the auriculotemporal nerve, enters the middle cranial fossa through the **foramen
    spinosum**, and divides into **anterior (frontal)** and **posterior (parietal)** branches. The anterior branch runs
    deep to the **pterion**.
  branches:
    - "**Anterior (frontal) branch**: crosses deep to the pterion toward the vertex"
    - "**Posterior (parietal) branch**"
    - "Small branches to the trigeminal ganglion, facial nerve and tympanic cavity"
  supplies: The dura mater and the bones of the cranial vault (not the brain).
  anastomoses: With the ophthalmic artery (via the orbit), and with other meningeal arteries.
  termination: Ends in the dura over the vault.
clinical:
  - title: Epidural (extradural) hematoma
    highYield: true
    mechanism: A temporal/parietal skull fracture (often at the **pterion**) tears the middle meningeal artery; arterial blood strips the dura from the skull.
    presentation: >-
      Classic but inconsistent **lucid interval** after brief loss of consciousness, then headache, vomiting,
      decreasing consciousness, **ipsilateral fixed dilated pupil** (uncal herniation compressing CN III) and
      contralateral hemiparesis. CT: **biconvex (lentiform) hyperdensity** that **does not cross suture lines**.
      Treatment: urgent **craniotomy** and evacuation.
pance:
  - "Epidural = **arterial** (middle meningeal), **biconvex**, doesn't cross sutures, lucid interval."
  - "Subdural = **venous** (bridging veins), **crescent**, crosses sutures, elderly/alcohol use/anticoagulants."
  - "Middle meningeal artery enters through the **foramen spinosum**."
imaging:
  ct: '**Non-contrast head CT**: biconvex hyperdense extra-axial collection limited by sutures, often with an overlying fracture; "swirl sign" suggests active bleeding.'
  xray: Conventional angiography occasionally for traumatic pseudoaneurysm; embolization of the middle meningeal artery is also used to treat chronic subdural hematomas. {{verify}}
  keyViews:
    - "Non-contrast head CT (brain and bone windows)"
exam:
  landmarks: The pterion lies about 3–4 cm above the midpoint of the zygomatic arch (two finger-breadths above the arch and a thumb's breadth behind the frontal process of the zygomatic bone).
  testing: Serial Glasgow Coma Scale and pupil checks after head injury; a newly dilated pupil or falling GCS demands immediate CT and neurosurgery.
quiz:
  - stem: The middle meningeal artery is a branch of which artery?
    choices:
      - Internal carotid artery

    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The middle meningeal artery arises from the first part of the **maxillary artery** (a terminal branch of the external
      carotid) and enters the skull through the foramen spinosum.
  - stem: Which CT finding best distinguishes an epidural from a subdural hematoma?
    choices:
      - Hyperdensity
      - Biconvex shape that does not cross suture lines
      - Presence of midline shift
      - Crescent shape crossing suture lines
      - Location in the posterior fossa
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Epidural hematomas are **biconvex** and limited by **suture lines**, where the dura is firmly attached. Subdural
      hematomas are crescent-shaped and cross sutures (but not the midline falx).
