---
name: Cervical plexus
type: nerve
region: head-neck
subregion: Neck
taName: Plexus cervicalis
aka: [Ansa cervicalis, Great auricular nerve, Lesser occipital nerve, Supraclavicular nerves]
summary: Network of the C1–C4 ventral rami deep to sternocleidomastoid. It gives cutaneous branches to the neck and ear, the ansa cervicalis to the strap muscles, and most importantly the phrenic nerve (C3–C5) to the diaphragm.
tags: [neck, nerve-block, diaphragm]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  roots: C1–C4 ventral rami (phrenic nerve C3–C5)
  origin: Ventral rami of **C1–C4**, forming loops in front of levator scapulae and middle scalene, deep to [[sternocleidomastoid]] and the internal jugular vein.
  course: >-
    The **cutaneous branches** emerge around the **midpoint of the posterior border of sternocleidomastoid** (the
    **nerve point of the neck**, Erb point) and fan out over the neck, ear, and shoulder. Motor branches pass to deep
    neck muscles and, via the **ansa cervicalis** on the carotid sheath, to the infrahyoid muscles.
  branches:
    - "**Lesser occipital** (C2) — skin behind the ear"
    - "**Great auricular** (C2–C3) — skin over the parotid, angle of the jaw and auricle (at risk in parotid surgery; see [[parotid-gland]])"
    - "**Transverse cervical** (C2–C3) — skin of the anterior neck"
    - "**Supraclavicular nerves** (C3–C4) — skin over the clavicle and top of the shoulder (referred shoulder pain)"
    - "**Ansa cervicalis** (C1–C3) — superior root (C1, running with the hypoglossal nerve) and inferior root (C2–C3); supplies the **infrahyoid (strap) muscles** — sternohyoid, sternothyroid, omohyoid (C1 fibers via CN XII also reach thyrohyoid and geniohyoid)"
    - "**[[phrenic-nerve]]** (C3–C5) — **motor to the diaphragm**; sensory to the central diaphragm, pericardium and mediastinal pleura"
    - "Branches to SCM and trapezius (C2–C4, proprioceptive), prevertebral muscles, levator scapulae and scalenes"
  motor: Infrahyoid muscles (ansa cervicalis), the **diaphragm** (phrenic), prevertebral and scalene muscles.
  sensory: Skin of the anterolateral neck, the angle of the jaw and parotid region, the lower auricle and back of the ear, and over the clavicle and shoulder. (The face above is trigeminal; the back of the scalp is the [[greater-occipital-nerve]], a dorsal ramus.)
  relations: Lies on the prevertebral fascia deep to the carotid sheath; the phrenic nerve descends on the **anterior scalene**. The spinal accessory nerve emerges near the nerve point of the neck.
  notes: '"**C3, 4, 5 keeps the diaphragm alive.**"'
clinical:
  - title: Phrenic nerve and high cervical spinal cord injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Cord injury at or above C3–C5** disrupts phrenic output.'
    presentation: >-
      Injury **above C3** → **apnea and ventilator dependence**; C4–C5 → weakened diaphragmatic breathing. Unilateral
      phrenic palsy (surgery, tumor, interscalene block) → **elevated hemidiaphragm** on chest X-ray and **paradoxical
      movement on fluoroscopic sniff test**.
  - title: Referred shoulder pain
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Diaphragmatic or peritoneal irritation (phrenic, C3–C5) is felt in the **C3–C4 dermatome** — the supraclavicular nerve territory over the shoulder.
    presentation: '**Kehr sign** (left shoulder pain with splenic rupture), right shoulder pain with gallbladder disease or subphrenic abscess, shoulder pain after laparoscopy.'
  - title: Superficial cervical plexus block and nerve injury
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Local anesthetic injected at the midpoint of the posterior border of SCM anesthetizes the cutaneous branches (carotid, thyroid, clavicle surgery). **Interscalene brachial plexus blocks** almost always cause temporary ipsilateral phrenic palsy.
    presentation: Great auricular nerve injury after parotidectomy or facelift → numbness of the earlobe and angle of the jaw.
pance:
  - "\"**C3, 4, 5 keeps the diaphragm alive**\" — phrenic nerve."
  - "Spinal cord injury **above C3** → ventilator dependence."
  - "Shoulder-tip pain from diaphragm irritation = **referred via phrenic (C3–C5)** → Kehr sign."
  - "Elevated hemidiaphragm → phrenic nerve palsy → **sniff test**."
  - "Ansa cervicalis → **strap muscles**."
imaging:
  xray: Chest X-ray shows an **elevated hemidiaphragm** in phrenic palsy.
  ultrasound: Diaphragm ultrasound (or fluoroscopic **sniff test**) shows reduced or paradoxical movement; ultrasound also guides cervical plexus blocks.
  mri: Cervical spinal cord injury level.
exam:
  landmarks: '**Nerve point of the neck** — midpoint of the posterior border of SCM.'
  testing: Sensation over the angle of the jaw and earlobe (great auricular, C2–C3), the anterior neck and over the clavicle (C3–C4); watch diaphragmatic breathing and look for paradoxical abdominal movement in suspected phrenic palsy.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 22-year-old has a spinal cord transection at C2 after a diving accident. Which consequence is most expected?
    choices:
      - Isolated loss of the biceps reflex
      - Apnea requiring mechanical ventilation
      - Weakness limited to the hand muscles
      - Loss of the Achilles reflex only
      - Horner syndrome only
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **phrenic nerve** arises from **C3–C5**. A complete lesion at C2 cuts off descending respiratory drive to the
      phrenic motor neurons, causing **apnea** and ventilator dependence.
  - stem: >-
      Twelve hours after laparoscopic cholecystectomy, a patient has pain at the tip of the right shoulder. Which nerve
      roots explain this referred pain?
    choices:
      - C5–C6
      - C3–C5
      - T7–T9
      - L1–L2
      - C7–C8
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Irritation of the diaphragm (by residual CO₂ or blood) is carried by the **phrenic nerve (C3–C5)** and felt in the
      **C3–C4** dermatome over the shoulder (supraclavicular nerves).
