summary: Bundle of lumbar and sacral nerve roots descending in the lumbar cistern below the end of the spinal cord. Compression causes cauda equina syndrome, a surgical emergency.
tags: [spine, neuro, emergency]
systems: [neurologic, musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Lumbar cistern (subarachnoid space) from the conus medullaris (L1–L2) to the end of the dural sac (S2), and beyond in the sacral canal.
  roots: Dorsal and ventral roots of **L2–Co1**, descending to their exit foramina.
  origin: Lumbar, sacral and coccygeal segments of the conus medullaris.
  course: The roots descend steeply within the dural sac, each leaving through the intervertebral foramen below its numbered vertebra (the sacral roots through the sacral foramina).
  motor: Lower limb muscles (via the lumbar and sacral plexuses), external anal and urethral sphincters (S2–S4), pelvic floor.
  sensory: Lower limbs, **perineum and saddle area (S2–S5)**, bladder and rectum.
  relations: Floats in CSF, which lets it move aside from an LP needle. Surrounded by the [[spinal-meninges]]; the filum terminale runs among the roots.
clinical:
  - title: Cauda equina syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Compression of multiple roots, most often by a **large central lumbar disc herniation (L4–L5 or L5–S1)**; also
      tumor, epidural abscess or hematoma, spinal stenosis, or trauma.
    presentation: >-
      Low back pain with **bilateral sciatica** (sometimes unilateral), **saddle anesthesia**, **urinary retention**
      (with overflow incontinence), fecal incontinence, **reduced anal tone**, sexual dysfunction, and **lower motor
      neuron** leg weakness with reduced reflexes. **Emergency MRI and surgical decompression**: delay worsens bladder
      and neurologic outcomes.
  - title: Conus medullaris syndrome (for comparison)
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Lesion at the conus (around L1–L2), e.g. fracture, tumor or disc.
    presentation: More sudden and symmetric, with **early** bladder, bowel and sexual dysfunction and perianal numbness. Leg findings can be mixed UMN/LMN (e.g. brisk knee jerk with absent ankle jerk).
pance:
  - "**Saddle anesthesia + urinary retention + bilateral sciatica + reduced anal tone** → cauda equina syndrome → **emergency MRI + decompression**."
  - "Cauda equina = **lower motor neuron** signs (reduced reflexes, flaccid weakness)."
  - "Check **post-void residual** (retention) and perianal sensation in any back pain with red flags."
imaging:
  mri: '**Emergency MRI** of the lumbosacral spine is the test of choice; CT myelography if MRI is unavailable or contraindicated.'
  ultrasound: Bladder scan measures **post-void residual** (large residual volumes support retention).
  keyViews:
    - "Urgent MRI lumbar spine"
    - "Bladder scan / post-void residual"
exam:
  landmarks: The cauda equina lies deep to the L2–S2 spinous processes.
  testing: >-
    **Perianal (saddle) sensation** to pinprick, **anal tone** and voluntary squeeze on digital rectal exam, the anal
    wink (S2–S4) and bulbocavernosus reflexes, lower limb strength and reflexes, and post-void residual.

      Measuring the **post-void residual** (by bladder scan or catheter) detects **urinary retention**, a key feature of

  - stem: >-

    choices:

      - S1 nerve root only
      - Cervical spinal cord
      - Conus medullaris
      - Common fibular nerves
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      A **conus medullaris** lesion (around L1–L2) gives **early**, symmetric bladder, bowel and sexual dysfunction with
      perianal numbness and **mixed UMN/LMN** leg findings. Cauda equina lesions produce purely lower motor neuron signs
      and are often asymmetric.
