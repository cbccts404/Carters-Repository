name: Atlas and axis (C1 and C2)
type: bone
region: back
subregion: Vertebral column
taName: Atlas et axis
aka: [Atlas, Axis, C1, C2, Dens, Odontoid process]
summary: The first two cervical vertebrae, specialized for head movement. The atlas (C1) has no body; the axis (C2) carries the dens, around which the atlas rotates. Their fractures and instability are high-yield.
tags: [spine, neck, trauma]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Upper neck, between the occiput and C3.
  classification: Atypical cervical vertebrae.
  features:
    - "**Atlas (C1)**: ring of **anterior and posterior arches** with two **lateral masses**; **no body and no spinous process**. Superior articular facets receive the occipital condyles; a groove on the posterior arch carries the [[vertebral-artery]]"
    - "**Axis (C2)**: strong body projecting upward as the **dens (odontoid process)**, the embryologic body of C1. Large spinous process (first palpable below the occiput)"
    - "**Transverse ligament of the atlas** holds the dens against the anterior arch; alar and apical ligaments tie the dens to the skull; the tectorial membrane continues the posterior longitudinal ligament"
  relations: >-
    **Atlanto-occipital joints**: flexion and extension (nodding "yes"). **Atlantoaxial joints**: rotation (shaking
    "no"); they provide roughly half of cervical rotation. The spinal cord and medulla lie just behind the dens.
clinical:
  - title: Jefferson fracture (C1 burst)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Axial loading** onto the top of the head (e.g. diving into shallow water) drives the occipital condyles into the lateral masses, breaking the ring in several places.'
    presentation: Upper neck pain, often without neurologic deficit (the canal widens). On the open-mouth view, the lateral masses overhang C2. Stability depends on the transverse ligament.
  - title: Odontoid (dens) fractures
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Falls in older adults (hyperextension or flexion) or high-energy trauma in younger patients.
    presentation: >-
      Upper neck pain, sometimes minimal findings in older adults. Classified by level; **type II (base of the dens)** is
      the most common and has a high risk of **nonunion** (poor blood supply).
  - title: Hangman fracture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Bilateral fractures of the **pars interarticularis/pedicles of C2** from **hyperextension** with distraction (judicial hanging historically; now usually vehicle collisions).
    presentation: Neck pain; traumatic spondylolisthesis of C2 on C3. Neurologic injury is less common because the canal widens.
  - title: Atlantoaxial instability
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Laxity or erosion of the transverse ligament, as in **Down syndrome** and **rheumatoid arthritis** (pannus).
    presentation: >-
      Neck pain, occipital headache, or myelopathy. Risk of cord injury during neck manipulation or **intubation**. Patients
      with long-standing RA may need **flexion–extension films** before surgery. An increased **atlantodental interval**
      on lateral films suggests instability. {{verify: normal ADI limits — about 3 mm adults, 5 mm children}}
pance:
  - "**Jefferson** = C1 burst from **axial load** (diving)."
  - "**Hangman** = C2 pars/pedicle fractures from **hyperextension**."
  - "Odontoid **type II** fracture is most common and prone to nonunion."
  - "**RA and Down syndrome** → atlantoaxial instability (transverse ligament); caution with intubation."
  - "Atlanto-occipital = \"yes\" (nodding); atlantoaxial = \"no\" (rotation)."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    **Open-mouth (odontoid) AP view** shows the dens and lateral masses of C1 (overhang in Jefferson fracture). Lateral
    view: atlantodental interval, prevertebral swelling. Flexion–extension views for instability (only in stable, alert
    patients).
  ct: '**CT** is the test of choice for suspected C1–C2 fractures.'
  mri: Transverse ligament integrity, cord compression, soft-tissue injury.
  keyViews:
    - "Open-mouth odontoid view"
    - "CT cervical spine with sagittal and coronal reformats"
exam:
  landmarks: C2 spinous process is the first bony prominence palpable in the midline below the external occipital protuberance. The transverse process of C1 can be felt between the mastoid process and the angle of the mandible.
  testing: Neck rotation (mostly atlantoaxial) and nodding (atlanto-occipital) — only once fracture is excluded in trauma. Full neurologic exam for myelopathy.
