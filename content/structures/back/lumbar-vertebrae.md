region: back

taName: Vertebrae lumbales
aka: [L-spine, Pars interarticularis]
summary: Five large weight-bearing vertebrae of the lower back. Spondylolysis, spondylolisthesis and lumbar spinal stenosis are their key clinical problems.
tags: [spine, low-back-pain]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Lower back, L1–L5, between T12 and the sacrum.
  classification: Irregular bones.
  features:
    - "Large, **kidney-shaped bodies** (the heaviest load)"
    - "Short, thick, **hatchet-shaped spinous processes** (easy to palpate; interspaces widen in flexion for lumbar puncture)"
    - "Articular facets oriented roughly **sagittally**, allowing flexion and extension but little rotation"
    - "Mammillary and accessory processes (muscle attachments)"
    - "**Pars interarticularis**: the bone between the superior and inferior articular processes, the site of stress fractures"
    - "Triangular vertebral foramen containing the conus medullaris (L1) and [[cauda-equina]]"
  relations: >-
    Lumbar nerve roots exit **below** the pedicle of the same-numbered vertebra (L4 exits through the L4–L5 foramen). The
    [[psoas-major]] arises from the sides of the bodies; the [[abdominal-aorta]] and [[inferior-vena-cava]] lie anteriorly.
clinical:
  - title: Spondylolysis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Stress fracture of the pars interarticularis**, most often at **L5**, from repetitive hyperextension in adolescent athletes (gymnastics, football linemen, diving, dance).'
    presentation: >-
      Low back pain in an adolescent athlete, **worse with extension**. Positive single-leg hyperextension (stork) test.
      On oblique X-ray, a "collar" on the **"Scottie dog"**.
  - title: Spondylolisthesis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Forward slip of one vertebra on the one below. **Isthmic** (from bilateral spondylolysis, typically **L5 on S1**) in
      younger patients; **degenerative** (facet arthropathy, typically **L4 on L5**) in older adults, especially women.
    presentation: Low back pain, hamstring tightness, radicular symptoms, and a palpable **step-off** of the spinous processes. Graded by percentage of slip on a lateral X-ray.
  - title: Lumbar spinal stenosis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Narrowing of the central canal, lateral recesses or foramina from disc bulging, facet hypertrophy and ligamentum flavum thickening, usually in adults over 50.
    presentation: >-
      **Neurogenic claudication**: buttock and leg pain, numbness or weakness on **walking and standing (extension)**,
      **relieved by sitting or bending forward** (the "shopping cart sign": leaning on a cart helps). Pedal pulses are
      normal (unlike vascular claudication).
pance:
  - "Adolescent athlete + back pain with **extension** → **spondylolysis** (L5 pars); Scottie dog collar."
  - "**Spondylolisthesis**: step-off; isthmic L5–S1 (young), degenerative L4–L5 (older)."
  - "**Spinal stenosis**: pain with walking/standing, **relief with flexion/sitting**; MRI; normal pulses."
  - "Lumbar roots exit **below** their same-numbered pedicle."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    Lateral view grades **spondylolisthesis**; oblique views show the pars (the "Scottie dog": its neck is the pars, so a
    lucent "collar" indicates spondylolysis). Flexion–extension views show instability. X-rays aren't needed for acute
    nonspecific low back pain without red flags.
  ct: Best to confirm pars fractures and assess healing.
  mri: >-
    First choice for **spinal stenosis** and radiculopathy (canal and foraminal narrowing, nerve root compression); STIR
    shows early pars stress reaction.
  keyViews:
    - "Standing lateral lumbar radiograph (spondylolisthesis)"
    - "MRI lumbar spine (stenosis, radiculopathy with red flags or persistent symptoms)"
exam:
  landmarks: The iliac crests mark about **L4**; the L5 spinous process lies just above the sacrum.
  palpation: Step-off between spinous processes (spondylolisthesis); paraspinal tenderness.
  testing: Lumbar range of motion (pain with extension suggests posterior element pathology; with flexion, disc). Lower limb neurologic exam and pulses (neurogenic vs vascular claudication).
  specialTests:
    - name: Single-leg hyperextension (stork) test
      technique: The patient stands on one leg and extends the lumbar spine (± rotation toward the stance side).
      positive: Reproduction of low back pain on the stance side.
      significance: Suggests spondylolysis (pars stress injury), though it isn't specific.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 15-year-old gymnast has 2 months of low back pain that worsens with back walkovers and standing on one leg while
      arching her back. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Lumbar disc herniation
      - Spondylolysis
      - Lumbar spinal stenosis
      - Ankylosing spondylitis
      - Vertebral osteomyelitis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Spondylolysis** (a pars interarticularis stress fracture, usually L5) is typical in adolescents doing repetitive
      **hyperextension**. The stork test reproduces the pain.
  - stem: >-
      A 72-year-old man has buttock and leg pain after walking two blocks that improves when he sits or leans forward on a
      shopping cart. Pedal pulses are normal. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Peripheral arterial disease
      - Lumbar spinal stenosis
      - Hip osteoarthritis
      - Piriformis syndrome
      - Diabetic neuropathy
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Neurogenic claudication** relieved by **flexion** (sitting, leaning forward) with **normal pulses** is classic for
      **lumbar spinal stenosis**. Vascular claudication is relieved by standing still and has reduced pulses.
  - stem: On an oblique lumbar radiograph, a lucent defect across the "neck of the Scottie dog" represents a defect in which structure?
    choices:
      - Pedicle
      - Lamina
      - Pars interarticularis
      - Transverse process
      - Vertebral body
    answer: C
    explanation: The Scottie dog's **neck is the pars interarticularis**; a lucent "collar" indicates **spondylolysis**.
flashcards:
  - front: Spondylolysis vs spondylolisthesis?
    back: "Spondylolysis: pars defect (stress fracture). Spondylolisthesis: forward slip of a vertebra"
  - front: Neurogenic vs vascular claudication?
    back: "Neurogenic: relieved by flexion/sitting, normal pulses. Vascular: relieved by standing still, reduced pulses"
  - front: Lumbar root exit rule?
    back: Each lumbar root exits below the pedicle of its own vertebra (L4 at the L4–L5 foramen)
