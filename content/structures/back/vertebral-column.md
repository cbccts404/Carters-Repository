subregion: Vertebral column
taName: Columna vertebralis
aka: [Spine, Spinal column]
summary: Column of 33 vertebrae (7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 fused sacral, about 4 coccygeal) with four curvatures. It supports the trunk, protects the spinal cord, and is the site of scoliosis, kyphosis and inflammatory back disease.
tags: [spine, landmarks]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Posterior midline of the neck and trunk, from the skull to the coccyx.
  classification: Irregular bones joined by intervertebral discs and synovial facet joints.
  features:
    - "**Regions**: [[cervical-vertebrae|cervical]] (7), [[thoracic-vertebrae|thoracic]] (12), [[lumbar-vertebrae|lumbar]] (5), [[sacrum]] (5 fused), coccyx (usually 4 fused, range 3–5)"
    - "**Typical vertebra**: body (anterior, weight-bearing), vertebral arch (pedicles + laminae) enclosing the vertebral foramen, spinous process, two transverse processes, four articular processes; the **pars interarticularis** lies between the superior and inferior articular processes"
    - "**Curvatures**: **primary** (kyphoses, concave anteriorly, present at birth) in the thoracic and sacral regions; **secondary** (lordoses) in the cervical (head control) and lumbar (walking) regions"
    - "**Joints**: [[intervertebral-disc|intervertebral discs]] between bodies; [[zygapophysial-joints|zygapophysial (facet) joints]] between articular processes; supported by the [[vertebral-ligaments]]"
  relations: The [[spinal-cord]] and [[spinal-meninges|meninges]] occupy the vertebral canal; spinal nerves exit through the intervertebral foramina. The [[erector-spinae]] and deep back muscles lie in the grooves beside the spinous processes.
  notes: >-
    **Surface landmarks (patient standing, arms at sides)**: **C7** (vertebra prominens) is the first clearly palpable
    spinous process at the base of the neck; the medial end of the scapular spine is at about **T3**; the inferior angle
    of the scapula at about **T7**; the line joining the top of the iliac crests (**Tuffier/intercristal line**) crosses
    the **L4 spinous process or L4–L5 interspace**; the dimples over the **posterior superior iliac spines** mark
    **S2**. {{verify: surface landmark levels vary between individuals}}
clinical:
  - title: Scoliosis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Lateral curvature of the spine with vertebral rotation. **Adolescent idiopathic scoliosis** is most common and
      more likely to progress in girls, especially during growth spurts. Others are congenital, neuromuscular or
      degenerative.
    presentation: >-
      Shoulder or scapular asymmetry, uneven waist, and a **rib hump on forward bending** (Adams test). Usually painless;
      significant pain or neurologic signs suggest another cause. Measured by the **Cobb angle** on standing X-ray (10° or
      more defines scoliosis). Bracing is used for moderate curves in growing children; large curves may need surgery.
      {{verify: bracing and surgical thresholds}}
  - title: Hyperkyphosis
    highYield: false
    mechanism: '**Scheuermann disease** (wedging of adjacent thoracic vertebrae in adolescents) and **osteoporotic compression fractures** in older adults.'
    presentation: Rounded back that doesn't correct fully on extension (Scheuermann), or progressive height loss and "dowager's hump" (osteoporosis).
  - title: Ankylosing spondylitis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: HLA-B27–associated seronegative spondyloarthropathy, starting in the sacroiliac joints and ascending the spine.
    presentation: >-
      **Inflammatory back pain** in a young adult (usually under 40): morning stiffness lasting more than 30 minutes,
      **improves with exercise**, not with rest; night pain. Reduced lumbar flexion (**Schober test**), reduced chest
      expansion, anterior uveitis. X-ray: sacroiliitis, later **"bamboo spine"** from syndesmophytes.

imaging:
  xray: >-
    Standing **PA and lateral full-spine** films for scoliosis (Cobb angle) and sagittal balance. Lateral views show
    vertebral height loss, spondylolisthesis and syndesmophytes.
  ct: Best for bony detail in trauma (fractures, alignment) and surgical planning.
  mri: Best for discs, spinal cord, nerve roots, marrow (infection, metastases, acute vs old fractures), and early sacroiliitis.
  keyViews:
    - "Standing full-spine PA and lateral (scoliosis)"
    - "CT for trauma; MRI for neurologic symptoms, infection, malignancy"
exam:
  landmarks: C7 vertebra prominens, spinous processes, scapular landmarks (T3, T7), iliac crests (L4), PSIS dimples (S2).
  palpation: Palpate spinous processes and paraspinal muscles for tenderness, step-offs and spasm; percuss for point tenderness (fracture, infection, malignancy).
  testing: Range of motion (flexion, extension, lateral bending, rotation); gait; neurologic exam of the limbs.
  specialTests:
    - name: Adams forward bend test
      technique: The patient bends forward at the waist with arms hanging and knees straight; the examiner views the back from behind (and from the side).
      positive: A rib or lumbar prominence (hump) on one side.
      significance: Structural scoliosis (the rotational component). A scoliometer quantifies the rotation.
    - name: Modified Schober test
      technique: Mark the skin at the level of the PSIS dimples and 10 cm above in the midline (some versions add 5 cm below); the patient bends forward maximally and the distance is re-measured.
      positive: An increase of less than about 5 cm. {{verify}}
      significance: Reduced lumbar flexion, e.g. ankylosing spondylitis.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 13-year-old girl has uneven shoulders noticed by her mother. When she bends forward, the right side of her back
      is higher than the left. She has no pain. What is the next step?
    choices:
      - MRI of the spine
      - Standing posteroanterior and lateral spine radiographs
      - Reassurance without imaging
      - Bone scan
      - CT of the thoracic spine
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A positive **Adams forward bend test** suggests structural **scoliosis**. Standing full-spine radiographs measure the
      **Cobb angle** and skeletal maturity. MRI is reserved for atypical features (pain, neurologic signs, left thoracic curve).
  - stem: >-
      A 24-year-old man has 6 months of low back pain and 1 hour of morning stiffness that improves with activity. He has
      reduced lumbar flexion and a history of red painful eye. Which finding is most likely on imaging?
    choices:
      - Osteophytes at L4–L5 only
      - Sacroiliitis
      - Vertebral compression fracture
      - Disc herniation at L5–S1
      - Spondylolysis of L5
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Inflammatory back pain with uveitis suggests **ankylosing spondylitis**, which begins with **sacroiliitis**. Later
      films may show a bamboo spine.
  - stem: Which vertebral level corresponds to a line joining the highest points of the iliac crests?
    choices:
      - T12
      - L1
      - L2–L3
      - L4 or L4–L5 interspace
      - S2
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      The **intercristal (Tuffier) line** crosses the **L4 spinous process or the L4–L5 interspace**, the landmark for
      lumbar puncture below the end of the spinal cord.
