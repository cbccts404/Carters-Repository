name: Suboccipital triangle
type: space
region: back
subregion: Suboccipital region
taName: Trigonum suboccipitale
summary: Small triangle deep in the back of the neck, bounded by three suboccipital muscles. It contains the vertebral artery and the suboccipital nerve, and the greater occipital nerve emerges just below it.
tags: [neck, suboccipital]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Deep posterior neck just below the occiput, beneath trapezius and semispinalis capitis.
  boundaries:
    superomedial: Rectus capitis posterior major
    superolateral: Obliquus capitis superior
    inferolateral: Obliquus capitis inferior
    roof: Semispinalis capitis (deep to trapezius and splenius capitis)
    floor: Posterior atlanto-occipital membrane and posterior arch of the atlas (C1)
  contents:
    - "[[vertebral-artery]] (3rd part, V3), running in the groove on the posterior arch of the atlas"
    - "**Suboccipital nerve** (dorsal ramus of **C1**): motor to the suboccipital muscles; usually no cutaneous branch"
  communications: The vertebral artery passes from the triangle through the posterior atlanto-occipital membrane into the vertebral canal and foramen magnum.
  notes: >-
    The **[[greater-occipital-nerve]] (dorsal ramus of C2)** emerges below obliquus capitis inferior and ascends
    superficial to the triangle; it is not a content of the triangle itself. The suboccipital muscles (rectus capitis
    posterior major and minor, obliquus superior and inferior) fine-tune head posture and are rich in proprioceptors.
clinical:
  - title: Vertebral artery exposure and injury
    highYield: false
    mechanism: The artery lies relatively superficially on the posterior arch of C1 here; it is at risk in posterior C1–C2 surgery and in some upper cervical injuries.
    presentation: Bleeding at surgery or posterior circulation ischemia (see [[vertebral-artery]]).
  - title: Cervicogenic headache
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Pain referred from upper cervical structures (C1–C3 joints and muscles) through convergence with the trigeminal nucleus.
    presentation: Unilateral occipital headache spreading forward, provoked by neck movement or pressure on the suboccipital region, with reduced neck range of motion.
pance:
  - "Suboccipital triangle contents: **vertebral artery (V3) + suboccipital nerve (C1)**."
  - "Greater occipital nerve (C2) emerges **below obliquus capitis inferior** → occipital neuralgia."
imaging:
  ct: CT angiography shows the V3 segment looping over the posterior arch of C1.
  mri: Suboccipital soft tissues, craniovertebral junction (e.g. Chiari malformation).
  ultrasound: Used to guide greater occipital nerve blocks at the level of obliquus capitis inferior.
  keyViews:
    - "CT angiography of the neck (vertebral artery)"
exam:
  landmarks: Just below the external occipital protuberance and superior nuchal line, deep to the palpable trapezius and semispinalis.
  palpation: Deep suboccipital tenderness in cervicogenic headache; tenderness over the greater occipital nerve (about a third of the way from the occipital protuberance to the mastoid) in occipital neuralgia.
