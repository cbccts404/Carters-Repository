---
name: Vertebral venous plexus
type: vein
region: back
subregion: Spinal cord & meninges
taName: Plexus venosi vertebrales
aka: [Batson plexus]
summary: Valveless network of veins inside and around the vertebral column that connects the pelvic, abdominal, thoracic and intracranial veins. It explains how prostate and breast cancer spread to the spine.
tags: [spine, vascular, oncology]
systems: [cardiovascular]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: '**Internal** plexus in the epidural space; **external** plexus around the outside of the vertebrae.'
  formation: Basivertebral veins from the vertebral bodies, spinal veins, and segmental veins.
  course: Extends from the sacrum to the skull, communicating with the dural venous sinuses through the foramen magnum.
  tributaries:
    - Basivertebral veins (from the vertebral bodies)
    - Veins of the spinal cord
    - Intervertebral veins
  drainage: Intervertebral veins → vertebral, posterior intercostal ([[azygos-vein]] system), lumbar and lateral sacral veins → caval systems.
  relations: The internal plexus lies in the epidural fat within the [[spinal-meninges|epidural space]].
  notes: The plexus has **no functional valves**, so flow can reverse with changes in intra-abdominal or intrathoracic pressure (coughing, straining, Valsalva). It provides a route that **bypasses the lungs and liver**.
clinical:
  - title: Vertebral metastases
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Tumor cells from the pelvis and trunk (classically **prostate**, also breast, lung, kidney) travel through the valveless plexus to the vertebral bodies and even the brain without passing through the lungs.
    presentation: >-
      Back pain from spinal metastases; **prostate cancer** metastases to the lumbar spine and pelvis are typically
      **osteoblastic** (sclerotic). Risk of spinal cord compression.
  - title: Spread of infection
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Pelvic or urinary infections can seed the vertebrae and disc through the plexus (vertebral osteomyelitis/discitis).
    presentation: Back pain, fever and elevated inflammatory markers; MRI shows discitis/osteomyelitis.
  - title: Epidural bleeding
    highYield: false
    mechanism: The thin-walled internal plexus can bleed during epidural catheter placement, especially with anticoagulation.
    presentation: Blood in the epidural needle or catheter; rarely a spinal epidural hematoma with cord compression.
pance:
  - "**Batson plexus** (valveless) → **prostate cancer** metastases to the **lumbar spine** (osteoblastic)."
  - "Pathway bypasses the lungs, allowing spread from pelvis to spine and brain."
imaging:
  xray: Osteoblastic (sclerotic, dense) lesions in prostate cancer; osteolytic lesions in many other cancers.
  ct: Characterizes blastic vs lytic lesions and fracture risk.
  mri: Most sensitive for marrow metastases and epidural extension.
  keyViews:
    - "Bone scan or PET (e.g. PSMA PET in prostate cancer) for skeletal staging"
exam:
  testing: Percussion tenderness over the spine; neurologic exam for cord compression; digital rectal exam in men with new spinal lesions of unknown primary.
