---
name: Rhomboid major and minor
type: muscle
region: back
subregion: Muscles of the back
taName: Musculi rhomboidei major et minor
aka: [Rhomboids, Dorsal scapular nerve]
summary: Two flat muscles between the upper thoracic spine and the medial border of the scapula that retract the scapula. They are supplied by the dorsal scapular nerve (C5).
tags: [back, shoulder]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Deep to trapezius, between the vertebral column and the medial border of the scapula.
  group: Superficial extrinsic back muscles (deep to trapezius), with levator scapulae.
  origin: '**Minor**: nuchal ligament and spinous processes of C7–T1. **Major**: spinous processes of T2–T5.'
  insertion: 'Medial border of the [[scapula]]: minor at the level of the scapular spine; major from the spine to the inferior angle.'
  innervation: '**Dorsal scapular nerve** (C5, often with C4), from the C5 root of the [[brachial-plexus]].'
  bloodSupply: Dorsal scapular artery (or the deep branch of the transverse cervical artery) and posterior intercostal branches.
  action:
    - '**Retract** the scapula (squeeze the shoulder blades together)'
    - '**Rotate the glenoid downward**; hold the scapula against the thoracic wall'
  relations: The dorsal scapular nerve pierces the middle scalene and descends deep to [[levator-scapulae]] and the rhomboids with the dorsal scapular artery.
clinical:
  - title: Dorsal scapular nerve injury or entrapment
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Entrapment in the middle scalene, or injury in the neck.
    presentation: Aching along the medial border of the scapula, weak retraction, and mild winging of the medial border (less marked than with serratus paralysis).
  - title: Interscapular (rhomboid) muscle pain
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Postural strain and overuse. Interscapular pain can also be referred from cervical disease, gallbladder, or thoracic aortic pathology.
    presentation: Aching between the shoulder blades with tender trigger points; always consider referred causes.
pance:
  - "Rhomboids: **retraction**; **dorsal scapular nerve (C5)** from the root."
  - "Scapular winging: medial (long thoracic/serratus) vs lateral (CN XI/trapezius); rhomboid weakness gives only mild medial-border prominence."
imaging:
  mri: Muscle atrophy with chronic denervation.
  keyViews:
    - "EMG for dorsal scapular neuropathy"
exam:
  landmarks: Between the medial borders of the scapulae and the spinous processes of C7–T5.
  testing: Resisted retraction — hands on hips, the patient pushes the elbows backward against resistance while the examiner palpates the rhomboids medial to the scapula.
quiz:
  - stem: Which nerve supplies the rhomboid muscles?
    choices:
      - Long thoracic nerve
      - Dorsal scapular nerve
      - Spinal accessory nerve
      - Thoracodorsal nerve
      - Suprascapular nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: The **dorsal scapular nerve** (C5), arising directly from the C5 root, supplies both rhomboids and levator scapulae (with C3–C4).
  - stem: What is the main action of the rhomboids?
    choices:
      - Protraction of the scapula
      - Retraction of the scapula
      - Upward rotation of the glenoid
      - Abduction of the arm
      - Elevation of the rib cage
    answer: B
    explanation: The rhomboids **retract** the scapula and rotate the glenoid downward. Serratus anterior protracts and upwardly rotates.
  - stem: Rhomboid major attaches to the spinous processes of which vertebrae?
    choices:
      - C1–C4
      - C7–T1
      - T2–T5
      - T7–T12
      - L1–L5
    answer: C
    explanation: Rhomboid **major** arises from **T2–T5**; rhomboid minor from C7–T1 (and the nuchal ligament).
