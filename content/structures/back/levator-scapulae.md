---
name: Levator scapulae
type: muscle
region: back
subregion: Muscles of the back
taName: Musculus levator scapulae
summary: Strap-like muscle from the upper cervical transverse processes to the superior angle of the scapula. It elevates the scapula and is a frequent source of neck and shoulder stiffness.
tags: [back, neck, shoulder]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Posterolateral neck, forming part of the floor of the posterior triangle, deep to trapezius and sternocleidomastoid.
  group: Superficial extrinsic back muscles (with the rhomboids, deep to trapezius).
  origin: Posterior tubercles of the transverse processes of **C1–C4**.
  insertion: Medial border of the [[scapula]] from the superior angle to the root of the spine.
  innervation: Dorsal scapular nerve (C5) and cervical plexus branches (C3–C4).
  bloodSupply: Dorsal scapular artery and branches of the transverse cervical and ascending cervical arteries.
  action:
    - Elevates the scapula; rotates the glenoid downward (with the rhomboids)
    - With the scapula fixed, laterally flexes and extends the neck
  relations: Lies in the floor of the posterior triangle; the spinal accessory nerve crosses superficial to it.
clinical:
  - title: Levator scapulae syndrome ("stiff neck")
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Muscle strain or spasm from posture (phone and desk use), sleeping position, or carrying bags on one shoulder.
    presentation: Pain at the superior angle of the scapula and side of the neck, limited neck rotation toward the affected side, and a tender trigger point near the superior angle.
pance:
  - "Levator scapulae: **C1–C4 transverse processes → superior angle of scapula**; elevates the scapula."
  - "Tender superior angle of the scapula + limited neck rotation → levator scapulae strain."
imaging:
  mri: Rarely needed; excludes cervical spine pathology if neurologic signs or red flags are present.
exam:
  landmarks: Palpable at the superior angle of the scapula, deep to trapezius.
  palpation: Palpate the insertion at the superior angle for tenderness.
  testing: Resisted shoulder elevation (with trapezius); neck rotation and lateral flexion stretch it.
