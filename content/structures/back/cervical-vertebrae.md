name: Cervical vertebrae
type: bone
region: back
subregion: Vertebral column
taName: Vertebrae cervicales
aka: [C-spine, C3–C7]
summary: Seven small vertebrae of the neck with transverse foramina for the vertebral arteries. Cervical radiculopathy, myelopathy and trauma clearance are the key clinical topics.
tags: [spine, neck]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Neck, between the skull and T1. C1 and C2 are atypical (see [[atlas-and-axis]]).
  classification: Irregular bones.
  features:
    - "Small, broad bodies with raised lateral lips (**uncinate processes**) forming **uncovertebral joints** (of Luschka) with the vertebra above"
    - "**Transverse foramina** in every transverse process; the [[vertebral-artery]] usually passes through those of **C6–C1**"
    - "**Bifid spinous processes** (typically C3–C6)"
    - "**C7 (vertebra prominens)**: long, non-bifid spinous process, the first clearly palpable at the base of the neck"
    - "Large triangular vertebral foramen (cervical enlargement of the cord)"
    - "Anterior tubercle of C6 (**carotid tubercle**): the common carotid can be compressed against it"
  relations: >-
    Spinal nerves **C1–C7 exit above** the vertebra of the same number (C8 exits between C7 and T1), so a **C5–C6 disc
    herniation affects the C6 root** and C6–C7 affects C7. Facet joints are inclined at about 45°, allowing flexion,
    extension, lateral flexion and rotation.
clinical:
  - title: Cervical radiculopathy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Nerve root compression by disc herniation or foraminal narrowing (uncovertebral and facet osteophytes). **C7** and **C6** roots are most often involved.
    presentation: >-
      Neck and arm pain in a dermatomal pattern with numbness, weakness and reduced reflexes. **C5**: deltoid, biceps
      reflex, lateral arm. **C6**: wrist extension, biceps/brachioradialis reflexes, thumb. **C7**: triceps, triceps
      reflex, middle finger. **C8**: finger flexion, little finger. Pain often eases with the hand on the head (shoulder
      abduction relief sign). Positive **Spurling test**.
  - title: Cervical spondylotic myelopathy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Degenerative narrowing of the canal (disc bulge, osteophytes, ligamentum flavum hypertrophy, OPLL) compressing the spinal cord.
    presentation: >-
      Clumsy hands, gait imbalance, **upper motor neuron signs** (hyperreflexia, **Hoffmann sign**, Babinski, clonus),
      Lhermitte sign. Progressive; MRI and surgical referral.
  - title: Cervical spine trauma clearance
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Decision rules identify alert trauma patients who don't need imaging.
    presentation: >-
      **NEXUS**: imaging is not needed if there is no midline tenderness, no focal neurologic deficit, normal alertness,
      no intoxication, and no painful distracting injury. **Canadian C-spine rule**: high-risk factors (age 65 or more,
      dangerous mechanism, extremity paresthesias) mandate imaging; otherwise assess low-risk factors and active neck
      rotation. **CT** is the imaging test of choice in adults.
pance:
  - "C-roots exit **above** their vertebra: **C5–C6 disc → C6 root**; C6–C7 → C7 (most common radiculopathy)."
  - "Root quick map: **C5 deltoid/biceps reflex**, **C6 wrist extension/brachioradialis reflex/thumb**, **C7 triceps reflex/middle finger**, **C8 finger flexion/little finger**."
  - "**Spurling test** → radiculopathy. **Hoffmann sign**, hyperreflexia → myelopathy."
  - "Trauma: **NEXUS** or **Canadian C-spine rule**; CT is first-line imaging in adults."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    Lateral view must show **C1 through the C7–T1 junction** (swimmer's view if needed); check the anterior vertebral,
    posterior vertebral and spinolaminar lines and prevertebral soft tissue. Obliques show foraminal narrowing. Largely
    replaced by CT in trauma.
  ct: First-line for cervical trauma in adults (fractures, alignment). CT angiography if vertebral artery injury is suspected (e.g. transverse foramen fracture).
  mri: Discs, nerve roots, cord compression and signal change (myelopathy), ligamentous injury.
  keyViews:
    - "CT cervical spine (trauma)"
    - "MRI cervical spine (radiculopathy with red flags, myelopathy)"
exam:
  landmarks: C7 spinous process (vertebra prominens); the C6 carotid tubercle at about the level of the cricoid cartilage.
  palpation: Midline tenderness (trauma), paraspinal spasm.
  testing: Neck range of motion; myotomes, dermatomes and reflexes of the upper limbs; UMN signs (Hoffmann, Babinski, clonus).
  specialTests:
    - name: Spurling test
      technique: Extend the neck and rotate/laterally flex it toward the symptomatic side, then apply gentle axial compression.
      positive: Reproduction of radicular arm pain (not just neck pain).
      significance: Cervical radiculopathy (foraminal narrowing). Specific but not very sensitive.
    - name: Hoffmann sign
      technique: Flick the distal phalanx of the patient's middle finger downward.
      positive: Reflex flexion of the thumb and index finger.
      significance: Upper motor neuron sign, e.g. cervical myelopathy.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 47-year-old man has neck pain radiating to the middle finger, weak elbow extension and a reduced triceps reflex.
      Which nerve root is affected?
    choices:
      - C5
      - C6
      - C7
      - C8
      - T1
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **C7** radiculopathy: triceps weakness, reduced **triceps reflex**, and middle finger symptoms. It usually results
      from a **C6–C7** disc, because cervical roots exit above their numbered vertebra.
  - stem: A C5–C6 disc herniation most commonly compresses which nerve root?
    choices:
      - C4
      - C5
      - C6
      - C7
      - Both C5 and C7
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      Cervical nerves exit **above** the vertebra of the same number, so the root leaving the C5–C6 foramen is **C6**.
  - stem: >-
      A 68-year-old woman has progressive difficulty buttoning shirts and an unsteady gait. Reflexes are brisk in all four
      limbs, and flicking the middle fingernail causes thumb flexion. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Carpal tunnel syndrome
      - Cervical spondylotic myelopathy
      - Peripheral neuropathy
      - Parkinson disease
      - C6 radiculopathy
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Hand clumsiness, gait imbalance and **UMN signs** (hyperreflexia, **Hoffmann sign**) indicate **cervical
      myelopathy**. Obtain an MRI.
  - stem: Which structure passes through the transverse foramina of C1–C6?
    choices:
      - Common carotid artery
      - Vertebral artery
      - Internal jugular vein
      - Phrenic nerve
      - Sympathetic trunk
    answer: B
    explanation: The **vertebral artery** typically enters at C6 and ascends through the transverse foramina of C6–C1.
  - stem: Under the NEXUS criteria, which finding in an alert trauma patient means cervical spine imaging is needed?
    choices:
      - Age 40
      - Low-speed rear-end collision
      - Midline cervical spine tenderness
      - A minor abrasion on the forearm
      - Neck stiffness that develops the next day
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **NEXUS**: imaging isn't needed if there is **no midline tenderness**, no focal neurologic deficit, normal
      alertness, no intoxication and no painful distracting injury. **Midline tenderness** means imaging (CT in adults)
      is needed.
