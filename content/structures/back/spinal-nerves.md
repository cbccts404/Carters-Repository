aka: [Dorsal root, Ventral root, Dorsal ramus, Ventral ramus, Radiculopathy, Dorsal root ganglion]
summary: The 31 pairs of mixed nerves formed from dorsal (sensory) and ventral (motor) roots. Knowing where each root exits and what it supplies lets you localize radiculopathy.
tags: [spine, neuro, radiculopathy]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Intervertebral foramina along the length of the vertebral column.
  roots: "**31 pairs**: 8 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral, 1 coccygeal."
  origin: >-
    **Dorsal root** (sensory; its cell bodies are in the **dorsal root ganglion**, in the intervertebral foramen) and
    **ventral root** (motor; cell bodies in the ventral horn) unite in the foramen to form the short spinal nerve.
  course: >-
    **Exit rule**: C1–C7 exit **above** the vertebra of the same number; **C8** exits between C7 and T1; from T1 down each
    nerve exits **below** its same-numbered vertebra. Lower roots descend progressively farther within the canal before
    exiting (forming the [[cauda-equina]]).
  branches:
    - "**Dorsal (posterior) ramus**: deep back muscles (e.g. [[erector-spinae]]), facet joints, and a strip of skin of the back"
    - "**Ventral (anterior) ramus**: limbs and the anterolateral trunk; forms the cervical, [[brachial-plexus|brachial]], [[lumbar-plexus|lumbar]] and sacral plexuses, and the intercostal nerves"
    - "**Rami communicantes**: white (preganglionic sympathetic, T1–L2 only) and gray (postganglionic, all levels) connect to the sympathetic trunk"
    - "**Meningeal (sinuvertebral) branch**: back into the canal (dura, PLL, outer anulus)"
  motor: Via ventral roots (myotomes).
  sensory: Via dorsal roots (dermatomes; e.g. C6 thumb, C7 middle finger, C8 little finger, T4 nipple, T10 umbilicus, L1 inguinal region, L4 medial leg, L5 dorsum of foot, S1 lateral foot).
  notes: >-
    **Lower limb root summary.** **L3**: hip flexion/knee extension, anterior thigh. **L4**: knee extension, ankle
    dorsiflexion (tibialis anterior), **patellar reflex**, medial leg. **L5**: great-toe extension (EHL), hip abduction,
    dorsum of foot and first web space, no reliable common reflex. **S1**: plantarflexion, foot eversion, **Achilles
    reflex**, lateral foot and sole.
clinical:
  - title: Radiculopathy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Root compression or inflammation by disc herniation, foraminal stenosis (osteophytes, facet hypertrophy), or rarely tumor or infection.
    presentation: Radiating pain, dermatomal sensory loss, myotomal weakness, and reduced reflexes in a single root distribution. Distinguished from peripheral nerve lesions by the pattern of affected muscles and skin.
  - title: Herpes zoster
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Reactivation of varicella-zoster virus latent in the **dorsal root ganglion** (or cranial nerve ganglia).
    presentation: Prodromal pain then a **unilateral dermatomal vesicular rash** that doesn't cross the midline; possible postherpetic neuralgia.
pance:
  - "Root exit: **C1–C7 above**, **C8 between C7–T1**, **T1 and below exit below** their vertebra."
  - "Dermatomes: **C6 thumb, C7 middle finger, C8 little finger, T4 nipple, T10 umbilicus, L4 medial leg, L5 dorsum of foot, S1 lateral foot**."
  - "Reflexes: **biceps C5–C6, brachioradialis C6, triceps C7, patellar L4, Achilles S1**."
  - "Zoster lives in the **dorsal root ganglion**."
imaging:
  mri: MRI shows root compression in the foramen or lateral recess.
  ct: CT (or CT myelography) for bony foraminal stenosis.
  keyViews:
    - "EMG/nerve conduction studies: distinguish radiculopathy from plexopathy or peripheral neuropathy (sensory nerve action potentials are preserved in radiculopathy because the lesion is proximal to the dorsal root ganglion)"
exam:
  testing: Map motor weakness by myotome, sensory loss by dermatome, and reflexes by root; compare sides. Nerve tension tests (straight leg raise, Spurling) provoke radicular pain.
quiz:
  - stem: Which spinal nerve exits through the intervertebral foramen between C7 and T1?
    choices:
      - C7
      - C8
      - T1
      - T2
      - C6
    answer: B
    explanation: There are 8 cervical nerves but 7 cervical vertebrae, so **C8** exits between C7 and T1. Below that, each nerve exits below its own vertebra.
  - stem: Where are the cell bodies of sensory neurons carried in the spinal nerves located?
    choices:
      - Ventral horn
      - Dorsal root ganglion
      - Sympathetic chain ganglion
      - Lateral horn
      - Thalamus
    answer: B
    explanation: Sensory (pseudounipolar) neuron cell bodies lie in the **dorsal root ganglion**, the site where varicella-zoster virus lies latent.
  - stem: Which reflex is most associated with the L4 root?
    choices:
      - Achilles reflex
      - Patellar reflex
      - Biceps reflex
      - Cremasteric reflex
      - Plantar reflex
    answer: B
    explanation: The **patellar reflex** tests **L4** (with L3). The Achilles reflex tests S1.
