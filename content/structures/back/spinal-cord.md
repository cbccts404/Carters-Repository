name: Spinal cord
type: organ
region: back
subregion: Spinal cord & meninges
taName: Medulla spinalis
aka: [Conus medullaris, Anterior spinal artery, Artery of Adamkiewicz]
summary: Continuation of the medulla within the vertebral canal, ending at the conus medullaris at about L1–L2 in adults. Its blood supply and level of termination explain spinal cord injury syndromes and safe lumbar puncture sites.
tags: [spine, neuro]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: "Vertebral canal, from the foramen magnum to the **conus medullaris at about the L1–L2 disc** in adults (lower, about L3, in newborns). {{verify: newborn level quoted as L2–L3 or L3}}"
  parts:
    - "**Cervical enlargement** (about C4–T1, for the brachial plexus) and **lumbosacral enlargement** (about L1–S3 segments, for the lumbosacral plexus)"
    - "**Conus medullaris**: tapered lower end; **filum terminale** continues to the coccyx"
    - "**31 pairs of spinal nerves** (8 C, 12 T, 5 L, 5 S, 1 Co) from dorsal (sensory) and ventral (motor) rootlets"
    - "Internal organization: central gray matter (dorsal, lateral T1–L2 sympathetic, and ventral horns) surrounded by white matter tracts (see Neuroanatomy)"
  relations: >-
    Surrounded by the [[spinal-meninges]]; roots below the conus form the [[cauda-equina]]. Because the cord is shorter
    than the column, lower cord **segments lie higher than their vertebrae** (e.g. the lumbar segments lie at about T10–T12).
  bloodSupply: >-
    **One anterior spinal artery** (from the vertebral arteries) supplies the **anterior two-thirds** of the cord.
    **Two posterior spinal arteries** supply the posterior third (dorsal columns). They are reinforced by **segmental
    medullary arteries**, the largest being the **great anterior segmental medullary artery (of Adamkiewicz)**, usually
    on the **left** between about **T9 and T12**, supplying the lower cord. {{verify: level range T8–L1 is also quoted}}
  venousDrainage: Spinal veins → internal vertebral venous plexus ([[vertebral-venous-plexus]]).
clinical:
  - title: Anterior spinal artery (anterior cord) syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Infarction of the anterior two-thirds of the cord, e.g. after **aortic surgery or dissection** (loss of the artery of Adamkiewicz), hypotension, or flexion injury.
    presentation: >-
      **Paraplegia** or quadriplegia and **loss of pain and temperature** below the lesion, with **preserved proprioception
      and vibration** (dorsal columns). Bladder dysfunction.
  - title: Incomplete cord injury syndromes
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      **Central cord**: hyperextension in an older patient with cervical spondylosis. **Brown-Séquard**: hemisection
      (penetrating trauma). See also the anterior cord syndrome above.
    presentation: >-
      **Central cord**: weakness **greater in the arms than the legs**, variable sensory loss. **Brown-Séquard**:
      **ipsilateral** weakness and loss of proprioception/vibration, **contralateral** loss of pain and temperature below
      the lesion.
  - title: Neurogenic shock
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Loss of sympathetic outflow after cervical or high thoracic (above about T6) cord injury.
    presentation: '**Hypotension with bradycardia** and warm, dry skin (unlike hemorrhagic shock with tachycardia). Treat with fluids and vasopressors.'
  - title: Neural tube defects
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Failure of neural tube closure. Risk reduced by periconceptional **folic acid**; elevated maternal AFP.
    presentation: >-
      **Spina bifida occulta**: bony defect with a tuft of hair or dimple, usually asymptomatic. **Meningocele**: meninges
      protrude. **Myelomeningocele**: cord and meninges protrude, with leg paralysis, bladder dysfunction, and Chiari II
      malformation/hydrocephalus. **Tethered cord**: low-lying conus causing back pain, gait and bladder problems.
pance:
  - "Cord ends at **L1–L2** in adults → LP at **L3–L4 or L4–L5**."
  - "Anterior spinal artery syndrome: **motor + pain/temperature loss, preserved vibration/proprioception**; after aortic surgery (Adamkiewicz)."
  - "Central cord: **arms > legs**, older patient, hyperextension. Brown-Séquard: ipsilateral motor/dorsal columns, contralateral pain/temperature."
  - "Neurogenic shock: **hypotension + bradycardia** after high cord injury."
  - "Folic acid prevents neural tube defects; hair tuft over the spine → spina bifida occulta."
imaging:
  xray: Plain films show bony injury and alignment only.
  ct: Bony injury in trauma; CT myelography when MRI is contraindicated.
  mri: '**MRI** is the test of choice for the cord: compression, contusion/edema, infarction (diffusion changes), demyelination, syrinx, tumor, tethered cord.'
  ultrasound: In infants (before posterior element ossification), spinal ultrasound evaluates a low-lying conus and tethered cord (e.g. a sacral dimple).
  keyViews:
    - "MRI spine (sagittal T2 and STIR, axial T2)"
exam:
  landmarks: The conus lies at about the L1–L2 vertebral level; the intercristal line (L4) is safely below it.
  testing: >-
    Localize by **sensory level**, myotomes and reflexes; UMN signs below the lesion (spasticity, hyperreflexia,
    Babinski) and LMN signs at the level. Assess **rectal tone and perianal sensation** (sacral sparing indicates an
    incomplete injury). The ASIA/ISNCSCI exam grades injury severity.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      After repair of a thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysm, a patient cannot move his legs and cannot feel pinprick below the
      umbilicus, but vibration and position sense are preserved. Which vessel was compromised?
    choices:
      - Posterior spinal arteries
      - Artery of Adamkiewicz (great anterior segmental medullary artery)
      - Vertebral arteries
      - Internal iliac arteries
      - Basilar artery
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Anterior spinal artery syndrome** spares the dorsal columns (vibration, proprioception). The lower cord's anterior
      supply depends on the **artery of Adamkiewicz**, which can be lost during aortic surgery.
  - stem: A 70-year-old man falls forward and hits his chin, hyperextending his neck. He has marked weakness of both hands with much milder leg weakness. What is the most likely syndrome?
    choices:
      - Anterior cord syndrome
      - Central cord syndrome
      - Brown-Séquard syndrome
      - Cauda equina syndrome
      - Posterior cord syndrome
    answer: B
    explanation: '**Central cord syndrome** (arms weaker than legs) typically follows hyperextension in an older patient with cervical spondylosis.'
  - stem: A patient with a C5 spinal cord injury is hypotensive (80/40) with a heart rate of 48 and warm extremities. What is the cause?
    choices:
      - Hemorrhagic shock
      - Neurogenic shock
      - Cardiogenic shock from myocardial contusion
      - Septic shock
      - Tension pneumothorax
    answer: B
    explanation: "Loss of sympathetic tone after high cord injury causes **hypotension with bradycardia** and warm skin: **neurogenic shock**."
  - stem: At what vertebral level does the spinal cord typically end in adults?
    choices:
      - T10
      - T12
      - L1–L2
      - L4–L5
      - S2
    answer: C
    explanation: The **conus medullaris** ends at about **L1–L2** in adults, so lumbar puncture is performed at L3–L4 or L4–L5.
