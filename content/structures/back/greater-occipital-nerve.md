---
name: Greater occipital nerve
type: nerve
region: back
subregion: Suboccipital region
taName: Nervus occipitalis major
summary: Medial branch of the dorsal ramus of C2 that supplies the skin of the back of the scalp. Its irritation causes occipital neuralgia, which responds to nerve block.
tags: [neck, headache]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Posterior neck and scalp.
  roots: C2 (dorsal ramus).
  origin: Medial branch of the **dorsal ramus of C2**.
  course: >-
    Emerges **below obliquus capitis inferior**, curves upward over the [[suboccipital-triangle]], pierces semispinalis
    capitis and then the trapezius aponeurosis near the superior nuchal line, a few centimeters lateral to the external
    occipital protuberance, and ascends with the **occipital artery** over the back of the scalp.
    {{verify: exact emergence point varies between individuals}}
  motor: Semispinalis capitis (some fibers).
  sensory: Skin of the **posterior scalp** up to about the vertex.
  relations: >-
    The **lesser occipital nerve** (C2, ventral ramus, via the cervical plexus) supplies the skin behind the ear, and the
    **third occipital nerve** (C3 dorsal ramus) supplies the skin of the lower occiput and upper neck.
clinical:
  - title: Occipital neuralgia
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Irritation or entrapment of the greater (or lesser) occipital nerve, e.g. by muscle tension, trauma or upper cervical disease.
    presentation: >-
      **Paroxysmal shooting or stabbing pain** in the distribution of the nerve (from the occiput toward the vertex),
      scalp tenderness or dysesthesia, and **tenderness over the nerve** where it emerges. Temporary relief after a local
      anesthetic **greater occipital nerve block** supports the diagnosis.
pance:
  - "Occipital neuralgia: shooting pain from the occiput to the vertex, tender nerve, **relief with nerve block**."
  - "Greater occipital = **C2 dorsal ramus**; lesser occipital = C2 ventral ramus (cervical plexus); third occipital = C3."
imaging:
  mri: Usually normal; used to exclude structural causes at the craniovertebral junction.
  ultrasound: Guides greater occipital nerve block at the superior nuchal line or at obliquus capitis inferior.
  keyViews:
    - "Diagnostic nerve block (clinical)"
exam:
  landmarks: About one-third of the distance from the external occipital protuberance to the mastoid process, along the superior nuchal line (next to the palpable occipital artery pulse).
  palpation: Pressing over the nerve reproduces the pain (sometimes a Tinel-like sign).
  testing: Sensory testing of the posterior scalp.
