---
name: Trapezius
type: muscle
region: back
subregion: Muscles of the back
taName: Musculus trapezius
summary: Large superficial triangular muscle of the upper back and neck that elevates, retracts and rotates the scapula. It is supplied by the spinal accessory nerve (CN XI), whose injury causes a drooping shoulder and lateral winging.
tags: [back, shoulder, cranial-nerves]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Superficial posterior neck and upper back, from the occiput to T12 and out to the shoulder.
  group: Superficial extrinsic back muscles (posterior axioappendicular), with latissimus dorsi.
  origin: Medial third of the superior nuchal line, external occipital protuberance, **nuchal ligament**, and the spinous processes of **C7–T12**.
  insertion: Lateral third of the [[clavicle]], acromion, and spine of the [[scapula]].
  innervation: '**[[accessory-nerve|Spinal accessory nerve (CN XI)]]** for motor; C3–C4 (cervical plexus) for pain and proprioception.'
  bloodSupply: Transverse cervical artery (superficial branch), plus the occipital artery and dorsal branches of posterior intercostal arteries.
  action:
    - "**Upper (descending) fibers**: elevate the scapula (shrug)"
    - "**Middle (transverse) fibers**: retract the scapula"
    - "**Lower (ascending) fibers**: depress the scapula"
    - "Upper and lower fibers together **rotate the glenoid upward** (with [[serratus-anterior]]) for abduction above 90°"
  relations: >-
    The spinal accessory nerve crosses the **posterior triangle of the neck** superficially (within the investing fascia)
    to reach the deep surface of trapezius, where it is vulnerable. Trapezius and latissimus dorsi bound the
    [[back-triangles|triangle of auscultation]].
clinical:
  - title: Spinal accessory nerve (CN XI) injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Iatrogenic injury during **posterior triangle lymph node biopsy** or neck dissection, or penetrating trauma to the posterior triangle.
    presentation: >-
      **Drooping shoulder**, weak **shoulder shrug** on the affected side, difficulty abducting the arm above the
      horizontal, **lateral winging** of the scapula, and trapezius atrophy. Sternocleidomastoid is spared if the injury is
      distal to its branch.
  - title: Trapezius strain and myofascial pain
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Posture, stress or overuse (desk work), whiplash.
    presentation: Neck and upper shoulder pain with tender trigger points in the upper trapezius, sometimes causing tension-type headache.
pance:
  - "**CN XI** injury (posterior triangle biopsy) → weak shrug, drooping shoulder, **lateral winging**."
  - "Test CN XI: resisted **shoulder shrug** (trapezius) and head turning (SCM)."
  - "Lateral winging = trapezius (CN XI); medial winging = serratus anterior (long thoracic)."
imaging:
  mri: Denervation atrophy of trapezius after CN XI injury; soft-tissue masses.
  ultrasound: The spinal accessory nerve can be traced in the posterior triangle with high-frequency ultrasound.
  keyViews:
    - "EMG confirms CN XI denervation"
exam:
  landmarks: The upper border forms the slope of the neck; the muscle outline is visible with the shoulders braced back.
  palpation: Palpate the upper trapezius for bulk, tenderness and trigger points.
  testing: Resisted shoulder shrug (upper fibers); resisted retraction (middle); observe scapular position at rest and during arm elevation for lateral winging.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      Three weeks after excisional biopsy of a lymph node in the right posterior triangle of the neck, a patient can't
      shrug her right shoulder fully and the shoulder droops. Which nerve was injured?
    choices:
      - Long thoracic nerve
      - Spinal accessory nerve
      - Dorsal scapular nerve
      - Suprascapular nerve
      - Axillary nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **spinal accessory nerve (CN XI)** runs superficially across the posterior triangle to trapezius; injury weakens
      the **shrug**, causes a **drooping shoulder** and lateral winging.
  - stem: Trapezius inserts on which structures?
    choices:
      - Medial border of the scapula
      - Lateral third of the clavicle, acromion and scapular spine
      - Coracoid process
      - Intertubercular groove
      - Superior angle of the scapula only
    answer: B
    explanation: Trapezius inserts on the **lateral third of the clavicle, acromion and spine of the scapula**, the mirror image of the deltoid origin.
  - stem: Which pattern of scapular winging results from trapezius paralysis?
    choices:
      - Medial winging (inferior angle moves medially)
      - Lateral winging with a drooping shoulder
      - No winging
      - Winging only when pushing against a wall
      - Superior displacement of the scapula
    answer: B
    explanation: Trapezius paralysis (CN XI) produces **lateral winging** with a drooping shoulder. Serratus anterior paralysis produces medial winging.
  - stem: Which part of trapezius retracts the scapula?
    choices:
      - Middle (transverse) fibers
      - Upper (descending) fibers
      - Lower (ascending) fibers
      - None; trapezius only elevates the scapula
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      The **middle fibers retract** the scapula, the **upper fibers elevate** it (shrug) and the **lower fibers
      depress** it. Upper and lower fibers together rotate the glenoid upward.
  - stem: Trapezius receives motor supply from CN XI. Which spinal nerves carry its pain and proprioception?
    choices:
      - C1–C2
      - C5–C6
      - T1–T2
      - Dorsal rami of C7–T12
      - C3–C4 (cervical plexus)
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      Trapezius is motor-supplied by the **spinal accessory nerve (CN XI)**, with pain and proprioception from **C3–C4**
      through the cervical plexus. It is an extrinsic back muscle, so it isn't supplied by dorsal rami.
