---
name: Erector spinae
type: muscle
region: back
subregion: Muscles of the back
taName: Musculus erector spinae
aka: [Iliocostalis, Longissimus, Spinalis, Paraspinal muscles]
summary: Main extensor of the vertebral column, running in three columns beside the spine. It is supplied by the dorsal rami of spinal nerves and is the usual site of mechanical low back strain.
tags: [back, low-back-pain]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Paravertebral grooves on each side of the spinous processes, from the sacrum to the skull, deep to the thoracolumbar fascia.
  group: '**Intrinsic (deep) back muscles**, intermediate layer; three columns from lateral to medial: **iliocostalis, longissimus, spinalis** (mnemonic "I Love Spaghetti").'
  origin: A broad common tendon from the posterior iliac crest, posterior sacrum, sacroiliac ligaments, and the sacral and inferior lumbar spinous processes.
  insertion: >-
    **Iliocostalis**: angles of the ribs and cervical transverse processes. **Longissimus**: transverse processes of the
    thoracic and cervical vertebrae, ribs, and the **mastoid process** (longissimus capitis). **Spinalis**: spinous
    processes of the upper thoracic and cervical vertebrae (and the skull).
  innervation: '**Dorsal (posterior) rami** of the [[spinal-nerves]], segmentally.'
  bloodSupply: Dorsal branches of the posterior intercostal, subcostal and lumbar arteries.
  action:
    - Bilaterally, **extends** the vertebral column and head; controls flexion eccentrically (e.g. when bending forward)
    - Unilaterally, **laterally flexes** the vertebral column
  relations: Enclosed with the other intrinsic muscles by the **thoracolumbar fascia**. The deeper transversospinalis group (multifidus, rotatores, semispinalis) lies beneath.
clinical:
  - title: Mechanical low back strain
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Muscle or ligament strain from lifting, twisting or sudden movement. It's the most common cause of acute low back pain.
    presentation: >-
      Localized low back pain and **paraspinal muscle spasm and tenderness**, worse with movement, **without radiation
      below the knee or neurologic deficit**. Most cases improve within weeks. **No imaging** without red flags; stay
      active, NSAIDs, heat, and physical therapy for persistent symptoms.
pance:
  - "Erector spinae columns (lateral → medial): **Iliocostalis, Longissimus, Spinalis** (\"I Love Spaghetti\")."
  - "Innervated by **dorsal rami** (unlike limb muscles, which use ventral rami)."
  - "Acute mechanical low back pain without red flags → **no imaging**; stay active."
imaging:
  xray: Not indicated for uncomplicated low back strain.
  mri: Reserved for red flags or persistent radicular symptoms; muscle edema after strain is rarely clinically useful.
  keyViews:
    - "No imaging for acute nonspecific low back pain without red flags"
exam:
  landmarks: Visible as two vertical columns beside the lumbar spinous processes.
  palpation: Paraspinal tenderness and spasm (a palpable, firm band).
  testing: Range of motion of the lumbar spine (flexion is often painful and limited). Neurologic exam and straight leg raise are normal in simple strain.
