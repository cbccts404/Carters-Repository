---
name: Vertebral ligaments
type: ligament
region: back
subregion: Vertebral column
taName: Ligamenta columnae vertebralis
aka: [Anterior longitudinal ligament, Posterior longitudinal ligament, Ligamentum flavum, Interspinous ligament, Supraspinous ligament, Nuchal ligament]
summary: Ligaments that bind the vertebrae. The longitudinal ligaments run along the bodies and the ligamentum flavum joins the laminae; these are the layers a needle passes through during lumbar puncture and epidural anesthesia.
tags: [spine, procedures]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Along the full length of the vertebral column.
  attachments: >-
    **Anterior longitudinal ligament (ALL)**: broad band on the anterior surfaces of the vertebral bodies and discs, from
    the occiput and atlas to the sacrum. **Posterior longitudinal ligament (PLL)**: narrower band on the posterior
    surfaces of the bodies within the canal (continuous above as the tectorial membrane). **Ligamentum flavum**: joins
    the **laminae** of adjacent vertebrae (yellow, elastic). **Interspinous ligaments**: between spinous processes.
    **Supraspinous ligament**: along the tips of the spinous processes, expanding in the neck as the **nuchal
    ligament** (ligamentum nuchae). **Intertransverse ligaments**: between transverse processes.
  function: >-
    The ALL resists **hyperextension**; the PLL and ligamentum flavum resist **hyperflexion** (the flavum's elasticity
    helps restore the upright posture). The PLL's narrowness in the lumbar region leaves the posterolateral disc
    unsupported.
  relations: The ligamentum flavum forms the posterior boundary of the epidural space and canal; hypertrophy narrows the canal.
clinical:
  - title: Layers traversed by a lumbar puncture or epidural needle
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Midline approach between spinous processes.
    presentation: >-
      Skin → subcutaneous tissue → **supraspinous ligament** → **interspinous ligament** → **ligamentum flavum** (a
      distinct resistance, then "loss of resistance" on entering the **epidural space**, where an epidural needle stops)
      → **dura mater** → **arachnoid mater** → **subarachnoid space** (CSF, for spinal puncture).
  - title: Ligamentum flavum hypertrophy and OPLL
    highYield: false
    mechanism: '**Ligamentum flavum** thickening (with facet and disc degeneration) narrows the canal in **lumbar and cervical stenosis**. **Ossification of the PLL** (OPLL) is a cause of cervical myelopathy, more common in East Asian populations.'
    presentation: Neurogenic claudication (lumbar) or myelopathy (cervical).
  - title: Diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis (DISH)
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Flowing ossification along the **anterior longitudinal ligament** over at least four contiguous vertebrae, typically in older adults (associated with diabetes and obesity); disc spaces and SI joints are preserved.
    presentation: Stiffness, mild pain, dysphagia from cervical osteophytes. Distinguished from ankylosing spondylitis radiographically.
pance:
  - "LP/epidural needle path: **supraspinous → interspinous → ligamentum flavum → epidural space → dura → arachnoid → CSF**."
  - "ALL resists hyperextension; PLL resists hyperflexion and is narrow in the lumbar spine."
  - "Ligamentum flavum hypertrophy → spinal stenosis."
imaging:
  xray: DISH shows flowing anterior ossification with preserved disc spaces. OPLL may be visible on a lateral cervical film.
  ct: Best for ossified ligaments (OPLL, DISH).
  mri: Ligament injury after trauma (disruption of the PLL or interspinous ligaments suggests instability); ligamentum flavum thickness.
  ultrasound: Pre-procedure ultrasound of the lumbar spine can identify the midline, interspaces and the ligamentum flavum–dura complex, especially in obese patients.
  keyViews:
    - "CT (ossification); MRI (ligamentous injury)"
exam:
  landmarks: The nuchal ligament is palpable in the posterior neck midline; interspinous spaces open with lumbar flexion (fetal position) for LP.
  testing: During LP or epidural placement, note the increased resistance of the ligamentum flavum and the loss of resistance on entering the epidural space.
quiz:
  - stem: During an epidural, which structure provides the last firm resistance before the needle enters the epidural space?
    choices:
      - Supraspinous ligament
      - Posterior longitudinal ligament
      - Ligamentum flavum
      - Dura mater
      - Anterior longitudinal ligament
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **ligamentum flavum** gives a characteristic firm resistance; the sudden "loss of resistance" marks entry into
      the epidural space. The dura is deeper (spinal puncture).
  - stem: Which ligament primarily resists hyperextension of the spine?
    choices:
      - Posterior longitudinal ligament
      - Ligamentum flavum
      - Interspinous ligament
      - Anterior longitudinal ligament
      - Supraspinous ligament
    answer: D
    explanation: The **anterior longitudinal ligament** on the front of the vertebral bodies resists **hyperextension**.
  - stem: Which structure connects the laminae of adjacent vertebrae?
    choices:
      - Anterior longitudinal ligament
      - Posterior longitudinal ligament
      - Ligamentum flavum
      - Nuchal ligament
      - Intertransverse ligament
    answer: C
    explanation: The elastic **ligamentum flavum** joins adjacent **laminae**, forming part of the posterior wall of the canal.
