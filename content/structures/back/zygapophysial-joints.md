---
name: Zygapophysial (facet) joints
type: joint
region: back
subregion: Vertebral column
taName: Articulationes zygapophysiales
aka: [Facet joints]
summary: Paired synovial joints between the articular processes of adjacent vertebrae. Their orientation determines spinal motion at each level, and facet arthropathy is a common cause of axial back and neck pain.
tags: [spine, low-back-pain]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Posterolaterally between the superior articular process of one vertebra and the inferior articular process of the vertebra above, from C2–C3 to L5–S1.
  classification: Synovial **plane** joints.
  bones: Articular processes of adjacent vertebrae.
  capsule: Thin, loose capsule (loosest in the cervical region).
  ligaments:
    - Joint capsule; ligamentum flavum anteriorly
  movements:
    - "**Cervical**: facets inclined about 45°, allowing flexion–extension, lateral flexion and rotation"
    - "**Thoracic**: roughly coronal, favoring rotation (limited by ribs)"
    - "**Lumbar**: roughly sagittal, favoring flexion–extension and limiting rotation"
  stability: Resist shear forces (anterior slippage) and limit rotation, together with the discs and ligaments.
  innervation: '**Medial branches of the dorsal rami** of the spinal nerves; each joint receives branches from **two levels** (the level above and at the joint).'
  relations: Form the posterior boundary of the intervertebral foramen; facet hypertrophy narrows the foramen and lateral recess.
clinical:
  - title: Facet joint arthropathy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Osteoarthritis of the facets, often with disc degeneration, in older adults.
    presentation: >-
      Axial back or neck pain, often **worse with extension and rotation** and after rest, with poorly localized referral
      to the buttock or thigh (not below the knee). Diagnosis is confirmed by pain relief after **medial branch blocks**;
      radiofrequency ablation of the medial branches is a treatment.
  - title: Facet dislocation (cervical)
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Flexion–distraction injury; unilateral or bilateral locked facets.
    presentation: Neck pain, radiculopathy or cord injury. Bilateral facet dislocation causes marked anterior translation and is highly unstable.
pance:
  - "Facet pain: axial pain **worse with extension/rotation**; no radiation below the knee."
  - "Innervated by **medial branches of dorsal rami** (two levels) → medial branch block/radiofrequency ablation."
  - "Lumbar facets are sagittal (flexion–extension); thoracic are coronal (rotation)."
imaging:
  xray: Facet sclerosis, joint space narrowing and osteophytes on oblique views.
  ct: Best for facet osteoarthritis and fractures or dislocations.
  mri: Facet effusions, synovial cysts (which can compress roots), and foraminal narrowing.
  keyViews:
    - "CT or MRI; fluoroscopy-guided diagnostic blocks"
exam:
  testing: Reproduce pain with combined extension and rotation of the spine (facet loading); paraspinal tenderness over the joints. Neurologic exam is typically normal.
