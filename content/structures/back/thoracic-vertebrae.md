region: back
subregion: Vertebral column
taName: Vertebrae thoracicae
aka: [T-spine]
summary: Twelve vertebrae that articulate with the ribs. They are the common site of osteoporotic compression fractures, metastases and spinal tuberculosis.
tags: [spine]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Posterior thorax, T1–T12.
  classification: Irregular bones.
  features:
    - "**Costal facets** on the bodies (for the heads of the ribs) and on the transverse processes of T1–T10 (for the rib tubercles)"
    - "Heart-shaped bodies; small circular vertebral foramen"
    - "**Long spinous processes** sloping inferiorly (overlapping the vertebra below in the mid-thoracic region)"
    - "Articular facets oriented roughly in the coronal plane, favoring rotation; motion is limited by the rib cage"
  relations: Articulate with the ribs at costovertebral and costotransverse joints. The descending aorta lies to the left of the bodies and the azygos vein and thoracic duct to the right; the sympathetic trunks lie on the rib heads.
  notes: The thoracolumbar junction (T11–L2) is a transition from the stiff, rib-supported thoracic spine to the mobile lumbar spine and is the most common site of traumatic fractures.
clinical:
  - title: Osteoporotic vertebral compression fracture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Fracture of a weakened vertebral body with minimal trauma (bending, lifting, coughing), often in the **mid-thoracic or thoracolumbar** region, in older adults, especially postmenopausal women and those on glucocorticoids.
    presentation: >-
      Sudden back pain with point tenderness, or silent **height loss** and progressive kyphosis. A fragility vertebral
      fracture **diagnoses osteoporosis** and should prompt DEXA and treatment.
  - title: Spinal metastases
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Hematogenous spread to vertebral bodies (breast, lung, prostate, kidney, thyroid; multiple myeloma). The thoracic spine is most often involved.
    presentation: >-
      Progressive back pain, **worse at night or supine**, with weight loss or a cancer history. **Malignant spinal cord
      compression** (weakness, sensory level, bladder dysfunction) is an emergency: high-dose steroids, MRI of the whole
      spine, and radiotherapy or surgery.
  - title: Spinal tuberculosis (Pott disease)
    highYield: false
    mechanism: TB spondylitis, usually of the lower thoracic and upper lumbar spine, destroying adjacent vertebral bodies and the disc.
    presentation: Back pain, systemic symptoms, **gibbus deformity** (sharp angular kyphosis), possible paraspinal/psoas cold abscess and paraplegia.
pance:
  - "Minimal-trauma vertebral fracture = **osteoporosis** → DEXA and treatment."
  - "Back pain worse at night/supine + cancer history → **metastases**; new weakness or sensory level → emergency MRI (cord compression)."
  - "Thoracic spine has **costal facets** and long downward spinous processes."
  - "Pott disease: TB of the lower thoracic spine, gibbus, psoas abscess."
imaging:
  xray: Loss of vertebral body height (wedge compression). Pedicle destruction ("winking owl" sign) suggests metastasis on the AP view.
  ct: Fracture morphology and posterior wall involvement (burst fracture).
  mri: '**Acute vs chronic** compression fracture (marrow edema on STIR), malignant vs osteoporotic fracture, **spinal cord compression**, infection.'
  keyViews:
    - "Lateral thoracic/lumbar radiographs"
    - "MRI whole spine for suspected cord compression"
    - "DEXA after fragility fracture"
exam:
  landmarks: T3 at the medial end of the scapular spine; T7 at the inferior angle of the scapula (arm at side); T12 at the lowest rib attachment.
  palpation: Percussion tenderness over a spinous process (fracture, infection, malignancy). Measure height and look for kyphosis.
  testing: Neurologic exam for a sensory level and UMN signs in the legs (cord compression).
quiz:
  - stem: A 74-year-old woman felt sudden mid-back pain after lifting a laundry basket. A radiograph shows anterior wedging of T8. What is the most appropriate next step for long-term management?
    choices:
      - Reassurance only
      - DEXA scan and osteoporosis treatment
      - Surgical fusion
      - Bone marrow biopsy
      - Long-term opioid therapy
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **low-trauma vertebral compression fracture** establishes **osteoporosis** clinically. Evaluate with DEXA (and
      secondary causes) and start treatment to prevent further fractures.
  - stem: A 66-year-old man with prostate cancer has 3 weeks of worsening thoracic back pain, now with bilateral leg weakness and urinary retention. What is the most urgent step?
    choices:
      - Outpatient bone scan
      - IV dexamethasone and emergency MRI of the whole spine
      - Physical therapy referral
      - NSAIDs and follow-up in 2 weeks
      - Lumbar puncture
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      This is **malignant spinal cord compression**, an oncologic emergency: give **high-dose steroids** and obtain an
      **urgent MRI of the whole spine**, followed by radiotherapy and/or surgery.
  - stem: Which feature is unique to thoracic vertebrae?
    choices:
      - Transverse foramina
      - Bifid spinous processes
      - Costal facets
      - Pars interarticularis
      - Mammillary processes
    answer: C
    explanation: Thoracic vertebrae have **costal facets** on their bodies (and most on their transverse processes) for articulation with the ribs.
