summary: The fused sacral vertebrae form the posterior wall of the pelvis and articulate with the ilia at the sacroiliac joints; the coccyx is the vestigial tail. The sacral hiatus is the route for caudal epidural block.

highYield: false

anatomy:
  location: Base of the vertebral column, wedged between the hip bones; the coccyx lies below it in the natal cleft.
  classification: Fused irregular bones (5 sacral; usually 4 coccygeal).
  features:
    - "**Sacral promontory**: anterior upper edge of S1; a landmark for pelvic measurements"
    - "**Anterior (pelvic) and posterior sacral foramina** for the ventral and dorsal rami of the sacral nerves"
    - "**Sacral canal** (continuation of the vertebral canal) containing the lower cauda equina and filum terminale; the dural sac usually ends at about **S2**"
    - "**Sacral hiatus**: opening at the lower end of the sacral canal (absent laminae of S5 ± S4), flanked by the **sacral cornua**"
    - "**Auricular surfaces**: articulate with the ilia at the **sacroiliac joints**"
    - "**Coccyx**: small fused segments; attachment for the gluteus maximus, coccygeus and anococcygeal ligament"
  relations: "The PSIS dimples overlie the SI joints at about S2. Anteriorly: rectum, sacral plexus, median sacral vessels."
clinical:
  - title: Caudal epidural block
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Local anesthetic injected through the **sacral hiatus** into the epidural space of the sacral canal.
    presentation: Common in pediatric anesthesia and for some chronic pain procedures. The hiatus is palpated between the sacral cornua.
  - title: Coccydynia
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Pain at the coccyx from a fall onto the buttocks, childbirth, or prolonged sitting.
    presentation: Localized pain over the coccyx, worse sitting (especially leaning back) and rising from sitting. Tenderness on palpation.
  - title: Pilonidal disease
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Hair and debris penetrating the skin of the natal cleft over the sacrococcygeal region, forming a sinus or abscess; common in young, hirsute men.
    presentation: Painful, fluctuant swelling in the upper natal cleft, often with midline pits. Abscesses need incision and drainage.
  - title: Sacral insufficiency fracture
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Osteoporotic fracture of the sacrum in older adults (often after pelvic radiation or on steroids).
    presentation: Low back, buttock or groin pain without major trauma; radiographs are often normal. MRI or CT shows the fracture.
pance:
  - "Dural sac ends at about **S2**; sacral hiatus → **caudal epidural** block."
  - "Coccydynia: fall or childbirth, pain with sitting."

imaging:
  xray: Pelvis AP and lateral sacrum/coccyx; sacral fractures are often hard to see.
  ct: Sacral fractures and SI joint detail.
  mri: Insufficiency fractures (edema), sacroiliitis, sacral tumors (e.g. chordoma).
  keyViews:
    - "Lateral sacrum/coccyx radiograph"
    - "MRI or CT for suspected sacral fracture"
exam:
  landmarks: PSIS dimples at about S2; sacral hiatus palpable as a depression between the cornua at the upper natal cleft; coccyx tip in the natal cleft.
  palpation: Palpate the coccyx externally (and per rectum for mobility) for tenderness. Inspect the natal cleft for pits and abscess.
