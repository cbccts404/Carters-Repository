taName: Meninges spinales; spatium epidurale; spatium subarachnoideum
aka: [Epidural space, Subarachnoid space, Lumbar cistern, Lumbar puncture, Dura mater]
summary: Three membranes (dura, arachnoid, pia) and the spaces between them surrounding the spinal cord. The epidural space is the target of epidural anesthesia, and the lumbar cistern is sampled by lumbar puncture.
tags: [spine, procedures, neuro]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Within the vertebral canal, surrounding the [[spinal-cord]] and [[cauda-equina]].
  boundaries:
    epidural space: Between the dura and the walls of the canal (bone, ligamentum flavum). Contains **fat** and the **internal vertebral venous plexus**. Unlike the cranial epidural space, it is a real space
    dura mater: Tough outer membrane; the dural sac ends at about **S2**
    subdural space: Potential space between dura and arachnoid
    subarachnoid space: Between arachnoid and pia; contains **CSF**. Below the conus it enlarges as the **lumbar cistern** (about L2–S2), holding the cauda equina
    pia mater: Adheres to the cord; forms the **denticulate ligaments** (lateral anchors) and the filum terminale
  contents:
    - CSF, cauda equina nerve roots, spinal vessels
  communications: The subarachnoid space is continuous with the cranial subarachnoid space and ventricular system through the foramen magnum.
  notes: >-
    **Lumbar puncture** is performed at **L3–L4 or L4–L5** (identified by the **intercristal line at L4**), below the
    conus, where the needle pushes cauda equina roots aside rather than piercing the cord. **Epidural anesthesia** stops
    in the epidural space (loss of resistance after the ligamentum flavum); **spinal anesthesia** goes into the
    subarachnoid space.
clinical:
  - title: Lumbar puncture indications, contraindications and CSF
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Sampling CSF from the lumbar cistern (meningitis, subarachnoid hemorrhage with a negative CT, demyelinating disease, pressure measurement).
    presentation: >-
      Before LP, obtain **head CT** if there are signs of raised intracranial pressure or a mass (papilledema, focal
      deficits, altered consciousness, new seizure, immunocompromise). Other contraindications: coagulopathy or
      thrombocytopenia, skin infection at the site. In suspected bacterial meningitis, don't delay antibiotics for
      imaging or LP.
  - title: Post-dural puncture headache
    highYield: true
    mechanism: CSF leak through the dural hole lowers intracranial pressure.
    presentation: >-
      **Positional headache**, worse sitting or standing and relieved lying flat, within days of LP or accidental dural
      puncture during epidural placement. Smaller, atraumatic (pencil-point) needles reduce the risk. Treatment:
      conservative care, caffeine, and an **epidural blood patch** if persistent.
  - title: Spinal epidural abscess
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Infection (usually ***Staphylococcus aureus***) in the epidural space, via hematogenous spread (IV drug use, bacteremia) or procedures; risk factors include diabetes.
    presentation: >-
      Classic triad of **back pain, fever and neurologic deficit** (often incomplete early). **Emergent MRI with
      gadolinium**, blood cultures, IV antibiotics and surgical decompression.
  - title: Spinal epidural hematoma
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Bleeding into the epidural space, especially with anticoagulation and neuraxial procedures (epidural catheter placement or removal).
    presentation: Sudden back pain with rapidly progressive weakness or numbness and bladder dysfunction; emergency MRI and decompression.
pance:
  - "LP at **L3–L4 or L4–L5** (intercristal line = L4)."
  - "Get a **head CT before LP** if there are signs of raised ICP or focal deficits."
  - "**Post-dural puncture headache**: worse upright, better lying flat → epidural blood patch if persistent."
  - "Epidural abscess: **fever + back pain + neurologic deficit**, *S. aureus*, IV drug use → MRI with contrast."
  - "Epidural anesthesia stops in the epidural space; spinal anesthesia enters the subarachnoid space."
imaging:
  ct: Head CT before LP when indicated. CT myelography instills contrast into the subarachnoid space.
  mri: '**MRI with gadolinium** is the test of choice for epidural abscess, hematoma, and leptomeningeal disease; it also shows CSF leaks (with dedicated protocols).'
  ultrasound: Ultrasound-assisted LP identifies the midline and interspaces in obese patients or difficult anatomy.
  keyViews:
    - "MRI spine with and without contrast"
exam:
  landmarks: Intercristal (Tuffier) line crossing L4 or the L4–L5 interspace; midline spinous processes.
  palpation: For LP, position the patient in the lateral decubitus (fetal) position or sitting and flexed to open the interspaces; measure opening pressure only in the lateral position.
  testing: Before LP, fundoscopy for papilledema and a focal neurologic exam. Afterward, monitor for headache and neurologic change.
quiz:
  - stem: A 25-year-old woman develops a severe headache 2 days after a lumbar puncture. It is much worse when she sits up and improves when she lies down. What is the definitive treatment if conservative measures fail?
    choices:
      - Repeat lumbar puncture
      - Epidural blood patch
      - IV antibiotics
      - Emergency MRI and neurosurgery
      - Triptan therapy
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **positional post-dural puncture headache** comes from a CSF leak. If conservative treatment fails, an **epidural
      blood patch** seals the dural hole.
  - stem: A 40-year-old man who injects drugs has fever, severe thoracic back pain and new leg weakness. What is the most appropriate next step?
    choices:
      - Plain radiographs of the spine
      - Emergent MRI of the spine with gadolinium
      - Lumbar puncture
      - NSAIDs and outpatient follow-up
      - Bone scan
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The triad of fever, back pain and neurologic deficit suggests a **spinal epidural abscess**. Obtain an **emergent MRI
      with contrast**, blood cultures, IV antibiotics, and surgical consultation.
  - stem: Where does the needle tip lie in a correctly placed lumbar epidural (not spinal) anesthetic?
    choices:
      - Subarachnoid space
      - Subdural space
      - Epidural space, just deep to the ligamentum flavum
      - Within the spinal cord
      - Interspinous ligament
    answer: C
    explanation: The epidural needle stops in the **epidural space** after the loss of resistance past the **ligamentum flavum**; puncturing the dura gives a "wet tap".
  - stem: The subarachnoid space (lumbar cistern) extends inferiorly to about which level?
    choices:
      - L1
      - L3
      - L5
      - S2
      - Tip of coccyx
    answer: D
    explanation: The dural sac and subarachnoid space extend to about **S2**, well below the end of the cord (L1–L2).
