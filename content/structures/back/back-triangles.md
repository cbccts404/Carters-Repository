aka: [Triangle of auscultation, Lumbar triangle, Petit triangle, Lumbar hernia]
summary: Two gaps in the superficial muscles of the back. The triangle of auscultation lets breath sounds be heard clearly; the lumbar (Petit) triangle is a rare site of lumbar hernia.
tags: [back, exam, hernia]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Posterior thoracic wall (triangle of auscultation) and lower posterolateral abdominal wall just above the iliac crest (lumbar triangle).
  boundaries:
    auscultation — medial: Lateral border of [[trapezius]]
    auscultation — inferior: Superior border of [[latissimus-dorsi]]
    auscultation — lateral: Medial border of the [[scapula]] (with rhomboid major)
    auscultation — floor: Thoracic wall (about the 6th–7th ribs and intercostal space), relatively free of overlying muscle
    lumbar (Petit) — inferior: Iliac crest
    lumbar (Petit) — medial: Lateral border of latissimus dorsi
    lumbar (Petit) — lateral: Posterior border of the external oblique
    lumbar (Petit) — floor: Internal oblique
  contents:
    - No major neurovascular structures; each is simply a relatively thin area of the wall
  notes: The triangle of auscultation **enlarges when the scapula is drawn forward** (arms folded across the chest and trunk flexed), improving access to breath sounds over the lower lobe posteriorly.
clinical:
  - title: Auscultation of the lungs
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Thinner musculature over this area transmits breath sounds well.
    presentation: Clear breath sounds from the lower lobe posteriorly. Historically also a surgical approach to the posterior thorax.
  - title: Lumbar (Petit) hernia
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Herniation through the inferior lumbar triangle (or the superior, Grynfeltt, triangle), congenital or acquired (trauma, surgery, iliac crest bone graft harvest).
    presentation: A reducible bulge in the flank just above the iliac crest that enlarges with coughing; risk of incarceration.
pance:
  - "Triangle of auscultation: **trapezius, latissimus dorsi, medial border of scapula**; best heard with arms folded and trunk flexed."
  - "Lumbar (Petit) triangle: **iliac crest, latissimus dorsi, external oblique** → lumbar hernia."
imaging:
  ct: CT confirms lumbar hernia and its contents.
  ultrasound: Dynamic ultrasound with Valsalva shows a lumbar hernia.
  keyViews:
    - "CT abdomen for a flank bulge"
exam:
  landmarks: Triangle of auscultation near the inferior angle of the scapula, medial to it; lumbar triangle just above the iliac crest at the posterior axillary line region.
  palpation: Palpate the flank for a bulge while the patient coughs (lumbar hernia).
  testing: Auscultate posteriorly over the triangle with the patient's arms folded and trunk flexed forward.
