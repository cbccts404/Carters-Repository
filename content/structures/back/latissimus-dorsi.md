---
name: Latissimus dorsi
type: muscle
region: back
subregion: Muscles of the back
taName: Musculus latissimus dorsi
summary: Broad flat muscle of the lower back that extends, adducts and medially rotates the arm. It is supplied by the thoracodorsal nerve and forms the posterior axillary fold.
tags: [back, shoulder]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Lower and middle back, sweeping up to the axilla.
  group: Superficial extrinsic back muscles (posterior axioappendicular).
  origin: Spinous processes of **T7–T12**, the **thoracolumbar fascia** (and through it the lumbar and sacral spinous processes), the posterior iliac crest, the inferior 3–4 ribs, and sometimes the inferior angle of the scapula.
  insertion: '**Floor of the intertubercular (bicipital) groove** of the [[humerus]].'
  innervation: '**Thoracodorsal nerve** (C6, C7, C8), from the posterior cord of the [[brachial-plexus]].'
  bloodSupply: Thoracodorsal artery (from the subscapular artery) and dorsal perforators of posterior intercostal and lumbar arteries.
  action:
    - '**Extends, adducts and medially rotates** the humerus (climbing, pull-ups, swimming)'
    - Raises the trunk toward the arm when the arm is fixed; assists forced expiration and coughing
  relations: >-
    With teres major it forms the **posterior axillary fold**. The thoracodorsal nerve and vessels run on its deep surface
    in the posterior wall of the [[axilla]]. Its upper border helps bound the [[back-triangles|triangle of auscultation]];
    its lateral border helps bound the lumbar triangle.
clinical:
  - title: Thoracodorsal nerve injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Injury during **axillary lymph node dissection** (the nerve runs on the posterior axillary wall).
    presentation: Weak extension, adduction and medial rotation of the arm (e.g. difficulty with climbing or pushing up from a chair). No winging.
  - title: Latissimus dorsi flap
    highYield: false
    mechanism: The muscle, with its thoracodorsal pedicle, is transferred for **breast reconstruction** after mastectomy or to cover large defects.
    presentation: Usually minimal functional loss because other muscles compensate. If the thoracodorsal vessels were damaged in axillary surgery, the flap may not be possible.
pance:
  - "Latissimus dorsi: **extension + adduction + medial rotation**; **thoracodorsal nerve** (posterior cord)."
  - "Axillary dissection risks: thoracodorsal (latissimus), long thoracic (serratus, winging), intercostobrachial (medial arm numbness)."
  - "Intertubercular groove: \"lady between two majors\" — latissimus in the floor between pectoralis major and teres major."
imaging:
  mri: Tendon avulsions (rare, in athletes such as pitchers) and muscle atrophy.
  ct: Shows muscle bulk and flap viability after reconstruction (CT angiography of the pedicle).
exam:
  landmarks: Forms the posterior axillary fold with teres major.
  palpation: Palpate the posterior axillary fold while the patient coughs or presses the arm down against resistance (contraction is felt).
  testing: Resisted extension, adduction and medial rotation of the arm (e.g. pushing the elbow down against the examiner's hand).
quiz:
  - stem: Latissimus dorsi inserts on which part of the humerus?
    choices:
      - Lateral lip of the intertubercular groove
      - Floor of the intertubercular groove
      - Greater tubercle
      - Deltoid tuberosity
      - Medial epicondyle
    answer: B
    explanation: Latissimus dorsi inserts in the **floor** of the intertubercular groove, between pectoralis major (lateral lip) and teres major (medial lip).
  - stem: After axillary lymph node dissection, a patient has weak extension and adduction of the arm but no scapular winging. Which nerve was injured?
    choices:
      - Long thoracic nerve
      - Thoracodorsal nerve
      - Axillary nerve
      - Musculocutaneous nerve
      - Suprascapular nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: The **thoracodorsal nerve** supplies latissimus dorsi. Long thoracic injury would cause medial winging.
  - stem: Which nerve roots supply latissimus dorsi through the thoracodorsal nerve?
    choices:
      - C3–C5
      - C5–C6
      - C6–C8
      - C8–T1
      - T7–T12
    answer: C
    explanation: The thoracodorsal nerve carries **C6–C8** from the posterior cord.
