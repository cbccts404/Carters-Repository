name: Right coronary artery
type: artery
region: thorax
subregion: Heart & pericardium
taName: Arteria coronaria dextra
aka: [RCA]
summary: Arises from the right aortic sinus and runs in the right atrioventricular groove. It usually supplies the SA and AV nodes, the right ventricle and (in right dominance) the inferior wall; occlusion causes inferior MI.
tags: [heart, coronary, cardiology]
systems: [cardiovascular]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Right atrioventricular (coronary) groove, then the posterior interventricular groove (in right-dominant hearts).
  origin: Right aortic sinus of the ascending aorta (above the right cusp of the [[aortic-valve]]).
  course: >-
    Passes between the right auricle and the pulmonary trunk, descends in the **right atrioventricular groove** around
    the acute margin of the heart to the posterior surface. In a **right-dominant** circulation (most people) it gives
    the **posterior interventricular (posterior descending) artery** at the crux.
  branches:
    - "**SA nodal artery** (in about 60%)"
    - "Conus artery (to the RV outflow tract)"
    - "**Right marginal (acute marginal) artery**: along the inferior (acute) margin, supplying the RV"
    - "**AV nodal artery** (from the dominant artery, usually the RCA)"
    - "**Posterior interventricular (posterior descending) artery**: in right dominance; supplies the posterior third of the septum and the inferior LV wall, and the posteromedial papillary muscle"
    - "Posterolateral branches to the LV (in right dominance)"
  supplies: >-
    Right atrium, most of the right ventricle, SA node (about 60%), AV node (about 80%), the posterior third of the
    interventricular septum and the inferior (diaphragmatic) LV wall (in right dominance), and the posteromedial
    papillary muscle. {{verify: dominance and nodal supply percentages vary between sources}}
  anastomoses: Small anastomoses with branches of the [[left-coronary-artery]] (e.g. septal and apical collaterals), usually inadequate for sudden occlusion.
  notes: >-
    **Coronary dominance** is defined by which artery gives the posterior interventricular artery: **right dominant**
    in most people (roughly 70–80%), left dominant (from the [[circumflex-artery]]) in a minority, and codominant in the
    rest. {{verify}}
clinical:
  - title: Inferior myocardial infarction
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Occlusion of the RCA (in right-dominant circulation).
    presentation: >-
      **ST elevation in II, III and aVF**, with reciprocal ST depression in I and aVL. **Bradycardia and AV block** (nodal
      ischemia). Possible **posteromedial papillary muscle rupture** (acute MR).
  - title: Right ventricular infarction
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Proximal RCA occlusion (before the acute marginal branches) with an inferior MI.
    presentation: >-
      **Hypotension, clear lungs, elevated JVP** (the preload-dependent RV fails). ST elevation in **right-sided leads
      (V4R)**. Avoid **nitrates** and other preload reducers; give IV fluids.
pance:
  - "Inferior MI: **II, III, aVF** → **RCA** (usually)."
  - "Inferior MI + hypotension + clear lungs + JVD → **RV infarct**: get V4R; **avoid nitrates**, give fluids."
  - "RCA usually supplies the **SA and AV nodes** → bradycardia and heart block."
  - "Right dominance = posterior interventricular artery comes from the RCA."
imaging:
  ct: Coronary CT angiography shows stenosis, plaque, anomalous origin (e.g. an RCA from the left sinus coursing between the aorta and pulmonary trunk, a cause of sudden death in young athletes), and dominance.
  ultrasound: Echo shows **regional wall motion abnormalities** of the inferior wall and RV free wall in RCA ischemia.
  mri: Late gadolinium enhancement localizes infarcted territory.
  keyViews:
    - "**Invasive coronary angiography**: the definitive study (LAO view shows the RCA well)"
    - "12-lead ECG with right-sided leads (V4R)"
exam:
  testing: In inferior MI, check heart rate and rhythm (bradycardia, AV block), JVP and lung fields (RV infarct), and listen for a new holosystolic murmur (papillary muscle rupture).
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 64-year-old man with inferior ST elevation (II, III, aVF) becomes hypotensive after sublingual nitroglycerin.
      His lungs are clear and his JVP is elevated. What is the most likely explanation?
    choices:
      - Left main occlusion
      - Right ventricular infarction
      - Acute aortic regurgitation
      - Tension pneumothorax
      - Anterior wall rupture
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A proximal **RCA** occlusion can infarct the **right ventricle**, which depends on preload. Nitrates reduce preload
      and cause hypotension. Treat with IV fluids and confirm with right-sided leads (V4R).
  - stem: Coronary dominance is determined by which artery gives rise to which branch?
    choices:
      - The artery supplying the SA node
      - The artery supplying the anterior two-thirds of the septum
      - The artery giving rise to the posterior interventricular (posterior descending) artery
      - The artery giving rise to the left marginal artery
      - The artery arising from the larger aortic sinus
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Dominance** is defined by the origin of the **posterior interventricular artery**: the RCA in right dominance
      (most people) and the circumflex in left dominance.
  - stem: Which ECG leads show ST elevation in an inferior wall MI?
    choices:
      - V1–V2
      - V3–V4
      - I, aVL, V5–V6
      - II, III, aVF
      - aVR only
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      **II, III and aVF** face the inferior wall, usually supplied by the RCA. V1–V4 are septal/anterior (LAD); I, aVL,
      V5–V6 are lateral (circumflex).
  - stem: Which branch of the RCA runs along the inferior (acute) margin of the heart to supply the right ventricle?
    choices:
      - Conus artery
      - SA nodal artery
      - Right (acute) marginal artery
      - Obtuse marginal artery
      - Diagonal artery
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **right (acute) marginal artery** runs along the acute margin and supplies the RV. Obtuse marginals come from
      the circumflex, and diagonals from the LAD.
  - stem: In an inferior STEMI, in which leads is reciprocal ST depression usually seen?
    choices:
      - V1–V2
      - V4R
      - V5–V6
      - I and aVL
      - aVR only
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      Inferior MI (II, III, aVF) typically shows **reciprocal ST depression in I and aVL**. ST elevation in V4R suggests
      right ventricular involvement.
