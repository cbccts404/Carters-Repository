name: Pulmonary arteries
type: artery
region: thorax
subregion: Heart & pericardium
taName: Truncus pulmonalis; arteriae pulmonales dextra et sinistra
aka: [Pulmonary trunk, Right pulmonary artery, Left pulmonary artery]
summary: The pulmonary trunk and its right and left branches carry deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle to the lungs. They're where pulmonary emboli lodge.
tags: [lungs, vascular]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Middle mediastinum (pulmonary trunk within the pericardium) to the hila of the lungs.
  origin: Right ventricle (conus arteriosus), through the [[pulmonary-valve]].
  course: >-
    The **pulmonary trunk** ascends to the left of the ascending aorta and divides, at about the level of the sternal
    angle (T4–T5) under the aortic arch, into the right and left pulmonary arteries. The **right pulmonary artery**
    passes horizontally **behind the ascending aorta and SVC** to the right hilum. The **left pulmonary artery** is
    shorter, passes anterior to the descending aorta and left main bronchus, and is connected to the aortic arch by the
    **ligamentum arteriosum**.
  branches:
    - Lobar and segmental arteries accompanying the bronchi into the bronchopulmonary segments
  supplies: Alveolar capillaries for gas exchange (not the nutritive supply of the lung tissue itself, which comes from the bronchial arteries).
  relations: >-
    At the hilum: the pulmonary artery lies **anterior** to the bronchus on the right and **superior** on the left
    (RALS). The ligamentum arteriosum lies between the left pulmonary artery and the arch, with the left recurrent
    laryngeal nerve hooking around the arch lateral to it.
  notes: >-
    **Fetal ductus arteriosus**: in the fetus it shunts blood from the pulmonary trunk to the aorta, bypassing the lungs.
    It normally closes functionally after birth (rising oxygen, falling prostaglandins) to become the **ligamentum
    arteriosum**.
clinical:
  - title: Pulmonary embolism
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Thromboembolus, usually from deep veins of the legs or pelvis. A large clot at the bifurcation is a **saddle
      embolus**. Risk factors (Virchow triad): stasis, hypercoagulability, endothelial injury.
    presentation: >-
      Sudden dyspnea, **pleuritic chest pain**, tachycardia, hypoxemia, sometimes hemoptysis or syncope. Massive PE causes
      hypotension and RV failure. ECG most often shows **sinus tachycardia**; S1Q3T3 is classic but uncommon.
  - title: Patent ductus arteriosus
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Failure of the ductus arteriosus to close. More common in **preterm infants** and with **congenital rubella**.
    presentation: >-
      **Continuous "machine-like" murmur** at the left infraclavicular area, with bounding pulses and wide pulse pressure.
      **Indomethacin (or ibuprofen/acetaminophen)** promotes closure; **prostaglandin E1** keeps it open in
      ductal-dependent lesions. Large untreated PDAs can cause Eisenmenger syndrome (differential cyanosis of the lower
      body).
  - title: Pulmonary hypertension
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Elevated pulmonary arterial pressure (left heart disease, lung disease/hypoxia, chronic thromboembolism, pulmonary arterial hypertension).
    presentation: Dyspnea, fatigue, a **loud P2**, RV heave, and signs of right heart failure. Enlarged pulmonary arteries on imaging.
pance:
  - "PE workup: **CT pulmonary angiography**; D-dimer to rule out in low/intermediate pretest probability; V/Q scan if CT contrast is contraindicated."
  - "PE on ECG: most common = **sinus tachycardia**; S1Q3T3 classic but uncommon."
  - "PDA: **continuous machine-like murmur**; indomethacin closes, **PGE1 keeps open**."
  - "Ligamentum arteriosum = remnant of the ductus arteriosus; left recurrent laryngeal nerve hooks nearby."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    Often normal in PE (useful to exclude other causes). Classic but uncommon signs: **Hampton hump** (peripheral
    wedge-shaped opacity from infarction) and **Westermark sign** (regional oligemia). Enlarged central pulmonary
    arteries in pulmonary hypertension.
  ct: >-
    **CT pulmonary angiography (CTPA)** shows filling defects in the pulmonary arteries. Also shows RV dilation
    (RV/LV ratio greater than 1 indicates strain) and a pulmonary trunk diameter larger than the ascending aorta
    (pulmonary hypertension).
  ultrasound: >-
    Echo in massive PE: **RV dilation**, hypokinesis with apical sparing (McConnell sign), septal flattening (D-shaped LV),
    and sometimes clot in transit. Compression ultrasound of the leg veins finds the source DVT.
  keyViews:
    - "CT pulmonary angiography"
    - "V/Q scan (pregnancy, contrast allergy, renal failure)"
exam:
  landmarks: The pulmonary trunk lies behind the left 2nd intercostal space (pulmonic area).
  testing: Listen for a loud P2 (pulmonary hypertension) and a continuous murmur (PDA). In suspected PE, assess vital signs, oxygen saturation, and signs of DVT.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      Three days after hip replacement, a 67-year-old woman has sudden dyspnea and pleuritic chest pain. HR is 118/min
      and SpO₂ is 89%. Renal function is normal. What is the best diagnostic test?
    choices:
      - D-dimer only
      - CT pulmonary angiography
      - Chest radiograph only
      - Coronary angiography
      - Pulmonary function tests
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      With a **high pretest probability** of **PE** (recent surgery, sudden hypoxic dyspnea), go straight to **CTPA**. A
      D-dimer is most useful to rule out PE when probability is low.
  - stem: A premature infant has a continuous machine-like murmur at the left infraclavicular area and bounding pulses. Which drug is used to close the lesion?
    choices:
      - Prostaglandin E1
      - Indomethacin
      - Digoxin
      - Propranolol
      - Furosemide
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Patent ductus arteriosus**: **indomethacin** (a prostaglandin synthesis inhibitor) promotes closure.
      **Prostaglandin E1** keeps the ductus open when the circulation depends on it.
  - stem: Which structure connects the left pulmonary artery to the arch of the aorta in adults?
    choices:
      - Ligamentum venosum
      - Ligamentum arteriosum
      - Ligamentum teres
      - Pulmonary ligament
      - Medial umbilical ligament
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **ligamentum arteriosum** is the fibrous remnant of the **ductus arteriosus**. The ligamentum venosum is the
      remnant of the ductus venosus; the ligamentum teres is the remnant of the umbilical vein.
