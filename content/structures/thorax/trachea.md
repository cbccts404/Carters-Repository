name: Trachea
type: organ
region: thorax
subregion: Mediastinum
taName: Trachea
aka: [Windpipe]
summary: Cartilage-reinforced airway from the larynx (C6) to its bifurcation at the carina (about T4–T5). Its midline position is an important exam and radiographic sign.
tags: [airway, mediastinum]
systems: [pulmonary]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Neck and superior [[mediastinum]], in the midline anterior to the [[esophagus]].
  parts:
    - "Begins at the inferior border of the **cricoid cartilage (C6)**"
    - "16–20 **C-shaped hyaline cartilage rings**, open posteriorly, bridged by the **trachealis** (smooth muscle)"
    - "**Carina**: internal ridge at the bifurcation into the right and left [[main-bronchi]], at about the level of the **sternal angle (T4–T5)**"
  relations: >-
    **Anterior** (neck): isthmus of the thyroid gland (over rings 2–4), inferior thyroid veins. **Anterior**
    (thorax): manubrium, thymus remnant, left brachiocephalic vein, brachiocephalic trunk and left common carotid
    artery, arch of the aorta. **Posterior**: [[esophagus]]. **Lateral**: thyroid lobes and carotid sheaths (neck), the
    recurrent laryngeal nerves in the tracheoesophageal grooves, the aortic arch (left) and the [[azygos-vein]] (right).
  bloodSupply: Inferior thyroid arteries (cervical part); bronchial arteries (thoracic part).
  innervation: Vagus nerve and recurrent laryngeal nerves (sensory and parasympathetic); sympathetic trunk. The carina is highly sensitive (cough reflex).
clinical:
  - title: Tracheal deviation
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      **Pushed away** by tension pneumothorax, a large pleural effusion or a mass. **Pulled toward** by atelectasis or
      after pneumonectomy, or by fibrosis.
    presentation: Palpable shift of the trachea in the suprasternal notch; mediastinal shift on CXR.
  - title: Endotracheal intubation
    highYield: true
    mechanism: A tube placed through the glottis into the trachea.
    presentation: >-
      The tip should lie a few centimeters above the carina. Too deep usually means a **right main bronchus**
      intubation, with left lung collapse and absent left breath sounds. Confirm with end-tidal CO₂, auscultation
      and chest X-ray. {{verify: target tip position is usually quoted as about 3–5 cm above the carina in adults}}
  - title: Tracheoesophageal fistula with esophageal atresia
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Failure of separation of the foregut into the trachea and esophagus. The most common type is proximal esophageal atresia with a distal TE fistula.
    presentation: Newborn with drooling, choking or coughing with feeds, inability to pass an NG tube (it coils on X-ray), and a gas-filled stomach if a distal fistula is present. Associated with VACTERL.
  - title: Tracheostomy and tracheal stenosis
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Surgical airway below the cricoid; stenosis can follow prolonged intubation or tracheostomy.
    presentation: Stenosis causes progressive dyspnea and stridor, sometimes misdiagnosed as asthma.
pance:
  - "Trachea: **C6 → T4/T5** (carina at the sternal angle)."
  - "Tension pneumothorax pushes the trachea **away**; atelectasis pulls it **toward**."
  - "Too-deep ETT → right main bronchus; absent breath sounds on the **left**."
  - "Newborn with drooling, choking on feeds, NG tube coiling → esophageal atresia ± TE fistula."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    The tracheal air column should be midline, projecting over the spinous processes on a non-rotated PA film. Check
    **ETT tip position** relative to the carina. A widened carinal angle can suggest left atrial enlargement or
    subcarinal lymphadenopathy.
  ct: Stenosis, tumors, tracheomalacia (dynamic expiratory CT), and extrinsic compression (goiter, mass).
  ultrasound: >-
    Point-of-care airway ultrasound: the tracheal rings appear as curved hypoechoic arcs anterior to a bright air
    line. It can confirm ETT placement (a single air–mucosa interface, with the esophagus not distended) and identify
    the cricothyroid membrane for emergency airway access.
  keyViews:
    - "PA chest radiograph (tracheal position, ETT tip)"
    - "CT neck/chest for airway narrowing"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    Palpable in the **suprasternal (jugular) notch** in the midline. Above it, the cricoid cartilage and the
    cricothyroid membrane (between the thyroid and cricoid cartilages) are the landmarks for emergency
    cricothyrotomy.
  palpation: Place a finger in the suprasternal notch and feel the space on each side of the trachea; asymmetry means deviation.
  testing: Auscultate over the trachea for stridor and normal tracheal (loud, harsh) breath sounds.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      After emergency intubation, breath sounds are present on the right and absent on the left. End-tidal CO₂ is
      detected. What is the most likely problem?
    choices:
      - Esophageal intubation
      - Left tension pneumothorax
      - Right main bronchus intubation
      - Tracheal stenosis
      - Bronchospasm
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      A tube advanced too far usually enters the **right main bronchus** (wider and more vertical), ventilating only the
      right lung. Withdraw the tube and reconfirm. CO₂ detection rules out esophageal intubation.
  - stem: At which vertebral level does the trachea bifurcate?
    choices:
      - C6
      - T1
      - T4–T5
      - T8
      - T10
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **carina** lies at about **T4–T5**, at the level of the sternal angle. The trachea begins at C6 (cricoid).
  - stem: A patient's trachea is shifted to the right. Breath sounds and percussion over the left hemithorax are decreased and dull, and the chest X-ray shows a white-out of the left hemithorax. What is the most likely cause?
    choices:
      - Left lower lobe atelectasis
      - Large left pleural effusion
      - Left pneumonectomy
      - Right tension pneumothorax
      - Right upper lobe collapse
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **large left pleural effusion** pushes the trachea and mediastinum **away** (to the right). Left-sided
      atelectasis or pneumonectomy would pull the trachea **toward** the white-out.
  - stem: What bridges the posterior gap in the C-shaped tracheal cartilages?
    choices:
      - The thyroid isthmus
      - The esophagus
      - Elastic cartilage
      - The trachealis (smooth muscle)
      - The posterior longitudinal ligament
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      The trachea has 16–20 **C-shaped hyaline cartilage rings**, **open posteriorly** and bridged by the **trachealis**
      smooth muscle, where the trachea lies against the esophagus.
  - stem: >-
      A newborn drools, chokes with feeds, and a nasogastric tube coils in the upper chest on X-ray. The stomach is
      filled with gas. What is the most likely anomaly?
    choices:
      - Isolated tracheoesophageal fistula without atresia
      - Pyloric stenosis
      - Esophageal atresia with a distal tracheoesophageal fistula
      - Isolated esophageal atresia without a fistula
      - Duodenal atresia
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The most common type is **proximal esophageal atresia with a distal TE fistula**: the NG tube coils in the blind
      pouch, and air reaches the stomach through the fistula. Isolated atresia leaves a gasless abdomen. It is
      associated with VACTERL.
