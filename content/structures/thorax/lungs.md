anatomy:
  location: Each lung occupies its pleural cavity on either side of the mediastinum, from the apex (above the clavicle) to the base on the diaphragm.
  parts:
    - "**Right lung**: superior, middle and inferior lobes, separated by the **oblique** and **horizontal** fissures; 10 bronchopulmonary segments"
    - "**Left lung**: superior and inferior lobes separated by the **oblique** fissure; the **lingula** (part of the superior lobe) is the counterpart of the right middle lobe; **cardiac notch**; 8–10 segments (some fused)"
    - "**Hilum (root)**: [[main-bronchi|main bronchus]], pulmonary artery, two pulmonary veins, bronchial vessels, nerves and lymphatics"
    - "Apex, base (diaphragmatic surface), costal and mediastinal surfaces"
  relations: >-
    **Hilum arrangement**: the pulmonary veins are the most anterior and inferior. On the **right**, the
    eparterial (superior lobe) bronchus lies superior to the pulmonary artery. On the **left**, the pulmonary artery is
    most superior. Mnemonic **RALS**: the pulmonary artery is **R**ight **A**nterior, **L**eft **S**uperior to the
    bronchus. The mediastinal surface bears impressions of the heart, the [[thoracic-aorta]] (left) and the
    [[superior-vena-cava]], [[azygos-vein]] and [[esophagus]] (right).
  bloodSupply: >-
    **Functional**: [[pulmonary-arteries]] (deoxygenated blood for gas exchange). **Nutritive**: bronchial arteries
    (from the thoracic aorta) supplying the bronchi, connective tissue and visceral pleura.
  venousDrainage: Pulmonary veins (usually two per side) to the left atrium. Bronchial veins to the azygos system (part of the flow joins the pulmonary veins).
  lymphatics: Pulmonary → bronchopulmonary (hilar) → tracheobronchial (including subcarinal) → bronchomediastinal trunks.
  innervation: >-
    Pulmonary plexuses. **Vagus** (parasympathetic: bronchoconstriction, secretion) and **sympathetic** trunk
    (bronchodilation). Visceral afferents for cough and stretch reflexes. The lungs and visceral pleura are
    insensitive to pain.
  notes: >-
    **Surface markings.** Apex: 2–3 cm above the medial third of the clavicle. **Oblique fissure**: from about the
    T2–T3 spinous process posteriorly, around the chest to the 6th costal cartilage anteriorly (roughly following the
    medial border of the abducted scapula). **Horizontal fissure (right)**: along the **4th rib/costal cartilage**
    from the sternum to the mid-axillary line. **Inferior border**: 6th rib (midclavicular), 8th rib (mid-axillary),
    10th rib (posteriorly). So the **lower lobes lie mostly posteriorly** and the **right middle lobe
    anteriorly**. {{verify: surface projections vary between texts}}
clinical:
  - title: Lobar pneumonia and aspiration
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Infection of the alveoli. **Aspiration** favors the **right** lung because the right main bronchus is wider and
      more vertical: **upright** → basal segments of the right lower lobe; **supine** → the posterior segment of the
      right upper lobe and the superior segment of the right lower lobe.
    presentation: >-
      Fever, productive cough and pleuritic pain. Over the consolidation: **dullness, increased fremitus, bronchial
      breath sounds, egophony, crackles**.
  - title: Lung cancer by location
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      **Central**: squamous cell and small cell carcinoma (associated with smoking). **Peripheral**: adenocarcinoma (the
      most common type overall and in non-smokers). **Apical (Pancoast)** tumors invade the [[brachial-plexus]] and
      sympathetic chain.
    presentation: >-
      Cough, hemoptysis, weight loss. Local invasion causes hoarseness (left recurrent laryngeal nerve), an
      elevated hemidiaphragm ([[phrenic-nerve]]), **SVC syndrome**, or **Horner syndrome** (Pancoast). Paraneoplastic
      syndromes include SIADH and Cushing syndrome (small cell) and hypercalcemia (squamous).
  - title: Pulmonary embolism
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Thrombus (usually from deep leg veins) lodges in the [[pulmonary-arteries]].
    presentation: Sudden dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain, tachycardia, hypoxemia; massive PE causes hypotension and RV strain.
pance:
  - "Right lung: 3 lobes, 2 fissures. Left lung: 2 lobes + lingula, 1 fissure, cardiac notch."
  - "Aspirated material → **right lung** (upright: RLL basal; supine: RUL posterior / RLL superior segments)."
  - "**Silhouette sign**: RML pneumonia obscures the right heart border; lingular pneumonia obscures the left heart border; lower lobe disease obscures the diaphragm."
  - "Central tumors: squamous and small cell; peripheral: adenocarcinoma."
  - "Hilum: **RALS** (pulmonary artery Right Anterior, Left Superior)."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    **PA and lateral** chest radiographs. Use the **silhouette sign** to localize disease: loss of the right heart
    border = right middle lobe; loss of the left heart border = lingula; loss of the hemidiaphragm = lower lobe. The
    lateral view shows the fissures and retrocardiac/posterior lower-lobe opacities. Look for air bronchograms
    (consolidation), volume loss (atelectasis: shift toward), masses, and hyperinflation (COPD: flattened diaphragms).
  ct: >-
    CT chest defines nodules, masses, interstitial disease and lymphadenopathy. **CT pulmonary angiography (CTPA)** is
    the main test for PE. Low-dose CT is used for lung cancer screening in eligible high-risk smokers.
  ultrasound: >-
    Lung ultrasound shows consolidation ("hepatization", dynamic air bronchograms), interstitial fluid (**B-lines**,
    e.g. in pulmonary edema), effusion, and pneumothorax (absent sliding).
  keyViews:
    - "PA and lateral chest radiograph"
    - "CT chest (CTPA for PE)"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    **Anteriorly** you mostly hear the upper lobes (and the right middle lobe below the 4th rib on the right).
    **Posteriorly** you mostly hear the lower lobes (below about T3). **Laterally** you hear upper and lower lobes, plus
    the middle lobe on the right.
  palpation: Chest expansion (thumbs at the 10th ribs posteriorly), tactile fremitus.
  testing: >-
    Percussion (resonant, dull, hyperresonant) and diaphragmatic excursion. Auscultation (vesicular, bronchial and
    bronchovesicular breath sounds; crackles, wheezes, rhonchi, rubs). Transmitted voice sounds (egophony "E"→"A",
    bronchophony, whispered pectoriloquy) suggest consolidation.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      An intoxicated man found lying supine develops fever and a foul-smelling productive cough 3 days later. Which lung
      segment is most likely involved?
    choices:
      - Lingula
      - Basal segments of the left lower lobe
      - Posterior segment of the right upper lobe
      - Medial segment of the right middle lobe
      - Apical segment of the left upper lobe
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      In the **supine** position, aspirated material flows to the dependent **posterior segment of the right upper
      lobe** and the **superior segment of the right lower lobe**. Upright aspiration goes to the right lower lobe
      basal segments.
  - stem: On a PA chest radiograph, a patient's right heart border is obscured by an opacity while the right hemidiaphragm is sharply seen. Where is the pneumonia?
    choices:
      - Right upper lobe
      - Right middle lobe
      - Right lower lobe
      - Lingula
      - Left lower lobe
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **right middle lobe** lies against the right heart border, so consolidation there obscures it (**silhouette
      sign**). Right lower lobe disease obscures the hemidiaphragm instead.
  - stem: A patient has dullness to percussion, increased tactile fremitus and bronchial breath sounds over the right lower chest posteriorly. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Pleural effusion
      - Pneumothorax
      - Lobar consolidation
      - Emphysema
      - Atelectasis with bronchial obstruction
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Consolidation** transmits vibrations and sounds better: dull percussion, **increased fremitus**, bronchial
      breath sounds and egophony. An effusion is dull with decreased fremitus.
  - stem: Where does the horizontal fissure of the right lung lie anteriorly?
    choices:
      - Along the 2nd rib
      - Along the 4th rib/costal cartilage
      - Along the 6th rib
      - Along the 8th rib
      - It lies only on the posterior surface
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **horizontal fissure** follows the **4th rib and costal cartilage** from the sternum to about the mid-axillary
      line, where it meets the oblique fissure.
