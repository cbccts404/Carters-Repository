anatomy:
  location: Neck → superior and posterior [[mediastinum]] → through the esophageal hiatus of the [[diaphragm]] → abdomen.
  parts:
    - "Begins at the inferior border of the cricoid cartilage (**C6**) as the continuation of the pharynx; ends at the cardiac orifice of the stomach (about **T11**). About 25 cm long"
    - "**Muscle**: upper third skeletal, middle third mixed, lower third smooth"
    - "**Upper esophageal sphincter** (cricopharyngeus) and a physiological **lower esophageal sphincter** at the GE junction"
    - "**Three constrictions** (where foreign bodies lodge): **cervical** (cricopharyngeus, about 15 cm from the incisors), **thoracic** (crossed by the **aortic arch** and **left main bronchus**, about 25 cm), **diaphragmatic** (esophageal hiatus, about 40 cm). {{verify: distances from the incisors vary between sources}}"
  relations: >-
    Posterior to the [[trachea]] and the **left atrium** (enlargement of the LA indents it). The [[thoracic-aorta]]
    lies to its left above and then passes posterior to it. The [[thoracic-duct]] and [[azygos-vein]] lie posterior
    and to the right. The vagus nerves form the esophageal plexus on its surface, then the anterior (mostly left
    vagus) and posterior (mostly right vagus) vagal trunks pass with it through the hiatus at **T10**.
  bloodSupply: "**Cervical**: inferior thyroid arteries. **Thoracic**: esophageal branches of the thoracic aorta (and bronchial arteries). **Abdominal**: left gastric artery (and left inferior phrenic)."
  venousDrainage: >-
    Cervical → inferior thyroid veins. Thoracic → **azygos** and hemiazygos veins. Abdominal part → **left gastric vein
    (portal system)**. The submucosal plexus of the distal esophagus is a **portosystemic anastomosis**.
  lymphatics: Deep cervical, posterior mediastinal, and left gastric/celiac nodes by region. Lymph drains longitudinally, so tumors spread widely.
  innervation: Vagus nerves (esophageal plexus, recurrent laryngeal nerves to the cervical part) and sympathetic trunks.
clinical:
  - title: Esophageal varices
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      **Portal hypertension** (usually cirrhosis) diverts portal blood through the **left gastric vein → esophageal
      submucosal veins → azygos vein**, dilating the distal esophageal veins.
    presentation: "**Painless massive hematemesis** in a patient with liver disease. Life-threatening; managed with resuscitation, octreotide, antibiotics and urgent endoscopic band ligation."
  - title: Boerhaave syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      **Full-thickness rupture**, usually of the **distal left posterolateral** wall, from a sudden rise in pressure
      during forceful vomiting (often after binge drinking or eating).
    presentation: >-
      Severe retrosternal/epigastric pain after vomiting, subcutaneous emphysema, and **Hamman crunch**
      (pneumomediastinum). Left pleural effusion. Mackler triad: vomiting, chest pain, subcutaneous emphysema.
  - title: Mallory–Weiss tear
    highYield: true
    mechanism: "**Mucosal (partial-thickness)** laceration at the GE junction from retching or vomiting."
    presentation: Hematemesis after repeated retching; usually self-limited.
  - title: Achalasia
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Loss of inhibitory neurons in the myenteric (Auerbach) plexus → failure of LES relaxation and absent peristalsis. Chagas disease is a secondary cause.
    presentation: Progressive dysphagia to **both solids and liquids**, regurgitation, weight loss. Barium swallow shows a **"bird's beak"**. Diagnosed with manometry.
  - title: Esophageal cancer
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      **Squamous cell carcinoma** (upper/middle third; smoking, alcohol, hot beverages). **Adenocarcinoma** (distal
      third; **GERD → Barrett esophagus**, obesity).
    presentation: Progressive dysphagia (solids → liquids), weight loss, odynophagia.
pance:
  - "Three constrictions: **cricopharyngeus, aortic arch/left main bronchus, diaphragm** → foreign body impaction sites."
  - "**Boerhaave** = full-thickness rupture (emergency); **Mallory–Weiss** = mucosal tear."
  - "Varices: left gastric (portal) ↔ esophageal/azygos (systemic)."
  - "Achalasia: dysphagia to solids **and** liquids, bird's beak on barium, manometry for diagnosis."
  - "Barrett esophagus → **adenocarcinoma** (distal); smoking/alcohol → **squamous cell** (upper/middle)."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    Swallowed coins in the esophagus appear **en face (as a disk) on a frontal view** (a coin in the trachea tends to
    appear on edge). Pneumomediastinum, left effusion and subcutaneous air in Boerhaave. An air–fluid level behind the
    heart can mean a hiatal hernia or achalasia.
  ct: >-
    CT (with oral water-soluble contrast) for suspected perforation: extraluminal air or contrast, mediastinal fluid.
    Cancer staging.
  ultrasound: Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) stages the depth of tumor invasion and nearby nodes.
  keyViews:
    - "**Barium esophagram**: bird's beak (achalasia), corkscrew (diffuse spasm), strictures, rings, webs"
    - "**Water-soluble contrast study** first when perforation is suspected (barium can cause mediastinitis)"
    - "Endoscopy (EGD) for mucosal diagnosis, biopsy and variceal banding"
exam:
  landmarks: The cervical part lies behind the trachea, slightly to the left, where it is approached surgically.
  palpation: Crepitus in the neck or chest wall (subcutaneous emphysema) with perforation.
  testing: Auscultate for **Hamman crunch** (a crunching sound synchronous with the heartbeat) in pneumomediastinum.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      After a night of heavy drinking and repeated forceful vomiting, a 45-year-old man has severe chest pain and crepitus
      over the neck. CXR shows pneumomediastinum and a left pleural effusion. What is the diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Mallory–Weiss tear
      - Boerhaave syndrome
      - Esophageal varices
      - Achalasia
      - Diffuse esophageal spasm
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Boerhaave syndrome** is a **full-thickness** esophageal rupture, usually distal left posterolateral, causing
      mediastinal air and a left effusion. It's a surgical emergency. A Mallory–Weiss tear is mucosal and presents with
      hematemesis.
  - stem: Esophageal varices in portal hypertension form at an anastomosis between which veins?
    choices:
      - Superior rectal and inferior rectal veins
      - Left gastric and esophageal veins (azygos)
      - Paraumbilical and epigastric veins
      - Splenic and left renal veins
      - Internal thoracic and intercostal veins
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **left gastric vein** (portal) anastomoses with **esophageal veins** draining to the **azygos** (systemic) in
      the distal esophagus. The other options describe rectal and umbilical anastomoses.
  - stem: A child swallowed a coin. At which site is it most likely to lodge?
    choices:
      - At the level of the carina only
      - At the cricopharyngeus (upper esophageal sphincter)
      - In the pylorus
      - In the right main bronchus
      - At the esophageal hiatus only
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **cricopharyngeal (cervical) constriction** is the narrowest point of the esophagus and the most common site of
      foreign body impaction in children. The other constrictions are at the aortic arch and the diaphragm.
  - stem: A 40-year-old woman has 1 year of progressive dysphagia to both solids and liquids with regurgitation of undigested food. Barium swallow shows a dilated esophagus with a narrow distal "bird's beak." What confirms the diagnosis?
    choices:
      - CT abdomen
      - Esophageal manometry
      - 24-hour pH monitoring
      - H. pylori stool antigen
      - Chest radiograph
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Achalasia** is confirmed by **esophageal manometry** (incomplete LES relaxation, absent peristalsis). Endoscopy is
      done to exclude pseudoachalasia from a tumor at the GE junction.
  - stem: Adenocarcinoma of the distal esophagus is most strongly associated with which condition?
    choices:
      - Achalasia
      - Plummer–Vinson syndrome
      - Esophageal varices
      - Barrett esophagus from chronic GERD
      - Mallory–Weiss tear
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      **Adenocarcinoma** arises in the **distal third**, linked to **GERD → Barrett esophagus** and obesity. **Squamous
      cell carcinoma** affects the upper and middle thirds and is linked to smoking, alcohol and hot beverages.
