---
name: Main bronchi
type: organ
region: thorax
subregion: Pleura & lungs
taName: Bronchi principales
aka: [Primary bronchi, Right main bronchus, Left main bronchus]
summary: The two airways arising at the carina. The right main bronchus is wider, shorter and more vertical, so foreign bodies and aspirate usually enter the right lung.
tags: [airway]
systems: [pulmonary]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: From the carina (about T4–T5) to the hilum of each lung.
  parts:
    - "**Right main bronchus**: wider, shorter (about 2.5 cm), more vertical; gives the right superior lobar bronchus before entering the hilum, then divides into middle and inferior lobar bronchi"
    - "**Left main bronchus**: narrower, longer (about 5 cm), more horizontal; passes **inferior to the arch of the aorta** and anterior to the [[esophagus]] and [[thoracic-aorta]]; divides into superior and inferior lobar bronchi"
    - "Lobar (secondary) bronchi → segmental (tertiary) bronchi, each supplying a **bronchopulmonary segment**"
  relations: >-
    The [[azygos-vein]] arches over the right main bronchus to reach the [[superior-vena-cava]]. The arch of the aorta
    passes over the left main bronchus. Subcarinal lymph nodes lie below the carina, and the left atrium lies
    posteroinferior to the carina.
  bloodSupply: Bronchial arteries (usually one right, two left; from the thoracic aorta or an intercostal artery). {{verify}}
  innervation: Pulmonary plexus (vagus and sympathetic).
clinical:
  - title: Foreign body aspiration
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Because the right main bronchus is wider and more vertical, inhaled objects most often lodge on the **right**. Common in toddlers (nuts, small toys).
    presentation: >-
      Sudden choking and coughing, then **unilateral wheeze**, decreased breath sounds or recurrent pneumonia on one side.
      **Unilateral hyperinflation** (air trapping) on expiratory or decubitus films if the object acts as a check
      valve.
  - title: Bronchogenic carcinoma (central)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Squamous cell and small cell carcinomas typically arise in central (main/lobar) bronchi.
    presentation: Cough, hemoptysis, postobstructive pneumonia or lobar collapse, and hilar mass on imaging.
  - title: Tracheobronchial injury
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Blunt chest trauma with rapid deceleration, usually within about 2.5 cm of the carina.
    presentation: Pneumomediastinum, subcutaneous emphysema, and a **persistent large air leak** despite a chest tube. The collapsed lung falls away from the hilum ("fallen lung" sign).
pance:
  - "Right main bronchus: **wider, shorter, more vertical** → foreign bodies and aspiration go right."
  - "Child with sudden cough and unilateral wheeze → foreign body; bronchoscopy for diagnosis and removal."
  - "Air trapping on expiratory films → check-valve foreign body."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    Most aspirated foreign bodies are **radiolucent**. Look for indirect signs: **unilateral hyperinflation** (air
    trapping, best seen on expiratory or lateral decubitus films), atelectasis, or postobstructive pneumonia.
  ct: Defines endobronchial lesions, central tumors, bronchiectasis and airway injury. Virtual bronchoscopy is possible.
  keyViews:
    - "Inspiratory and expiratory chest films (or bilateral decubitus in young children)"
    - "Bronchoscopy (rigid for foreign body removal) — diagnostic and therapeutic"
exam:
  landmarks: The bronchi lie deep behind the sternal angle and upper sternum. Bronchovesicular breath sounds are normally heard over the main bronchi (1st–2nd intercostal spaces anteriorly and between the scapulae posteriorly).
  testing: Compare breath sounds and wheeze side to side. A unilateral, fixed wheeze suggests a focal airway obstruction.
quiz:
  - stem: A 2-year-old choked while eating peanuts. He now has a persistent cough and wheezing over one side of the chest. Where is the foreign body most likely lodged?
    choices:
      - Left main bronchus
      - Right main bronchus
      - Larynx
      - Esophagus
      - Trachea above the carina
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **right main bronchus** is wider, shorter and more vertical, so aspirated foreign bodies lodge there most
      often. Confirm and remove with rigid bronchoscopy.
  - stem: Which structure arches over the right main bronchus?
    choices:
      - Arch of the aorta
      - Azygos vein
      - Left brachiocephalic vein
      - Thoracic duct
      - Pulmonary trunk
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **azygos vein** arches over the right main bronchus (root of the right lung) at about T4 to enter the SVC. The
      aortic arch passes over the **left** main bronchus.
  - stem: A child who aspirated a small toy has a chest radiograph on expiration showing the left lung hyperlucent while the right lung decreases in volume normally. What does this indicate?
    choices:
      - Right-sided pneumothorax
      - Check-valve obstruction of the left main bronchus
      - Normal expiratory film
      - Left lower lobe pneumonia
      - Right main bronchus complete obstruction with atelectasis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Air enters the left lung around the object during inspiration but can't leave on expiration (**check-valve**),
      so that side stays **hyperinflated** while the normal lung deflates. Foreign bodies occur on the left too, even
      though the right is more common.
