highYield: true

anatomy:
  location: Middle [[mediastinum]], enclosing the [[heart]] and the roots of the great vessels. Fused to the central tendon of the [[diaphragm]] below.
  parts:
    - "**Fibrous pericardium**: tough outer layer, continuous with the adventitia of the great vessels, attached to the diaphragm and sternum; limits acute dilation"
    - "**Serous pericardium, parietal layer**: lines the fibrous pericardium"
    - "**Serous pericardium, visceral layer (epicardium)**: covers the heart surface"
    - "**Pericardial cavity**: potential space between the parietal and visceral serous layers, normally containing a small amount of lubricating fluid"
    - "**Transverse pericardial sinus**: passage posterior to the ascending aorta and pulmonary trunk, anterior to the SVC. Surgeons pass a finger or ligature through it to clamp the great arteries during cardiac surgery"
    - "**Oblique pericardial sinus**: blind recess behind the left atrium, bounded by the pulmonary veins and IVC"
  relations: The [[phrenic-nerve]] and pericardiacophrenic vessels descend on each side between the fibrous pericardium and the mediastinal pleura.
  bloodSupply: Pericardiacophrenic artery (from the [[internal-thoracic-artery]]) mainly, plus musculophrenic, bronchial, esophageal and superior phrenic branches.
  innervation: >-
    **Phrenic nerves** (fibrous and parietal layers): sensory, so pain from pericarditis can be referred to the
    shoulder or trapezius ridge (C3–C5). The visceral layer (epicardium) is insensitive to pain.
    Also vagus and sympathetic fibers.
clinical:
  - title: Acute pericarditis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Inflammation of the pericardium, most often idiopathic or viral. Other causes include uremia, post-MI (including Dressler syndrome), autoimmune disease, malignancy and TB.
    presentation: >-
      Sharp, **pleuritic** chest pain that is **better sitting up and leaning forward**, worse lying flat, and may
      radiate to the trapezius ridge. **Pericardial friction rub**. ECG: **diffuse concave ST elevation with PR
      depression**.
  - title: Cardiac tamponade
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Fluid (blood, effusion) accumulating in the pericardial cavity faster than the fibrous pericardium can stretch, compressing the heart and impairing filling.
    presentation: >-
      **Beck's triad**: hypotension, **distended neck veins (JVD)**, **muffled heart sounds**. Also tachycardia,
      **pulsus paradoxus** (a drop in systolic BP of more than 10 mmHg on inspiration), and **electrical alternans** on ECG.
      Treat with **pericardiocentesis** (or surgical drainage).
  - title: Constrictive pericarditis
    highYield: false
    mechanism: A thickened, fibrotic (sometimes calcified) pericardium restricts diastolic filling. Causes include prior cardiac surgery, radiation, TB, and previous pericarditis.
    presentation: Right heart failure signs (JVD, edema, ascites), **Kussmaul sign** (JVP rises on inspiration), and a **pericardial knock** in early diastole.
pance:
  - "Pericarditis: pain relieved **leaning forward**, friction rub, **diffuse ST elevation + PR depression**."
  - "Tamponade: **Beck's triad** (hypotension, JVD, muffled heart sounds) + pulsus paradoxus + electrical alternans."
  - "Constrictive pericarditis: **Kussmaul sign**, pericardial knock, calcified pericardium."
  - "Pericardial pain can refer to the trapezius ridge via the **phrenic nerve**."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    A large effusion gives a globular **"water bottle" heart** with clear lungs. Pericardial calcification suggests
    constrictive pericarditis. A normal CXR doesn't exclude tamponade (it depends on the rate of accumulation).
  ct: Pericardial thickening (more than about 4 mm suggests constriction), calcification, and effusion characteristics. {{verify}}
  mri: Pericardial thickening, inflammation (late enhancement), and constrictive physiology (septal bounce on real-time cine).
  ultrasound: >-
    **Echocardiography is the key test.** Effusion appears as an anechoic space around the heart. **Tamponade signs**:
    **right atrial systolic collapse** and **right ventricular diastolic collapse**, a plethoric IVC without
    respiratory variation, and exaggerated respiratory variation in mitral and tricuspid inflow. The **subcostal
    view** in the FAST exam screens for pericardial fluid in trauma. Echo guides pericardiocentesis.
  keyViews:
    - "Subcostal (FAST) and parasternal long axis views"
    - "ECG: PR depression, diffuse ST elevation (pericarditis); low voltage and electrical alternans (effusion/tamponade)"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    **Subxiphoid pericardiocentesis** (the classic approach): the needle enters between the xiphoid process and the left
    costal margin and is directed toward the left shoulder at about 30–45° to the skin, ideally under echo guidance.
    {{verify: angle and direction vary between protocols; echo-guided apical and parasternal approaches are also used}}
  palpation: The apical impulse may be impalpable with a large effusion.
  testing: >-
    Auscultate for a **friction rub** (scratchy, with up to three components; best heard at the left lower sternal
    border with the patient leaning forward and holding the breath). Measure **pulsus paradoxus** with a manual cuff.
    Assess the JVP and Kussmaul sign.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 30-year-old man with a recent viral illness has sharp chest pain that improves when he sits up and leans
      forward. A scratchy sound is heard at the left sternal border. ECG shows diffuse ST elevation with PR depression.
      What is the diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Acute anterior MI
      - Acute pericarditis
      - Aortic dissection
      - Pulmonary embolism
      - Costochondritis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Positional pleuritic pain, a **friction rub**, and **diffuse ST elevation with PR depression** are classic for
      **acute pericarditis**. An MI would show territorial ST elevation with reciprocal changes.
  - stem: >-
      After a stab wound to the chest, a patient has BP 78/50, distended neck veins and distant heart sounds. Breath
      sounds are equal bilaterally. What is the most appropriate immediate intervention?
    choices:
      - Needle decompression of the left chest
      - Pericardiocentesis or surgical pericardial drainage
      - Chest tube placement
      - IV nitroglycerin
      - Emergent coronary angiography
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Beck's triad** with equal breath sounds indicates **cardiac tamponade**. Relieve it with **pericardiocentesis**
      as a temporizing measure, or surgical drainage (definitive in trauma). Tension pneumothorax would have absent
      breath sounds on one side.
  - stem: During cardiac surgery, the surgeon passes a finger posterior to the ascending aorta and pulmonary trunk to place a clamp. Which space is used?
    choices:
      - Oblique pericardial sinus
      - Transverse pericardial sinus
      - Costomediastinal recess
      - Retrosternal space
      - Pericardial cavity anterior to the right ventricle
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **transverse pericardial sinus** lies posterior to the ascending aorta and pulmonary trunk and anterior to the
      SVC, allowing the great arteries to be isolated.
  - stem: >-
      Years after mediastinal radiation, a patient has edema, ascites and distended neck veins. The JVP rises on
      inspiration, and there is an early diastolic knock. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Acute pericarditis
      - Cardiac tamponade
      - Constrictive pericarditis
      - Dilated cardiomyopathy
      - Tricuspid stenosis
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Constrictive pericarditis** (after radiation, cardiac surgery, TB or prior pericarditis) restricts diastolic
      filling, causing right heart failure signs, a **Kussmaul sign** (JVP rising on inspiration) and a **pericardial
      knock**.
  - stem: In cardiac tamponade, what does pulsus paradoxus mean?
    choices:
      - A rise in systolic BP of more than 10 mmHg on inspiration
      - A fall in systolic BP of more than 10 mmHg on inspiration
      - Alternating strong and weak beats
      - A delayed carotid upstroke
      - A wide pulse pressure with bounding pulses
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Pulsus paradoxus** is an exaggerated **fall in systolic BP (more than 10 mmHg) during inspiration**. With Beck's
      triad (hypotension, JVD, muffled heart sounds) and electrical alternans, it supports tamponade.
