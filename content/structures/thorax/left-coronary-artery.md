---
name: Left coronary artery
type: artery
region: thorax
subregion: Heart & pericardium
taName: Arteria coronaria sinistra
aka: [Left main coronary artery, LMCA]
summary: Short trunk from the left aortic sinus that divides into the anterior interventricular (LAD) and circumflex arteries, supplying most of the left ventricle.
tags: [heart, coronary, cardiology]
systems: [cardiovascular]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Between the pulmonary trunk and the left auricle, in the left part of the coronary sulcus.
  origin: Left aortic sinus of the ascending aorta (above the left cusp of the [[aortic-valve]]).
  course: >-
    A short trunk (about 1–2 cm) passing between the left auricle and the pulmonary trunk before dividing.
    {{verify: length quoted variably}}
  branches:
    - "[[anterior-interventricular-artery]] (left anterior descending, LAD)"
    - "[[circumflex-artery]] (LCx)"
    - "Sometimes a third branch, the **ramus intermedius**, between the two"
  supplies: >-
    Through its branches: most of the left ventricle, the anterior two-thirds of the interventricular septum, the left
    atrium, and part of the right ventricle near the anterior interventricular groove. In left-dominant hearts, also the
    posterior septum and inferior wall.
  termination: Bifurcates into the LAD and circumflex arteries.
clinical:
  - title: Left main coronary artery disease
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Significant stenosis or occlusion of the left main trunk puts both the LAD and circumflex territories at risk.
    presentation: >-
      Extensive anterolateral ischemia. Acute occlusion often causes **cardiogenic shock** or sudden death. ECG may show
      **ST elevation in aVR** with widespread ST depression. Significant left main disease is an indication for
      revascularization (CABG or PCI). {{verify: ECG pattern is suggestive, not specific}}
pance:
  - "Left main → LAD + circumflex; left main occlusion = most of the LV at risk."
  - "Diffuse ST depression + **ST elevation in aVR** → consider left main or severe multivessel disease."
  - "Left dominance: the posterior interventricular artery comes from the **circumflex**."
imaging:
  ct: Coronary CT angiography and calcium scoring. CT shows anomalous origins (e.g. left main from the right sinus).
  ultrasound: Echo shows extensive anterior and lateral wall motion abnormalities and reduced ejection fraction.
  keyViews:
    - "Invasive coronary angiography (RAO and LAO caudal \"spider\" view show the left main bifurcation)"
exam:
  testing: Assess for cardiogenic shock (hypotension, cool extremities, altered mental status, pulmonary edema) in suspected left main occlusion.
quiz:
  - stem: The left coronary artery divides into which two branches?
    choices:
      - Right marginal and posterior interventricular
      - Anterior interventricular and circumflex
      - SA nodal and AV nodal
      - Diagonal and obtuse marginal
      - Conus and septal
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The short left main trunk bifurcates into the **anterior interventricular (LAD)** and **circumflex** arteries.
      Diagonals come from the LAD; obtuse marginals from the circumflex.
  - stem: >-
      A patient with crushing chest pain is hypotensive. The ECG shows ST depression in most leads with ST elevation in
      aVR. Which lesion should be suspected?
    choices:
      - Isolated right coronary artery occlusion
      - Left main coronary artery occlusion or severe multivessel disease
      - Acute pericarditis
      - Isolated first diagonal branch occlusion
      - Pulmonary embolism with right heart strain
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Diffuse ST depression with ST elevation in aVR** in an unstable patient suggests **left main** or severe
      three-vessel ischemia. This needs urgent angiography.
  - stem: From which aortic sinus does the left coronary artery arise?
    choices:
      - Right aortic sinus
      - Left aortic sinus
      - Posterior (non-coronary) sinus
      - Pulmonary sinus
      - Transverse pericardial sinus
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **left coronary artery** arises from the **left aortic sinus**, the RCA from the right, and the posterior sinus
      has no coronary artery ("non-coronary cusp").
  - stem: >-
      The left coronary artery sometimes has a third branch arising between the LAD and circumflex. What is it called?
    choices:
      - Conus artery
      - Acute marginal artery
      - Ramus intermedius
      - Posterior interventricular artery
      - SA nodal artery
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      A **ramus intermedius** arises between the LAD and circumflex in some hearts. The conus and acute marginal
      arteries are branches of the RCA.
  - stem: Where does the short left main coronary trunk run before it divides?
    choices:
      - In the posterior interventricular groove
      - Behind the superior vena cava
      - In the right atrioventricular groove
      - Between the pulmonary trunk and the left auricle
      - Between the ascending aorta and the right auricle
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      The left coronary artery arises from the **left aortic sinus** and passes **between the pulmonary trunk and the
      left auricle** before dividing into the LAD and circumflex. The RCA passes between the right auricle and the
      pulmonary trunk.
