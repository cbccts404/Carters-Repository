taName: Mediastinum
summary: Central compartment of the thorax between the pleural cavities, divided into superior and inferior parts. Knowing what is in each compartment leads you to the differential for a mediastinal mass.
tags: [mediastinum]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Between the two pleural cavities, from the thoracic inlet above to the diaphragm below, and from the sternum in front to the thoracic vertebrae behind.
  boundaries:
    superior: Superior thoracic aperture (thoracic inlet)
    inferior: "[[diaphragm]]"
    anterior: "[[sternum]] and costal cartilages"
    posterior: Bodies of the thoracic vertebrae
    lateral: Mediastinal parietal pleura on each side
    internal division: >-
      The **transverse thoracic plane** through the **sternal angle and the T4/T5 disc** separates the **superior**
      mediastinum from the **inferior** mediastinum, which the pericardium divides into **anterior**, **middle** and
      **posterior** parts.
  contents:
    - "**Superior**: [[thymus]] (or remnant), great veins (brachiocephalic veins, upper [[superior-vena-cava]]), aortic arch and its branches, [[trachea]], [[esophagus]], [[thoracic-duct]], vagus and [[phrenic-nerve|phrenic]] nerves, left recurrent laryngeal nerve"
    - "**Anterior** (between the sternum and pericardium): thymus remnants in children, fat, lymph nodes, sternopericardial ligaments, branches of the internal thoracic vessels"
    - "**Middle**: [[heart]] and [[pericardium]], ascending aorta, pulmonary trunk ([[pulmonary-arteries]]), lower SVC, [[main-bronchi]] and the tracheal bifurcation, phrenic nerves"
    - "**Posterior** (behind the pericardium): descending [[thoracic-aorta]], esophagus, thoracic duct, [[azygos-vein]] and hemiazygos veins, vagus nerves, sympathetic trunks and splanchnic nerves, posterior mediastinal lymph nodes"
  communications: With the neck (through the thoracic inlet, so neck infections and air can spread into it) and the abdomen (through the diaphragmatic openings).
  notes: >-
    Radiologists increasingly use the **ITMIG three-compartment model**: **prevascular** (roughly anterior),
    **visceral** (roughly middle), and **paravertebral** (roughly posterior), based on CT landmarks. The classic masses
    map similarly.
clinical:
  - title: Mediastinal masses by compartment
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      **Anterior (4 T's)**: thymoma, teratoma/germ cell tumor, thyroid (retrosternal goiter), "terrible" lymphoma.
      **Middle**: lymphadenopathy (lymphoma, sarcoidosis, metastases, TB), bronchogenic and pericardial cysts, aortic
      aneurysm. **Posterior**: **neurogenic tumors** (schwannoma and neurofibroma in adults, neuroblastoma in children),
      esophageal lesions, descending aortic aneurysm.
    presentation: Often incidental. Compression of adjacent structures causes cough, stridor, dysphagia, SVC syndrome, hoarseness (recurrent laryngeal nerve), or Horner syndrome.
  - title: Widened mediastinum
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Aortic dissection or traumatic aortic injury (mediastinal hematoma), lymphadenopathy (lymphoma, sarcoidosis, **inhalational anthrax**), masses.
    presentation: Seen on CXR. In trauma or tearing chest pain it demands **CT angiography**.
  - title: Pneumomediastinum and mediastinitis
    highYield: false
    mechanism: >-
      **Pneumomediastinum**: alveolar rupture (asthma, Valsalva, drug inhalation), esophageal perforation, or airway
      injury. **Mediastinitis**: esophageal perforation, post-sternotomy infection, or descending spread of neck infection.
    presentation: Chest pain, subcutaneous emphysema, Hamman crunch. Mediastinitis adds fever, sepsis and a widened mediastinum; it is life-threatening.
pance:
  - "Sternal angle plane (**T4/T5**) = superior/inferior mediastinum boundary."
  - "**Anterior**: 4 T's. **Middle**: lymph nodes, cysts. **Posterior**: **neurogenic tumors**."
  - "Widened mediastinum → **aortic dissection/injury** until proven otherwise; also lymphoma and anthrax."
  - "Hamman crunch = pneumomediastinum."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    **PA and lateral CXR**. The lateral film localizes masses (retrosternal = anterior; overlying the heart = middle;
    over the spine = posterior). The **hilum overlay sign** (hilar vessels visible through a mass) suggests an anterior
    or posterior mass rather than hilar enlargement. The **cervicothoracic sign** (a mass whose upper border is sharp
    above the clavicles) places it posteriorly.
  ct: "**Contrast CT** is the main modality for localization, characterization (fat, calcium, cystic vs solid, vascular), and biopsy planning."
  mri: MRI for posterior (neurogenic) masses with possible spinal canal extension, and for distinguishing cysts and thymic hyperplasia.
  keyViews:
    - "PA and lateral CXR"
    - "CT chest with IV contrast"
exam:
  landmarks: The sternal angle marks the boundary between the superior and inferior mediastinum.
  testing: >-
    Look for signs of compression: facial swelling and neck vein distension (SVC), stridor (trachea), dysphagia
    (esophagus), hoarseness (left recurrent laryngeal nerve), ptosis and miosis (Horner syndrome, sympathetic chain).
    Palpate cervical and supraclavicular lymph nodes. Auscultate for Hamman crunch.
quiz:
  - stem: A 30-year-old man has an incidental mass on CXR that overlies the vertebral bodies on the lateral view. CT shows a well-circumscribed paravertebral mass widening a neural foramen. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Thymoma
      - Lymphoma
      - Neurogenic tumor (schwannoma)
      - Bronchogenic cyst
      - Retrosternal goiter
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      A **posterior (paravertebral)** mediastinal mass with foraminal widening is typically a **neurogenic tumor** such as
      a schwannoma or neurofibroma in adults.
  - stem: The transverse thoracic plane separating the superior and inferior mediastinum passes through which vertebral level?
    choices:
      - T1
      - T2/T3
      - T4/T5
      - T8
      - T10
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The plane through the **sternal angle** and the **T4/T5 disc** divides the superior mediastinum from the inferior.
  - stem: Which structure is located in the middle mediastinum?
    choices:
      - Thymus
      - Descending thoracic aorta
      - Sympathetic trunk
      - Heart and pericardium
      - Thoracic duct
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      The **middle mediastinum** contains the **heart and pericardium**, the roots of the great vessels, the lower SVC,
      and the main bronchi. The descending aorta, sympathetic trunks and thoracic duct are posterior; the thymus is
      superior and anterior.
