summary: Primary lymphoid organ in the anterior and superior mediastinum where T cells mature. It is large in children, involutes after puberty, and thymoma is associated with myasthenia gravis.
tags: [mediastinum, immunology]
systems: [hematologic]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Superior and anterior [[mediastinum]], behind the manubrium and in front of the great vessels and pericardium. It may extend into the neck.
  parts:
    - Two lobes, each with an outer **cortex** (immature T cells) and inner **medulla** (mature T cells, Hassall corpuscles)
    - Relatively large in infancy; replaced progressively by fat after puberty (involution)
  relations: Posterior to the [[sternum]] (manubrium); anterior to the left brachiocephalic vein, the arch of the aorta and its branches, and the pericardium.
  bloodSupply: Branches of the [[internal-thoracic-artery]] and inferior thyroid arteries.
  venousDrainage: Left brachiocephalic, internal thoracic and inferior thyroid veins.
  notes: Develops from the **third pharyngeal pouch** (endoderm), together with the inferior parathyroid glands.
clinical:
  - title: Thymoma and myasthenia gravis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Thymic epithelial tumor. A substantial proportion of thymoma patients have **myasthenia gravis**, and a minority of
      myasthenia patients have a thymoma; thymic hyperplasia is more common in MG. Other associations: pure red cell
      aplasia, hypogammaglobulinemia. {{verify: commonly quoted: ~30–50% of thymomas with MG; ~10–15% of MG patients with thymoma}}
    presentation: >-
      Anterior mediastinal mass (often incidental), or symptoms of MG: **fatigable weakness**, ptosis, diplopia, bulbar
      symptoms. All MG patients should have **chest CT** to look for thymoma. Thymectomy benefits selected patients.
  - title: DiGeorge syndrome (22q11.2 deletion)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Failure of development of the **3rd and 4th pharyngeal pouches**.
    presentation: >-
      **Thymic aplasia** (T-cell deficiency, recurrent viral/fungal infections), **hypocalcemia** (absent parathyroids,
      tetany), **conotruncal heart defects** (tetralogy of Fallot, truncus arteriosus), and facial anomalies. The
      **thymic shadow is absent** on neonatal CXR.
pance:
  - "Anterior mediastinal mass: **4 T's**: **T**hymoma, **T**eratoma (germ cell), **T**hyroid (retrosternal goiter), **T**errible lymphoma."
  - "**Myasthenia gravis** → CT chest for **thymoma**."
  - "**DiGeorge**: absent thymus (no thymic shadow), hypocalcemia, conotruncal heart defects; 22q11.2 deletion."
  - "The thymus is normally prominent on infant CXR (**sail sign**), not a mass."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    In infants the normal thymus can look like a mediastinal mass; the **"sail sign"** (a triangular thymic shadow) is
    normal. The thymic shadow is absent in DiGeorge syndrome. In adults, anterior mediastinal masses are seen on the
    lateral film as retrosternal opacities.
  ct: CT chest is the main test for thymoma (a well-defined anterior mediastinal mass) and for distinguishing it from lymphoma, germ cell tumors and goiter.
  mri: Chemical-shift MRI helps distinguish thymic hyperplasia (which contains fat and loses signal) from thymoma.
  keyViews:
    - "CT chest with contrast (anterior mediastinum)"
exam:
  testing: >-
    For MG: sustained upgaze (fatigable ptosis), the **ice pack test** (ptosis improves with cold), and repetitive
    testing of proximal muscle strength.

    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **DiGeorge syndrome** (22q11.2 deletion): the **3rd and 4th pharyngeal pouches** fail to develop, so the thymus

    explanation: >-
      **Neurogenic tumors** arise from the sympathetic chain and nerve roots in the **posterior** mediastinum. The anterior
      "4 T's" are thymoma, teratoma, thyroid, and "terrible" lymphoma.
