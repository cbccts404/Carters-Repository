name: Pectoralis major
type: muscle
region: thorax
subregion: Thoracic wall
taName: Musculus pectoralis major
summary: Large fan-shaped muscle of the anterior chest that adducts and medially rotates the arm. It forms the anterior axillary fold and lies deep to the breast.
tags: [thoracic-wall, breast]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Anterior thoracic wall, deep to the [[breast]], forming the anterior wall of the [[axilla]].
  group: Anterior axioappendicular (pectoral) muscles.
  origin: >-
    **Clavicular head:** anterior surface of the medial half of the [[clavicle]]. **Sternocostal head:** anterior
    surface of the [[sternum]], the superior six costal cartilages, and the aponeurosis of the external oblique.
  insertion: Lateral lip of the intertubercular groove (crest of the greater tubercle) of the [[humerus]].
  innervation: "Lateral and medial pectoral nerves (clavicular head mainly C5–C6; sternocostal head C7, C8, T1) {{verify}}"
  bloodSupply: Pectoral branches of the thoracoacromial artery (from the [[axillary-artery]]), with contributions from perforating branches of the [[internal-thoracic-artery]].
  action:
    - Adducts and medially rotates the humerus
    - The clavicular head flexes the arm; the sternocostal head extends it from a flexed position
    - Draws the scapula anteriorly and inferiorly (with the arm fixed, acts as an accessory muscle of inspiration)
  relations: >-
    Its inferolateral border forms the **anterior axillary fold**. The breast lies on the pectoral fascia, separated by
    the retromammary space. The **deltopectoral groove** (cephalic vein) separates it from deltoid. Pectoralis minor lies
    deep to it.
clinical:
  - title: Pectoralis major tendon rupture
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Eccentric overload, classically during **bench pressing**, usually in young men (anabolic steroid use is a risk factor).
    presentation: >-
      Sudden pain and a pop at the anterior shoulder, bruising of the arm and chest, loss of the anterior axillary fold
      contour, and weak adduction and internal rotation.
  - title: Poland syndrome
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Congenital unilateral absence of the sternocostal head of pectoralis major.
    presentation: Chest wall asymmetry, often with an ipsilateral hand anomaly (syndactyly or brachydactyly) and breast/nipple hypoplasia.
  - title: Breast cancer fixation to the pectoral fascia
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Infiltration of the retromammary space and pectoral fascia by a deep breast carcinoma.
    presentation: >-
      The breast mass moves with contraction of pectoralis major (tested with the hands pressed on the hips). Fixation
      indicates deeper invasion.
pance:
  - "Pectoralis major = adduction + medial rotation; lateral and medial pectoral nerves."
  - "Bench-press injury with loss of the anterior axillary fold → pectoralis major rupture."
  - "During breast exam, have the patient press hands on hips to contract pectoralis and reveal fixation or dimpling."
imaging:
  xray: Absence of the muscle (Poland syndrome) can make one hemithorax appear hyperlucent on CXR.
  mri: MRI defines the site of rupture (musculotendinous junction vs tendon avulsion from the humerus) and retraction.
  ultrasound: Dynamic assessment of tendon continuity at the humeral insertion.
exam:
  landmarks: The anterior axillary fold. The clavicular and sternocostal heads are visible on resisted adduction.
  palpation: Palpate the anterior axillary fold bilaterally while the patient presses the palms together in front of the chest.
  testing: Resisted adduction and medial rotation. Resisted adduction from 90° flexion (clavicular head) vs from 60° abduction (sternocostal head).
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 28-year-old weightlifter felt a pop in his right anterior shoulder while bench pressing. He has bruising and loss
      of the anterior axillary fold. Which action is weakest?
    choices:
      - Abduction
      - External rotation
      - Adduction and internal rotation
      - Elbow flexion
      - Scapular retraction
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Pectoralis major** rupture weakens **adduction and medial (internal) rotation** of the humerus. It forms the
      anterior axillary fold.
  - stem: Pectoralis major inserts onto which part of the humerus?
    choices:
      - Lesser tubercle
      - Floor of the intertubercular groove
      - Lateral lip of the intertubercular groove
      - Medial lip of the intertubercular groove
      - Deltoid tuberosity
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      Pectoralis major inserts on the **lateral lip** of the intertubercular groove. Latissimus dorsi inserts in the
      floor and teres major on the medial lip ("a lady between two majors").
  - stem: During a breast exam, the patient presses her hands firmly on her hips. What is the purpose of this maneuver?
    choices:
      - To relax the breast tissue for palpation
      - To contract pectoralis major and reveal dimpling or fixation
      - To expose the axillary lymph nodes
      - To test the long thoracic nerve
      - To elevate the inframammary fold
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Pressing on the hips **contracts pectoralis major**. Skin dimpling or a mass that moves with the muscle suggests
      malignancy tethering suspensory ligaments or fixing to the pectoral fascia.
