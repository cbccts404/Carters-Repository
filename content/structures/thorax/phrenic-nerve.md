---
name: Phrenic nerve
type: nerve
region: thorax
subregion: Mediastinum
taName: Nervus phrenicus
summary: Nerve from C3–C5 that descends through the neck and mediastinum. It is the sole motor supply of the diaphragm and carries sensation from the pericardium, mediastinal pleura and central diaphragm.
tags: [respiration, mediastinum]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Neck (on anterior scalene) → superior mediastinum → middle mediastinum (on the pericardium) → diaphragm.
  roots: C3, C4, C5 (mainly C4)
  origin: Ventral rami of C3–C5 (cervical plexus, with a contribution from C5).
  course: >-
    Descends on the **anterior surface of anterior scalene**, deep to the prevertebral fascia, crossing it from lateral
    to medial. Enters the thorax **between the subclavian artery and vein**. In the thorax both nerves pass **anterior to
    the root of the lung** (the vagus nerves pass posterior), running between the **fibrous pericardium and the
    mediastinal pleura** with the pericardiacophrenic vessels. The **right** phrenic nerve runs along the right
    brachiocephalic vein, [[superior-vena-cava]] and right atrium and pierces the diaphragm near the caval opening. The
    **left** phrenic nerve descends over the arch of the aorta and the left ventricle and pierces the muscular diaphragm
    to the left of the pericardium.
  branches:
    - Motor branches to the [[diaphragm]] (entering from its abdominal surface)
    - Sensory branches to the fibrous and parietal pericardium, mediastinal and diaphragmatic pleura, and central diaphragmatic peritoneum
  motor: "[[diaphragm]], its only motor supply."
  sensory: Pericardium, mediastinal pleura, and the central parts of the diaphragmatic pleura and peritoneum. Pain from these sites is **referred to the C3–C5 dermatomes** (shoulder and neck).
  relations: >-
    Accompanied in the thorax by the pericardiacophrenic artery and vein (from the [[internal-thoracic-artery]]).
    Anterior to the lung root; the vagus passes posterior to it.
clinical:
  - title: Phrenic nerve palsy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Injury during cardiac or thoracic surgery (including cold cardioplegia), invasion by lung cancer or mediastinal
      tumors, neck trauma or surgery, **interscalene brachial plexus block** (ipsilateral phrenic blockade is very
      common), and herpes zoster or neuralgic amyotrophy.
    presentation: >-
      **Elevated hemidiaphragm** on CXR. Unilateral paralysis may be asymptomatic or cause exertional dyspnea.
      Bilateral paralysis causes orthopnea and respiratory failure.
  - title: Referred shoulder pain
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Irritation of the diaphragm, pericardium or mediastinal pleura (e.g. pericarditis, hemoperitoneum, subphrenic abscess).
    presentation: >-
      Pain at the shoulder or neck, which share the C3–C5 dermatomes. Example: **Kehr sign** (left shoulder pain) with
      splenic rupture.
  - title: High cervical spinal cord injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Injury at or above C3–C5 disrupts phrenic motor neurons.
    presentation: Loss of diaphragmatic breathing. Injuries above about C3 usually need **long-term ventilatory support**; partial C4–C5 injuries may leave some function.
pance:
  - "\"**C3, 4, 5 keeps the diaphragm alive.**\""
  - "Phrenic nerve passes **anterior** to the lung root; vagus passes **posterior**."
  - "Interscalene block → ipsilateral **diaphragmatic paresis**; use caution in patients with significant lung disease."
  - "New elevated hemidiaphragm in a smoker → consider **lung cancer** involving the phrenic nerve."
imaging:
  xray: The nerve isn't visible. An **elevated hemidiaphragm** is the indirect sign of palsy.
  ct: CT chest looks for a cause (mediastinal mass, lung cancer) along the course of the nerve.
  ultrasound: >-
    Diaphragmatic ultrasound (M-mode excursion, thickening fraction) diagnoses paralysis at the bedside. The phrenic
    nerve itself can be seen on anterior scalene in the neck with high-frequency ultrasound.
  keyViews:
    - "Fluoroscopic or ultrasound **sniff test**: paradoxical upward movement of the paralyzed side"
exam:
  landmarks: In the neck, lies on anterior scalene deep to sternocleidomastoid.
  testing: >-
    Observe for paradoxical abdominal motion (inward on inspiration) when supine in bilateral paralysis. Percuss for
    reduced diaphragmatic excursion on the affected side.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      Shortly after an interscalene brachial plexus block for shoulder surgery, a patient with COPD becomes dyspneic.
      Chest radiograph shows a new elevation of the ipsilateral hemidiaphragm. Which nerve has been affected?
    choices:
      - Long thoracic nerve
      - Phrenic nerve
      - Vagus nerve
      - Recurrent laryngeal nerve
      - Intercostal nerves
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **phrenic nerve** lies on anterior scalene, next to the interscalene groove, and is almost always blocked by
      an interscalene injection. In patients with limited respiratory reserve this can cause significant dyspnea.
  - stem: Which relationship of the phrenic nerve to the root of the lung is correct?
    choices:
      - Posterior to the root, with the vagus anterior
      - Anterior to the root, with the vagus posterior
      - Both nerves anterior to the root
      - Both nerves posterior to the root
      - The phrenic nerve passes through the lung hilum
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      In the mediastinum the **phrenic nerve passes anterior** to the lung root, between the pericardium and pleura. The
      **vagus passes posterior** to it, contributing to the pulmonary and esophageal plexuses.
  - stem: A patient with acute pericarditis reports sharp chest pain that also radiates to the trapezius ridge. Which nerve carries this pain?
    choices:
      - Vagus nerve
      - Phrenic nerve
      - Greater splanchnic nerve
      - Intercostobrachial nerve
      - Cardiac sympathetic nerves
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The pericardium is innervated by the **phrenic nerve** (C3–C5). Pain from pericarditis radiating to the
      **trapezius ridge** is characteristic and reflects the shared C3–C5 dermatomes.
