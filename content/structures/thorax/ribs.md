region: thorax
subregion: Thoracic wall
taName: Costae
summary: Twelve pairs of curved bones forming most of the thoracic cage. They are grouped as true, false and floating ribs, and rib fractures are the most common thoracic injury.
tags: [thoracic-wall]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Thoracic cage, articulating posteriorly with the thoracic vertebrae and anteriorly (via costal cartilages) with the [[sternum]] or each other.
  classification: Flat bones.
  features:
    - "**True ribs (1–7)**: attach directly to the sternum via their own costal cartilages (vertebrosternal)"
    - "**False ribs (8–10)**: cartilages join the cartilage of the rib above (vertebrochondral), forming the costal margin"
    - "**Floating ribs (11–12)**: no anterior attachment"
    - "**Typical rib (3–9)**: head with two facets (articulates with its own vertebra and the one above), neck, tubercle (articulates with the transverse process), angle, shaft, and **costal groove** on the inferior internal surface for the intercostal vessels and nerve"
    - "**1st rib**: broadest, shortest and most curved; **scalene tubercle** (anterior scalene) separates the groove for the **subclavian vein** (anterior) from the groove for the **subclavian artery** (posterior)"
    - "**Ribs 10–12**: single facet on the head; 11 and 12 have no neck or tubercle"
  relations: The intercostal neurovascular bundle runs in the costal groove at the inferior border of each rib (see [[intercostal-space]]).
clinical:
  - title: Rib fractures
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Blunt chest trauma. The middle ribs are most commonly fractured, usually just anterior to the angle (the weakest point).
    presentation: >-
      Point tenderness, pain with breathing and coughing, and splinting. Complications include **pneumothorax,
      hemothorax and pulmonary contusion**. In older adults, pain-limited breathing leads to atelectasis and
      **pneumonia**. **Lower rib (9–12) fractures** raise concern for **splenic, hepatic or renal injury**. **1st and
      2nd rib fractures** imply high-energy trauma, with possible injury to the subclavian vessels or brachial plexus.
  - title: Flail chest
    highYield: true
    mechanism: "Fracture of **three or more adjacent ribs in two or more places**, creating a free-floating segment. {{verify: definitions range from two to three adjacent ribs}}"
    presentation: >-
      **Paradoxical movement**: the segment moves inward on inspiration and outward on expiration. Often accompanied by
      underlying **pulmonary contusion**, which drives the respiratory failure.
  - title: Cervical rib
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Supernumerary rib articulating with C7, present in a small percentage of people.
    presentation: Often asymptomatic. Can compress the lower trunk of the brachial plexus or the subclavian artery (**thoracic outlet syndrome**).
  - title: Costochondritis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Inflammation of the costochondral or costosternal junctions (Tietze syndrome if there is visible swelling).
    presentation: >-
      Anterior chest wall pain reproduced by palpation of the costal cartilages. A diagnosis of exclusion after cardiac
      and other causes of chest pain are considered.
pance:
  - "**Flail chest** = paradoxical motion of a chest-wall segment; the real danger is the underlying **pulmonary contusion**."
  - "Lower rib fractures → look for **spleen/liver/kidney** injury."
  - "1st rib fracture → high-energy mechanism; consider **great vessel** and brachial plexus injury."
  - "**Rib notching** (inferior margins, ribs ~3–8) on CXR = **coarctation of the aorta** (enlarged intercostal collaterals)."
  - "Costochondritis: chest wall pain reproducible on palpation; rule out cardiac causes first."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    The chest X-ray is used mainly to detect **complications** (pneumothorax, hemothorax, contusion). It misses many rib
    fractures, especially anterior and costochondral ones. Dedicated rib series add sensitivity. **Rib notching** of the
    inferior rib margins suggests coarctation.
  ct: CT chest is the most sensitive test for rib fractures and for associated injuries (contusion, hemothorax, solid-organ injury).
  ultrasound: Can show cortical disruption at a point of tenderness and detect pneumothorax and effusion.
  keyViews:
    - "PA/AP chest radiograph"
    - "CT chest (trauma protocol)"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    Count ribs anteriorly from the **2nd costal cartilage at the sternal angle**. The costal margin is formed by
    cartilages 7–10. Rib 12 is felt posteriorly; the inferior angle of the scapula (arm at the side) lies at about rib 7 or the 7th intercostal space.
  palpation: Palpate along each rib for point tenderness, crepitus and step-off. Compress the chest anteroposteriorly and laterally (pain at a distant site suggests fracture).
  testing: Observe chest expansion for symmetry and paradoxical movement (flail segment).
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      After a motorcycle crash, a patient has a segment of the right chest wall that moves inward during inspiration.
      Imaging shows fractures of ribs 4–7, each broken in two places. Which associated condition most often
      causes respiratory failure in this setting?
    choices:
      - Tension pneumothorax
      - Pulmonary contusion
      - Costochondritis
      - Diaphragmatic paralysis
      - Pleural effusion
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Flail chest** is almost always accompanied by **pulmonary contusion** of the underlying lung, which is the main
      cause of hypoxemia and respiratory failure.
  - stem: Which ribs are classified as "floating" ribs?
    choices:
      - Ribs 1 and 2
      - Ribs 8–10
      - Ribs 11 and 12
      - Ribs 1–7
      - Ribs 10–12
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Ribs 11 and 12** have no anterior cartilaginous attachment. Ribs 8–10 are false ribs; ribs 1–7 are true ribs.
  - stem: A 19-year-old man with hypertension has weak, delayed femoral pulses. Chest radiograph shows notching of the inferior margins of several ribs. What is the cause of the notching?
    choices:
      - Erosion by enlarged intercostal collateral arteries
      - Metastatic disease
      - Healed rib fractures
      - Neurofibromas of intercostal nerves
      - Osteomyelitis
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      In **coarctation of the aorta**, blood bypasses the narrowing through the [[internal-thoracic-artery]] and
      intercostal arteries. The dilated, tortuous intercostal arteries erode the **inferior rib margins**.
