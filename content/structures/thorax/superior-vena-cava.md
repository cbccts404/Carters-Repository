name: Superior vena cava
type: vein
region: thorax
subregion: Mediastinum
taName: Vena cava superior
aka: [SVC]
summary: Large vein returning blood from the head, neck, upper limbs and upper thorax to the right atrium. It is the site of SVC syndrome and the target position for central venous catheter tips.
tags: [mediastinum, vascular, procedures]
systems: [cardiovascular]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Superior and middle mediastinum, to the right of the ascending aorta.
  formation: Union of the **right and left brachiocephalic veins** posterior to the lower border of the **right 1st costal cartilage**.
  course: >-
    Descends vertically to the right of the ascending aorta. Receives the [[azygos-vein]] posteriorly at about the level
    of the sternal angle, just before entering the pericardium, and ends in the **right atrium** at about the level of
    the **right 3rd costal cartilage**. {{verify}}
  tributaries:
    - Right and left brachiocephalic veins (formed by the internal jugular and subclavian veins)
    - "[[azygos-vein]]"
    - Small pericardial and mediastinal veins
  drainage: Right atrium of the [[heart]].
  relations: >-
    The right [[phrenic-nerve]] runs on its right side, and the right lung and pleura lie lateral to it. The ascending
    aorta is on its left, and the right pulmonary artery passes behind it. The SA node lies at its junction with the
    right atrium.
clinical:
  - title: Superior vena cava syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Obstruction of the SVC, most often by **malignancy** (lung cancer, especially **small cell**, and **lymphoma**),
      and increasingly by **thrombosis** related to central venous catheters or pacemaker leads.
    presentation: >-
      **Facial and neck swelling/plethora**, upper-extremity edema, dilated collateral veins over the chest wall,
      dyspnea, cough, headache worse when bending forward or lying down. **Pemberton sign**: facial plethora on raising
      both arms. Severe cases have laryngeal or cerebral edema.
  - title: Central venous catheter tip position
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Catheters placed via the internal jugular or subclavian veins are advanced into the SVC.
    presentation: >-
      The tip should lie in the **lower SVC or at the cavoatrial junction**. On CXR, the carina is a practical landmark
      (the cavoatrial junction lies below it). Always check for pneumothorax after IJ or subclavian placement.
      {{verify: ideal tip position recommendations differ between guidelines}}
  - title: Persistent left superior vena cava
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Failure of the left anterior cardinal vein to regress. It usually drains into the **coronary sinus**.
    presentation: Usually asymptomatic. Seen as a left-sided catheter course on CXR or a dilated coronary sinus on echo.

imaging:
  xray: Widening of the right superior mediastinum (mass). Catheter tip position.
  ct: >-
    **Contrast CT** is the key test for SVC syndrome: shows the cause (mass, thrombus) and the collateral pathways
    (azygos, internal thoracic, chest wall veins).
  ultrasound: Ultrasound guides internal jugular and subclavian cannulation. Upper-extremity duplex detects associated thrombosis.
  keyViews:
    - "CT chest with IV contrast (venous phase)"
    - "CXR after central line placement"
exam:
  landmarks: Lies behind the right border of the sternum from the 1st to the 3rd costal cartilages.
  palpation: Distended, non-pulsatile neck veins in SVC obstruction (unlike the pulsatile JVP of right heart failure).
  testing: >-
    Inspect for facial plethora, arm swelling and dilated chest wall veins. **Pemberton maneuver**: raise both arms
    above the head for about a minute and watch for facial plethora, cyanosis or respiratory distress.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 64-year-old man with a 60-pack-year smoking history has facial swelling, arm edema and dilated veins over his upper
      chest. His face becomes plethoric when he raises both arms. What is the most likely underlying cause?
    choices:
      - Right heart failure
      - Small cell lung cancer compressing the SVC
      - Constrictive pericarditis
      - Hypothyroidism
      - Nephrotic syndrome
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **SVC syndrome** (positive **Pemberton sign**) in a heavy smoker is most likely caused by a **lung cancer**,
      classically small cell, compressing or invading the SVC. Lymphoma is the other major malignant cause.
  - stem: Where should the tip of a central venous catheter placed through the right internal jugular vein ideally lie?
    choices:
      - Right ventricle
      - Right subclavian vein
      - Lower SVC or cavoatrial junction
      - Left brachiocephalic vein
      - Inferior vena cava
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The tip should lie in the **lower SVC or at the cavoatrial junction**. Tips in the right ventricle can cause
      arrhythmias, and tips in small veins increase the risk of thrombosis and perforation.
  - stem: Which vein enters the posterior aspect of the SVC just above the pericardium?
    choices:
      - Hemiazygos vein
      - Azygos vein
      - Left brachiocephalic vein
      - Internal thoracic vein
      - Coronary sinus
    answer: B
    explanation: The **azygos vein** arches over the right lung root at about the level of the sternal angle (T4) and drains into the posterior SVC.
  - stem: A persistent left superior vena cava usually drains into which structure?
    choices:
      - Left atrium
      - Left brachiocephalic vein
      - Azygos vein
      - Hemiazygos vein
      - Coronary sinus
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      A **persistent left SVC** (failure of the left anterior cardinal vein to regress) usually drains into the
      **coronary sinus**, which appears dilated on echocardiography. It is usually asymptomatic and may be found as a
      left-sided catheter course.
  - stem: How and where is the superior vena cava formed?
    choices:
      - Union of the internal jugular and subclavian veins at the left venous angle
      - Union of the right and left brachiocephalic veins behind the right 1st costal cartilage
      - Continuation of the azygos vein at the sternal angle
      - Union of the brachiocephalic veins behind the left 3rd costal cartilage
      - Continuation of the right subclavian vein
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The SVC is formed by the **right and left brachiocephalic veins** behind the lower border of the **right 1st
      costal cartilage**. It receives the azygos vein posteriorly and ends in the right atrium.
