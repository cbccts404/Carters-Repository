anatomy:
  location: Midline of the anterior thoracic wall.
  classification: Flat bone.
  features:
    - "**Manubrium**: jugular (suprasternal) notch; clavicular notches (sternoclavicular joints); articulates with the 1st costal cartilage"
    - "**Sternal angle (of Louis)**: manubriosternal joint; the **2nd costal cartilage** articulates here"
    - "**Body**: articulates with costal cartilages 3–7 (the 2nd at the sternal angle, the 7th at the xiphisternal junction)"
    - "**Xiphoid process**: cartilaginous in youth, ossifies with age; xiphisternal joint at about the level of T9"
  relations: >-
    Posterior to the manubrium lie the thymus (or its remnant), the left brachiocephalic vein and the arch of the
    aorta with its branches. Posterior to the body lie the pericardium and the [[heart]], mainly the right ventricle.
    The [[internal-thoracic-artery]] descends about 1 cm lateral to each sternal border.
  notes: >-
    **The sternal angle marks a horizontal plane at the level of the T4/T5 intervertebral disc**, which passes through:
    the 2nd costal cartilage (for counting ribs and intercostal spaces); the boundary between the superior and inferior
    [[mediastinum]]; the beginning and end of the aortic arch; the bifurcation of the [[trachea]] (carina, about
    T4–T5); the bifurcation of the pulmonary trunk; and the point where the [[azygos-vein]] arches into the
    [[superior-vena-cava]]. {{verify: the exact levels of the carina and pulmonary trunk bifurcation vary with posture and respiration}}
clinical:
  - title: Sternal fracture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Blunt anterior chest trauma, classically a steering wheel or seatbelt in a deceleration injury.
    presentation: >-
      Anterior chest pain and point tenderness, possibly with a palpable step. The main concern is **blunt cardiac
      injury** (myocardial contusion, arrhythmia): obtain an **ECG** (and troponin) and evaluate for other intrathoracic injuries.
  - title: Median sternotomy
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Surgical splitting of the sternum in the midline for cardiac surgery.
    presentation: Complications include sternal wound infection, mediastinitis and sternal dehiscence ("clicking" sternum).
  - title: Chest compressions (CPR)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Compression of the heart between the sternum and the vertebral column.
    presentation: >-
      Hands are placed on the **lower half of the sternum** (center of the chest). Pressing on the xiphoid can injure
      the liver. Sternal and rib fractures are common complications of effective CPR.
pance:
  - "**Sternal angle = 2nd rib = T4/T5**: aortic arch starts and ends, carina, superior/inferior mediastinum boundary, azygos enters SVC."
  - "Sternal fracture → screen for **blunt cardiac injury** with an ECG."
  - "Count intercostal spaces from the 2nd costal cartilage at the sternal angle."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    The sternum is poorly seen on a PA chest film (superimposed on the mediastinum). A **lateral sternal view** shows
    fractures and displacement.
  ct: CT (with sagittal reformats) is the most sensitive for sternal fractures and retrosternal hematoma, and evaluates sternal wound infection after sternotomy.
  ultrasound: Point-of-care ultrasound can detect cortical disruption of the sternum and assess for pericardial effusion.
  keyViews:
    - "Lateral sternal radiograph"
    - "CT chest with sagittal reformats"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    Jugular notch (at about the level of the T2/T3 vertebrae), **sternal angle** (a palpable transverse ridge about 5 cm
    below the notch), body, and xiphoid process in the epigastric angle.
    {{verify: vertebral level of the jugular notch differs slightly between sources}}
  palpation: >-
    Slide the fingers down from the jugular notch to the ridge of the sternal angle, then move laterally onto the 2nd
    costal cartilage. The space below it is the 2nd intercostal space. Count down from there.
quiz:
  - stem: Which structure articulates with the sternum at the level of the sternal angle?
    choices:
      - 1st costal cartilage
      - 2nd costal cartilage
      - 3rd costal cartilage
      - Clavicle
      - 7th costal cartilage
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **2nd costal cartilage** articulates at the manubriosternal joint (sternal angle), so it's the starting point
      for counting ribs. The 1st costal cartilage joins the manubrium below the clavicle.
  - stem: A horizontal plane through the sternal angle passes through which vertebral level?
    choices:
      - T2
      - T4/T5 intervertebral disc
      - T6
      - T8
      - T10
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **sternal (transverse thoracic) plane** passes through the **T4/T5 disc**. It separates the superior and
      inferior mediastinum and marks the beginning and end of the aortic arch and roughly the tracheal bifurcation.
  - stem: >-
      A driver in a head-on collision has anterior chest pain and a palpable step-off over the mid-sternum. Which initial
      test is most important to evaluate a potentially lethal associated injury?
    choices:
      - Echocardiogram only if hypotensive
      - 12-lead ECG
      - Pulmonary function testing
      - Bone scan
      - D-dimer
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Sternal fractures raise concern for **blunt cardiac injury**. A **12-lead ECG** is the initial screening test
      (with troponin). A normal ECG and troponin make significant blunt cardiac injury unlikely.
  - stem: The xiphisternal joint lies at about which vertebral level?
    choices:
      - T2
      - T4/T5
      - T9
      - T12
      - L1
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **xiphisternal joint** lies at about **T9**. The sternal angle marks the T4/T5 disc; the jugular notch lies at
      about the level of T2.
  - stem: Where should the hands be placed for chest compressions during CPR?
    choices:
      - On the lower half of the sternum (center of the chest)
      - On the manubrium
      - On the xiphoid process
      - Over the left 5th intercostal space
      - Over the upper sternum at the sternal angle
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      Compressions are delivered on the **lower half of the sternum**, compressing the heart between the sternum and
