name: Circumflex artery
type: artery
region: thorax
subregion: Heart & pericardium
taName: Ramus circumflexus arteriae coronariae sinistrae
aka: [LCx, Left circumflex artery]
summary: Branch of the left coronary artery in the left atrioventricular groove that supplies the lateral wall of the left ventricle and the left atrium; its occlusion causes lateral (and sometimes posterior) MI.
tags: [heart, coronary, cardiology]
systems: [cardiovascular]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Left atrioventricular (coronary) groove, curving around the left (obtuse) margin to the posterior surface.
  origin: "[[left-coronary-artery]]"
  course: Runs in the left AV groove around the obtuse margin toward the posterior heart. In left-dominant hearts it continues to give the posterior interventricular artery.
  branches:
    - "**Left (obtuse) marginal arteries**: to the lateral LV wall"
    - "Atrial branches; the **SA nodal artery** in about 40%"
    - "Posterior interventricular artery in **left-dominant** hearts"
  supplies: Left atrium, the **lateral (and posterolateral) wall of the LV**, the anterolateral papillary muscle (shared with the LAD), and in left dominance the posterior septum and inferior wall (including the AV node).
  relations: Runs alongside the great cardiac vein/coronary sinus in the AV groove, close to the mitral annulus.
clinical:
  - title: Lateral myocardial infarction
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Occlusion of the circumflex or an obtuse marginal branch.
    presentation: >-
      **ST elevation in I, aVL, V5–V6**. Circumflex occlusions are often **electrically silent** on the standard 12-lead
      ECG because the lateral/posterior wall is poorly represented.
  - title: Posterior myocardial infarction
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Occlusion of the circumflex (or of a dominant RCA's posterolateral branches), often with inferior or lateral MI.
    presentation: >-
      **ST depression with tall R waves in V1–V3** (the mirror image of posterior ST elevation). Confirm with **posterior
      leads V7–V9** showing ST elevation.
pance:
  - "Lateral MI: **I, aVL, V5–V6** → circumflex."
  - "ST depression + tall R in **V1–V3** → think **posterior MI**; get **V7–V9**."
  - "Circumflex occlusion can be ECG-silent; rely on symptoms, troponin and echo."
imaging:
  ultrasound: Echo shows lateral and posterolateral wall motion abnormalities; may show MR from papillary muscle dysfunction.
  ct: Coronary CTA; the circumflex runs near the mitral annulus.
  keyViews:
    - "Invasive angiography (RAO caudal view shows the circumflex and obtuse marginals)"
    - "Posterior leads V7–V9"
exam:
  testing: Listen for a new MR murmur (papillary muscle dysfunction) after lateral or posterior MI.
quiz:
  - stem: A patient has ST elevation in I, aVL, V5 and V6. Which artery is most likely occluded?
    choices:
      - Right coronary artery
      - Left anterior descending artery (proximal)
      - Left circumflex artery
      - Posterior interventricular artery
      - Conus artery
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **I, aVL, V5–V6** are the lateral leads, the **circumflex** territory (or a large diagonal of the LAD).
  - stem: A patient with chest pain has horizontal ST depression and tall R waves in V1–V3. What is the next best diagnostic step?
    choices:
      - Treat as non-ST-elevation ACS only
      - Obtain posterior leads V7–V9
      - Obtain right-sided leads only
      - Exercise stress testing
      - Repeat ECG in 24 hours
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      ST depression with tall R waves in V1–V3 may be a **posterior STEMI** seen from the front. **Leads V7–V9** show the
      ST elevation directly.
  - stem: In a left-dominant heart, which artery gives rise to the posterior interventricular artery?
    choices:
      - Right coronary artery
      - Circumflex artery
      - Anterior interventricular artery
      - Right marginal artery
      - Conus artery
    answer: B
    explanation: In **left dominance**, the **circumflex** continues around the posterior AV groove to give the posterior interventricular artery.
  - stem: In which groove does the circumflex artery run?
    choices:
      - Left atrioventricular (coronary) groove
      - Anterior interventricular groove
      - Posterior interventricular groove
      - Right atrioventricular groove
      - Interatrial groove
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      The circumflex runs in the **left AV groove** around the obtuse margin, alongside the great cardiac vein and
      coronary sinus and close to the mitral annulus. The LAD lies in the anterior interventricular groove and the RCA
      in the right AV groove.
  - stem: Why can a circumflex occlusion be missed on a standard 12-lead ECG?
    choices:
      - The circumflex supplies only the atria
      - Circumflex occlusions never cause infarction
      - Troponin doesn't rise with circumflex occlusion
      - The lateral and posterior walls it supplies are poorly represented on the standard leads
      - The circumflex is supplied by the RCA
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      The circumflex supplies the **lateral and posterolateral LV wall**, which the standard 12 leads represent poorly,
      so occlusion can be **electrically silent**. Rely on symptoms, troponin, echocardiography, and posterior leads
      (V7–V9).
