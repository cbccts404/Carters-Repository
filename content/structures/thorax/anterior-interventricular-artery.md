---
name: Anterior interventricular artery
type: artery
region: thorax
subregion: Heart & pericardium
taName: Ramus interventricularis anterior
aka: [LAD, Left anterior descending artery]
summary: Branch of the left coronary artery in the anterior interventricular groove. It supplies the anterior LV wall, the anterior two-thirds of the septum and the apex, and is the most commonly occluded coronary artery.
tags: [heart, coronary, cardiology, cabg]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Anterior interventricular groove, from the left main bifurcation around the apex.
  origin: "[[left-coronary-artery]]"
  course: Descends in the anterior interventricular groove toward the apex, often curving around it onto the inferior surface.
  branches:
    - "**Diagonal branches**: to the anterolateral LV wall"
    - "**Septal perforating branches**: to the anterior two-thirds of the interventricular septum (including the bundle branches)"
    - "Branches to the anterolateral papillary muscle (shared with the circumflex)"
  supplies: >-
    Anterior wall of the left ventricle, the **anterior two-thirds of the interventricular septum**, the **apex**, part
    of the anterior right ventricle, and (with the circumflex) the anterolateral papillary muscle.
  anastomoses: At the apex with the posterior interventricular artery.
clinical:
  - title: Anterior (anteroseptal) myocardial infarction
    highYield: true
    mechanism: LAD occlusion. Proximal occlusion (the "widowmaker") endangers a large area of myocardium.
    presentation: >-
      **ST elevation in V1–V4** (septal V1–V2, anterior V3–V4), with extension to V5–V6, I and aVL for anterolateral
      involvement. Complications: **heart failure/cardiogenic shock**, new bundle branch block, **LV apical aneurysm and
      thrombus**, and ventricular septal rupture.
  - title: Wellens syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Critical proximal LAD stenosis in a patient whose pain has resolved.
    presentation: >-
      **Deeply inverted or biphasic T waves in V2–V3** in a pain-free patient. High risk of an extensive anterior MI; needs
      urgent angiography, and stress testing is **contraindicated**.
  - title: CABG target
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Surgical bypass of LAD stenosis.
    presentation: The **LIMA → LAD** graft ([[internal-thoracic-artery]]) has the best long-term patency.
pance:
  - "**LAD = most commonly occluded** coronary artery; anterior MI **V1–V4**."
  - "Proximal LAD = \"widowmaker\" (large territory at risk)."
  - "Wellens: biphasic/deep T inversions V2–V3 → critical LAD stenosis; **no stress test**."
  - "LIMA–LAD is the preferred bypass graft."
imaging:
  ct: Coronary CTA shows stenosis and plaque. LV apical aneurysm or thrombus may be visible.
  mri: Late gadolinium enhancement in the anterior wall, septum and apex.
  ultrasound: Echo shows **anterior, septal and apical hypokinesis/akinesis**, reduced EF, apical aneurysm or LV thrombus.
  keyViews:
    - "Invasive angiography (RAO cranial and LAO cranial views show the LAD and diagonals)"
    - "12-lead ECG: V1–V4"
exam:
  testing: Assess for heart failure (S3, crackles, JVD) and a new holosystolic murmur (ventricular septal rupture) after anterior MI.
quiz:
  - stem: A 58-year-old woman has chest pain with ST elevation in V1–V4. Which artery is most likely occluded?
    choices:
      - Right coronary artery
      - Left circumflex artery
      - Left anterior descending artery
      - Posterior interventricular artery
      - Right marginal artery
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **V1–V4** = anteroseptal wall = **LAD** territory. Inferior (II, III, aVF) = RCA; lateral (I, aVL, V5–V6) =
      circumflex.
  - stem: Which region of the heart receives its blood supply mainly from the LAD?
    choices:
      - Posterior third of the interventricular septum
      - Anterior two-thirds of the interventricular septum
      - Sinoatrial node
      - Posteromedial papillary muscle
      - Inferior wall of the right ventricle
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **septal perforators** of the LAD supply the **anterior two-thirds of the septum**. The posterior third comes
      from the posterior interventricular artery.
  - stem: A pain-free 55-year-old man admitted after an episode of chest pain has deeply inverted T waves in V2 and V3 with normal troponin. What is the most appropriate next step?
    choices:
      - Exercise stress test
      - Discharge with outpatient follow-up
      - Urgent coronary angiography
      - Start a proton pump inhibitor
      - Repeat ECG in 1 month
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      This is **Wellens syndrome**, a marker of **critical proximal LAD stenosis**. Stress testing may precipitate an MI;
      the patient needs **coronary angiography**.
