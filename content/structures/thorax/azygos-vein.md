---
name: Azygos vein
type: vein
region: thorax
subregion: Mediastinum
taName: Vena azygos
summary: Vein on the right side of the posterior mediastinum that drains the posterior thoracic wall. It arches over the right lung root into the SVC and is a key collateral pathway between the caval systems.
tags: [mediastinum, vascular]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Posterior mediastinum, on the right side of the thoracic vertebral bodies.
  formation: Usually from the right ascending lumbar and right subcostal veins, and sometimes a connection with the IVC, entering the thorax through the aortic hiatus or the right crus of the diaphragm. {{verify}}
  course: >-
    Ascends on the right of the vertebral bodies (with the [[thoracic-duct]] and [[thoracic-aorta]] to its left). At
    about **T4** it **arches anteriorly over the root of the right lung** (right main bronchus) to join the posterior
    [[superior-vena-cava]].
  tributaries:
    - Right posterior intercostal veins (4th–11th) and the right superior intercostal vein
    - "**Hemiazygos vein** (lower left posterior thorax) and **accessory hemiazygos vein** (upper left), which cross to the right behind the aorta"
    - Esophageal, bronchial, mediastinal and pericardial veins
  drainage: "[[superior-vena-cava]]"
  relations: >-
    The arch of the azygos vein is on the right of the trachea and esophagus above the right main bronchus. It lies
    behind the esophagus lower down.
clinical:
  - title: Collateral pathway in caval obstruction
    highYield: true
    mechanism: The azygos system connects the SVC and IVC via the lumbar veins.
    presentation: >-
      **SVC obstruction below the azygos entry**: blood returns from the upper body through the azygos in reverse. **IVC
      obstruction**: lower-body blood returns through the lumbar–azygos route. The azygos vein becomes dilated on imaging.
  - title: Esophageal varices (systemic side)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: In portal hypertension, portal blood reaches the systemic circulation via the left gastric vein → esophageal veins → **azygos vein**.
    presentation: Dilated submucosal veins of the distal [[esophagus]] that can bleed massively.
  - title: Azygos lobe
    highYield: false
    mechanism: A normal variant in which the azygos vein passes through the apical right upper lobe, carrying a fold of pleura with it (the azygos fissure).
    presentation: A curved thin line in the right upper zone on CXR ending in a teardrop density. Clinically insignificant.
pance:
  - "Azygos arches over the **right main bronchus at T4** into the SVC."
  - "Azygos = **SVC–IVC collateral**; dilated with caval obstruction or azygos continuation of the IVC."
  - "Esophageal varices: left gastric (portal) ↔ esophageal veins → azygos (systemic)."
imaging:
  xray: The azygos arch appears as a small oval opacity in the right tracheobronchial angle; enlargement suggests raised central venous pressure, caval obstruction, or azygos continuation. Azygos fissure (variant).
  ct: Shows the course, dilation (azygos continuation of an interrupted IVC, SVC obstruction) and collaterals.
  keyViews:
    - "CT chest with contrast"
exam:
  testing: Not examined directly. Look for chest and abdominal wall collateral veins in caval obstruction.
