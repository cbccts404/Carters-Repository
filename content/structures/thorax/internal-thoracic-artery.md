---
name: Internal thoracic artery
type: artery
region: thorax
subregion: Thoracic wall
taName: Arteria thoracica interna
aka: [Internal mammary artery, IMA, LIMA/RIMA]
summary: Branch of the subclavian artery that descends just lateral to the sternum. It supplies the anterior chest wall and breast and is the preferred conduit for coronary bypass (LIMA to LAD).
tags: [thoracic-wall, cabg]
systems: [cardiovascular]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Deep surface of the anterior thoracic wall, about 1 cm lateral to the sternal border, behind the costal cartilages.
  origin: Inferior surface of the **first part of the subclavian artery**.
  course: >-
    Descends behind the clavicle and the upper six costal cartilages, anterior to the pleura (and, lower down, to
    transversus thoracis), about 1 cm lateral to the sternum.
  branches:
    - "Pericardiacophrenic artery (accompanies the [[phrenic-nerve]])"
    - "**Anterior intercostal arteries** (spaces 1–6)"
    - "**Perforating branches** (including medial mammary branches to the [[breast]])"
    - "Thymic and mediastinal branches"
    - "**Terminal branches** at the 6th intercostal space: the **superior epigastric artery** (continues into the rectus sheath) and the **musculophrenic artery**"
  supplies: Anterior thoracic wall, breast, thymus, pericardium, diaphragm, and (via the superior epigastric) the upper anterior abdominal wall.
  anastomoses: >-
    With the posterior intercostal arteries (from the aorta) via the anterior intercostals. With the **inferior
    epigastric artery** (external iliac) via the superior epigastric artery in the rectus sheath, a route for collateral
    flow between the subclavian and iliac systems.
  termination: Divides at about the 6th intercostal space into the superior epigastric and musculophrenic arteries.
clinical:
  - title: Coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      The **left internal thoracic (mammary) artery (LIMA)** is usually left attached to the subclavian artery and
      anastomosed to the **[[anterior-interventricular-artery]] (LAD)**.
    presentation: The LIMA-to-LAD graft has the best long-term patency of any bypass conduit, better than saphenous vein grafts.
  - title: Collateral pathway in coarctation of the aorta
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      With narrowing of the aorta beyond the left subclavian artery, blood flows subclavian → internal thoracic →
      anterior intercostals → posterior intercostals → descending aorta (and via the superior and inferior epigastrics).
    presentation: Enlarged, tortuous intercostal arteries cause **rib notching**. Pulsatile collaterals may be felt in the intercostal spaces or around the scapula.
  - title: Injury in parasternal procedures or trauma
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Penetrating trauma near the sternum, or needles placed parasternally (e.g. a parasternal pericardiocentesis approach).
    presentation: Hemothorax or anterior mediastinal hematoma. Procedures stay away from the immediate parasternal line, or use ultrasound guidance.
pance:
  - "**LIMA → LAD** is the gold-standard CABG graft (best long-term patency)."
  - "Coarctation: internal thoracic → intercostal collaterals → **rib notching**."
  - "Terminal branches: superior epigastric + musculophrenic (6th intercostal space)."
imaging:
  ct: CT angiography assesses graft patency after CABG and shows enlarged collaterals in coarctation. The vessels are visible just behind the costal cartilages on contrast CT.
  ultrasound: Parasternal Doppler can identify the vessel before procedures; also used to assess its suitability as a graft. {{verify}}
  keyViews:
    - "Coronary CT angiography or invasive angiography for graft patency"
exam:
  landmarks: Runs about 1 cm lateral to the sternal border, deep to the costal cartilages. Not palpable.
  testing: In suspected coarctation, compare upper and lower limb blood pressures and palpate for radiofemoral delay.
