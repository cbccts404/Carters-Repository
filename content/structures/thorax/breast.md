anatomy:
  location: >-
    On the anterior thoracic wall, from about the 2nd to the 6th rib vertically and from the lateral sternal border to the
    mid-axillary line horizontally. The **axillary tail (of Spence)** extends toward the axilla.
  parts:
    - "Glandular tissue arranged in 15–20 lobes, each drained by a **lactiferous duct** opening on the nipple"
    - "**Suspensory ligaments (of Cooper)**: fibrous bands from the dermis through the gland to the deep fascia"
    - "**Retromammary space**: loose connective tissue between the breast and the pectoral fascia, allowing the breast to move"
    - "Nipple and areola (areolar glands of Montgomery)"
  relations: Lies on the fascia over [[pectoralis-major]], serratus anterior and the external oblique aponeurosis.
  bloodSupply: >-
    Medial mammary branches of perforating branches of the [[internal-thoracic-artery]] (the largest supply); lateral
    thoracic and thoracoacromial arteries (from the [[axillary-artery]]); posterior intercostal arteries.
  venousDrainage: Mainly to the axillary vein, with tributaries to the internal thoracic and intercostal veins.
  lymphatics: >-
    About **75% drains to the axillary nodes**, most of it first to the **pectoral (anterior) group** (see [[axilla]]).
    The medial breast drains mainly to the **parasternal nodes** along the internal thoracic vessels. Some lymph passes
    to the opposite breast or to abdominal (inferior phrenic) nodes.
  innervation: Anterior and lateral cutaneous branches of the 4th–6th intercostal nerves. The nipple is at about the **T4** dermatome.
clinical:
  - title: Breast cancer
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Most are ductal carcinomas. The **upper outer quadrant** is the most common site (it contains the most glandular
      tissue). Spreads by lymphatics (axillary nodes first) and blood (bone, lung, liver, brain).
    presentation: >-
      A hard, fixed, painless mass. **Skin dimpling/retraction** (tethering of Cooper ligaments), **nipple retraction**,
      **peau d'orange** (lymphatic obstruction causing edema around hair follicles), bloody nipple discharge,
      axillary lymphadenopathy.
  - title: Paget disease of the nipple
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Intraepithelial spread of carcinoma cells (Paget cells) to the nipple epidermis, usually with underlying ductal carcinoma.
    presentation: Eczematous, scaly, crusting or ulcerated nipple that fails to respond to topical treatment. Requires biopsy.
  - title: Benign breast conditions
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      **Fibroadenoma** (young women): benign fibroepithelial tumor. **Fibrocystic changes**: hormonally influenced.
      **Mastitis/abscess**: usually *S. aureus* in lactating women. **Gynecomastia**: glandular proliferation in males
      (puberty, drugs such as spironolactone, liver disease).
    presentation: >-
      Fibroadenoma: firm, rubbery, **mobile**, well-circumscribed mass. Fibrocystic: cyclic bilateral tenderness and
      nodularity. Mastitis: painful, red, warm wedge of breast with fever.
  - title: Supernumerary nipples (polythelia)
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Persistence of tissue along the embryonic **mammary ridge (milk line)** from the axilla to the groin.
    presentation: Small pigmented nodules along the milk line, often mistaken for moles.

imaging:
  xray: >-
    **Mammography** is the main screening test, with two standard views per breast: **craniocaudal (CC)** and
    **mediolateral oblique (MLO)**. The MLO includes the axillary tail and low axilla. Suspicious findings: spiculated
    mass, clustered pleomorphic microcalcifications, architectural distortion. Findings are reported with BI-RADS
    categories. Screening recommendations (starting age, interval) differ between organizations.
    {{verify: check the current USPSTF/ACR/ACS screening recommendations}}
  mri: Used for high-risk screening (e.g. BRCA carriers), extent of disease, implant integrity, and problem-solving.
  ultrasound: >-
    First-line for a **palpable mass in women under about 30**, pregnant or lactating women, and to tell cystic from solid
    lesions at any age. Simple cysts are anechoic with posterior acoustic enhancement. Guides core biopsy.
    Also evaluates axillary nodes.
  keyViews:
    - "Mammography: craniocaudal (CC) and mediolateral oblique (MLO)"
    - "Targeted ultrasound of a palpable lump"
exam:
  landmarks: The nipple in males and young women is at about the **4th intercostal space** (T4 dermatome); position varies considerably in women.
  palpation: >-
    **Inspection** with the patient seated: arms at the sides, arms overhead, then hands pressed on the hips (look for
    asymmetry, dimpling, retraction, skin changes). **Palpation** with the patient supine and the ipsilateral arm
    raised: use the pads of the middle three fingers in small circles at three pressures, covering the whole breast
    systematically (vertical strip pattern) from the clavicle to the inframammary fold and from the sternum to the
    mid-axillary line, including the **axillary tail**, nipple and areola. Then examine the axillary,
    supraclavicular and infraclavicular nodes.
  testing: Describe any mass by location (clock face and distance from the nipple), size, shape, consistency, mobility and tenderness.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 56-year-old woman has a firm, fixed mass in the upper outer quadrant of the left breast. The overlying skin has
      a pitted, orange-peel appearance. What causes the skin change?
    choices:
      - Tethering of suspensory ligaments
      - Obstruction of dermal lymphatics
      - Infiltration of the lactiferous ducts
      - Fat necrosis
      - Invasion of pectoralis major
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Peau d'orange** comes from **lymphatic obstruction**: dermal edema pulls on hair follicles and pores, producing
      the pitted appearance. Dimpling and retraction come from tethering of the **suspensory (Cooper) ligaments**.
  - stem: Which group of lymph nodes receives most of the lymphatic drainage of the breast?
    choices:
      - Parasternal nodes
      - Axillary nodes (pectoral group first)
      - Supraclavicular nodes
      - Contralateral axillary nodes
      - Inferior phrenic nodes
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      About **75%** of breast lymph drains to the **axillary nodes**, mostly first to the **pectoral (anterior)**
      group. The parasternal nodes receive drainage mainly from the medial breast.
  - stem: A 23-year-old woman has a 2-cm, firm, rubbery, highly mobile, nontender breast mass. What is the most appropriate initial imaging?
    choices:
      - Mammography
      - Breast ultrasound
      - Breast MRI
      - CT chest
      - PET scan
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      In women under about 30, **ultrasound** is the initial test for a palpable mass (dense tissue limits mammography,
      and radiation dose is a consideration). This presentation suggests a **fibroadenoma**.
  - stem: Which artery provides the largest blood supply to the medial breast?
    choices:
      - Lateral thoracic artery
      - Thoracoacromial artery
      - Internal thoracic artery
      - Superior epigastric artery
      - Thoracodorsal artery
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Perforating branches of the internal thoracic artery** (medial mammary branches) supply the medial breast. The
      lateral thoracic artery supplies the lateral part.
  - stem: A breast cancer causes dimpling of the overlying skin. What causes the dimpling?
    choices:
      - Obstruction of dermal lymphatics
      - Invasion of the pectoralis major
      - Retraction of the lactiferous ducts
      - Fat necrosis
      - Tethering of the suspensory (Cooper) ligaments
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      **Skin dimpling** comes from the tumor **tethering Cooper (suspensory) ligaments**, which run from the dermis to
      the deep fascia. **Peau d'orange** is caused by obstruction of dermal lymphatics; nipple retraction reflects
      involvement of the ducts behind the nipple.
