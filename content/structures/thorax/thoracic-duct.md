---
name: Thoracic duct
type: lymphatic
region: thorax
subregion: Mediastinum
taName: Ductus thoracicus
summary: Largest lymphatic vessel. It drains lymph from most of the body, ascends through the posterior mediastinum, and empties into the left venous angle. Injury causes chylothorax.
tags: [mediastinum, lymphatic]
systems: [hematologic]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Posterior mediastinum, between the thoracic aorta and the azygos vein, behind the esophagus.
  drains: >-
    Lymph (and absorbed dietary fat as chyle) from the **entire body except the right upper quadrant**: the right side of
    the head and neck, the right upper limb, and the right side of the thorax drain to the **right lymphatic duct**.
  drainsTo: >-
    The **left venous angle**, the junction of the left internal jugular and left subclavian veins.
  notes: >-
    **Course**: begins at the **cisterna chyli** (at about L1–L2) in the abdomen. It passes through the **aortic hiatus
    (T12)** of the [[diaphragm]], ascends in the posterior mediastinum between the [[thoracic-aorta]] (left) and the
    [[azygos-vein]] (right), posterior to the [[esophagus]]. At about **T4–T6** it crosses to the left and ascends
    along the left side of the esophagus into the neck. There it arches laterally (at about C7) to open into the left
    venous angle. {{verify: exact crossing level varies between texts and individuals}}
clinical:
  - title: Chylothorax
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Leakage of chyle into the pleural cavity from injury to the duct (thoracic or esophageal surgery, trauma, central
      line placement in the left neck) or obstruction by malignancy (**lymphoma** is a classic cause). Injury **below**
      the crossing point usually causes a **right** chylothorax; injury **above** it, a **left** chylothorax.
    presentation: >-
      Pleural effusion with **milky white fluid**. **Triglycerides above 110 mg/dL** (and chylomicrons) confirm it.
      Prolonged leaks cause malnutrition, lymphopenia and immunosuppression.
  - title: Virchow node
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Metastatic spread (classically gastric and other abdominal cancers) along the thoracic duct to the left supraclavicular node near its termination.
    presentation: A hard, enlarged **left supraclavicular lymph node** (Troisier sign).
pance:
  - "Thoracic duct: **cisterna chyli (L1–L2) → aortic hiatus (T12) → crosses to the left (~T5) → left venous angle**."
  - "**Chylothorax**: milky pleural fluid, **triglycerides > 110 mg/dL**; after thoracic surgery, trauma, or lymphoma."
  - "Virchow node (left supraclavicular) → think gastric/abdominal malignancy."
  - "Drains everything except the right head/neck, right arm, and right thorax (right lymphatic duct)."
imaging:
  xray: Pleural effusion (not specific for chyle).
  ct: Effusion and possible causes (mediastinal lymphoma, post-surgical changes). Fat attenuation of the fluid is not reliable.
  mri: MR lymphangiography can map the duct and localize leaks.
  keyViews:
    - "Thoracentesis with triglyceride and chylomicron measurement (the diagnostic test)"
    - "Lymphangiography (conventional or MR) to localize a leak and guide embolization"
exam:
  landmarks: Terminates near the junction of the left internal jugular and subclavian veins, behind the left sternoclavicular joint area.
  palpation: Palpate the **left supraclavicular fossa** for a Virchow node.
  testing: Effusion signs on the chest exam (dullness, decreased fremitus and breath sounds).
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      Five days after an esophagectomy, a patient has an enlarging right pleural effusion. Thoracentesis yields milky white
      fluid with a triglyceride level of 350 mg/dL. Which structure was injured?
    choices:
      - Azygos vein
      - Thoracic duct
      - Right lymphatic duct
      - Esophageal anastomosis
      - Right main bronchus
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Chylothorax** after esophageal surgery comes from **thoracic duct** injury. Below its crossing point the duct
      lies on the right, so lower injuries produce a **right-sided** chylothorax.
  - stem: Where does the thoracic duct usually empty?
    choices:
      - Right venous angle
      - Left venous angle (junction of left internal jugular and subclavian veins)
      - Superior vena cava
      - Azygos vein
      - Left atrium
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The thoracic duct ends at the **left venous angle**. The right lymphatic duct drains into the right venous angle.
  - stem: A 70-year-old man with weight loss and early satiety has a hard, enlarged left supraclavicular lymph node. Which malignancy should be suspected?
    choices:
      - Basal cell carcinoma of the scalp
      - Gastric adenocarcinoma
      - Papillary thyroid carcinoma
      - Melanoma of the right hand
      - Laryngeal carcinoma
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **Virchow node** (left supraclavicular) reflects spread of abdominal malignancy, classically **gastric
      cancer**, via the thoracic duct.
