name: Diaphragm
type: muscle
region: thorax
subregion: Thoracic wall
taName: Diaphragma
summary: Dome-shaped musculotendinous partition between the thorax and abdomen. It is the main muscle of inspiration and is supplied by the phrenic nerves (C3–C5).
tags: [thoracic-wall, respiration]
systems: [pulmonary, gastrointestinal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Between the thoracic and abdominal cavities. The right dome sits slightly higher than the left (liver below).
  group: Muscles of respiration.
  origin: >-
    **Sternal part:** posterior xiphoid process. **Costal part:** internal surfaces of the lower six costal cartilages
    and adjacent ribs. **Lumbar part:** medial and lateral arcuate ligaments and the **crura**; the right crus arises
    from the bodies of L1–L3, the left from L1–L2.
  insertion: Central tendon (converging from all parts).
  innervation: >-
    **Motor:** [[phrenic-nerve]] (C3, C4, C5), the sole motor supply. **Sensory:** phrenic nerve to the central part;
    the lower intercostal and subcostal nerves to the peripheral costal part.
  bloodSupply: Pericardiacophrenic and musculophrenic arteries (from the [[internal-thoracic-artery]]), superior phrenic arteries (thoracic aorta), and inferior phrenic arteries (abdominal aorta).
  action:
    - Principal muscle of **inspiration**. Contraction flattens and lowers the domes, increasing thoracic volume
    - Increases intra-abdominal pressure (with the abdominal wall) for coughing, vomiting, defecation and lifting
  relations: >-
    **Openings** (approximate vertebral levels): **caval opening, T8**, in the central tendon (inferior vena cava,
    branches of the right phrenic nerve); **esophageal hiatus, T10**, in the right crus (esophagus, anterior and
    posterior vagal trunks, esophageal branches of the left gastric vessels); **aortic hiatus, T12**, behind the median
    arcuate ligament (aorta, thoracic duct, and often the azygos vein). Mnemonic: "I 8 10 Eggs At 12"
    (IVC T8, esophagus T10, aorta T12).
clinical:
  - title: Hiatal hernia
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Herniation of the stomach through the esophageal hiatus. In a **sliding** hernia (type I, the most common) the
      gastroesophageal junction moves above the diaphragm. In a **paraesophageal** hernia the fundus herniates beside a
      GE junction that stays in place.
    presentation: >-
      Sliding: GERD symptoms (heartburn, regurgitation). Paraesophageal: often asymptomatic, but risk of incarceration or
      volvulus (chest pain, retching, inability to swallow).
  - title: Congenital diaphragmatic hernia
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Defective closure of the pleuroperitoneal membrane, most often **posterolateral and left-sided (Bochdalek)**.
      Abdominal viscera in the thorax impair lung development (**pulmonary hypoplasia**).
    presentation: >-
      Newborn with respiratory distress, a **scaphoid abdomen**, bowel sounds in the chest, and shift of heart sounds.
      Often detected on prenatal ultrasound.
  - title: Diaphragmatic paralysis
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Phrenic nerve injury (cardiac or thoracic surgery, tumor invasion, interscalene block, trauma, cervical spinal cord injury at C3–C5).
    presentation: >-
      Unilateral: often asymptomatic or exertional dyspnea, with an **elevated hemidiaphragm** on CXR. Bilateral:
      orthopnea and paradoxical inward movement of the abdomen on inspiration.
  - title: Traumatic diaphragmatic rupture
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Blunt or penetrating thoracoabdominal trauma; blunt ruptures are more often on the **left** (the liver protects the right).
    presentation: Abdominal contents in the chest on imaging (e.g. an NG tube curling in the left chest). Easily missed initially.
pance:
  - "Openings: **IVC T8, esophagus T10, aorta T12** (\"I 8 10 Eggs At 12\")."
  - "\"**C3, 4, 5 keeps the diaphragm alive**\" (phrenic nerve)."
  - "Diaphragmatic irritation → **referred shoulder pain** (C3–C5), e.g. Kehr sign with splenic rupture."
  - "Newborn with respiratory distress + scaphoid abdomen → **congenital diaphragmatic hernia** (left Bochdalek)."
  - "Free air under the diaphragm on an upright CXR → perforated viscus."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    Upright CXR: the domes project at about the 5th–6th anterior ribs. The right dome is usually higher. **Free
    intraperitoneal air** appears as a lucent crescent under the diaphragm. An **elevated hemidiaphragm** suggests
    paralysis, eventration, subpulmonic effusion or volume loss. Blunting of the costophrenic angle suggests effusion.
    {{verify: normal dome position varies with inspiration and body habitus}}
  ct: Multiplanar CT shows hernias, traumatic rupture ("collar sign" where herniated viscus is constricted by the torn diaphragm) and masses.
  mri: Occasionally used to confirm diaphragmatic defects when CT is equivocal.
  ultrasound: >-
    M-mode ultrasound measures **diaphragmatic excursion** and thickening, identifying paralysis at the bedside. It also
    shows effusions above the diaphragm and free fluid below it.
  keyViews:
    - "Upright PA and lateral CXR"
    - "Fluoroscopic **sniff test**: a paralyzed hemidiaphragm moves paradoxically upward on a sharp sniff"
exam:
  landmarks: The domes rise to about the 5th intercostal space in expiration; excursion is roughly 3–5 cm in adults. {{verify}}
  palpation: Not directly palpable.
  testing: >-
    **Percussion of diaphragmatic excursion** posteriorly: find the level of dullness on full expiration and full
    inspiration. Reduced excursion suggests paralysis, effusion or hyperinflation. Observe for paradoxical abdominal
    movement when supine.
quiz:
  - stem: At approximately which vertebral level does the esophagus pass through the diaphragm?
    choices:
      - T8
      - T10
      - T12
      - L1
      - L3
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **esophageal hiatus** is at **T10**, with the vagal trunks. The IVC passes at T8 through the central tendon;
      the aorta at T12 behind the median arcuate ligament.
  - stem: >-
      A term newborn has respiratory distress immediately after birth. The abdomen is scaphoid and bowel sounds are
      heard over the left chest. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Esophageal atresia
      - Congenital diaphragmatic hernia
      - Transient tachypnea of the newborn
      - Pyloric stenosis
      - Tension pneumothorax
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **Bochdalek** (posterolateral, usually left) congenital diaphragmatic hernia puts bowel in the chest, leaving a
      scaphoid abdomen, and causes pulmonary hypoplasia.
  - stem: A patient with splenic rupture reports pain at the tip of the left shoulder. Which nerve carries the referred pain?
    choices:
      - Vagus nerve
      - Phrenic nerve
      - Intercostobrachial nerve
      - Long thoracic nerve
      - Greater splanchnic nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Blood irritating the diaphragm stimulates **phrenic nerve** (C3–C5) sensory fibers. The pain is referred to the
      shoulder, which shares the C3–C5 dermatomes (**Kehr sign**).
