name: Pelvic diaphragm
type: muscle
region: pelvis-perineum
subregion: Pelvic floor
taName: Diaphragma pelvis
aka: [Pelvic floor, Levator ani and coccygeus]
summary: Funnel-shaped muscular floor of the pelvis formed by levator ani and coccygeus. It supports the pelvic organs and maintains urinary and fecal continence; its weakening underlies pelvic organ prolapse and stress incontinence.
tags: [pelvic-floor, continence, prolapse]
systems: [genitourinary, reproductive, musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: >-
    Spans the lesser pelvis between the pubis anteriorly, the lateral pelvic walls and the coccyx posteriorly, above
    the [[perineum]] and [[ischioanal-fossa|ischioanal fossae]].
  group: Muscles of the pelvic floor
  origin: >-
    **Levator ani**: the body of the pubis, the **tendinous arch of levator ani** (a thickening of the obturator
    fascia), and the ischial spine. **Coccygeus**: the ischial spine and [[sacrotuberous-and-sacrospinous-ligaments|sacrospinous ligament]].
  insertion: >-
    **Levator ani**: the perineal body, the walls of the prostate or vagina, the rectum and anal canal, the anococcygeal
    body (ligament) and the coccyx. **Coccygeus**: the lateral margins of the lower sacrum and the coccyx.
  innervation: >-
    **Nerve to levator ani** (direct branches of the ventral rami of **S3–S4**), with contributions from the inferior
    rectal and perineal branches of the [[pudendal-nerve]]. Coccygeus: S3–S4 ventral rami.
    {{verify: the pudendal contribution to levator ani is described differently between sources}}
  bloodSupply: Branches of the inferior gluteal, inferior vesical and internal pudendal arteries (from the [[internal-iliac-artery]]).
  action:
    - "Supports the pelvic viscera and resists rises in intra-abdominal pressure (coughing, lifting, forced expiration)"
    - "**Puborectalis** forms a U-shaped sling around the anorectal junction, maintaining the **anorectal angle** (fecal continence); it relaxes during defecation"
    - "Supports the urethra and bladder neck (urinary continence), and contributes to vaginal closure"
    - "Coccygeus supports the coccyx and pulls it forward after defecation or childbirth"
  relations: >-
    Parts of levator ani (medial to lateral): **puborectalis**, **pubococcygeus** (including puboprostaticus/
    pubovaginalis and puboanalis fibers) and **iliococcygeus**. The **urogenital hiatus** between the medial borders
    transmits the urethra (and vagina); the rectum passes posteriorly. Above: the pelvic viscera and pelvic fascia.
    Below: the ischioanal fossae and the perineal membrane.
clinical:
  - title: Pelvic organ prolapse
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Weakening or injury of levator ani and the connective-tissue supports (cardinal and uterosacral ligaments, pelvic
      fascia). Risk factors: **vaginal delivery** (especially operative or with a large baby), age and menopause,
      obesity, chronic cough or constipation, and heavy lifting.
    presentation: >-
      Vaginal bulge or pressure, worse at the end of the day or with straining. Anterior wall (**cystocele**: incomplete
      emptying, UTIs), posterior wall (**rectocele**: need to splint the vagina to defecate), and apical (uterine or
      vault) prolapse.
    notes: Graded with the **POP-Q** system. Management ranges from pelvic floor muscle training and a **pessary** to surgical repair.
  - title: Stress urinary incontinence
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Loss of urethral support (hypermobility) and/or weakness of the urethral sphincter (intrinsic sphincter deficiency), often after childbirth.
    presentation: >-
      Leakage of small volumes with **coughing, sneezing, laughing or lifting**; no urgency. Positive cough stress test.
      First-line treatment is **pelvic floor muscle (Kegel) training**; midurethral sling surgery if that fails.
  - title: Levator ani avulsion
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Detachment of pubococcygeus/puborectalis from the pubis during vaginal delivery, especially forceps-assisted.
    presentation: Widened urogenital hiatus on imaging; a strong risk factor for later prolapse.
pance:
  - "**Kegel (pelvic floor) exercises** are first-line for stress incontinence."
  - "Leak with cough/sneeze = **stress** incontinence; sudden urge then leak = **urge** incontinence (detrusor overactivity)."
  - "**Puborectalis** sling maintains the anorectal angle for fecal continence."
  - "Prolapse risk factor most often tested: **vaginal childbirth**."
  - "Pelvic organ prolapse: pessary (non-surgical) vs surgical repair; grade with POP-Q."
imaging:
  mri: >-
    **Dynamic MRI (MR defecography)** at rest and with straining shows descent of the bladder, cervix/vault and rectum
    relative to the pubococcygeal line, and levator defects.
  ultrasound: >-
    **Translabial (transperineal) 3-D/4-D ultrasound** measures hiatal area and shows levator avulsion; used in
    urogynecology.
  keyViews:
    - "Dynamic pelvic floor MRI at rest, squeeze and Valsalva"
    - "Translabial ultrasound"
exam:
  landmarks: Levator ani is felt on digital vaginal exam as a muscular ridge along the lateral vaginal walls, and puborectalis as a sling posteriorly on digital rectal exam.
  palpation: >-
    Ask the patient to squeeze around the examining finger: grade strength (e.g. **modified Oxford scale 0–5**),
    endurance and symmetry, and feel for a defect of the puborectalis near its pubic attachment.
  testing: Inspect at rest and with Valsalva for prolapse (split speculum to see each vaginal wall separately); **cough stress test** with a comfortably full bladder.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 52-year-old woman who has had three vaginal deliveries reports leaking small amounts of urine when she coughs,
      laughs or lifts groceries. She has no urgency and no nocturia. What is the most appropriate first-line treatment?
    choices:
      - Oxybutynin
      - Mirabegron
      - Pelvic floor muscle training
      - Midurethral sling surgery
      - Indwelling catheter
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      This is **stress urinary incontinence** from weakened pelvic floor and urethral support. **Pelvic floor muscle
      (Kegel) training** is first-line; a midurethral sling is for failure of conservative therapy. Antimuscarinics and
      β3-agonists treat urge incontinence.
  - stem: Which part of levator ani forms a sling around the anorectal junction and maintains the anorectal angle?
    choices:
      - Iliococcygeus
      - Coccygeus
      - Puborectalis
      - External anal sphincter
      - Obturator internus
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Puborectalis** loops around the anorectal junction from the pubis, pulling it forward to create the anorectal
      angle. It relaxes during defecation to straighten the angle.
  - stem: Levator ani arises laterally from which structure?
    choices:
      - Sacrotuberous ligament
      - Tendinous arch of the obturator fascia
      - Inguinal ligament
      - Iliac crest
      - Perineal membrane
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Levator ani arises from the pubic body anteriorly, the **tendinous arch of levator ani** (a thickening of the
      obturator internus fascia) laterally, and the ischial spine posteriorly.
