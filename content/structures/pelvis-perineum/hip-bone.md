name: Hip bone
type: bone
region: pelvis-perineum
subregion: Bony pelvis
taName: Os coxae
aka: [Innominate bone, Pelvic bone, Os coxae]
summary: Large flat bone formed by the fusion of the ilium, ischium and pubis at the acetabulum. Its palpable landmarks guide the exam, and its apophyses are classic sites of avulsion fractures in adolescents.
tags: [pelvis, avulsion]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: >-
    The two hip bones form the lateral and anterior walls of the [[bony-pelvis]]. Each articulates posteriorly with the
    [[sacrum]] at the [[sacroiliac-joint]], anteriorly with its partner at the [[pubic-symphysis]], and laterally with the
    femoral head at the acetabulum.
  classification: Irregular (flat) bone
  features:
    - "**Ilium**: iliac crest (highest point at about **L4**), anterior superior iliac spine (**ASIS**; sartorius and the inguinal ligament), anterior inferior iliac spine (**AIIS**; straight head of rectus femoris and the iliofemoral ligament), posterior superior iliac spine (**PSIS**; at about **S2**), iliac fossa (iliacus), arcuate line, auricular surface for the sacrum"
    - "**Ischium**: ischial spine (sacrospinous ligament; landmark for the pudendal nerve), lesser sciatic notch, **ischial tuberosity** (hamstrings and the sacrotuberous ligament; bears weight when sitting), ischial ramus"
    - "**Pubis**: body, pubic tubercle (medial attachment of the inguinal ligament), pubic crest, superior ramus with the pectineal line, inferior ramus"
    - "**Greater sciatic notch**: converted into the greater sciatic foramen by the [[sacrotuberous-and-sacrospinous-ligaments]]"
    - "**Obturator foramen**: closed by the obturator membrane except for the obturator canal (obturator nerve and vessels)"
    - "**Acetabulum**: cup for the femoral head, formed by all three bones; the acetabular notch is bridged by the transverse acetabular ligament"
    - "**Ischiopubic ramus**: joined inferior pubic and ischial rami; forms the side of the pubic arch and anchors the crura of the penis or clitoris"
  ossification: >-
    Three primary centers (ilium, ischium, pubis) appear before birth. They meet at the acetabulum in a Y-shaped
    **triradiate cartilage** that fuses around puberty (about 15–17 years) {{verify: age of triradiate fusion varies
    between sources}}. Secondary centers include the iliac crest, the ASIS/AIIS, the ischial tuberosity and the pubic
    symphysis. The progress of iliac crest apophysis ossification (the **Risser sign**) is used to judge skeletal
    maturity in scoliosis.
  relations: >-
    The external iliac vessels and femoral nerve leave the pelvis over the superior pubic ramus under the inguinal
    ligament. The sciatic nerve exits through the greater sciatic notch below piriformis. The obturator nerve runs along
    the lateral pelvic wall to the obturator canal.
clinical:
  - title: Apophyseal avulsion fractures
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Forceful muscle contraction in adolescents (kicking, sprinting, jumping) pulls off an unfused apophysis: **ASIS**
      (sartorius), **AIIS** (rectus femoris), **ischial tuberosity** (hamstrings), iliac crest (abdominal muscles).
    presentation: >-
      Sudden pain and a "pop" at the attachment, focal tenderness, and pain on resisted action of the muscle. Most are
      managed conservatively; widely displaced fragments (especially the ischial tuberosity) may need fixation.
  - title: Iliac crest contusion ("hip pointer")
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Direct blow to the iliac crest in contact sports.
    presentation: Tenderness and bruising over the crest, pain with trunk rotation or side-bending. Managed conservatively.
  - title: Bone marrow aspiration and biopsy site
    highYield: false
    mechanism: The **posterior iliac crest** (near the PSIS) is the preferred site because it is superficial and away from major vessels.
    presentation: Performed with the patient prone or in the lateral position.
  - title: Iliac crest bone graft harvest
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Graft is taken from the anterior or posterior crest.
    presentation: >-
      Complications include donor-site pain and injury to the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve (numbness of the lateral
      thigh) when harvesting near the ASIS.
pance:
  - "Adolescent athlete + sudden hip/pelvic pain after kicking or sprinting → **apophyseal avulsion** (ASIS–sartorius, AIIS–rectus femoris, ischial tuberosity–hamstrings)."
  - "Iliac crest = **L4** (intercristal line); PSIS/dimples of Venus = **S2**."
  - "The ischial spine is the landmark for a **pudendal nerve block**."
  - "Posterior iliac crest is the usual site for **bone marrow biopsy**."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    **AP pelvis** shows the whole ring, both hip bones and the proximal femora. **Judet (45° oblique) views** show the
    anterior and posterior columns and walls of the acetabulum. Avulsions appear as a displaced crescent of bone next to
    the apophysis; compare with the other side.
  ct: >-
    CT with 3-D reconstruction is the standard for acetabular and complex pelvic fractures and for surgical planning.
  mri: Detects occult fractures, stress fractures and apophysitis, and shows muscle–tendon injury at avulsion sites.
  keyViews:
    - "AP pelvis"
    - "Judet views (iliac and obturator obliques) for the acetabulum"
    - "Inlet and outlet views for the pelvic ring"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    Iliac crests (their tops are level with **L4**), **ASIS** at the anterior end of the crest, **PSIS** beneath the
    skin dimples above the buttocks (level of **S2**), **pubic tubercle** about 2–3 cm lateral to the midline on the
    pubic crest, and the **ischial tuberosity** deep in the lower buttock (easiest to feel with the hip flexed).
  palpation: >-
    Palpate the crest from the ASIS to the PSIS for tenderness (hip pointer), and each apophysis in an adolescent with
    acute pain. Check that both ASIS are level (pelvic obliquity) with the patient standing.
  testing: >-
    **True leg length** is measured from the ASIS to the medial malleolus; **apparent leg length** from the umbilicus
    to the medial malleolus.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 15-year-old soccer player feels a pop in the front of the hip while taking a hard shot on goal. There is
      tenderness just below the anterior superior iliac spine and pain on resisted hip flexion with the knee extended.
      Which muscle most likely avulsed its attachment?
    choices:
      - Sartorius
      - Rectus femoris
      - Iliopsoas
      - Tensor fasciae latae
      - Adductor longus
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The straight head of **rectus femoris** arises from the **AIIS**, just below the ASIS. Kicking loads rectus
      femoris strongly (hip flexion plus knee extension). Sartorius avulses the ASIS itself; iliopsoas avulses the
      lesser trochanter of the femur.
  - stem: Which landmark is at the level of the second sacral vertebra?
    choices:
      - Highest point of the iliac crest
      - Anterior superior iliac spine
      - Posterior superior iliac spine
      - Ischial tuberosity
      - Pubic tubercle
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **PSIS** (marked by the skin dimples above the buttocks) lies at about **S2**, which is also roughly where the
      dural sac ends. The highest points of the iliac crests are at about L4.
  - stem: The three parts of the hip bone fuse at which structure?
    choices:
      - Obturator foramen
      - Greater sciatic notch
      - Acetabulum
      - Pubic symphysis
      - Iliac crest
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The ilium, ischium and pubis meet at the **acetabulum** through the Y-shaped triradiate cartilage, which fuses
      around puberty.
