summary: Ring formed by the two hip bones, the sacrum and the coccyx. Its inlet and outlet define the birth canal, and ring fractures from high-energy trauma can cause life-threatening hemorrhage.
tags: [pelvis, trauma, obstetrics]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: >-
    Between the lumbar spine and the lower limbs. Formed by the two [[hip-bone|hip bones]] (anteriorly and laterally)
    and the [[sacrum]] and coccyx (posteriorly), joined at the [[sacroiliac-joint|sacroiliac joints]] and the
    [[pubic-symphysis]].
  classification: Bony ring (pelvic girdle); strictly the pelvic girdle is the two hip bones and the sacrum.
  features:
    - "**Pelvic inlet (brim)**: sacral promontory, ala of the sacrum, arcuate line of the ilium, pectineal line, pubic crest and the upper margin of the symphysis"
    - "**Greater (false) pelvis**: above the brim, part of the abdominal cavity (iliac fossae)"
    - "**Lesser (true) pelvis**: below the brim; contains the pelvic viscera and forms the bony birth canal"
    - "**Pelvic outlet**: diamond-shaped, bounded by the pubic arch, ischial tuberosities, [[sacrotuberous-and-sacrospinous-ligaments|sacrotuberous ligaments]] and the tip of the coccyx"
    - "**Obstetric conjugate**: sacral promontory to the nearest point on the back of the symphysis; the **shortest AP diameter** the fetal head must pass (about 10–11 cm)"
    - "**Diagonal conjugate**: sacral promontory to the lower border of the symphysis; the one measured clinically (about 12.5 cm). The obstetric conjugate is about 1.5–2 cm shorter. {{verify: typical values vary slightly between obstetric texts}}"
    - "**Interspinous diameter**: between the ischial spines (about 10 cm); usually the narrowest diameter of the midpelvis. The ischial spines also mark fetal **station 0**."
    - "**Subpubic angle**: wide in females (about 80–90° or more), narrow in males (about 50–60°)"
  ossification: See [[hip-bone]] and [[sacrum]].
  relations: >-
    The lesser pelvis contains the [[urinary-bladder]], [[rectum]] and reproductive organs; its floor is the
    [[pelvic-diaphragm]]. The lateral walls are lined by obturator internus; the posterior wall carries piriformis and
    the [[sacral-plexus]].
  notes: >-
    **Female vs male pelvis**: the female pelvis is lighter, with a round or oval inlet, a wider subpubic angle, a
    larger outlet, everted ischial tuberosities and less prominent ischial spines. The male inlet is heart-shaped.
    Caldwell–Moloy shapes are **gynecoid** (most favorable for vaginal delivery), android, anthropoid and platypelloid.
clinical:
  - title: Pelvic ring fracture and hemorrhage
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      High-energy trauma (motor vehicle collision, fall from height). A ring usually breaks in **two places**.
      **Anteroposterior compression** ("open book") widens the symphysis and disrupts the sacroiliac joints, enlarging
      pelvic volume. **Lateral compression** is the most common pattern. **Vertical shear** displaces a hemipelvis
      upward.
    presentation: >-
      Pelvic pain, instability, perineal or scrotal bruising, and **hemorrhagic shock**. Bleeding is mostly **venous**
      (presacral and vesical plexuses) and from fracture surfaces; arterial bleeding (e.g. superior gluteal, internal
      pudendal, obturator from the [[internal-iliac-artery]]) is less common but may need embolization. Associated
      injuries include the [[urethra]] and [[urinary-bladder|bladder]].
    notes: >-
      Initial management: a **pelvic binder centered over the greater trochanters**, resuscitation, then angioembolization,
      preperitoneal packing or external fixation for ongoing bleeding. Do not repeatedly "spring" the pelvis to test
      stability, as this can dislodge clot.
  - title: Pubic ramus fracture in older adults
    highYield: true
    mechanism: A low-energy fall in an older adult with osteoporosis.
    presentation: >-
      Groin pain and difficulty walking after a fall, often with normal hip X-rays at first. Usually treated with pain
      control and weight-bearing as tolerated. Look for an associated (often occult) sacral insufficiency fracture.
  - title: Cephalopelvic disproportion
    highYield: false
    mechanism: A fetal head too large for the maternal pelvis (or poorly positioned), including a contracted pelvis.
    presentation: Arrest of labor despite adequate contractions; usually leads to cesarean delivery.
pance:
  - "Hypotensive trauma patient with a pelvic fracture → **pelvic binder at the greater trochanters**; bleeding is mostly venous."
  - "Pelvic fracture + **blood at the urethral meatus** → retrograde urethrogram **before** a Foley catheter."
  - "Obstetric conjugate = shortest AP diameter; diagonal conjugate is measured clinically."
  - "Ischial spines = fetal station 0 and the landmark for a pudendal block."
  - "Gynecoid pelvis is the most favorable for vaginal delivery."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    **AP pelvis** is a standard trauma adjunct in unstable patients. **Inlet view** (beam angled caudally) shows AP
    displacement; **outlet view** (angled cranially) shows vertical displacement and the sacral foramina. Symphyseal
    widening above about 2.5 cm suggests disruption of the pelvic floor and sacroiliac ligaments.
    {{verify: threshold for symphyseal diastasis in APC injuries}}
  ct: >-
    CT of the abdomen and pelvis with IV contrast is the definitive study in stable patients: fracture pattern,
    sacroiliac involvement, and an **active contrast blush** indicating arterial bleeding.
  mri: Detects occult insufficiency fractures of the sacrum and pubic rami in older adults.
  keyViews:
    - "AP pelvis (trauma series)"
    - "Inlet and outlet views"
    - "CT abdomen/pelvis with IV contrast"
exam:
  landmarks: Iliac crests, ASIS, pubic symphysis and tubercles, ischial tuberosities, PSIS and sacrum.
  palpation: >-
    In trauma, gentle palpation of the pubic symphysis and iliac wings once only; avoid repeated compression. Inspect
    the perineum, scrotum and urethral meatus for blood, and perform a rectal (and in females, vaginal) exam for an open
    fracture.
  testing: >-
    Clinical pelvimetry on bimanual exam estimates the diagonal conjugate, the prominence of the ischial spines and the
    subpubic angle.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 34-year-old man is brought in after a motorcycle collision. BP 78/50 mm Hg, pulse 136/min. The pelvis is
      unstable to gentle compression and a FAST exam is negative. What is the most appropriate immediate step?
    choices:
      - Apply a pelvic binder centered over the greater trochanters
      - Insert a Foley catheter to monitor urine output
      - Obtain inlet and outlet pelvic radiographs
      - Perform an exploratory laparotomy
      - Repeat the pelvic compression test to confirm instability
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      Hemodynamic instability with an unstable pelvis and negative FAST suggests **pelvic hemorrhage**. A **binder over
      the greater trochanters** reduces pelvic volume and tamponades venous bleeding while resuscitation continues. A
      Foley should wait until urethral injury is excluded, and repeated compression can dislodge clot.
  - stem: Which pelvic diameter is the shortest anteroposterior diameter through which the fetal head must pass?
    choices:
      - True (anatomical) conjugate
      - Obstetric conjugate
      - Diagonal conjugate
      - Interspinous diameter
      - Transverse diameter of the inlet
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **obstetric conjugate** runs from the sacral promontory to the closest point on the posterior surface of the
      symphysis. It can't be measured directly, so it is estimated by subtracting about 1.5–2 cm from the **diagonal
      conjugate** measured on exam. The interspinous diameter is transverse, not AP.
  - stem: Compared with the male pelvis, the female pelvis typically has which feature?
    choices:
      - Heart-shaped inlet
      - Narrower subpubic angle
      - Wider subpubic angle and larger outlet
      - More prominent ischial spines
      - Deeper, narrower lesser pelvis
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The female pelvis is adapted for childbirth: a **wider subpubic angle**, an oval inlet, a larger outlet, and less
      prominent ischial spines. A heart-shaped inlet and narrow subpubic angle are male (android) features.
