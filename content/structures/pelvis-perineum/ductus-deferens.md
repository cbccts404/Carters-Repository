name: Ductus deferens and seminal glands
type: organ
region: pelvis-perineum
subregion: Male reproductive organs
taName: Ductus deferens; Glandula vesiculosa
aka: [Vas deferens, Seminal vesicles]
summary: Thick-walled muscular duct carrying sperm from the epididymis to the ejaculatory duct, joined behind the bladder by the seminal gland. It is cut in vasectomy and absent in men with cystic fibrosis.
tags: [male, genitourinary, infertility]
systems: [genitourinary]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: >-
    From the tail of the epididymis up the back of the [[testis]] in the [[spermatic-cord]], through the
    [[inguinal-canal]], then across the lateral pelvic wall and behind the [[urinary-bladder]] to the base of the
    [[prostate]]. About 45 cm long.
  parts:
    - "**Scrotal part**: easily palpable as a firm cord (the site of vasectomy)"
    - "**Inguinal part**: within the spermatic cord in the inguinal canal"
    - "**Pelvic part**: at the deep inguinal ring it leaves the cord, **hooks lateral to the inferior epigastric artery**, crosses the external iliac vessels, and passes **over the ureter** near the bladder"
    - "**Ampulla**: dilated end behind the bladder, medial to the seminal gland"
    - "**Seminal glands (vesicles)**: coiled glands lateral to the ampullae, behind the bladder; secrete an alkaline, **fructose-rich** fluid forming most of the semen volume"
    - "**Ejaculatory ducts**: formed by the union of the ductus and the seminal gland duct; pass through the prostate to open on the **seminal colliculus** of the prostatic [[urethra]]"
  bloodSupply: Artery to the ductus deferens (usually from the superior or inferior vesical artery, anterior division of the [[internal-iliac-artery]]); seminal glands from the inferior vesical and middle rectal arteries.
  venousDrainage: Pampiniform plexus (scrotal part) and the vesical/prostatic venous plexuses.
  lymphatics: External and internal iliac nodes.
  innervation: >-
    Sympathetic fibers (about L1–L2) via the hypogastric plexuses cause the peristaltic contraction of the ductus and
    seminal glands during **emission**. See [[pelvic-autonomic-nerves]].
  relations: The [[ureter]] passes **under** the ductus deferens near the posterolateral bladder (the male "water under the bridge"). The ampullae and seminal glands lie against the anterior wall of the [[rectum]].
clinical:
  - title: Vasectomy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Division or occlusion of the scrotal ductus deferens bilaterally.
    presentation: >-
      Ejaculate volume is almost unchanged (most comes from the seminal glands and prostate). Sperm remain downstream for
      weeks, so contraception continues until a **post-vasectomy semen analysis** confirms azoospermia (the AUA
      allows testing from **8 weeks** after vasectomy).
  - title: Congenital bilateral absence of the vas deferens (CBAVD)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Associated with **CFTR mutations** — present in almost all men with cystic fibrosis.
    presentation: '**Obstructive azoospermia** with low ejaculate volume and acidic semen (no seminal gland fluid); the ductus cannot be felt in the scrotum. Offer CFTR testing to the patient and partner.'
  - title: Injury during inguinal hernia repair
    highYield: false
    mechanism: The ductus runs through the inguinal canal in the spermatic cord.
    presentation: Obstruction (unilateral obstructive azoospermia contributes to infertility).
pance:
  - "Male infertility with absent vas deferens on exam → **cystic fibrosis/CFTR** testing."
  - "After vasectomy, use contraception until semen analysis shows **azoospermia**."
  - "Emission = **sympathetic** (ductus, seminal glands, prostate contract)."
  - "Seminal glands supply most of the semen volume (fructose)."
imaging:
  ultrasound: >-
    **Transrectal ultrasound** shows dilated seminal glands or ejaculatory duct cysts in ejaculatory duct obstruction,
    and absent or hypoplastic seminal glands in CBAVD. Scrotal ultrasound shows the epididymis and scrotal ductus.
  mri: Shows seminal gland invasion in prostate cancer staging.
exam:
  landmarks: The scrotal ductus is palpable in the posterior part of the spermatic cord between the testis and the superficial inguinal ring.
  palpation: >-
    Roll the spermatic cord between the thumb and fingers: the ductus is a firm, smooth "whipcord". Its absence in an
    infertile man suggests CBAVD. Normal seminal glands are not palpable on DRE.
