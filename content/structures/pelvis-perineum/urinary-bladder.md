name: Urinary bladder
type: organ
region: pelvis-perineum
subregion: Pelvic viscera
taName: Vesica urinaria
summary: Distensible muscular reservoir for urine lying behind the pubis. Its autonomic control explains neurogenic bladder, and rupture type (intraperitoneal vs extraperitoneal) decides management.
tags: [genitourinary, trauma]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: >-
    When empty, in the lesser pelvis behind the [[pubic-symphysis]], separated from it by the retropubic (prevesical)
    space. As it fills it rises **extraperitoneally** into the lower abdomen, lifting the peritoneum off the anterior
    abdominal wall.
  parts:
    - "**Apex**: points toward the symphysis; continues as the **median umbilical ligament** (obliterated urachus) to the umbilicus"
    - "**Body** and **fundus (base)**: the base faces posteroinferiorly toward the rectum (male) or vagina (female)"
    - "**Neck**: where the base and inferolateral surfaces meet, around the internal urethral orifice; rests on the [[prostate]] in males"
    - "**Trigone**: smooth triangle between the two ureteric orifices and the internal urethral orifice (derived from the mesonephric ducts, so it lacks rugae)"
    - "**Detrusor muscle**: interlacing smooth muscle forming the wall; in males a circular **internal urethral sphincter** at the neck prevents retrograde ejaculation"
  bloodSupply: >-
    **Superior vesical arteries** (from the patent part of the umbilical artery) and **inferior vesical arteries**
    (males) or **vaginal arteries** (females), all from the anterior division of the [[internal-iliac-artery]].
  venousDrainage: Vesical venous plexus (continuous with the prostatic plexus in males) → internal iliac veins.
  lymphatics: External and internal iliac nodes (see [[pelvic-lymph-nodes]]).
  innervation: >-
    **Parasympathetic** (pelvic splanchnic nerves, **S2–S4**; muscarinic M3) → detrusor contraction (micturition).
    **Sympathetic** (T11–L2 via the hypogastric plexuses) → detrusor relaxation (β3) and contraction of the bladder
    neck/internal sphincter (α1) = storage. **Somatic**: [[pudendal-nerve]] (S2–S4) → voluntary external urethral
    sphincter. See [[pelvic-autonomic-nerves]].
  relations: >-
    Superior surface covered by peritoneum; in females the uterus lies on it (vesicouterine pouch). Males: the
    [[ductus-deferens]], seminal glands and [[rectum]] lie posterior to the base. The [[ureter|ureters]] enter the base
    obliquely, which forms a flap-valve against reflux.
clinical:
  - title: Bladder rupture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      **Extraperitoneal** (most common): associated with **pelvic fractures** (bone spicules or shearing).
      **Intraperitoneal**: a blow to the lower abdomen when the bladder is **full** bursts the **dome**, the weakest part
      covered by peritoneum.
    presentation: >-
      Gross hematuria, suprapubic pain, inability to void. Intraperitoneal rupture causes urinary ascites, peritonitis
      and a rising creatinine (peritoneal reabsorption).
    notes: Extraperitoneal rupture is usually managed with **catheter drainage**; intraperitoneal rupture needs **surgical repair**.
  - title: Acute urinary retention
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Outlet obstruction (**BPH** most common in men), anticholinergic or opioid drugs, constipation, postoperative
      state, or neurologic causes (spinal cord injury, **cauda equina syndrome**, diabetic neuropathy).
    presentation: Painful inability to void with a palpable, dull suprapubic mass; overflow incontinence in chronic retention. Treat with catheter drainage and address the cause.
  - title: Urge incontinence (overactive bladder)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Involuntary detrusor contractions (detrusor overactivity).
    presentation: >-
      Sudden strong urge followed by leakage, frequency and nocturia. First-line: behavioral therapy (bladder training,
      fluid and caffeine reduction). Then **antimuscarinics** (e.g. oxybutynin; anticholinergic side effects, caution in
      older adults) or the **β3-agonist mirabegron**.
  - title: Bladder cancer
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Usually **urothelial carcinoma**. Risk factors — **smoking** (most important), aromatic amines/aniline dyes, cyclophosphamide; *Schistosoma haematobium* causes squamous cell carcinoma.
    presentation: '**Painless gross hematuria** in an older smoker. Evaluate with **cystoscopy** plus CT urography.'
  - title: Urachal anomalies
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Failure of the urachus (allantois remnant) to obliterate.
    presentation: Patent urachus leaks urine from the umbilicus; urachal cyst or sinus may become infected; urachal adenocarcinoma arises at the bladder dome.
pance:
  - "Pelvic fracture + hematuria → **extraperitoneal** rupture (catheter). Full bladder + blow → **intraperitoneal** rupture at the dome (surgery)."
  - "Parasympathetic S2–S4 = **pee** (detrusor contraction); sympathetic = storage."
  - "Urge incontinence → behavioral therapy, then **oxybutynin** or **mirabegron**."
  - "Painless gross hematuria in a smoker = **bladder cancer** until proven otherwise → cystoscopy."
  - "Urine draining from the umbilicus in a newborn → **patent urachus**."
imaging:
  xray: Retrograde cystography (plain film) is an older method for detecting rupture.
  ct: >-
    **CT cystography** (retrograde filling of the bladder with dilute contrast through a catheter) is the test of
    choice for suspected rupture: contrast outlining bowel loops = intraperitoneal; "flame-shaped" or "molar tooth"
    extravasation around the bladder = extraperitoneal. **CT urography** for hematuria evaluation of the upper tracts.
  ultrasound: >-
    **Bedside bladder scan** measures post-void residual (PVR) volume. Ultrasound shows wall thickening, masses,
    stones, and ureteral jets.
  keyViews:
    - "CT cystogram (bladder distended with contrast)"
    - "Voiding cystourethrogram (VCUG) for vesicoureteral reflux in children"
exam:
  landmarks: The empty bladder lies behind the symphysis and can't be felt; a distended bladder rises toward the umbilicus.
  palpation: A distended bladder is a smooth, midline, dull-to-percussion suprapubic mass that may cause an urge to void when pressed.
  testing: >-
    Measure PVR after voiding (bladder scan or catheter). Neurologic exam for neurogenic bladder: saddle sensation,
    anal tone, bulbocavernosus reflex (S2–S4). Suprapubic catheters are inserted in the midline about 2 finger-breadths
    above the symphysis into a distended bladder, staying extraperitoneal.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      After a night of drinking, a 27-year-old man is punched in the lower abdomen. He has diffuse abdominal pain, gross
      hematuria and a rising serum creatinine. CT cystography shows contrast outlining loops of bowel. What is the most
      appropriate management?
    choices:
      - Foley catheter drainage alone
      - Surgical repair of the bladder
      - Suprapubic catheter and observation
      - Ureteral stenting
      - Oral antibiotics and discharge
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A blow to a **full bladder** ruptures the **dome** into the peritoneal cavity (contrast around bowel loops).
      **Intraperitoneal rupture** requires surgical repair; urine absorbed across the peritoneum raises creatinine.
      Extraperitoneal ruptures are usually managed with catheter drainage.
  - stem: Which receptor mediates detrusor contraction during micturition?
    choices:
      - α1-adrenergic
      - β3-adrenergic
      - Muscarinic M3
      - Nicotinic
      - Dopamine D2
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Parasympathetic** pelvic splanchnic nerves (S2–S4) release acetylcholine onto **M3 receptors** on the
      detrusor, which is why antimuscarinics treat overactive bladder. β3 stimulation relaxes the detrusor (mirabegron);
      α1 contracts the bladder neck.
  - stem: A 68-year-old man with a 50-pack-year smoking history has painless gross hematuria. Urinalysis shows no infection. What is the most important next step?
    choices:
      - Reassure and repeat urinalysis in a year
      - PSA measurement only
      - Cystoscopy and CT urography
      - Renal biopsy
      - Empiric antibiotics
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Painless gross hematuria** in an older smoker is highly concerning for **urothelial carcinoma**. Evaluation needs
      **cystoscopy** (bladder) plus **CT urography** (upper tracts).
