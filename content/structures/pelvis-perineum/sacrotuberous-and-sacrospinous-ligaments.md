---
name: Sacrotuberous and sacrospinous ligaments
type: ligament
region: pelvis-perineum
subregion: Bony pelvis
taName: Ligamentum sacrotuberale; Ligamentum sacrospinale
summary: Two strong ligaments from the sacrum and coccyx to the ischium that stabilize the sacrum and turn the sciatic notches into the greater and lesser sciatic foramina, the gateways between pelvis, gluteal region and perineum.
tags: [pelvis, sciatic-foramina]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Posterolateral wall of the [[bony-pelvis]], deep to gluteus maximus.
  attachments: >-
    **Sacrotuberous**: from the PSIS, the lateral border of the lower [[sacrum]] and the coccyx to the medial margin of
    the **ischial tuberosity**. **Sacrospinous**: from the lateral margin of the lower sacrum and coccyx to the
    **ischial spine**; it lies anterior (deep) to the sacrotuberous ligament and is fused with the coccygeus muscle.
  function: >-
    Resist upward rotation of the lower sacrum (and forward tilting of the upper sacrum) as body weight is transmitted
    through the [[sacroiliac-joint]]. Together they convert the greater and lesser sciatic notches into the **greater
    and lesser sciatic foramina**.
  relations: >-
    **Greater sciatic foramen** (above the sacrospinous ligament): piriformis passes through and divides it. **Above
    piriformis**: superior gluteal nerve and vessels. **Below piriformis**: inferior gluteal nerve and vessels, sciatic
    nerve, posterior femoral cutaneous nerve, nerve to quadratus femoris, and the **pudendal nerve, internal pudendal
    vessels and nerve to obturator internus**. **Lesser sciatic foramen** (between the two ligaments): tendon of
    obturator internus, and the [[pudendal-nerve]], internal pudendal vessels and nerve to obturator internus, which
    cross the ischial spine/sacrospinous ligament to re-enter the perineum.
  notes: >-
    Mnemonic **"PIN"**: the **P**udendal nerve, **I**nternal pudendal vessels and **N**erve to obturator internus leave
    through the greater and re-enter through the lesser sciatic foramen.
clinical:
  - title: Pudendal nerve block
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      The pudendal nerve crosses the posterior surface of the sacrospinous ligament just medial to the ischial spine.
      Transvaginally, the palpable ischial spine guides the needle through the ligament.
    presentation: >-
      Anesthesia of most of the perineum for the second stage of labor, operative vaginal delivery or perineal repair.
      Aspirate first: the internal pudendal vessels lie alongside.
  - title: Sacrospinous ligament fixation
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Surgical suspension of the vaginal apex to the sacrospinous ligament to treat apical (vault) prolapse.
    presentation: >-
      Sutures placed too close to the ischial spine risk the pudendal nerve and internal pudendal vessels; placed too
      superiorly they risk the inferior gluteal vessels and sacral plexus, causing buttock pain.
  - title: Pudendal nerve entrapment
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Compression of the [[pudendal-nerve]] between the sacrotuberous and sacrospinous ligaments or in the pudendal canal (e.g. long-distance cycling).
    presentation: Burning perineal or genital pain that is **worse sitting** and relieved by standing or lying down.
pance:
  - "Sacrospinous → ischial **spine**; sacrotuberous → ischial **tuberosity**."
  - "**PIN** (pudendal nerve, internal pudendal vessels, nerve to obturator internus) exit the greater and enter the lesser sciatic foramen."
  - "Pudendal block: needle through the sacrospinous ligament just medial to the ischial spine."
imaging:
  ct: CT shows ossification or avulsion at their attachments and guides pudendal nerve injections near the ischial spine.
  mri: MRI shows the ligaments as low-signal bands and the pudendal canal; used in suspected pudendal nerve entrapment.
exam:
  landmarks: The ischial spine is palpable on vaginal (or rectal) exam at about 4 and 8 o'clock; the sacrotuberous ligament can be felt deep in the buttock between the ischial tuberosity and the sacrum.
  palpation: Tenderness over the ischial spine or along the sacrospinous ligament on internal exam supports pudendal nerve entrapment.
