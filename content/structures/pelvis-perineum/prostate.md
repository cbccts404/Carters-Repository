anatomy:
  location: >-
    Below the bladder neck and above the external urethral sphincter/perineal membrane, behind the pubic symphysis
    (retropubic space) and in front of the [[rectum]], from which it is separated by the rectoprostatic
    (Denonvilliers) fascia.
  parts:
    - "**Base** against the bladder neck; **apex** inferiorly at the external sphincter"
    - "**Peripheral zone**: about 70% of the glandular tissue, posterior and lateral; the site of **most carcinomas**; the part palpated on DRE"
    - "**Central zone**: about 25%, surrounds the **ejaculatory ducts**"
    - "**Transition zone**: about 5–10%, surrounds the proximal prostatic urethra; the site of **benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH)**"
    - "**Anterior fibromuscular stroma**: non-glandular"
    - "Older lobar terms: anterior, median (middle), two lateral and posterior lobes; an enlarged **median lobe** projects into the bladder and obstructs the internal orifice"
  bloodSupply: Prostatic branches of the **inferior vesical artery**, plus the middle rectal and internal pudendal arteries (from the [[internal-iliac-artery]]).
  venousDrainage: >-
    **Prostatic venous plexus** (receiving the deep dorsal vein of the penis) → internal iliac veins. It communicates
    with the [[vertebral-venous-plexus]], a route for **metastasis to the lumbar spine and pelvis**.
  lymphatics: Internal iliac and sacral nodes, and obturator/external iliac nodes (see [[pelvic-lymph-nodes]]).
  innervation: >-
    Prostatic plexus from the inferior hypogastric plexus: sympathetic fibers (α1) contract the stromal smooth muscle
    during **emission**; parasympathetic (S2–S4) fibers are secretory. The **cavernous nerves** to the penis run
    posterolateral to the prostate (see [[pelvic-autonomic-nerves]]).
  relations: >-
    Traversed by the prostatic [[urethra]] and the two ejaculatory ducts (formed by the [[ductus-deferens]] and the
    seminal gland ducts). Seminal glands lie above its base behind the bladder.
clinical:
  - title: Benign prostatic hyperplasia
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Age- and androgen (DHT)-dependent nodular hyperplasia of the **transition zone** compressing the urethra.
    presentation: >-
      Lower urinary tract symptoms in older men — hesitancy, weak stream, straining, incomplete emptying, frequency,
      **nocturia**. DRE: **smooth, rubbery, symmetrically enlarged** gland. Complications: urinary retention, UTI,
      bladder stones, hydronephrosis.
    notes: >-
      **α1-blockers** (tamsulosin) relax smooth muscle for rapid relief (orthostatic hypotension; floppy iris syndrome
      in cataract surgery). **5-α-reductase inhibitors** (finasteride) shrink the gland over months and roughly halve
      PSA. Surgery (e.g. TURP) for refractory symptoms or complications.
  - title: Prostate cancer
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Adenocarcinoma, most often in the **peripheral zone**. Risk factors — age, Black race, family history, BRCA2.
    presentation: >-
      Usually asymptomatic; found by PSA or a **hard, nodular** area on DRE. Advanced disease causes bone pain from
      **osteoblastic** metastases (lumbar spine, pelvis) and raised alkaline phosphatase.
    notes: >-
      USPSTF: PSA screening for men **55–69** is an individual decision after shared decision-making; not recommended
      at 70 or older. {{verify: check the current USPSTF recommendation}} Evaluation of a raised PSA: repeat PSA, then
      **multiparametric MRI** and targeted biopsy.
  - title: Acute bacterial prostatitis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Ascending infection, usually gram-negative rods (*E. coli*); gonorrhea/chlamydia in younger sexually active men.
    presentation: >-
      Fever, chills, dysuria, perineal or low back pain, and obstructive symptoms. DRE: **exquisitely tender, warm,
      boggy prostate** — avoid vigorous massage (risk of bacteremia). Treat with a fluoroquinolone or TMP-SMX for
      several weeks. {{verify: antibiotic choice and duration}}
  - title: Erectile dysfunction and incontinence after radical prostatectomy
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Injury to the cavernous nerves (posterolateral neurovascular bundles) and the external sphincter.
    presentation: Erectile dysfunction and stress urinary incontinence; nerve-sparing technique reduces the risk.
pance:
  - "BPH = **transition** zone, smooth symmetric enlargement; cancer = **peripheral** zone, hard nodule."
  - "BPH: α1-blocker (fast) ± 5-α-reductase inhibitor (shrinks gland, halves PSA)."
  - "Febrile man with perineal pain and a **tender, boggy** prostate → acute prostatitis; no vigorous massage."
  - "Prostate cancer metastases are **osteoblastic**; spread to the spine via the vertebral venous (Batson) plexus."
  - "PSA screening, age 55–69 → shared decision."
imaging:
  ultrasound: >-
    **Transrectal ultrasound (TRUS)** measures gland volume and guides biopsy (or a transperineal approach).
    Transabdominal ultrasound measures post-void residual and shows bladder wall thickening in BPH.
  mri: >-
    **Multiparametric MRI** (T2, diffusion-weighted, dynamic contrast) scored with **PI-RADS** is used before biopsy
    for a raised PSA, and for local staging.
  ct: Staging of nodes; less useful for the gland itself. Bone scan or **PSMA PET** for metastases.
  keyViews:
    - "Multiparametric prostate MRI (PI-RADS)"
    - "TRUS for volume and biopsy guidance"
exam:
  landmarks: Palpable through the anterior rectal wall, about 3–4 cm above the anal verge.
  palpation: >-
    **DRE**: the normal gland is about walnut-sized, rubbery and non-tender, with a palpable **median sulcus** between
    the lateral lobes. Note size, symmetry, consistency (rubbery vs hard), nodules, obliteration of the median sulcus
    and tenderness.
  testing: Assess obstructive symptoms with the **IPSS** questionnaire; measure post-void residual.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 70-year-old man has nocturia, a weak stream and hesitancy. DRE shows a smooth, rubbery, symmetrically enlarged
      prostate. Which zone of the prostate is primarily involved?
    choices:
      - Peripheral zone
      - Central zone
      - Transition zone
      - Anterior fibromuscular stroma
      - Seminal colliculus
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **BPH** arises in the **transition zone** around the proximal urethra, which is why it causes obstructive symptoms
      early. Carcinoma arises mostly in the peripheral zone, which lies posteriorly and is felt on DRE.
  - stem: A man with metastatic prostate cancer develops low back pain. Through which route do tumor cells most likely reach the lumbar vertebrae?
    choices:
      - Thoracic duct
      - Prostatic venous plexus to the vertebral venous plexus
      - Hepatic portal vein
      - Testicular vein
      - Inferior mesenteric vein
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **prostatic venous plexus** communicates with the valveless **vertebral venous (Batson) plexus**, allowing
      spread to the lumbar spine and pelvis, where the metastases are typically osteoblastic.
  - stem: >-
      A 34-year-old man has fever, chills, dysuria and perineal pain. DRE reveals an exquisitely tender, warm, boggy
      prostate. Which action should be avoided?
    choices:
      - Urine culture
      - Blood cultures
      - Vigorous prostatic massage
      - Starting empiric antibiotics
      - Testing for gonorrhea and chlamydia
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      In **acute bacterial prostatitis**, vigorous prostatic massage can cause **bacteremia** and is contraindicated. A
      gentle DRE is acceptable; send urine (and blood) cultures and STI testing in younger men.
