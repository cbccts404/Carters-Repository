summary: Final part of the large intestine, from the rectosigmoid junction at S3 to the anorectal junction at the puborectalis sling. Rectal cancer staging and treatment depend on its peritoneal relations and pelvic surroundings.
tags: [gastrointestinal, oncology]
systems: [gastrointestinal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: >-
    Follows the concavity of the [[sacrum]] and coccyx in the posterior lesser pelvis, from the **rectosigmoid junction
    (about S3)** to the **anorectal junction**, where it passes through the [[pelvic-diaphragm]] and becomes the
    [[anal-canal]]. About 12–15 cm long.
  parts:
    - "**Rectosigmoid junction**: where the taeniae coli spread out into a continuous outer longitudinal layer and the sigmoid mesocolon ends; the rectum has no taeniae, haustra, epiploic appendices or mesentery"
    - "**Sacral flexure** following the sacrum; **anorectal flexure** (perineal flexure, about 80°) made by the puborectalis sling"
    - "**Transverse rectal folds** (valves of Houston): usually three, two on the left and one (the largest, middle) on the right"
    - "**Rectal ampulla**: the dilated lower part that stores feces"
    - "**Peritoneum**: covers the front and sides of the upper third, the front only of the middle third; the lower third is below the peritoneum. Reflects onto the bladder (**rectovesical pouch**) or posterior vaginal fornix and uterus (**[[rectouterine-pouch]]**)"
  bloodSupply: >-
    **Superior rectal artery** (continuation of the [[inferior-mesenteric-artery]]) — the main supply; **middle rectal
    arteries** (from the [[internal-iliac-artery]]); **inferior rectal arteries** (from the internal pudendal) to the
    anorectal junction.
  venousDrainage: >-
    Superior rectal vein → inferior mesenteric vein → **[[hepatic-portal-vein|portal system]]**; middle and inferior
    rectal veins → internal iliac/internal pudendal veins (**systemic**). The rectal venous plexus is a
    **portosystemic anastomosis**.
  lymphatics: Upper rectum → pararectal and inferior mesenteric nodes (along the superior rectal vessels); lower rectum → internal iliac and sacral nodes (see [[pelvic-lymph-nodes]]).
  innervation: >-
    Sympathetic from the lumbar splanchnic nerves via the hypogastric plexuses; parasympathetic from the **pelvic
    splanchnic nerves (S2–S4)** (see [[pelvic-autonomic-nerves]]). Visceral afferents carry the sensation of rectal
    fullness.
  relations: >-
    Posterior: sacrum, coccyx, the [[sacral-plexus]] and median sacral vessels. Anterior — male: [[urinary-bladder]],
    seminal glands, [[ductus-deferens]] and [[prostate]] (behind the rectoprostatic fascia). Female: [[vagina]], cervix
    and the rectouterine pouch. The **mesorectum** is the fatty sheath containing its vessels and nodes.
clinical:
  - title: Rectal cancer
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Adenocarcinoma arising from adenomatous polyps; risk factors as for colorectal cancer (age, family history, IBD, Lynch syndrome, FAP).
    presentation: >-
      **Hematochezia**, change in bowel habit, narrow stools, **tenesmus**, iron-deficiency anemia; a mass may be felt
      on DRE.
    notes: >-
      Local staging with **pelvic MRI** (depth, nodes, circumferential resection margin) or endorectal ultrasound.
      Locally advanced tumors get neoadjuvant chemoradiation, then **total mesorectal excision**. Screening for
      average-risk adults begins at **45** (USPSTF 2021).
  - title: Rectal prolapse
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Full-thickness protrusion of the rectal wall through the anus; associated with chronic straining, pelvic floor weakness, and cystic fibrosis in children.
    presentation: A protruding mass with **concentric** mucosal folds (hemorrhoids show **radial** folds), mucus discharge, incontinence.
  - title: Proctitis
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Inflammation of the rectal mucosa — ulcerative colitis (always starts in the rectum), STIs (gonorrhea, chlamydia including LGV, HSV, syphilis), radiation.
    presentation: Tenesmus, urgency, rectal bleeding and mucus.
pance:
  - "Ulcerative colitis **always involves the rectum** and extends proximally."
  - "Rectal cancer local staging = **pelvic MRI**; surgery = **total mesorectal excision**."
  - "CRC screening starts at **45** for average risk."
  - "Rectal prolapse = concentric rings; prolapsed hemorrhoids = radial folds."
  - "Rectal venous plexus = portosystemic anastomosis (portal hypertension)."
imaging:
  ct: CT chest/abdomen/pelvis for distant staging (liver and lung metastases).
  mri: >-
    **High-resolution pelvic MRI** is the key local staging study: tumor depth, mesorectal fascia involvement
    (predicts the circumferential resection margin), extramural vascular invasion and nodes.
  ultrasound: Endorectal ultrasound stages early (T1–T2) tumors.
  keyViews:
    - "Pelvic MRI rectal cancer protocol"
    - "Colonoscopy with biopsy"
exam:
  landmarks: The lower rectum is within reach of the examining finger (about 7–8 cm from the anal verge); the prostate or cervix is felt anteriorly.
  palpation: >-
    **DRE**: note masses (distance from the anal verge, fixed or mobile), tenderness, stool character, and blood on the
    glove. An anterior shelf-like mass can indicate peritoneal metastases in the rectovesical/rectouterine pouch
    (**Blumer shelf**).
  testing: Stool guaiac/FIT is for screening, not for evaluating a patient who has symptoms.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 61-year-old man has 3 months of rectal bleeding, tenesmus and thinner stools. A mass is felt 6 cm from the anal
      verge and biopsy shows adenocarcinoma. What is the best study for local staging?
    choices:
      - Barium enema
      - Pelvic MRI
      - Plain abdominal radiograph
      - CEA level alone
      - Transabdominal ultrasound
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Pelvic MRI** defines tumor depth, nodal involvement and the relationship to the **mesorectal fascia**, which
      guides neoadjuvant therapy and total mesorectal excision. CT staging is for distant spread.
  - stem: The superior rectal artery is a continuation of which artery?
    choices:
      - Internal iliac artery
      - Internal pudendal artery
      - Inferior mesenteric artery
      - Superior mesenteric artery
      - Median sacral artery
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **superior rectal artery** is the terminal continuation of the **inferior mesenteric artery**, and it is the
      main supply to the rectum. The middle rectal arteries come from the internal iliac and the inferior rectal from the
      internal pudendal.
  - stem: Which feature distinguishes the rectum from the sigmoid colon?
    choices:
      - Presence of haustra
      - Presence of taeniae coli
      - Absence of taeniae coli and a mesentery
      - Presence of epiploic appendices
      - Supply by the superior mesenteric artery
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      At the rectosigmoid junction the **taeniae coli spread out** into a continuous longitudinal layer; the rectum has
      no taeniae, haustra, epiploic appendices or mesentery.
  - stem: >-
      A 75-year-old woman has a mass protruding from the anus with concentric mucosal folds, mucus discharge and fecal
      incontinence. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Prolapsed internal hemorrhoids
      - Anal fissure
      - Thrombosed external hemorrhoid
      - Rectal prolapse
      - Anal cancer
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      **Rectal prolapse** is a full-thickness protrusion with **concentric** mucosal folds; prolapsed hemorrhoids show
      **radial** folds. It is associated with chronic straining and pelvic floor weakness.
  - stem: Why is the rectal venous plexus a site of portosystemic anastomosis?
    choices:
      - The rectum has no venous drainage below the peritoneal reflection
      - The superior rectal vein drains to the portal system, while the middle and inferior rectal veins drain to systemic veins
      - The superior rectal vein drains directly into the inferior vena cava
      - The inferior rectal veins drain into the inferior mesenteric vein
      - The rectum drains only to the internal iliac veins
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **superior rectal vein** → inferior mesenteric vein → **portal** system, whereas the **middle and inferior
      rectal veins** → internal iliac/internal pudendal veins (**systemic**). The plexus joining them is a portosystemic
      anastomosis that can dilate in portal hypertension.
