summary: Fibromuscular canal from the cervix to the vestibule. Its fornices, relations and split nerve and lymph supply matter for pelvic exams, vaginitis, prolapse and cancer spread.
tags: [female, gynecology, sti]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: >-
    Runs upward and backward from the vestibule of the [[vulva]] to the cervix, passing through the urogenital hiatus of
    the [[pelvic-diaphragm]]. About 7–9 cm long; the posterior wall is longer than the anterior wall.
  parts:
    - "**Fornices**: recesses around the cervix — anterior, posterior (the deepest, next to the [[rectouterine-pouch]]) and two lateral"
    - "**Vaginal orifice** in the vestibule, partly closed by the hymen (or its remnants)"
    - "Wall: non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium (no glands; lubrication from cervical mucus and transudate), smooth muscle, adventitia"
  bloodSupply: Vaginal arteries (from the [[internal-iliac-artery]] or [[uterine-artery]]) for the upper part; the internal pudendal artery for the lower part.
  venousDrainage: Vaginal venous plexus → internal iliac veins.
  lymphatics: >-
    Upper part → internal and external iliac nodes; middle → internal iliac nodes; **lower part (near the orifice)**
    → sacral, common iliac and **superficial inguinal** nodes. See [[pelvic-lymph-nodes]].
  innervation: >-
    Upper three-quarters: **visceral** (uterovaginal plexus), sensitive mainly to stretch. Lower quarter: **somatic**
    (deep perineal nerve from the [[pudendal-nerve]]), sensitive to pain and touch.
  relations: >-
    Anterior: the base of the [[urinary-bladder]] and the [[urethra]]. Posterior: the rectouterine pouch (upper), the
    [[rectum]] (middle) and the perineal body and [[anal-canal]] (lower). Lateral: levator ani, pelvic fascia and, near
    the lateral fornices, the [[ureter|ureters]] and uterine arteries.
clinical:
  - title: Vaginitis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Bacterial vaginosis (overgrowth of *Gardnerella* and anaerobes), vulvovaginal candidiasis, or trichomoniasis.
    presentation: >-
      **Bacterial vaginosis**: thin gray discharge, **fishy odor**, **clue cells**, pH > 4.5, positive whiff test →
      metronidazole. **Candidiasis**: thick white "cottage cheese" discharge, itching, **normal pH (≤ 4.5)**,
      pseudohyphae on KOH → fluconazole or topical azole. **Trichomoniasis**: frothy yellow-green discharge,
      **strawberry cervix**, motile trichomonads, pH > 4.5 → metronidazole, and **treat partners**.
  - title: Anterior and posterior wall prolapse
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Loss of fascial support of the vaginal walls. See [[pelvic-diaphragm]].
    presentation: Cystocele (anterior wall bulge, urinary symptoms) and rectocele (posterior wall bulge, difficulty emptying the rectum).
  - title: Obstetric and traumatic injuries
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Lacerations at delivery; rarely vesicovaginal or rectovaginal fistula after obstructed labor, surgery or radiation.
    presentation: Continuous leakage of urine (vesicovaginal) or passage of gas/stool (rectovaginal) through the vagina.
  - title: Gartner duct cyst
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Remnant of the mesonephric (Wolffian) duct in the lateral vaginal wall.
    presentation: Usually an asymptomatic cystic swelling of the lateral wall.
pance:
  - "BV: fishy odor, clue cells, pH > 4.5 → **metronidazole**."
  - "Candida: cottage-cheese discharge, **normal pH** → fluconazole."
  - "Trichomonas: frothy green discharge, **strawberry cervix** → metronidazole + treat partner."
  - "Posterior fornix is next to the rectouterine pouch (fluid, blood, abscess)."
  - "Lower vagina drains to **superficial inguinal** nodes."
imaging:
  ultrasound: The transvaginal probe images the uterus and adnexa; the vagina itself is best seen with a small amount of gel instilled.
  mri: Local staging of vaginal and cervical cancers and assessment of fistulas.
  keyViews:
    - "MRI pelvis (T2) with vaginal gel for tumors and fistulas"
exam:
  landmarks: The cervix is felt at the top of the vagina, surrounded by the fornices; the ischial spines are palpable laterally.
  palpation: >-
    Speculum exam (walls, discharge, cervix); bimanual exam through the vagina for the uterus and adnexa; rectovaginal
    exam for the rectouterine pouch and uterosacral ligaments.
  testing: 'Wet mount (saline and KOH), pH and whiff test for discharge; NAAT for gonorrhea, chlamydia and trichomonas.'
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 26-year-old woman has a thin gray vaginal discharge with a fishy odor, worse after intercourse. Vaginal pH is 5.0
      and the wet mount shows epithelial cells covered with bacteria. What is the most appropriate treatment?
    choices:
      - Fluconazole
      - Metronidazole
      - Ceftriaxone
      - Doxycycline
      - Topical estrogen
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Bacterial vaginosis**: thin gray discharge, fishy odor (positive whiff test), pH > 4.5 and **clue cells**.
      Treatment is **metronidazole** (or clindamycin). Fluconazole treats candidiasis.
  - stem: Which part of the vagina has somatic innervation and is sensitive to pain?
    choices:
      - Posterior fornix
      - Upper third
      - Anterior fornix
      - Lower quarter near the orifice
      - The entire vagina equally
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      Only the **lower part** near the orifice receives **somatic** fibers (pudendal nerve). The upper three-quarters is
      supplied by the visceral uterovaginal plexus and is relatively insensitive to pain.
  - stem: Which fornix is most closely related to the rectouterine pouch?
    choices:
      - Anterior fornix
      - Posterior fornix
      - Right lateral fornix
      - Left lateral fornix
      - None of the fornices
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **posterior fornix**, the deepest fornix, is separated from the rectouterine pouch only by the vaginal wall
      and peritoneum — the historical route for culdocentesis.
