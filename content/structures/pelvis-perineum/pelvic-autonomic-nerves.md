aka: [Pelvic splanchnic nerves, Nervi erigentes, Hypogastric plexuses]
summary: Sympathetic hypogastric nerves and parasympathetic pelvic splanchnic nerves (S2–S4) that meet in the inferior hypogastric plexus to control the bladder, rectum and sexual function. They are at risk in prostate and rectal surgery.

anatomy:
  location: >-
    **Superior hypogastric plexus** in front of the aortic bifurcation and sacral promontory (about L5); **hypogastric
    nerves** descending into the pelvis; **inferior hypogastric (pelvic) plexuses** on the lateral walls of the
    [[rectum]], bladder base and [[prostate]] or cervix/vagina.
  roots: >-
    **Sympathetic**: lumbar splanchnic nerves (**T11/T12–L2**) to the superior hypogastric plexus, plus sacral
    splanchnic nerves from the sacral sympathetic trunks. **Parasympathetic**: **pelvic splanchnic nerves** from the
    ventral rami of **S2–S4**.
  origin: Superior hypogastric plexus (sympathetic) and the S2–S4 ventral rami (parasympathetic).
  course: >-
    The superior hypogastric plexus divides into right and left **hypogastric nerves**, which descend to the inferior
    hypogastric plexuses. The pelvic splanchnic nerves join these plexuses directly; some parasympathetic fibers ascend
    through the hypogastric nerves and along the inferior mesenteric artery to supply the **descending and sigmoid
    colon** (the hindgut beyond the vagus nerve's territory).
  branches:
    - "**Middle rectal plexus** to the rectum"
    - "**Vesical plexus** to the bladder"
    - "**Prostatic plexus**, giving the **cavernous nerves** to the erectile tissue of the penis (posterolateral to the prostate)"
    - "**Uterovaginal plexus** to the uterus, cervix, vagina and the erectile tissue of the clitoris and vestibular bulbs"
  motor: >-
    **Parasympathetic**: detrusor contraction (voiding), rectal contraction with relaxation of the internal anal
    sphincter (defecation), **erection** (vasodilation). **Sympathetic**: contraction of the bladder neck/internal
    urethral sphincter (storage and preventing retrograde ejaculation), **emission** (contraction of the ductus
    deferens, seminal glands and prostate), vasoconstriction, and internal anal sphincter tone.
  sensory: >-
    **Pelvic pain line**: pain from viscera **in contact with peritoneum** (above the line — bladder dome, uterine body)
    travels with **sympathetic** fibers to T11–L2; pain from viscera **below** the peritoneum (bladder neck, cervix,
    upper vagina, rectum) travels with **parasympathetic** fibers to S2–S4. Stretch/reflex afferents follow the
    parasympathetic pathway. (The sigmoid colon is an exception, sending pain with the parasympathetic fibers.)
    {{verify: exceptions to the pelvic pain line}}
  relations: The ureters, internal iliac vessels and mesorectal fascia lie close to the inferior hypogastric plexus; the plexus lies within the endopelvic fascia lateral to the rectum.
  notes: '**"Point and Shoot"** — Parasympathetic for erection (point), Sympathetic for emission (shoot), with somatic [[pudendal-nerve]] fibers for ejaculation and the external sphincters.'
clinical:
  - title: Neurogenic bladder
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      **Suprasacral** spinal cord lesions (above S2) → detrusor overactivity and detrusor–sphincter dyssynergia
      (spastic, small-capacity bladder). **Sacral/cauda equina** lesions or peripheral neuropathy (diabetes) → an
      **areflexic, overdistended** bladder with overflow incontinence.
    presentation: Urgency and reflex incontinence (upper motor neuron) vs retention with overflow (lower motor neuron); recurrent UTIs; managed with clean intermittent catheterization and antimuscarinics.
  - title: Autonomic dysreflexia
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Spinal cord injury **at or above T6**: a stimulus below the lesion (most often a **distended bladder** or
      impacted stool) triggers massive, uncontrolled sympathetic discharge.
    presentation: >-
      Sudden severe **hypertension**, pounding headache, flushing and sweating above the lesion, and reflex
      **bradycardia**. Sit the patient up, loosen clothing, **drain the bladder** (check the catheter), and treat
      persistent hypertension with a fast-acting agent.
  - title: Pelvic nerve injury in surgery
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Radical prostatectomy (cavernous nerves), total mesorectal excision for rectal cancer, and radical hysterectomy can damage the inferior hypogastric plexus.
    presentation: Erectile dysfunction, ejaculatory dysfunction, and urinary retention or incontinence; nerve-sparing techniques reduce the risk.
  - title: Superior hypogastric plexus block
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Neurolytic block in front of L5–S1 for intractable pelvic cancer pain.
    presentation: Relieves visceral pelvic pain carried by sympathetic afferents.
pance:
  - "Parasympathetic **S2–S4**: bladder emptying, defecation, erection. Sympathetic **T11–L2**: storage, emission."
  - "SCI at/above **T6** + headache + severe HTN + bradycardia → **autonomic dysreflexia** → sit up, **check the bladder**."
  - "Nerve-sparing prostatectomy preserves the **cavernous nerves**."
  - "Upper motor neuron bladder = spastic/small; lower motor neuron (cauda equina, diabetes) = flaccid, retention + overflow."
imaging:
  mri: The nerves themselves are not usually seen; MRI shows lesions of the conus, cauda equina and pelvis that affect them.
  ultrasound: Post-void residual measurement and urodynamic studies assess bladder function.
exam:
  landmarks: The superior hypogastric plexus lies in front of L5/sacral promontory, just below the aortic bifurcation (about L4).
  testing: >-
    Post-void residual; saddle sensation, anal tone and voluntary contraction, **anal wink** and **bulbocavernosus
    reflex** (S2–S4 somatic arcs that travel with the same segments). Urodynamics for detailed bladder function.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 30-year-old man with a C6 spinal cord injury develops a pounding headache and sweating. BP is 210/110 mm Hg and
      heart rate 48/min. What is the most appropriate first step?
    choices:
      - Lay the patient flat and raise the legs
      - Sit the patient upright and check the bladder catheter for obstruction
      - Give IV atropine
      - Order a CT of the head
      - Give IV fluids
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      This is **autonomic dysreflexia** (injury at or above T6). The first steps are to **sit the patient up** (orthostatic
      lowering of BP) and remove the triggering stimulus, most often a **distended bladder** or blocked catheter.
      Antihypertensives are added if BP remains high.
  - stem: Which fibers are responsible for erection?
    choices:
      - Sympathetic fibers from T11–L2
      - Parasympathetic pelvic splanchnic nerves (S2–S4)
      - Somatic fibers of the pudendal nerve
      - Vagus nerve
      - Lumbar splanchnic nerves only
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Erection is **parasympathetic** (pelvic splanchnic nerves → cavernous nerves → nitric oxide). Emission is
      sympathetic, and ejaculation depends on somatic pudendal fibers to bulbospongiosus ("Point and Shoot").
  - stem: Pain from the uterine body during first-stage labor travels with which fibers?
    choices:
      - Pudendal nerve (S2–S4)
      - Sympathetic fibers to about T10–L1
      - Pelvic splanchnic nerves to S2–S4
      - Obturator nerve
      - Vagus nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The uterine body lies **above the pelvic pain line** (covered by peritoneum), so its pain afferents travel with
      **sympathetic** fibers to about T10–L1 — the target of labor epidurals. Cervical and perineal (second-stage) pain
      travels via S2–S4.
  - stem: >-
      Contraction of the ductus deferens, seminal glands and prostate during emission is mediated mainly by which
      fibers?
    choices:
      - Pelvic splanchnic (parasympathetic) nerves from S2–S4
      - Somatic fibers of the pudendal nerve
      - The vagus nerve
      - Sympathetic fibers from about T11/T12–L2
      - The obturator nerve
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      "Point and Shoot": **parasympathetic** fibers (S2–S4) cause erection, **sympathetic** fibers (lumbar splanchnic
      nerves, T11/T12–L2) cause **emission**, and somatic pudendal fibers (bulbospongiosus) produce ejaculation.
      Sympathetic contraction of the bladder neck also prevents retrograde ejaculation.
  - stem: >-
      A 64-year-old man with long-standing diabetes has a large post-void residual volume and dribbling overflow
      incontinence. Which bladder pattern is most likely?
    choices:
      - Areflexic, overdistended bladder (lower motor neuron pattern)
      - Small-capacity spastic bladder with detrusor–sphincter dyssynergia
      - Stress incontinence from urethral hypermobility
      - Bladder outlet obstruction from posterior urethral valves
      - Autonomic dysreflexia
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      Sacral or cauda equina lesions and **peripheral (diabetic) neuropathy** produce an **areflexic, overdistended**
      bladder with retention and overflow. Suprasacral cord lesions instead cause detrusor overactivity and
      detrusor–sphincter dyssynergia (a small, spastic bladder). Management includes clean intermittent catheterization.
