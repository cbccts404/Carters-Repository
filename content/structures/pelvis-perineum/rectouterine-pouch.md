name: Rectouterine pouch
type: space
region: pelvis-perineum
subregion: Female reproductive organs
taName: Excavatio rectouterina
aka: [Pouch of Douglas, Cul-de-sac]
summary: Deepest recess of the female peritoneal cavity, between the uterus and rectum. Blood, pus and ascites collect here; the male equivalent is the rectovesical pouch.
tags: [female, peritoneum, fast]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: >-
    The lowest part of the [[peritoneal-cavity]] in a woman who is upright or supine, behind the [[uterus]] and the
    posterior fornix of the [[vagina]], in front of the [[rectum]].
  boundaries:
    anterior: Posterior surface of the uterus, supravaginal cervix and posterior vaginal fornix
    posterior: Anterior surface of the rectum (middle third)
    lateral: Rectouterine (uterosacral) folds of peritoneum
    floor: Peritoneum reflected from the posterior vaginal fornix onto the rectum, about 5–6 cm above the anus {{verify}}
  contents:
    - "Normally loops of small intestine or sigmoid colon and a small amount of physiologic peritoneal fluid"
    - "Pathologically: **blood** (ruptured ectopic pregnancy or cyst), **pus** (pelvic abscess, PID), ascites, endometriosis, and peritoneal metastases"
  communications: >-
    Opens upward into the general peritoneal cavity (the paracolic gutters lead down into the pelvis). In males the
    equivalent recess between the bladder and rectum is the **rectovesical pouch**. Anteriorly, the shallow
    vesicouterine pouch lies between the bladder and uterus.
clinical:
  - title: Hemoperitoneum from ruptured ectopic pregnancy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Blood collects in the most dependent part of the peritoneal cavity.
    presentation: Free fluid in the pouch on transvaginal ultrasound or the pelvic view of FAST in a woman with a positive pregnancy test and pain — ruptured ectopic until proven otherwise.
  - title: Pelvic abscess
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Complication of PID/tubo-ovarian abscess, appendicitis, diverticulitis or surgery.
    presentation: Fever, pelvic pain, tenesmus or diarrhea; a tender, fluctuant mass on rectal or vaginal exam. Drained percutaneously, or transrectally/transvaginally.
  - title: Endometriosis of the cul-de-sac
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Ectopic endometrial tissue on the uterosacral ligaments, the pouch and the rectovaginal septum.
    presentation: '**Dysmenorrhea, dyspareunia (deep), dyschezia** and infertility; nodularity and tenderness of the uterosacral ligaments, fixed retroverted uterus on exam.'
  - title: Peritoneal metastases (Blumer shelf)
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Drop metastases from gastric, ovarian or other intra-abdominal cancers settle in the dependent pouch.
    presentation: A hard, shelf-like mass felt anteriorly on rectal exam (in the rectouterine or rectovesical pouch).
pance:
  - "Lowest point of the female peritoneal cavity = **pouch of Douglas**; male = rectovesical pouch."
  - "Pregnant + pain + free fluid in the cul-de-sac → **ruptured ectopic**."
  - "Endometriosis: dysmenorrhea, deep dyspareunia, dyschezia, **uterosacral nodularity**."
  - "**Blumer shelf** = palpable drop metastasis on rectal exam."
imaging:
  ultrasound: >-
    **Transvaginal ultrasound** detects small amounts of free fluid behind the uterus; echogenic (clotted) fluid
    suggests blood. The pelvic (suprapubic) view of the **FAST** exam looks for fluid behind the bladder/uterus.
  ct: Shows fluid density (blood vs simple fluid), abscesses and peritoneal nodules.
  mri: Maps deep infiltrating endometriosis of the cul-de-sac and rectovaginal septum.
  keyViews:
    - "FAST pelvic (suprapubic) views, longitudinal and transverse"
    - "Transvaginal ultrasound (sagittal)"
exam:
  landmarks: Lies behind the posterior vaginal fornix and in front of the rectum.
  palpation: '**Rectovaginal exam** (index finger in the vagina, middle finger in the rectum) palpates the pouch, the uterosacral ligaments and the rectovaginal septum for nodules, masses and tenderness.'

      - Adenomyosis
      - Endometriosis
      - Leiomyoma
      - Pelvic inflammatory disease

    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Endometriosis** deposits commonly involve the cul-de-sac and **uterosacral ligaments**, causing dysmenorrhea,
      deep dyspareunia and dyschezia. Adenomyosis causes a diffusely enlarged, boggy uterus.
