anatomy:
  location: In the urogenital triangle of the [[perineum]], below the pubic symphysis.
  parts:
    - "**Mons pubis**: fat pad over the pubic symphysis"
    - "**Labia majora**: hair-bearing folds (homologous to the scrotum); the round ligaments of the uterus end in them"
    - "**Labia minora**: hairless folds enclosing the vestibule; anteriorly they form the prepuce and frenulum of the clitoris"
    - "**Vestibule**: the space between the labia minora containing the **external urethral orifice**, the **vaginal orifice**, and the openings of the greater and lesser vestibular glands and paraurethral (Skene) glands"
    - "**Clitoris**: glans, body and two **crura** (attached to the ischiopubic rami, covered by ischiocavernosus); erectile tissue homologous to the corpora cavernosa of the [[penis]]"
    - "**Bulbs of the vestibule**: erectile tissue on either side of the vaginal orifice, covered by bulbospongiosus (homologous to the bulb of the penis)"
    - "**Greater vestibular (Bartholin) glands**: pea-sized glands behind the bulbs; ducts open into the vestibule at about **4 and 8 o'clock**, posterolateral to the vaginal orifice"
  bloodSupply: Internal pudendal artery (posterior labial branches, arteries of the bulb and clitoris) and external pudendal arteries (anterior labial branches).
  venousDrainage: Internal and external pudendal veins.
  lymphatics: '**Superficial inguinal nodes** (the glans of the clitoris may also drain to deep inguinal or internal iliac nodes). See [[pelvic-lymph-nodes]].'
  innervation: >-
    Anterior: ilioinguinal nerve and the genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve (L1, from the [[lumbar-plexus]]).
    Posterior: the [[pudendal-nerve]] (posterior labial nerves, dorsal nerve of the clitoris) and perineal branches of
    the posterior femoral cutaneous nerve. Cavernous nerves (parasympathetic) for erectile tissue.
  relations: >-
    Structures of the superficial perineal pouch (clitoris, bulbs, Bartholin glands, ischiocavernosus,
    bulbospongiosus) lie beneath the vulvar skin (see [[perineal-pouches]]). The [[urethra]] and [[vagina]] open into
    the vestibule.
clinical:
  - title: Bartholin duct cyst and abscess
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Obstruction of the greater vestibular gland duct; abscesses are polymicrobial (occasionally gonorrhea or chlamydia).
    presentation: >-
      Unilateral swelling of the posterior labium at 4 or 8 o'clock; an abscess is exquisitely painful, tender and
      fluctuant. Symptomatic abscesses need **incision and drainage with a Word catheter** or marsupialization.
      A new Bartholin mass in a woman over about 40 is biopsied to exclude carcinoma. {{verify: age threshold for biopsy}}
  - title: Lichen sclerosus
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Chronic inflammatory skin disease of the anogenital area, most common in postmenopausal women.
    presentation: >-
      Intense itching, thin white "cigarette-paper" skin, figure-of-eight pattern around the vulva and anus, loss of
      labial architecture. Treat with a **high-potency topical steroid** (clobetasol); long-term follow-up because of an
      increased risk of **squamous cell carcinoma**.
  - title: Vulvar cancer
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Usually squamous cell carcinoma — HPV-related (younger) or associated with lichen sclerosus (older).
    presentation: A pruritic lump or ulcer, often in an older woman; spreads to the **inguinal nodes**. Biopsy any persistent vulvar lesion.
pance:
  - "Painful fluctuant mass at 4 or 8 o'clock of the vestibule → **Bartholin abscess** → I&D with Word catheter."
  - "White, thin, itchy vulvar skin in a postmenopausal woman → **lichen sclerosus** → clobetasol; SCC risk."
  - "Vulvar lymph → **superficial inguinal** nodes."
imaging:
  ultrasound: Rarely needed; can distinguish a cyst from a solid mass.
  mri: Staging of vulvar cancer; CT/PET for inguinal and pelvic nodes.
exam:
  landmarks: The vestibule lies between the labia minora; the Bartholin gland openings are posterolateral to the vaginal orifice at 4 and 8 o'clock.
  palpation: Palpate the Bartholin gland areas between the thumb (outside) and index finger (inside the introitus) for swelling or tenderness; palpate the inguinal nodes.
  testing: Inspect the skin of the whole vulva and perianal area in good light; biopsy persistent or atypical lesions.

    choices:
      - Oral antibiotics only
      - Incision and drainage with Word catheter placement
      - Excisional biopsy
      - Needle aspiration only
      - Topical clobetasol
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A painful fluctuant mass at 4 or 8 o'clock is a **Bartholin gland abscess**. **Incision and drainage with a Word
      catheter** (or marsupialization) lets a tract epithelialize and reduces recurrence; simple aspiration often
      recurs.
  - stem: Which structure is homologous to the scrotum?
    choices:
      - Labia minora
      - Labia majora
      - Clitoris
      - Bulbs of the vestibule
      - Vestibule
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **labia majora** develop from the labioscrotal swellings, as does the scrotum. The labia minora correspond to
      the ventral penile skin (urethral folds) and the clitoris to the penis.
