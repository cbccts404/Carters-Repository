---
name: Pudendal nerve
type: nerve
region: pelvis-perineum
subregion: Vessels, nerves & lymphatics
taName: Nervus pudendus
summary: Main somatic nerve of the perineum (S2–S4), supplying the external anal and urethral sphincters, the perineal muscles and perineal skin. It is blocked at the ischial spine in labor and can be stretched in childbirth or entrapped in cyclists.
tags: [perineum, continence, sacral-plexus]
systems: [neurologic]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Leaves the pelvis, curves around the ischial spine, and runs forward in the lateral wall of the [[ischioanal-fossa]] to the [[perineum]].
  roots: Ventral rami of **S2, S3 and S4** (from the [[sacral-plexus]]).
  origin: Sacral plexus, on the posterior pelvic wall.
  course: >-
    Leaves the pelvis through the **greater sciatic foramen below piriformis**, crosses the back of the
    [[sacrotuberous-and-sacrospinous-ligaments|sacrospinous ligament]] just **medial to the ischial spine** (medial to the
    internal pudendal vessels), and re-enters the perineum through the **lesser sciatic foramen**. It then runs in the
    **pudendal (Alcock) canal**, a split in the obturator internus fascia, with the internal pudendal vessels.
  branches:
    - "**Inferior rectal (anal) nerve**: crosses the ischioanal fossa to the **external anal sphincter**, the lower [[anal-canal]] (below the pectinate line) and perianal skin"
    - "**Perineal nerve**: superficial branches (posterior scrotal/labial nerves) to the skin; deep branches to **bulbospongiosus, ischiocavernosus, the transverse perineal muscles and the external urethral sphincter**"
    - "**Dorsal nerve of the penis or clitoris**: sensory to the glans and most of the shaft"
  motor: External anal sphincter, external urethral sphincter, perineal muscles, and part of levator ani.
  sensory: Most of the perineal skin, the posterior scrotum or labia, the penis or clitoris, the lower quarter of the [[vagina]], and the anal canal below the pectinate line.
  relations: >-
    The internal pudendal vessels run with it throughout. The anterior perineum (mons, anterior scrotum/labia) is
    supplied instead by the ilioinguinal and genitofemoral nerves, and the lateral perineum by the posterior femoral
    cutaneous nerve.
  notes: '**"S2, 3, 4 keep the poop off the floor"** (and the pee): pudendal and pelvic splanchnic nerves serve continence.'
clinical:
  - title: Pudendal nerve block
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Local anesthetic injected transvaginally through the sacrospinous ligament just **medial and posterior to the
      ischial spine**, where the nerve crosses. Aspirate first to avoid the internal pudendal vessels.
    presentation: >-
      Anesthetizes most of the perineum for the second stage of labor, forceps delivery or perineal repair. The
      anterior perineum (ilioinguinal/genitofemoral) and lateral perineum (posterior femoral cutaneous) still need
      local infiltration.
  - title: Obstetric pudendal neuropathy
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Stretch injury during prolonged second stage or instrumental delivery.
    presentation: Fecal and/or urinary incontinence with reduced perineal sensation; usually improves over months.
  - title: Pudendal neuralgia (entrapment)
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Compression between the sacrotuberous and sacrospinous ligaments or in the pudendal canal; associated with prolonged cycling and pelvic surgery.
    presentation: >-
      Burning pain in the perineum, genitals or rectum that is **worse sitting** and relieved by standing, lying down or
      sitting on a toilet seat; no objective sensory loss (Nantes criteria). A diagnostic pudendal block helps confirm
      it.
pance:

  - "Pudendal block: needle just **medial to the ischial spine** through the sacrospinous ligament."

imaging:
  mri: MR neurography can show the nerve and sites of compression; MRI excludes masses along its course.
  ct: CT (or ultrasound/fluoroscopy) guides pudendal nerve blocks at the ischial spine or in the pudendal canal.
exam:
  landmarks: The ischial spine, palpable transvaginally or transrectally at about 4 and 8 o'clock, marks where the nerve crosses the sacrospinous ligament.
  palpation: Tenderness over the ischial spine on internal exam supports pudendal entrapment.
  testing: >-
    Perianal and perineal pinprick and light touch (S3–S5 "saddle"), **anal wink** (anocutaneous reflex),
    **bulbocavernosus reflex** and DRE for resting tone and **voluntary anal contraction** (a key part of the ASIA
    exam in spinal cord injury).
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      During a transvaginal pudendal block for a forceps delivery, local anesthetic is injected just medial to the
      ischial spine. After the block the patient still feels pain at the anterior labia. Which nerve supplies that area?
    choices:
      - Pudendal nerve
      - Ilioinguinal nerve
      - Obturator nerve
      - Inferior rectal nerve
      - Sciatic nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The anterior labia (and mons) are supplied by the **ilioinguinal nerve** and the genital branch of the
      genitofemoral nerve (L1), which a pudendal block does not reach, so local infiltration is added.
  - stem: Which muscle is supplied by the inferior rectal branch of the pudendal nerve?
    choices:
      - Internal anal sphincter
      - External anal sphincter
      - Bulbospongiosus
      - Detrusor
      - Piriformis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **inferior rectal nerve** supplies the voluntary **external anal sphincter** and perianal skin. The internal
      sphincter is smooth muscle under autonomic control; bulbospongiosus is supplied by the perineal nerve.
  - stem: The pudendal nerve re-enters the perineum through which opening?
    choices:
      - Obturator canal
      - Greater sciatic foramen
      - Lesser sciatic foramen
      - Deep inguinal ring
      - Urogenital hiatus
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The pudendal nerve leaves the pelvis through the **greater** sciatic foramen below piriformis, curves around the
      ischial spine, and enters the perineum through the **lesser** sciatic foramen into the pudendal canal.
  - stem: >-
      A 45-year-old competitive cyclist has burning perineal pain that is worse when sitting and relieved by standing or
      sitting on a toilet seat. Sensation is normal on exam. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Cauda equina syndrome
      - Anal fissure
      - Sacroiliac joint dysfunction
      - L5 radiculopathy
      - Pudendal neuralgia
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      **Pudendal neuralgia** (entrapment between the sacrotuberous and sacrospinous ligaments or in the pudendal canal)
      causes burning perineal, genital or rectal pain that is **worse sitting**, with no objective sensory loss. It is
      associated with prolonged cycling. A diagnostic pudendal block helps confirm it.
  - stem: What forms the pudendal (Alcock) canal?
    choices:
      - A split in the sacrotuberous ligament
      - A split in the obturator internus fascia
      - The perineal membrane
      - Fibers of levator ani
      - Colles fascia
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **pudendal canal** is a split in the **obturator internus fascia** on the lateral wall of the ischioanal
      fossa. The pudendal nerve runs in it with the internal pudendal vessels after re-entering through the lesser
      sciatic foramen.
