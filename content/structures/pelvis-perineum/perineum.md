anatomy:
  location: Between the thighs, below the [[pelvic-diaphragm]] and within the pelvic outlet.
  boundaries:
    anterior: Inferior margin of the [[pubic-symphysis]]
    anterolateral: Ischiopubic rami
    lateral: Ischial tuberosities
    posterolateral: '[[sacrotuberous-and-sacrospinous-ligaments|Sacrotuberous ligaments]]'
    posterior: Tip of the coccyx
    roof: Pelvic diaphragm (levator ani and coccygeus)
    floor: Skin and superficial fascia
  contents:
    - "**Urogenital triangle** (anterior, in front of a line between the ischial tuberosities; tilted forward): the perineal membrane, [[perineal-pouches]], external genitalia ([[penis]] and scrotum or [[vulva]]), and the [[urethra]] (and [[vagina]])"
    - "**Anal triangle** (posterior; tilted backward): the [[anal-canal]] with the external anal sphincter, the two [[ischioanal-fossa|ischioanal fossae]] and the anococcygeal body"
    - "**Perineal body** (central tendon of the perineum): a fibromuscular mass at the midpoint between the anus and the bulb of the penis or the vaginal vestibule, where bulbospongiosus, the external anal sphincter, the superficial and deep transverse perineal muscles and fibers of levator ani converge"
    - "Nerves and vessels: branches of the [[pudendal-nerve]] and internal pudendal vessels"
  communications: >-
    The superficial perineal pouch opens anteriorly into the space deep to the membranous layer of the anterior
    abdominal wall (Scarpa fascia). The ischioanal fossae extend forward above the perineal membrane as anterior
    recesses and communicate with each other behind the anal canal.
clinical:
  - title: Obstetric perineal lacerations
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Stretching and tearing of the perineum during vaginal delivery; risk increases with operative delivery, a large baby, first delivery and midline episiotomy.
    presentation: >-
      **1st degree**: perineal skin or vaginal epithelium only. **2nd degree**: perineal muscles (**perineal body**) but
      not the anal sphincter. **3rd degree**: the **anal sphincter complex** (3a < 50% of the external sphincter; 3b > 50%;
      3c internal sphincter also torn). **4th degree**: through the sphincters into the **anorectal mucosa**. Missed
      sphincter injuries cause later fecal incontinence.
    notes: A **mediolateral episiotomy** is less likely than a midline one to extend into the sphincter; routine episiotomy is not recommended.
  - title: Saddle anesthesia (cauda equina syndrome)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Compression of the S2–S5 roots in the [[cauda-equina]] (central disc herniation, tumor, epidural abscess or hematoma).
    presentation: Numbness of the perineum, buttocks and inner thighs, urinary retention with overflow, fecal incontinence, reduced anal tone, and sciatica. **Emergency MRI** and surgical decompression.
  - title: Fournier gangrene
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Polymicrobial **necrotizing fasciitis** of the perineum and genitalia, spreading along fascial planes (Colles,
      dartos, Scarpa). Risk factors — **diabetes**, obesity, alcohol use, immunosuppression, perianal or urinary
      infection, and SGLT2 inhibitors.
    presentation: >-
      Severe perineal/genital pain **out of proportion to the exam**, swelling, erythema then dusky skin, **crepitus**,
      fever and sepsis. Treat with emergency **surgical debridement** plus broad-spectrum IV antibiotics.

imaging:
  ultrasound: '**Endoanal ultrasound** assesses the anal sphincters after obstetric injury.'
  ct: Shows soft-tissue **gas** along fascial planes in Fournier gangrene (but don't delay surgery for imaging) and perineal abscesses.
  mri: Shows perineal masses, fistulas and sphincter anatomy; lumbosacral MRI for suspected cauda equina syndrome.
  keyViews:
    - "Endoanal ultrasound"
    - "CT pelvis/perineum (soft-tissue gas)"
exam:
  landmarks: Pubic symphysis, ischial tuberosities (palpable deep in the buttock creases) and the coccyx tip outline the diamond; the perineal body lies between the anus and the vaginal introitus or bulb of the penis.
  palpation: Inspect with the patient in the lithotomy or left lateral position; assess the perineal body's thickness postpartum and the anal sphincter by DRE.
  testing: >-
    **Saddle sensation** (S3–S5: pinprick and light touch around the anus and perineum), **anal wink** and voluntary
    anal squeeze — essential in any patient with back pain and red flags.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      After a forceps delivery, examination shows a tear through the perineal muscles and about two-thirds of the
      thickness of the external anal sphincter; the internal sphincter and rectal mucosa are intact. How is this tear
      classified?
    choices:
      - Second degree
      - Third degree (3a)
      - Third degree (3b)
      - Third degree (3c)
      - Fourth degree
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      A tear involving **more than 50% of the external anal sphincter** without the internal sphincter is a **3b**
      laceration. 3a involves under 50%, 3c includes the internal sphincter, and 4th degree extends through the
      anorectal mucosa.
  - stem: >-
      A 45-year-old man with low back pain for a week now has numbness around the anus and inner thighs and has not
      urinated for 12 hours. What is the most appropriate next step?
    choices:
      - NSAIDs and physical therapy
      - Plain radiographs of the lumbar spine
      - Emergency MRI of the lumbosacral spine
      - Outpatient neurology referral
      - Epidural steroid injection
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Saddle anesthesia** with **urinary retention** is **cauda equina syndrome** — a surgical emergency needing
      **emergency MRI** and decompression.
  - stem: Which structure is the attachment point where bulbospongiosus, the external anal sphincter and the transverse perineal muscles converge?
    choices:
      - Anococcygeal body
      - Perineal body
      - Ischial tuberosity
      - Perineal membrane
      - Sacrotuberous ligament
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **perineal body** (central tendon of the perineum) is the meeting point of these muscles and supports the
      pelvic floor. It is torn in second-degree and higher perineal lacerations.
  - stem: >-
      A 58-year-old man with poorly controlled diabetes has severe perineal and scrotal pain out of proportion to the
      exam, dusky skin with crepitus, and a fever of 39.2 °C. What is the most appropriate management?
    choices:
      - Oral antibiotics and review in 48 hours
      - Scrotal ultrasound and observation
      - Emergency surgical debridement plus broad-spectrum IV antibiotics
      - Incision and drainage in the clinic
      - Warm sitz baths and analgesia
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      This is **Fournier gangrene**, a polymicrobial necrotizing fasciitis spreading along Colles, dartos and Scarpa
      fascia. Diabetes is a major risk factor. Treatment is emergency **surgical debridement** plus broad-spectrum IV
      antibiotics.
  - stem: A line between which landmarks divides the perineum into the urogenital and anal triangles?
    choices:
      - The anterior superior iliac spines
      - The ischial tuberosities
      - The ischial spines
      - The pubic tubercles
      - The posterior superior iliac spines
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A line between the **ischial tuberosities** divides the diamond-shaped perineum into the anterior **urogenital
      triangle** (external genitalia, urethra, perineal pouches) and the posterior **anal triangle** (anal canal,
      ischioanal fossae).
