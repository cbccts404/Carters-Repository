summary: Paired male gonads in the scrotum with the epididymis along their back. Torsion is a surgical emergency, and aortic blood supply plus para-aortic lymph drainage reflect the testis's descent from the abdomen.
tags: [male, genitourinary, emergency, oncology]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: In the scrotum, suspended by the [[spermatic-cord]]. Each testis is about 4–5 cm long; the left usually hangs slightly lower.
  parts:
    - "**Tunica vaginalis**: closed peritoneal sac (from the processus vaginalis) with parietal and visceral layers covering the front and sides of the testis"
    - "**Tunica albuginea**: tough fibrous capsule; septa divide the testis into lobules of **seminiferous tubules** (spermatogenesis; Sertoli cells) with **Leydig cells** (testosterone) between them"
    - "Seminiferous tubules → straight tubules → **rete testis** → efferent ductules → **epididymis**"
    - "**Epididymis**: head (upper pole), body and tail along the **posterolateral** testis; stores and matures sperm; the tail continues as the [[ductus-deferens]]"
    - "Appendix testis and appendix epididymis: small remnants (paramesonephric and mesonephric duct) at the upper pole that can twist"
  bloodSupply: >-
    **Testicular artery** from the [[abdominal-aorta]] (about L2); collateral flow from the artery to the ductus
    deferens (from a vesical artery) and the cremasteric artery (from the [[inferior-epigastric-artery]]).
  venousDrainage: >-
    **Pampiniform plexus** (which also cools arterial blood) → testicular vein. **Right** → [[inferior-vena-cava]]
    (oblique entry). **Left** → **left renal vein** (right-angle entry, longer course) — one reason varicoceles are
    usually left-sided.
  lymphatics: '**Lumbar (para-aortic) nodes** at about L1–L2 — whereas the **scrotal skin** drains to the **superficial inguinal** nodes. See [[pelvic-lymph-nodes]].'
  innervation: Testicular plexus (sympathetic from about T10) with the testicular artery; testicular pain is referred to the periumbilical region. Scrotal skin is supplied by the ilioinguinal and genitofemoral (anterior) and the pudendal and posterior femoral cutaneous nerves (posterior).
  relations: >-
    The testes develop on the posterior abdominal wall and descend through the [[inguinal-canal]], guided by the
    gubernaculum, into the scrotum by birth. The layers of the spermatic cord and scrotum derive from the abdominal wall
    layers.
clinical:
  - title: Testicular torsion
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Twisting of the spermatic cord, usually with a **bell-clapper deformity** (tunica vaginalis attaching too high,
      leaving the testis free to rotate). Peaks in adolescence and the neonatal period.
    presentation: >-
      **Sudden severe unilateral scrotal pain**, often waking the patient, with nausea and vomiting; a **high-riding,
      horizontally lying** testis; **absent cremasteric reflex**; elevating the testis does not relieve pain.
    notes: >-
      A **surgical emergency** — testicular salvage falls sharply after about **6 hours**. When suspicion is high, go
      straight to **surgical exploration** (detorsion and **bilateral orchiopexy**); don't delay for ultrasound. Manual
      detorsion ("open the book", rotating outward) is a temporary measure.
  - title: Epididymitis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Ascending infection: **Chlamydia/gonorrhea** in sexually active men under about 35; **enteric organisms (E. coli)**
      in older men or with urinary tract instrumentation or insertive anal intercourse.
    presentation: >-
      **Gradual** onset of posterior scrotal pain and tenderness of the epididymis, fever, dysuria; cremasteric reflex
      present; pain may ease on elevation (**Prehn sign**).
    notes: 'STI-related: ceftriaxone + doxycycline; enteric: levofloxacin (plus ceftriaxone if both are possible). {{verify: current CDC regimens}}'
  - title: Testicular cancer
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Germ cell tumors (seminoma most common) in men aged about **15–35**; risk factors — **cryptorchidism**, prior testicular cancer, family history.
    presentation: >-
      **Painless, firm testicular mass** that does not transilluminate; sometimes gynecomastia (β-hCG). Scrotal
      ultrasound, then tumor markers (**AFP, β-hCG, LDH**). Treatment begins with **radical inguinal orchiectomy** — a
      trans-scrotal approach is avoided so the lymphatic drainage is not altered.
  - title: Hydrocele, varicocele and spermatocele
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Hydrocele**: fluid in the tunica vaginalis (patent processus vaginalis in infants). **Varicocele**: dilated pampiniform plexus. **Spermatocele**: cyst of the epididymal head.'
    presentation: >-
      Hydrocele: painless swelling that **transilluminates**. Varicocele: **"bag of worms"**, usually **left**, larger
      when standing or with Valsalva, may cause infertility. A **right-sided** or non-collapsing (supine) varicocele
      suggests IVC or renal vein obstruction (e.g. renal cell carcinoma) — image the abdomen.
  - title: Cryptorchidism
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Failure of descent, most often in the inguinal canal; more common in preterm infants.
    presentation: "Empty scrotum; increased risk of infertility and testicular cancer. Refer by about 6 months; **orchiopexy** is typically done by 18 months. {{verify: timing per current AUA guideline}}"
pance:
  - "Sudden severe testicular pain, high-riding horizontal testis, **absent cremasteric reflex** → **torsion** → OR within 6 h."
  - "Gradual pain, fever, dysuria, positive Prehn sign, reflex present → **epididymitis**."
  - "Painless firm testicular mass, young man → **testicular cancer** → ultrasound; **inguinal** orchiectomy."
  - "Testis lymph → **para-aortic**; scrotum → **superficial inguinal**."
  - "Left varicocele common (left renal vein); isolated right varicocele → look for a mass."
  - "Hydrocele transilluminates; a tumor does not."
imaging:
  ultrasound: >-
    **Scrotal ultrasound with color Doppler** is first-line for pain and masses: absent or reduced flow and a twisted
    cord ("whirlpool sign") in torsion; increased flow in epididymitis/orchitis; a hypoechoic intratesticular mass in
    cancer; anechoic fluid around the testis in hydrocele.
  ct: CT abdomen/pelvis stages testicular cancer (retroperitoneal nodes), and evaluates the cause of a right-sided varicocele.
  keyViews:
    - "Scrotal ultrasound with Doppler, comparing both sides"
exam:
  landmarks: The epididymis is felt as a soft ridge along the **posterolateral** testis; the spermatic cord (with the firm ductus deferens) can be followed up to the superficial inguinal ring.
  palpation: >-
    Examine standing and supine. Palpate each testis between the thumb and first two fingers for size, consistency and
    masses; then the epididymis and cord. Transilluminate any swelling. Look for a varicocele standing, with Valsalva.
  testing: >-
    **Cremasteric reflex** (L1–L2, genitofemoral nerve): stroking the upper inner thigh elevates the ipsilateral testis.
    Its absence strongly suggests torsion.
  specialTests:
    - name: Prehn sign
      technique: Gently elevate the affected testis while the patient is supine.
      positive: Pain is relieved (suggests epididymitis); no relief or worse pain suggests torsion.
      significance: Unreliable on its own; never use it to rule out torsion.
    - name: Blue dot sign
      technique: Inspect the upper pole of the scrotum in a child with acute scrotal pain.
      positive: A small blue-black spot visible through the skin with point tenderness at the upper pole.
      significance: Torsion of the appendix testis, which is managed conservatively.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 14-year-old boy wakes at 3 a.m. with sudden severe left scrotal pain and vomiting. The left testis is high-riding
      with a horizontal lie, and the left cremasteric reflex is absent. What is the most appropriate next step?
    choices:
      - Scrotal ultrasound in the morning
      - Urinalysis and antibiotics
      - Immediate surgical exploration
      - Ice and scrotal elevation
      - Tumor markers
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      This is classic **testicular torsion**. With high clinical suspicion, proceed to **immediate surgical exploration**
      (detorsion and bilateral orchiopexy); salvage rates fall steeply after about 6 hours. Imaging should not delay
      surgery.
  - stem: A 25-year-old man has a painless, firm right testicular mass. Ultrasound shows a solid intratesticular lesion. Which approach is used for orchiectomy?
    choices:
      - Trans-scrotal
      - Radical inguinal
      - Percutaneous biopsy first
      - Laparoscopic transabdominal
      - Perineal
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Radical inguinal orchiectomy** (with high ligation of the cord) is standard. A scrotal incision could spread
      tumor to the scrotal skin, which drains to the inguinal nodes instead of the para-aortic nodes.
  - stem: Why is a varicocele more common on the left?
    choices:
      - The left testicular artery is larger
      - The left testicular vein drains at a right angle into the left renal vein
      - The left testis is smaller
      - The left pampiniform plexus is absent
      - The left testicular vein drains into the IVC
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **left testicular vein** drains at a **right angle** into the **left renal vein** (a longer course with higher
      pressure, which can be compressed between the aorta and SMA — "nutcracker"). The right vein enters the IVC
      obliquely.
