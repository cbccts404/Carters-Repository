anatomy:
  location: >-
    The **root** is fixed in the superficial perineal pouch of the urogenital triangle; the **body** hangs free,
    suspended from the pubic symphysis by the suspensory and fundiform ligaments.
  parts:
    - "**Root**: the **bulb** (attached to the perineal membrane, covered by bulbospongiosus; contains the spongy urethra) and two **crura** (attached to the ischiopubic rami, covered by ischiocavernosus)"
    - "**Body**: two dorsal **corpora cavernosa** and one ventral **corpus spongiosum** (containing the spongy [[urethra]]), each surrounded by **tunica albuginea**"
    - "**Glans**: the expanded end of the corpus spongiosum; corona, external urethral orifice, and the **prepuce** (foreskin)"
    - "**Coverings (deep to superficial)**: tunica albuginea, **deep (Buck) fascia** enclosing all three bodies and the deep dorsal vessels and nerves, superficial (dartos) fascia, skin"
  bloodSupply: >-
    Branches of the **internal pudendal artery** (from the [[internal-iliac-artery]]): **deep arteries (cavernosal)**
    in the corpora cavernosa, whose helicine branches fill the erectile spaces; **dorsal arteries**; and the **artery
    of the bulb**. External pudendal arteries supply the skin.
  venousDrainage: Deep dorsal vein (beneath Buck fascia) → prostatic venous plexus; superficial dorsal vein → external pudendal → great saphenous vein.
  lymphatics: Skin → **superficial inguinal** nodes; glans and corpora → deep inguinal and internal iliac nodes. See [[pelvic-lymph-nodes]].
  innervation: >-
    **Dorsal nerve of the penis** (from the [[pudendal-nerve]], S2–S4): sensation of the skin and glans. **Cavernous
    nerves** (parasympathetic, pelvic splanchnic S2–S4): vasodilation (nitric oxide) → **erection**. Sympathetic:
    emission and detumescence. Perineal branches of the pudendal nerve supply bulbospongiosus and ischiocavernosus
    (ejaculation). See [[pelvic-autonomic-nerves]].
  relations: The deep dorsal vein lies in the midline with a dorsal artery and dorsal nerve on each side, all beneath Buck fascia (vein-artery-nerve from medial to lateral).
  notes: >-
    Mnemonic for sexual function: **"Point and Shoot"** — parasympathetic for erection, sympathetic for emission, and
    somatic pudendal (bulbospongiosus) for ejaculation.
clinical:
  - title: Erectile dysfunction
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Most often **vascular** (atherosclerosis — it can precede coronary disease by years); also neurogenic (diabetes,
      prostatectomy, spinal cord injury), hormonal (low testosterone), drugs (antihypertensives, SSRIs) and
      psychogenic (morning erections preserved).
    presentation: >-
      Persistent inability to achieve or maintain an erection. First-line treatment is a **PDE-5 inhibitor**
      (sildenafil, tadalafil), which is **contraindicated with nitrates** (severe hypotension). Screen for
      cardiovascular risk factors.
  - title: Priapism
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      **Ischemic (low-flow)** — the common, painful emergency: sickle cell disease, trazodone, antipsychotics,
      intracavernosal injections. **Non-ischemic (high-flow)** — painless, after perineal trauma (arteriocavernous
      fistula).
    presentation: >-
      Erection lasting over **4 hours** unrelated to sexual stimulation. Ischemic: rigid, painful, dark hypoxic
      corporal blood. Treat with **corporal aspiration and intracavernosal phenylephrine**; delay risks fibrosis and
      permanent ED.
  - title: Penile fracture
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Rupture of the **tunica albuginea** of a corpus cavernosum when the erect penis bends forcibly (usually during intercourse).
    presentation: >-
      A "pop", rapid detumescence, swelling and bruising (**"eggplant deformity"**). With Buck fascia intact, the
      hematoma stays on the shaft; if it is torn, bruising spreads to the perineum ("butterfly"). Treat with prompt
      surgical repair; up to about 1 in 5 have a urethral injury (blood at the meatus). {{verify}}
  - title: Phimosis and paraphimosis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Phimosis**: the foreskin cannot be retracted (normal in young boys). **Paraphimosis**: a retracted foreskin that cannot be returned over the glans, constricting it.'
    presentation: Paraphimosis is a **urologic emergency** — a swollen glans with a tight band; reduce manually (after compression and analgesia), and incise if that fails.
  - title: Peyronie disease
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Fibrous plaque in the tunica albuginea.
    presentation: Painful erection and penile curvature toward the plaque; a palpable plaque on the shaft.
pance:
  - "ED in a man with risk factors → think **cardiovascular disease**; PDE-5 inhibitors are contraindicated with **nitrates**."
  - "Painful erection > 4 h in sickle cell disease or on trazodone → **ischemic priapism** → aspiration + phenylephrine."
  - "Retracted foreskin that won't reduce → **paraphimosis** (emergency)."
  - "Erection = parasympathetic (NO, cGMP); emission = sympathetic; ejaculation = somatic (Point and Shoot)."
imaging:
  ultrasound: >-
    **Penile duplex Doppler ultrasound** after intracavernosal vasodilator injection assesses arterial inflow and venous
    leak in ED; it also shows tunica albuginea tears and Peyronie plaques. Cavernosal blood gas distinguishes ischemic
    from non-ischemic priapism.
  mri: Shows the site of tunica albuginea rupture when ultrasound is inconclusive.
  keyViews:
    - "Penile duplex Doppler ultrasound"
exam:
  landmarks: The corpus spongiosum and urethra lie on the ventral (underside) of the shaft; the corpora cavernosa form the dorsal bulk.
  palpation: Retract the foreskin (then replace it) to inspect the glans and meatus; palpate the shaft for plaques (Peyronie) and tenderness.
  testing: >-
    **Bulbocavernosus reflex** (S2–S4): squeezing the glans (or tugging an indwelling catheter) causes contraction of
    the anal sphincter felt on DRE. Its return marks the end of spinal shock; its absence is seen in cauda equina and
    conus lesions.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 58-year-old man with angina taking isosorbide mononitrate asks for sildenafil for erectile dysfunction. What is
      the main concern?
    choices:
      - Hepatotoxicity
      - Severe hypotension
      - Priapism in all patients
      - Hyperkalemia
      - Visual loss in all patients
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      PDE-5 inhibitors increase cGMP, as nitrates do; combined they can cause **profound hypotension**, so they are
      contraindicated together.
  - stem: A 19-year-old man with sickle cell disease has a painful erection that has lasted 6 hours. What is the most appropriate initial treatment?
    choices:
      - Observation and ice packs
      - Oral pseudoephedrine only
      - Corporal aspiration and intracavernosal phenylephrine
      - Immediate penile prosthesis
      - Oral sildenafil
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      This is **ischemic priapism**, a urologic emergency. **Aspiration of corporal blood and intracavernosal
      phenylephrine** (an α1-agonist) are first-line; prolonged ischemia causes fibrosis and permanent erectile
      dysfunction.
  - stem: Which nerve provides sensation to the glans penis?
    choices:
      - Ilioinguinal nerve
      - Genitofemoral nerve
      - Dorsal nerve of the penis
      - Cavernous nerve
      - Obturator nerve
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **dorsal nerve of the penis**, a branch of the pudendal nerve, provides sensation to the glans and most of the
      skin of the shaft — the target of a dorsal penile nerve block. The cavernous nerves are autonomic.
