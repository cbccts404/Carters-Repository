---
name: Sacral plexus
type: nerve
region: pelvis-perineum
subregion: Vessels, nerves & lymphatics
taName: Plexus sacralis
summary: Nerve network on the posterior pelvic wall (L4–S4) that gives the sciatic, gluteal and pudendal nerves. It supplies the buttock, posterior thigh, nearly all of the leg and foot, the pelvic floor and the perineum.
tags: [pelvis, lower-limb, sacral-plexus]
systems: [neurologic]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: >-
    On the anterior surface of **piriformis** on the posterior pelvic wall, behind the internal iliac vessels and the
    [[ureter]].
  roots: >-
    **Lumbosacral trunk** (part of **L4** plus **L5**, joining from the [[lumbar-plexus]] over the pelvic brim) and the
    ventral rami of **S1–S4**.
  origin: Ventral rami of L4–S4.
  course: >-
    The rami converge toward the greater sciatic foramen; most branches leave the pelvis through it, above or below
    piriformis.
  branches:
    - "[[sciatic-nerve]] (L4–S3): the largest nerve in the body; leaves **below piriformis** (usually), supplies the posterior thigh and, through the [[tibial-nerve|tibial]] and [[common-fibular-nerve|common fibular]] nerves, all muscles below the knee"
    - "**Superior gluteal nerve** (L4–S1): leaves **above piriformis**; supplies gluteus medius, gluteus minimus and tensor fasciae latae"
    - "**Inferior gluteal nerve** (L5–S2): below piriformis; supplies gluteus maximus"
    - "**Posterior femoral cutaneous nerve** (S1–S3): skin of the posterior thigh and part of the perineum"
    - "[[pudendal-nerve]] (S2–S4): perineum"
    - "**Nerve to obturator internus** (and superior gemellus) (L5–S2) and **nerve to quadratus femoris** (and inferior gemellus) (L4–S1)"
    - "**Nerve to piriformis** (S1–S2); nerves to levator ani and coccygeus (S3–S4)"
    - "**Pelvic splanchnic nerves** (S2–S4): parasympathetic (see [[pelvic-autonomic-nerves]])"
  motor: Gluteal muscles, the lateral rotators of the hip, hamstrings, all muscles of the leg and foot, the pelvic floor and the perineal muscles.
  sensory: Posterior thigh, most of the leg and foot (except the medial side supplied by the saphenous nerve), the buttock (partly) and the perineum.
  relations: >-
    The internal iliac vessels and their branches weave between the roots (the superior gluteal artery usually passes
    between L4/5 and S1, the inferior gluteal between S1/2 or S2/3). The rectum and sigmoid colon lie in front.
clinical:
  - title: Superior gluteal nerve injury (Trendelenburg sign)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Injury from a misplaced intramuscular injection, hip surgery, or pelvic fracture weakens gluteus medius and minimus.
    presentation: >-
      When standing on the affected leg, the **opposite side of the pelvis drops** (positive Trendelenburg sign), with a
      waddling or lurching (compensated Trendelenburg) gait.
  - title: Sciatic nerve injury from intramuscular injection
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Injection into the lower or medial buttock can hit the sciatic nerve as it leaves below piriformis.
    presentation: >-
      Pain and weakness below the knee (foot drop is common because the fibular division is most vulnerable). Give
      gluteal injections in the **upper outer quadrant** (or the ventrogluteal site).
  - title: Lumbosacral plexopathy
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Compression or infiltration by pelvic tumors (rectal, cervical, prostate), retroperitoneal hematoma, radiation, diabetic amyotrophy, or pressure from the fetal head during labor.
    presentation: Leg pain and weakness in more than one root or nerve distribution, with preserved paraspinal muscles on EMG (distinguishing it from radiculopathy).
pance:
  - "Positive **Trendelenburg** = **superior gluteal nerve** (gluteus medius/minimus) weakness; the opposite hip drops."
  - "Superior gluteal nerve and vessels are the only structures **above piriformis**."
  - "IM gluteal injections go in the **upper outer quadrant** to avoid the sciatic nerve."
  - "Sacral plexus roots: **L4–S4** (lumbosacral trunk + S1–S4)."
imaging:
  mri: MRI of the lumbosacral plexus (or MR neurography) shows tumor infiltration, hematoma, radiation change and nerve enlargement.
  ct: CT shows sacral fractures (with neural foramen involvement) and pelvic masses.
  keyViews:
    - "MRI lumbosacral plexus (coronal STIR/T1)"
exam:
  landmarks: The sciatic nerve emerges from under piriformis at about the midpoint of a line from the PSIS to the ischial tuberosity, then descends between the greater trochanter and ischial tuberosity.
  testing: >-
    Myotomes: hip abduction (L5, superior gluteal), hip extension (S1, inferior gluteal), knee flexion (L5–S1),
    ankle dorsiflexion (L4–L5), great toe extension (L5), plantarflexion (S1). **Ankle reflex (S1)**. Sensation over
    the posterior thigh, lateral leg and foot.
  specialTests:
    - name: Trendelenburg test
      technique: The patient stands on one leg (the tested side) with the other hip and knee flexed, for about 30 seconds.
      positive: The pelvis drops on the side of the lifted leg, or the patient leans the trunk over the stance leg.
      significance: Weakness of the hip abductors (gluteus medius/minimus) on the stance side — superior gluteal nerve injury, L5 radiculopathy or hip pathology.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      After an intramuscular injection into the buttock, a patient walks with a lurching gait. When standing on the
      right leg, the left side of the pelvis drops. Which nerve is most likely injured?
    choices:
      - Left superior gluteal nerve
      - Right superior gluteal nerve
      - Right inferior gluteal nerve
      - Right sciatic nerve
      - Left obturator nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      When standing on the **right** leg, the **right** gluteus medius and minimus must hold the pelvis level. Weakness
      (right **superior gluteal nerve**) lets the unsupported **left** side drop — a positive Trendelenburg sign.
  - stem: Which nerve supplies gluteus maximus?
    choices:
      - Superior gluteal nerve
      - Inferior gluteal nerve
      - Sciatic nerve
      - Pudendal nerve
      - Femoral nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **inferior gluteal nerve** (L5–S2) supplies gluteus maximus, the main hip extensor used to rise from a chair or
      climb stairs. The superior gluteal nerve supplies gluteus medius, minimus and tensor fasciae latae.
  - stem: Which structure passes through the greater sciatic foramen above the piriformis muscle?
    choices:
      - Sciatic nerve
      - Inferior gluteal nerve
      - Superior gluteal nerve
      - Pudendal nerve
      - Posterior femoral cutaneous nerve
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      Only the **superior gluteal nerve and vessels** pass above piriformis; everything else from the sacral plexus
      passes below it.
