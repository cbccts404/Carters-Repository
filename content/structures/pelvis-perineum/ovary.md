summary: Paired almond-shaped gonads on the lateral pelvic wall that produce oocytes and sex hormones. Their blood supply comes from the aorta, which explains torsion, and their lymph drains to the para-aortic nodes.
tags: [female, gynecology, oncology]
systems: [reproductive]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: >-
    In the **ovarian fossa** on the lateral pelvic wall, between the external and internal iliac vessels; the
    **obturator nerve** runs across the floor of the fossa. Attached to the back of the [[broad-ligament]] by the
    mesovarium.
  parts:
    - "**Suspensory ligament of the ovary** (infundibulopelvic ligament): fold of peritoneum from the ovary to the pelvic wall carrying the ovarian vessels, nerves and lymphatics"
    - "**Ligament of the ovary** (utero-ovarian ligament): fibromuscular cord from the ovary to the uterus just below the tube (a remnant of the gubernaculum, continuous with the round ligament)"
    - "Surface: covered by a modified cuboidal epithelium (not peritoneum), over the tunica albuginea, cortex (follicles) and medulla (vessels)"
  bloodSupply: >-
    **Ovarian artery** from the [[abdominal-aorta]] (at about L2), descending in the suspensory ligament; anastomoses
    with the ovarian branch of the [[uterine-artery]].
  venousDrainage: >-
    Pampiniform plexus → **ovarian vein**. **Right** ovarian vein → [[inferior-vena-cava]]; **left** ovarian vein →
    **left renal vein**.
  lymphatics: '**Lumbar (para-aortic) nodes**, following the ovarian vessels (see [[pelvic-lymph-nodes]]).'
  innervation: Ovarian plexus (from the aortic and renal plexuses) with the ovarian vessels; visceral pain is referred to the periumbilical region and lower abdomen (about T10–T11) {{verify}}
  relations: >-
    The [[uterine-tubes|uterine tube]] arches over it, its fimbriae touching the ovary. The [[ureter]] lies just behind
    the ovarian fossa and crosses the pelvic brim close to the ovarian vessels in the suspensory ligament.
clinical:
  - title: Ovarian torsion
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Twisting of the ovary (often with the tube) on its suspensory and ovarian ligaments, cutting off venous and then
      arterial flow. Usually an **enlarged ovary** (cyst or mass, often over 5 cm); also in pregnancy or with ovarian
      stimulation.
    presentation: >-
      **Sudden, severe unilateral pelvic pain** with nausea and vomiting; tender adnexal mass. Doppler may still show
      flow (dual supply), so normal flow does not exclude torsion.
    notes: A **surgical emergency** — laparoscopic detorsion, preserving the ovary whenever possible.
  - title: Ovarian cancer
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Most are **epithelial** (high-grade serous, many originating in the fimbriae). Risk factors — **BRCA1/2**, Lynch syndrome, family history, nulliparity, increasing age; oral contraceptives reduce risk.
    presentation: >-
      Vague symptoms: **bloating**, early satiety, pelvic pain, urinary frequency; later ascites and an adnexal mass.
      Often diagnosed at an advanced stage. **CA-125** helps evaluate a pelvic mass in postmenopausal women; there is
      **no screening** for average-risk women.
  - title: Functional ovarian cysts and cyst rupture
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Follicular or corpus luteum cysts; a ruptured corpus luteum can cause hemoperitoneum.
    presentation: Sudden pelvic pain, often mid-cycle or luteal phase, sometimes after intercourse; free fluid on ultrasound. Most simple cysts resolve spontaneously.
  - title: Polycystic ovary syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Hyperandrogenism, ovulatory dysfunction and polycystic ovarian morphology (Rotterdam criteria — two of three).
    presentation: Oligomenorrhea, hirsutism, acne, obesity and insulin resistance; increased risk of endometrial hyperplasia.
pance:
  - "Sudden unilateral pelvic pain + vomiting + adnexal mass → **ovarian torsion**; Doppler flow does **not** rule it out."
  - "Ovarian lymph → **para-aortic** nodes; left ovarian vein → **left renal vein**."
  - "Ovarian cancer: vague bloating/early satiety; BRCA; **no screening** for average risk."
  - "Obturator nerve in the ovarian fossa → ovarian disease may refer pain to the **medial thigh**."
imaging:
  ultrasound: >-
    **Transvaginal ultrasound** is first-line for adnexal masses (simple vs complex, solid parts, septations) and
    torsion (enlarged edematous ovary with peripheral follicles, "whirlpool sign" of the twisted pedicle). Risk is
    stratified with **O-RADS**.
  mri: Characterizes indeterminate adnexal masses (e.g. dermoid fat, endometrioma blood).
  ct: Staging of ovarian cancer (peritoneal implants, omental "cake", ascites, nodes).
  keyViews:
    - "Transvaginal ultrasound with color Doppler"
exam:
  landmarks: The ovaries lie on the lateral pelvic walls on either side of the uterus; they are usually palpable (as small, mobile, slightly tender structures) only in thin premenopausal women.
  palpation: >-
    Bimanual adnexal exam — size, mobility and tenderness. A **palpable ovary in a postmenopausal woman** needs
    evaluation, because the ovaries normally atrophy after menopause. {{verify}}
  testing: Pregnancy test in all women of reproductive age with pelvic pain.

      The presentation is classic for **ovarian torsion**. Because the ovary has a **dual blood supply** (ovarian and

      vessels. The ligament of the ovary (utero-ovarian ligament) runs from the ovary to the uterus.
