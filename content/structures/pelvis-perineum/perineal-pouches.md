---
name: Perineal pouches
type: space
region: pelvis-perineum
subregion: Perineum
taName: Spatium superficiale perinei; Spatium profundum perinei
aka: [Superficial perineal pouch, Deep perineal pouch, Urogenital diaphragm (older term)]
summary: Two fascial compartments of the urogenital triangle, separated by the perineal membrane. Their fascial attachments determine where urine spreads after urethral injury and how Fournier gangrene tracks.
tags: [perineum, trauma, urethra]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: In the urogenital triangle of the [[perineum]], between the ischiopubic rami.
  boundaries:
    superficial pouch — inferior: Membranous layer of the superficial perineal fascia (**Colles fascia**), continuous with the dartos fascia and **Scarpa fascia** of the abdominal wall
    superficial pouch — superior: '**Perineal membrane**'
    deep pouch — inferior: Perineal membrane
    deep pouch — superior: Inferior fascia of the pelvic diaphragm (the deep pouch is now regarded as open superiorly toward the pelvis)
    lateral: Ischiopubic rami (where Colles fascia and the perineal membrane attach)
    posterior: Fusion of Colles fascia with the posterior border of the perineal membrane at the perineal body
  contents:
    - "**Superficial pouch (male)**: root of the [[penis]] (bulb and crura), bulbospongiosus, ischiocavernosus, superficial transverse perineal muscles, the proximal spongy [[urethra]], and branches of the internal pudendal vessels and [[pudendal-nerve]]"
    - "**Superficial pouch (female)**: clitoris (crura), bulbs of the vestibule, **greater vestibular (Bartholin) glands**, the same muscles (see [[vulva]])"
    - "**Deep pouch (male)**: the **intermediate (membranous) urethra**, the **external urethral sphincter**, deep transverse perineal muscle, **bulbourethral (Cowper) glands**, and the dorsal vessels and nerves of the penis"
    - "**Deep pouch (female)**: the proximal urethra, the external urethral sphincter with compressor urethrae and urethrovaginal sphincter, and the dorsal vessels and nerves of the clitoris"
  communications: >-
    The superficial pouch is **open anteriorly** to the space beneath the membranous (Scarpa) layer of the anterior
    abdominal wall and the dartos of the scrotum and penis. It is **closed** laterally (ischiopubic rami),
    posteriorly (perineal body) and at the thighs (Scarpa/Colles fascia fuses with the **fascia lata** just below the
    inguinal ligament).
clinical:
  - title: Extravasation of urine after bulbar urethral rupture
    highYield: true
    mechanism: A straddle injury tears the spongy (bulbar) urethra and Buck fascia in the superficial pouch.
    presentation: >-
      Urine and blood spread into the **scrotum, penis and anterior abdominal wall** deep to Scarpa fascia, but **not
      into the thighs** (fusion with the fascia lata) or the anal triangle (fusion at the perineal body). Classic
      **"butterfly" perineal bruising** outlines Colles fascia.
  - title: Membranous urethral injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Pelvic fracture shears the membranous urethra in the deep pouch.
    presentation: >-
      Extravasation into the deep pouch and the **retropubic (extraperitoneal) space** around the bladder, with blood at
      the meatus and inability to void. Retrograde urethrogram before catheterization.
  - title: Spread of Fournier gangrene
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Necrotizing infection tracks along Colles, dartos and Scarpa fascia.
    presentation: It can spread rapidly from the perineum to the scrotum, penis and anterior abdominal wall; the testes are usually spared because they have their own blood supply. See [[perineum]].
pance:
  - "Bulbar (straddle) urethral injury → urine into scrotum, penis, abdominal wall — **not thighs**."
  - "Membranous urethral injury (pelvic fracture) → deep pouch and retropubic space."
  - "Colles fascia = perineal continuation of Scarpa fascia."
  - "Bulbourethral glands are in the **deep** pouch; Bartholin glands in the **superficial** pouch."
imaging:
  xray: '**Retrograde urethrogram** localizes the urethral tear and the pattern of extravasation.'
  ct: CT shows perineal hematoma, fluid and gas along fascial planes.
  mri: MRI defines perineal anatomy for urethral stricture planning and after trauma.
exam:
  landmarks: The perineal membrane spans the pubic arch between the ischiopubic rami; the bulb of the penis lies in the midline beneath bulbospongiosus.
  palpation: Inspect for a butterfly-shaped perineal hematoma, scrotal and penile swelling and blood at the meatus; check the distribution of swelling (spared thighs point to the superficial pouch).
quiz:
  - stem: The external urethral sphincter and bulbourethral glands in a male are located in which compartment?
    choices:
      - Superficial perineal pouch
      - Deep perineal pouch
      - Ischioanal fossa
      - Retropubic space
      - Rectovesical pouch
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **deep perineal pouch** contains the membranous urethra, the **external urethral sphincter** and the
      **bulbourethral (Cowper) glands**. The root of the penis and its muscles are in the superficial pouch.
  - stem: Why does urine from a ruptured bulbar urethra not spread into the thighs?
    choices:
      - The perineal membrane blocks it
      - Colles/Scarpa fascia fuses with the fascia lata just below the inguinal ligament
      - Buck fascia always stays intact
      - The tunica vaginalis contains it
      - The obturator membrane blocks it
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The membranous layer of superficial fascia (**Scarpa** in the abdomen, **Colles** in the perineum) **fuses with
      the fascia lata** just below the inguinal ligament, closing off the thigh; it also fuses with the perineal
      membrane posteriorly, closing off the anal triangle.
