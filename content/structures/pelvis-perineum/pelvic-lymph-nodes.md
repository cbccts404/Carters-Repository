---
name: Pelvic and inguinal lymph drainage
type: lymphatic
region: pelvis-perineum
subregion: Vessels, nerves & lymphatics
taName: Nodi lymphoidei pelvis; Nodi lymphoidei inguinales
aka: [Iliac lymph nodes, Pelvic lymph nodes]
summary: Iliac, sacral and inguinal node groups that drain the pelvis and perineum. The rule "lymph follows the arteries" (gonads to para-aortic, perineal skin to superficial inguinal) predicts where pelvic cancers spread.
tags: [lymphatic, oncology, sti]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: >-
    **External iliac** nodes along the external iliac vessels (including the **obturator** nodes near the obturator
    nerve); **internal iliac** nodes along the internal iliac vessels; **sacral** nodes in front of the sacrum;
    **common iliac** nodes around the common iliac vessels; **superficial inguinal** nodes in the subcutaneous tissue
    below the inguinal ligament (horizontal and vertical groups); **deep inguinal** nodes medial to the femoral vein.
  drains: >-
    **Superficial inguinal**: skin of the lower abdominal wall below the umbilicus, buttock, perineum, **scrotum**,
    **vulva**, lower [[vagina]], the [[anal-canal]] **below the pectinate line**, and the lower limb (via the vertical
    group). Also some lymph from the uterine fundus along the round ligament. **Deep inguinal**: glans of the
    [[penis]]/clitoris, spongy urethra and superficial inguinal efferents. **External iliac**: inguinal nodes, bladder,
    [[prostate]], upper vagina and cervix. **Internal iliac**: most pelvic viscera, the deep perineum and gluteal
    region. **Sacral**: [[rectum]], cervix and prostate (posterior). **Lumbar (para-aortic)**: **testis**, **ovary**,
    [[uterine-tubes]] and **uterine fundus** (following the gonadal vessels) and the common iliac nodes.
  drainsTo: >-
    Superficial → deep inguinal → external iliac → common iliac → **lumbar (para-aortic/caval) nodes** → lumbar trunks
    → cisterna chyli → [[thoracic-duct]]. The upper rectum drains separately to the inferior mesenteric nodes.
  relations: The obturator nodes lie on the lateral pelvic wall around the obturator nerve and vessels — the first-echelon nodes for the cervix, prostate and bladder, sampled in pelvic lymphadenectomy.
  notes: >-
    **Key contrasts**: testis → para-aortic, but scrotum → superficial inguinal. Anal canal above the pectinate line →
    internal iliac; below → superficial inguinal. Ovary/uterine fundus → para-aortic; cervix → iliac and sacral.
clinical:
  - title: Testicular vs scrotal cancer spread
    highYield: true
    mechanism: The testis drains to the **para-aortic** nodes (its embryologic origin); scrotal skin drains to the **superficial inguinal** nodes.
    presentation: Testicular cancer causes retroperitoneal, not inguinal, lymphadenopathy — unless the scrotum has been violated. Hence the inguinal (not scrotal) approach for orchiectomy.
  - title: Inguinal lymphadenopathy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Infection or cancer in the drainage area: sexually transmitted infections (**syphilis** — painless nodes with a
      painless chancre; **HSV** — tender nodes with painful vesicles; **chancroid** — painful ulcer with suppurative
      nodes; **LGV** — painless ulcer then painful "groove sign" buboes), lower limb infections, and cancers of the vulva,
      penis, anus (below the pectinate line) and lower-limb skin (melanoma).
    presentation: Enlarged inguinal nodes; examine the entire drainage area including the perineum, anus and feet.
  - title: Pelvic nodal metastases
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Cervical, prostate, bladder, endometrial and rectal cancers spread to the obturator, iliac and sacral nodes.
    presentation: Found on staging imaging or lymphadenectomy; nodal involvement upstages the cancer (e.g. FIGO stage IIIC for cervical cancer). Lymphedema of the legs can follow dissection or radiation.

imaging:
  ct: "CT of the abdomen and pelvis shows enlarged nodes (short-axis size criteria, about 1 cm for most pelvic groups) for staging. {{verify: node size thresholds vary by site}}"
  mri: MRI assesses pelvic nodes during local staging of rectal, cervical and prostate cancer.
  ultrasound: Ultrasound of inguinal nodes (cortical thickening, loss of fatty hilum) with fine-needle aspiration.
  keyViews:
    - "PET-CT or PSMA PET for nodal staging"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    Superficial inguinal nodes: a **horizontal group** just below and parallel to the inguinal ligament and a
    **vertical group** along the upper great saphenous vein. Pelvic nodes are not palpable.
  palpation: Palpate both groin regions with the patient supine; small (under about 1 cm), mobile, non-tender nodes are commonly normal. Note size, tenderness, consistency, fixation and whether they suppurate.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 30-year-old man has a seminoma of the left testis. Which lymph nodes are most likely to be involved first?
    choices:
      - Left superficial inguinal nodes
      - Left deep inguinal nodes
      - Left para-aortic (lumbar) nodes near the left renal vein
      - Left internal iliac nodes
      - Left supraclavicular nodes only
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      Testicular lymphatics follow the testicular vessels to the **lumbar (para-aortic) nodes**; on the left they
      drain near the **left renal vein**, where the left testicular vein ends. Inguinal nodes are involved only if the
      scrotal skin is affected.
  - stem: A squamous cell carcinoma of the labia majora would first metastasize to which nodes?
    choices:
      - Para-aortic nodes
      - Internal iliac nodes
      - Superficial inguinal nodes
      - Sacral nodes
      - Inferior mesenteric nodes
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The vulva, like other perineal skin, drains to the **superficial inguinal nodes**, which are removed or sampled in
      vulvar cancer surgery.
  - stem: A man has a painless, indurated penile ulcer and painless, rubbery bilateral inguinal lymphadenopathy. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Chancroid
      - Genital herpes
      - Primary syphilis
      - Lymphogranuloma venereum
      - Granuloma inguinale
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Primary syphilis** causes a **painless indurated chancre** with painless regional lymphadenopathy. Chancroid is
      painful with suppurative nodes; herpes causes painful vesicles and tender nodes.
