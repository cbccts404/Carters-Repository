name: Anal canal
type: organ
region: pelvis-perineum
subregion: Perineum
taName: Canalis analis
summary: Terminal 3–4 cm of the gut, surrounded by the internal and external anal sphincters. The pectinate line divides it into two halves with different blood supply, lymph drainage, nerves and diseases.
tags: [gastrointestinal, perineum, pectinate-line]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: >-
    In the anal triangle of the [[perineum]], from the anorectal junction (where the [[rectum]] passes through the
    puborectalis sling) to the anus. Surrounded laterally by the [[ischioanal-fossa|ischioanal fossae]].
  parts:
    - "**Anal columns** (of Morgagni): longitudinal mucosal ridges containing branches of the superior rectal vessels, joined at their lower ends by the **anal valves**; behind the valves lie the **anal sinuses**, into which the anal glands open"
    - "**Pectinate (dentate) line**: the line of the anal valves; the junction of hindgut endoderm (above) and ectodermal proctodeum (below)"
    - "**Anal pecten** and **anocutaneous line** (white line/intersphincteric groove) below the pectinate line; then perianal skin"
    - "**Internal anal sphincter**: involuntary thickening of the circular smooth muscle; provides most resting tone; sympathetic stimulation maintains contraction"
    - "**External anal sphincter**: voluntary skeletal muscle (subcutaneous, superficial and deep parts), blending with puborectalis above; supplied by the inferior rectal nerve (S4) from the [[pudendal-nerve]]"
  bloodSupply: >-
    **Above the pectinate line**: superior rectal artery (from the [[inferior-mesenteric-artery]]). **Below**:
    inferior rectal arteries (from the internal pudendal artery). Middle rectal arteries anastomose with both.
  venousDrainage: >-
    Above: superior rectal vein → IMV → **portal** system (internal hemorrhoidal plexus). Below: inferior rectal veins
    → internal pudendal → internal iliac (**systemic**; external plexus).
  lymphatics: Above → internal iliac (and inferior mesenteric) nodes. Below → **superficial inguinal nodes** (see [[pelvic-lymph-nodes]]).
  innervation: >-
    Above: **visceral** (autonomic) — sensitive only to stretch, so lesions are **painless**. Below: **somatic**
    (inferior rectal nerves from the pudendal nerve) — sensitive to pain, touch and temperature, so lesions are
    **painful**.
  relations: >-
    Anterior: the perineal body and the bulb of the penis/membranous urethra (male) or the lower [[vagina]] (female).
    Posterior: the anococcygeal body and coccyx. Lateral: the ischioanal fossae.
  notes: >-
    **Epithelium**: columnar above the pectinate line; stratified squamous (non-keratinized in the pecten, keratinized
    in the perianal skin) below. So **adenocarcinoma** arises above and **squamous cell carcinoma** below.
clinical:
  - title: Hemorrhoids
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Engorgement and descent of the vascular cushions. **Internal** hemorrhoids arise above the pectinate line (graded
      I–IV by prolapse); **external** hemorrhoids below it. Risk: constipation and straining, pregnancy, low-fiber diet.
    presentation: >-
      Internal: **painless bright-red bleeding** on the paper or dripping into the bowl, prolapse. External: perianal
      swelling; **thrombosed** external hemorrhoids are acutely **painful** blue lumps (excise if within about 72 hours).
    notes: First-line treatment is fiber, fluids and avoiding straining; rubber band ligation for grades I–III internal hemorrhoids.
  - title: Anal fissure
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Tear in the anoderm **below** the pectinate line, most often in the **posterior midline**, usually from passing hard stool, with internal sphincter spasm limiting healing.
    presentation: >-
      **Severe tearing pain with defecation** lasting minutes to hours, and bright-red blood. Chronic fissures have a
      sentinel skin tag. Fissures that are lateral or multiple suggest Crohn disease, HIV, TB, syphilis or cancer.
    notes: Fiber and sitz baths, topical nitroglycerin or nifedipine (or diltiazem), botulinum toxin; lateral internal sphincterotomy if refractory.
  - title: Anorectal abscess and fistula-in-ano
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Infection of an **anal gland** in an anal sinus (cryptoglandular), spreading into the perianal, ischioanal, intersphincteric or supralevator spaces.
    presentation: Throbbing perianal pain, swelling, fever. Treatment is **incision and drainage**; a persistent tract after drainage is a fistula. See [[ischioanal-fossa]].
  - title: Anal cancer
    highYield: false
    mechanism: '**Squamous cell carcinoma** (below the pectinate line) associated with **HPV** (16/18), HIV, receptive anal intercourse and immunosuppression.'
    presentation: Bleeding, mass, pain; inguinal lymphadenopathy. Treated primarily with chemoradiation (Nigro protocol).
pance:
  - "Pectinate line: above = painless, portal, internal iliac nodes, adenocarcinoma; below = painful, systemic, **superficial inguinal nodes**, squamous cell carcinoma."
  - "Painless bright-red bleeding = **internal hemorrhoids**; exquisitely painful lump = thrombosed external hemorrhoid."
  - "Tearing pain with defecation + posterior midline tear = **anal fissure**; off-midline → think Crohn."
  - "Internal sphincter = involuntary smooth muscle; external = voluntary skeletal (pudendal)."
  - "Anal cancer = SCC, HPV."
imaging:
  mri: Pelvic MRI (fistula protocol) maps complex or recurrent fistulas and abscesses, especially in Crohn disease.
  ultrasound: '**Endoanal ultrasound** shows sphincter defects (e.g. after obstetric injury) and fistula tracts.'
  ct: CT pelvis for deep (ischioanal or supralevator) abscesses.
  keyViews:
    - "Endoanal ultrasound for sphincter integrity"
    - "MRI pelvis fistula protocol"
exam:
  landmarks: The anal verge; the pectinate line lies about 2 cm above it (seen on anoscopy); the intersphincteric groove is felt as a shallow furrow.
  palpation: >-
    Inspect with the buttocks spread (fissure, tags, thrombosed hemorrhoid, fistula openings). DRE: resting tone
    (internal sphincter), voluntary squeeze (external sphincter and puborectalis), masses and tenderness. In a
    suspected fissure, DRE may be deferred because of pain.
  testing: '**Anal wink** (anocutaneous reflex, S2–S4): stroking the perianal skin causes contraction of the external sphincter. **Anoscopy** shows internal hemorrhoids and the pectinate line.'
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 28-year-old woman has severe, tearing anal pain with every bowel movement for 3 weeks, with bright-red blood on
      the tissue. Inspection shows a longitudinal tear in the posterior midline of the anal verge. What is the most
      appropriate initial management?
    choices:
      - Incision and drainage
      - Fiber, sitz baths and topical nifedipine or nitroglycerin
      - Rubber band ligation
      - Colonoscopy under anesthesia
      - Hemorrhoidectomy
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      This is an **acute anal fissure**. Initial treatment is conservative: **fiber, sitz baths**, and topical
      **calcium-channel blockers or nitrates** to relax the internal sphincter. Sphincterotomy is for refractory chronic
      fissures.
  - stem: A squamous cell carcinoma arising below the pectinate line would first spread to which lymph nodes?
    choices:
      - Internal iliac nodes
      - Superficial inguinal nodes
      - Inferior mesenteric nodes
      - Para-aortic nodes
      - Sacral nodes
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Below the pectinate line, lymph drains to the **superficial inguinal nodes**, like the perineal skin. Above it,
      lymph follows the arteries to the internal iliac and inferior mesenteric nodes.
  - stem: Which statement about internal hemorrhoids is correct?
    choices:
      - They are covered by squamous epithelium and are painful
      - They drain into the internal pudendal veins
      - They arise above the pectinate line and are usually painless
      - They are supplied by the inferior rectal nerve
      - They drain to the superficial inguinal nodes
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Internal hemorrhoids** arise above the pectinate line, where the mucosa has **visceral innervation**, so they
      usually cause **painless bleeding**. They drain via the superior rectal veins into the portal system.
