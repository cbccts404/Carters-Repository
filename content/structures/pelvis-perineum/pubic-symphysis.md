name: Pubic symphysis
type: joint
region: pelvis-perineum
subregion: Bony pelvis
taName: Symphysis pubica
summary: Fibrocartilaginous midline joint between the two pubic bones. It widens in pregnancy and after open-book pelvic injuries, and is a source of groin pain in athletes (osteitis pubis).
tags: [pelvis, groin-pain]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Anterior midline of the [[bony-pelvis]], between the bodies of the two pubic bones.
  classification: Secondary cartilaginous joint (symphysis)
  bones: The bodies of the right and left pubic bones (parts of the [[hip-bone|hip bones]])
  capsule: >-
    No synovial capsule. The hyaline-covered articular surfaces are joined by a fibrocartilaginous **interpubic disc**,
    which may contain a small slit-like cavity.
  ligaments:
    - "**Superior pubic ligament**: along the upper border, extending to the pubic tubercles"
    - "**Inferior (arcuate) pubic ligament**: arches under the joint and rounds off the apex of the pubic arch"
    - "Anterior and posterior fibers blend with the aponeuroses of the abdominal and adductor muscles"
  movements:
    - "Minimal; slight shearing and separation, increased in pregnancy"
  stability: >-
    Maintained by the interpubic disc and ligaments. Hormonal ligamentous laxity (relaxin) widens it slightly in
    late pregnancy.
  innervation: Branches of the iliohypogastric, ilioinguinal and pudendal nerves {{verify}}
  bloodSupply: Branches of the obturator, inferior epigastric and internal and external pudendal arteries {{verify}}
  relations: >-
    Posterior: the retropubic (prevesical) space and the [[urinary-bladder]]. Inferior: the dorsal vessels and nerves
    of the penis or clitoris pass beneath the arcuate ligament. Its upper border is the anterior limit of the pelvic
    inlet.
clinical:
  - title: Symphyseal diastasis (open-book pelvic injury)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Anteroposterior compression of the pelvis, often with sacroiliac ligament disruption.
    presentation: >-
      Widened symphysis on AP pelvis, pelvic instability and potential major hemorrhage. Associated with bladder and
      [[urethra|urethral]] injuries. See [[bony-pelvis]].
  - title: Osteitis pubis
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Repetitive stress at the symphysis from kicking, running and twisting sports, or after pelvic or urologic surgery.
    presentation: >-
      Anterior groin and pubic pain, worse with running, kicking or sit-ups, with tenderness over the symphysis.
      Sclerosis and erosions of the joint margins may appear on X-ray.
  - title: Symphysis pubis dysfunction (pregnancy-related pelvic girdle pain)
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Increased mobility of the pelvic joints in pregnancy.
    presentation: Suprapubic pain worse with walking, climbing stairs, standing on one leg or rolling over in bed. Managed with physical therapy and support belts; true peripartum separation is rare.
pance:
  - "Athlete with midline pubic pain worse with kicking and sit-ups → **osteitis pubis**."
  - "Widened symphysis after trauma = **open-book** (APC) injury: look for hemorrhage and urethral/bladder injury."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    AP pelvis: joint width, alignment of the superior pubic rami (step-off in instability) and sclerosis or erosions.
    **Flamingo (single-leg stance) views** can show dynamic instability (a vertical shift of more than about 2 mm)
    {{verify: flamingo view instability threshold}}.
  mri: Shows bone marrow edema of the pubic bodies (osteitis pubis) and adductor or rectus abdominis aponeurosis injury in athletic groin pain.
  ct: Defines fractures and diastasis in trauma.
  keyViews:
    - "AP pelvis"
    - "Flamingo views (standing on each leg)"
exam:
  landmarks: The pubic crest and tubercles are palpable just above the genitalia; the symphysis lies in the midline between them.
  palpation: Direct tenderness over the joint (osteitis pubis) versus tenderness at the adductor longus origin just lateral to it.
  testing: Pain on resisted hip adduction (squeeze test) and on resisted sit-up in athletic groin pain.
  specialTests:
    - name: Adductor squeeze test
      technique: Supine with the hips and knees flexed; the patient squeezes the examiner's fist (or a pressure cuff) between the knees.
      positive: Reproduction of groin or pubic pain, or reduced squeeze strength.
      significance: Supports adductor-related or pubic-related groin pain; not specific for osteitis pubis.
quiz:
  - stem: The pubic symphysis is which type of joint?
    choices:
      - Fibrous (syndesmosis)
      - Primary cartilaginous (synchondrosis)
      - Secondary cartilaginous (symphysis)
      - Synovial hinge
      - Synovial plane
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The pubic symphysis is a **secondary cartilaginous joint**: hyaline cartilage on the bone ends joined by a
      fibrocartilaginous disc, like the intervertebral discs. A primary cartilaginous joint is a growth plate.
  - stem: >-
      A 22-year-old soccer player has 6 weeks of midline pubic pain that worsens with kicking and sit-ups. There is
      tenderness directly over the pubic symphysis. MRI shows bone marrow edema of both pubic bodies. What is the most
      likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Inguinal hernia
      - Osteitis pubis
      - Femoral neck stress fracture
      - Adductor longus rupture
      - Septic arthritis of the hip
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Repetitive shear stress in kicking sports causes **osteitis pubis**: pain and tenderness at the symphysis with
      parasymphyseal marrow edema. Femoral neck stress fractures cause groin pain with tenderness over the hip and pain
      on hip range of motion.
  - stem: >-
      A pregnant woman at 32 weeks has suprapubic pain that is worse when climbing stairs, standing on one leg and
      rolling over in bed. What is the most likely cause?
    choices:
      - Osteitis pubis
      - Open-book pelvic fracture
      - Pubic ramus stress fracture
      - Round ligament pain
      - Symphysis pubis dysfunction (pregnancy-related pelvic girdle pain)
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      Increased mobility of the pelvic joints in pregnancy (relaxin) causes **symphysis pubis dysfunction**: suprapubic
      pain with walking, stairs, single-leg stance or rolling over. It is managed with physical therapy and support
      belts. Round ligament pain is brief, sharp groin pain with sudden movement.
