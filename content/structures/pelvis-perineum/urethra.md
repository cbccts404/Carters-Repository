name: Urethra
type: organ
region: pelvis-perineum
subregion: Pelvic viscera
taName: Urethra masculina; Urethra feminina
summary: Tube from the bladder to the exterior — about 18–20 cm and S-shaped in males, about 4 cm in females. The site of the injury determines where extravasated urine goes, and urethral injury must be excluded before catheterizing a trauma patient.
tags: [genitourinary, trauma, sti]
systems: [genitourinary]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: From the internal urethral orifice at the bladder neck to the external urethral orifice (glans of the penis, or the vestibule between the clitoris and vaginal orifice).
  parts:
    - "**Male — preprostatic (intramural) part**: in the bladder neck, surrounded by the internal urethral sphincter"
    - "**Male — prostatic urethra**: the widest and most dilatable part; urethral crest with the **seminal colliculus**, where the ejaculatory ducts open, and prostatic ducts in the sinuses on either side"
    - "**Male — intermediate (membranous) urethra**: the shortest and least distensible part, passing through the deep perineal pouch surrounded by the **external urethral sphincter**; the usual site of injury with pelvic fractures"
    - "**Male — spongy (penile) urethra**: the longest part, in the corpus spongiosum; the ducts of the bulbourethral glands open into its bulbar portion; dilates in the glans as the navicular fossa"
    - "**Female urethra**: about 4 cm, runs anteroinferiorly behind the symphysis, embedded in the anterior vaginal wall; paraurethral (**Skene**) glands open near its external orifice"
  bloodSupply: >-
    Male: prostatic branches of the inferior vesical and middle rectal arteries (prostatic part); branches of the
    internal pudendal artery — artery of the bulb and urethral artery (membranous and spongy parts). Female: internal
    pudendal and vaginal arteries.
  venousDrainage: Prostatic or vesical venous plexuses and the internal pudendal veins.
  lymphatics: Proximal parts → internal iliac nodes; spongy urethra → mainly deep inguinal nodes (see [[pelvic-lymph-nodes]]).
  innervation: Prostatic/vesical plexuses (autonomic); somatic sensation and the external sphincter via the [[pudendal-nerve]] (S2–S4).
  relations: >-
    Male: passes through the [[prostate]], the [[perineal-pouches|deep perineal pouch]] and the corpus spongiosum of the
    [[penis]]. Female: closely related to the anterior wall of the [[vagina]].
  notes: >-
    Catheterizing a male: hold the penis upward (perpendicular to the body) to straighten the prepubic curve; resistance
    is typical at the external sphincter (membranous part) and at an enlarged prostate. The external urethral orifice is
    the narrowest point.
clinical:
  - title: Posterior (membranous) urethral injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Pelvic fracture** (shearing at the prostatomembranous junction), usually in males.'
    presentation: >-
      **Blood at the urethral meatus**, inability to void, perineal or scrotal hematoma, and possibly a high-riding or
      non-palpable prostate on DRE (an unreliable sign). Urine extravasates into the deep perineal pouch and retropubic
      space.
    notes: '**Do not pass a Foley catheter** until a **retrograde urethrogram** excludes injury; a suprapubic catheter is used if the urethra is disrupted.'
  - title: Anterior (bulbar) urethral injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Straddle injury** (fall astride a bicycle bar or beam) crushing the bulbar urethra against the pubic arch.'
    presentation: >-
      Blood at the meatus and **"butterfly" perineal bruising**. If Buck fascia is torn, urine and blood spread in the
      **superficial perineal pouch** into the scrotum, penis and lower anterior abdominal wall — but **not the thighs or
      anal triangle** (see [[perineal-pouches]]).
  - title: Urethritis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Sexually transmitted infection — *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*, *Chlamydia trachomatis*, *Mycoplasma genitalium*, *Trichomonas*.
    presentation: >-
      Dysuria and urethral discharge (purulent with gonorrhea). Diagnose with **NAAT** of first-catch urine. Treat
      gonorrhea with **ceftriaxone 500 mg IM** (plus **doxycycline** if chlamydia is not excluded) per CDC 2021 guidance;
      treat partners. {{verify: check the current CDC STI treatment guidelines}}
  - title: Hypospadias
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Incomplete fusion of the **urethral folds**, so the meatus opens on the **ventral** penis, scrotum or perineum. (Epispadias, a dorsal opening, is associated with bladder exstrophy.)
    presentation: Abnormal meatus, dorsal hooded foreskin, often ventral curvature (chordee). **Circumcision is avoided** because the foreskin is used in the repair.
  - title: Posterior urethral valves
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Congenital obstructing membrane in the **prostatic urethra** of male infants.
    presentation: The most common cause of bladder outlet obstruction in male newborns — bilateral hydronephrosis and oligohydramnios on prenatal ultrasound, poor stream. Diagnosed by VCUG.
pance:
  - "Pelvic fracture + blood at the meatus → **retrograde urethrogram before Foley**."
  - "Straddle injury → bulbar (anterior) urethra → extravasation into scrotum, penis, abdominal wall, **not thighs**."
  - "Hypospadias = ventral meatus (urethral folds) → **no circumcision**."
  - "Posterior urethral valves: male newborn, bilateral hydronephrosis, **VCUG**."
  - "Short female urethra → higher UTI risk."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    **Retrograde urethrogram (RUG)**: contrast injected into the meatus under fluoroscopy shows extravasation (urethral
    injury) or stricture. **VCUG**: contrast instilled into the bladder, films during voiding — shows the posterior
    urethra (valves) and vesicoureteral reflux.
  mri: Used for female urethral diverticulum and urethral tumors.
  ultrasound: Sonourethrography can estimate the length of anterior urethral strictures.
  keyViews:
    - "Retrograde urethrogram (oblique position)"
    - "Voiding cystourethrogram"
exam:
  landmarks: The male bulbar urethra lies in the midline of the perineum, deep to bulbospongiosus; the female external urethral orifice lies between the clitoris and vaginal orifice.
  palpation: Female — urethral tenderness or a suburethral mass expressing pus (diverticulum or Skene gland abscess).
  testing: Inspect the meatus for blood, discharge, and position (hypospadias). In trauma check for perineal and scrotal hematoma and perform a DRE.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 30-year-old man is brought in after being crushed in a motor vehicle collision. He has a pelvic fracture, blood
      at the urethral meatus and a perineal hematoma. What is the most appropriate next step for his urinary tract?
    choices:
      - Insert a Foley catheter
      - Retrograde urethrogram
      - CT cystogram through a Foley catheter
      - Intravenous pyelogram
      - Observation until he voids
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Blood at the meatus with a pelvic fracture suggests **posterior urethral injury**. A **retrograde urethrogram**
      must come before any attempt at urethral catheterization, which could convert a partial into a complete tear.
  - stem: >-
      A 12-year-old boy falls astride the top bar of his bicycle. He develops swelling and bruising of the scrotum,
      penis and lower abdominal wall, but his thighs are spared. Which structure prevents spread into the thighs?
    choices:
      - Attachment of the membranous layer of superficial fascia (Scarpa/Colles) to the fascia lata
      - Perineal membrane
      - Inguinal ligament alone
      - Tunica vaginalis
      - Buck fascia
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      Urine from a **bulbar urethral tear** fills the superficial perineal pouch beneath **Colles fascia**, which is
      continuous with Scarpa fascia and dartos. The membranous layer **fuses with the fascia lata** just below the
      inguinal ligament, so fluid cannot enter the thigh; its fusion with the perineal membrane blocks spread into the
      anal triangle.
  - stem: Which is the shortest and least distensible part of the male urethra?
    choices:
      - Preprostatic
      - Prostatic
      - Intermediate (membranous)
      - Spongy
      - Navicular fossa
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **intermediate (membranous) urethra** (about 1–1.5 cm), surrounded by the external urethral sphincter in the
      deep perineal pouch, is the shortest and least distensible part (apart from the external orifice).
