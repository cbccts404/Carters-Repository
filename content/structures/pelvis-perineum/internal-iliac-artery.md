name: Internal iliac artery
type: artery
region: pelvis-perineum
subregion: Vessels, nerves & lymphatics
taName: Arteria iliaca interna
aka: [Hypogastric artery]
summary: Main artery of the pelvis, supplying the pelvic viscera, perineum, gluteal region and medial thigh. Its branches bleed in pelvic fractures and are embolized for postpartum hemorrhage and fibroids.
tags: [pelvis, trauma, vascular]
systems: [cardiovascular]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Lateral pelvic wall, from the pelvic brim to the upper border of the greater sciatic foramen.
  origin: Bifurcation of the common iliac artery at about the level of the **L5/S1 disc**, anterior to the [[sacroiliac-joint]].
  course: >-
    Descends posteromedially into the lesser pelvis, with the [[ureter]] anterior to it and the internal iliac vein and
    lumbosacral trunk posterior, and divides near the upper margin of the greater sciatic foramen into **anterior** and
    **posterior divisions** (the branching pattern varies considerably).
  branches:
    - "**Posterior division** (mnemonic **\"I Love Sex\"**): **iliolumbar**, **lateral sacral**, **superior gluteal** (largest branch; leaves through the greater sciatic foramen **above piriformis**)"
    - "**Anterior division — visceral**: **umbilical** (patent part gives the superior vesical arteries; its obliterated part is the **medial umbilical ligament**); **inferior vesical** (male) or **vaginal** (female); [[uterine-artery]] (female); **middle rectal**"
    - "**Anterior division — parietal**: **obturator** (through the obturator canal to the medial thigh); **internal pudendal** (out through the greater and back in through the lesser sciatic foramen, into the pudendal canal to supply the perineum, anal canal and erectile tissue); **inferior gluteal** (below piriformis)"
  supplies: Pelvic viscera ([[urinary-bladder]], [[rectum]], [[prostate]], [[uterus]], [[vagina]]), the perineum and external genitalia, the gluteal muscles, the medial thigh (obturator) and the pelvic walls.
  anastomoses: >-
    Iliolumbar ↔ lumbar arteries; lateral sacral ↔ median sacral; middle rectal ↔ superior rectal (from the
    [[inferior-mesenteric-artery]]) and inferior rectal; uterine ↔ ovarian; obturator ↔ inferior epigastric (pubic
    branches); inferior gluteal ↔ medial and lateral circumflex femoral (**cruciate anastomosis**). These collaterals
    allow bilateral internal iliac ligation or embolization without pelvic necrosis.
  termination: Divides into its anterior and posterior divisions.
  relations: >-
    An **accessory (aberrant) obturator artery** from the inferior epigastric or external iliac artery crosses the
    superior pubic ramus in a sizable minority of people (**"corona mortis"**) and can bleed during hernia repair or
    pelvic fracture fixation. {{verify: prevalence estimates vary widely}}
clinical:
  - title: Arterial hemorrhage in pelvic fractures
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Shearing of branches by fracture fragments — the **superior gluteal** (posterior ring and sacroiliac injuries),
      **internal pudendal** and **obturator** arteries are most often involved.
    presentation: >-
      Ongoing hemodynamic instability despite a binder and resuscitation; an active contrast blush on CT. Treated with
      **angioembolization** (or preperitoneal packing). See [[bony-pelvis]].
  - title: Postpartum hemorrhage — embolization or ligation
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Uterine atony or trauma not controlled by uterotonics.
    presentation: Uterine artery embolization, or ligation of the uterine or internal iliac arteries, reduces pulse pressure to the uterus while collaterals preserve perfusion.
  - title: Aortoiliac occlusive disease (Leriche syndrome)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Atherosclerotic occlusion of the distal aorta and/or both iliac arteries.
    presentation: '**Buttock and thigh claudication**, **erectile dysfunction** (reduced internal pudendal flow), and **diminished or absent femoral pulses**.'
pance:
  - "Leriche syndrome triad: **buttock claudication, impotence, absent femoral pulses**."
  - "Superior gluteal artery = largest branch; exits **above piriformis**; injured in posterior pelvic fractures."
  - "Posterior division: **I**liolumbar, **L**ateral sacral, **S**uperior gluteal."
  - "Obliterated umbilical artery = **medial umbilical ligament**."
imaging:
  ct: >-
    **CT angiography** shows active extravasation in trauma, aneurysms and occlusive disease. Internal iliac aneurysms
    are often associated with abdominal aortic aneurysms.
  xray: Conventional catheter angiography is used for diagnosis and **embolization** (trauma, PPH, uterine fibroids, tumor bleeding).
  ultrasound: Duplex ultrasound of the iliac arteries assesses aortoiliac disease (with ankle-brachial indices).
  keyViews:
    - "CT angiography of the abdomen and pelvis"
    - "Pelvic angiography with selective internal iliac injection"
exam:
  landmarks: Not palpable. Iliac inflow is judged from the **femoral pulses** (just below the midpoint of the inguinal ligament).
  testing: Compare femoral pulses, listen for iliac/femoral bruits, and measure the ankle-brachial index; ask about buttock claudication and erectile dysfunction.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 62-year-old man who smokes has cramping pain in both buttocks and thighs when walking two blocks, relieved by
      rest. He also reports erectile dysfunction. Femoral pulses are weak bilaterally. Where is the most likely site of
      arterial occlusion?
    choices:
      - Superficial femoral arteries
      - Popliteal arteries
      - Distal aorta and common iliac arteries
      - Renal arteries
      - Anterior tibial arteries
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Leriche syndrome** (aortoiliac occlusive disease) causes buttock and thigh claudication, erectile dysfunction
      (reduced flow to the internal iliac/internal pudendal arteries) and diminished femoral pulses. Superficial femoral
      disease causes calf claudication.
  - stem: Which artery leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen above the piriformis muscle?
    choices:
      - Inferior gluteal artery
      - Internal pudendal artery
      - Superior gluteal artery
      - Obturator artery
      - Middle rectal artery
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **superior gluteal artery** (with the superior gluteal nerve) is the only structure passing **above**
      piriformis. The inferior gluteal and internal pudendal arteries pass below it.
  - stem: The medial umbilical ligament is a remnant of which fetal vessel?
    choices:
      - Umbilical vein
      - Umbilical artery
      - Ductus venosus
      - Urachus
      - Vitelline artery
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The distal part of each **umbilical artery** becomes a medial umbilical ligament; the proximal part stays patent
      and gives the superior vesical arteries. The urachus becomes the median umbilical ligament, and the umbilical vein
      the ligamentum teres.
  - stem: Which artery is a branch of the posterior division of the internal iliac artery?
    choices:
      - Obturator artery
      - Internal pudendal artery
      - Inferior gluteal artery
      - Uterine artery
      - Iliolumbar artery
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      The posterior division gives the **iliolumbar**, **lateral sacral** and **superior gluteal** arteries (mnemonic "I
      Love Sex"). The obturator, internal pudendal, inferior gluteal and uterine arteries come from the anterior
      division.
  - stem: >-
      Which artery leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen, re-enters through the lesser sciatic foramen,
      and runs in the pudendal canal to supply the perineum and erectile tissue?
    choices:
      - Internal pudendal artery
      - Superior gluteal artery
      - Obturator artery
      - Middle rectal artery
      - Inferior vesical artery
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      The **internal pudendal artery** (anterior division) exits through the greater sciatic foramen, re-enters through
      the lesser sciatic foramen, and runs in the pudendal (Alcock) canal to supply the perineum, the anal canal below
      the pectinate line and the erectile tissue. Reduced flow in it contributes to erectile dysfunction in Leriche
      syndrome.
