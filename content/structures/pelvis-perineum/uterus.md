anatomy:
  location: >-
    In the lesser pelvis, between the [[urinary-bladder]] (anteriorly) and the [[rectum]] (posteriorly), with the
    [[broad-ligament]] extending from each side to the pelvic wall. Usually **anteverted** (the cervix angled forward on
    the [[vagina]]) and **anteflexed** (the body bent forward on the cervix), so it rests on the bladder.
  parts:
    - "**Fundus**: above the openings of the [[uterine-tubes]]"
    - "**Body (corpus)**: the main part; the uterine cavity is triangular in coronal section"
    - "**Isthmus**: the narrow part between the body and cervix; becomes the **lower uterine segment** in late pregnancy (site of the low transverse cesarean incision)"
    - "**Cervix**: supravaginal part and vaginal part (portio) with the **external os**; cervical canal from the internal to the external os. The **transformation zone** (at the squamocolumnar junction) is where cervical dysplasia arises"
    - "**Wall**: endometrium (functional and basal layers), myometrium, perimetrium (peritoneum)"
    - "**Supports**: the [[pelvic-diaphragm]] and the endopelvic fascial ligaments — **cardinal (transverse cervical)** ligaments (carrying the uterine vessels), **uterosacral** and pubocervical ligaments. The round ligament and broad ligament give little support"
  bloodSupply: >-
    **[[uterine-artery|Uterine arteries]]** (anterior division of the [[internal-iliac-artery]]), anastomosing with the
    ovarian arteries at the uterotubal junction.
  venousDrainage: Uterine venous plexus in the broad ligament → uterine veins → internal iliac veins.
  lymphatics: >-
    Fundus and upper body → **lumbar (para-aortic)** nodes along the ovarian vessels; some along the round ligament to
    the **superficial inguinal** nodes. Body → external iliac nodes. **Cervix** → internal and external iliac
    (obturator) and sacral nodes. See [[pelvic-lymph-nodes]].
  innervation: >-
    Uterovaginal plexus (from the inferior hypogastric plexus). Pain from the uterine body (above the pelvic pain line)
    travels with sympathetic fibers to about **T10–L1** (first-stage labor pain); pain from the cervix travels with the
    pelvic splanchnic nerves (**S2–S4**). See [[pelvic-autonomic-nerves]].
  relations: >-
    Anteriorly the vesicouterine pouch; posteriorly the [[rectouterine-pouch]]. The [[ureter]] passes under the uterine
    artery about 1–2 cm lateral to the supravaginal cervix ("water under the bridge").
clinical:
  - title: Leiomyoma (fibroids)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Benign, estrogen-sensitive smooth muscle tumors of the myometrium; the most common pelvic tumor in women, more common in Black women; shrink after menopause.
    presentation: >-
      Often asymptomatic. **Heavy menstrual bleeding**, pelvic pressure, urinary frequency, constipation, infertility.
      Exam: **enlarged, irregular, firm** uterus. Diagnosed by pelvic ultrasound.
  - title: Endometrial cancer
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Most commonly endometrioid adenocarcinoma driven by **unopposed estrogen** — obesity, nulliparity, early menarche/late menopause, tamoxifen, chronic anovulation (PCOS), Lynch syndrome.
    presentation: >-
      **Postmenopausal bleeding** (any bleeding after menopause must be evaluated). Evaluation: **transvaginal
      ultrasound** (endometrial thickness ≤ 4 mm is reassuring) and/or **endometrial biopsy**.
  - title: Cervical cancer and screening
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Persistent high-risk **HPV** (types 16 and 18) infection of the transformation zone → dysplasia (CIN) → squamous cell carcinoma (most) or adenocarcinoma.
    presentation: >-
      Early disease is asymptomatic; later postcoital bleeding, discharge, pelvic pain. Screening (USPSTF 2018):
      cytology every 3 years at **21–29**; at **30–65**, cytology every 3 years, primary hrHPV testing every 5 years, or
      co-testing every 5 years. HPV vaccination prevents
      most cases.
  - title: Postpartum hemorrhage
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Uterine atony** is the most common cause (the "4 Ts": tone, trauma, tissue, thrombin). Contraction of the myometrium normally compresses the spiral arteries ("living ligatures").'
    presentation: >-
      Excessive bleeding after delivery with a soft, boggy uterus. Management: **bimanual uterine massage**, oxytocin,
      then other uterotonics (methylergonovine — avoid in hypertension; carboprost — avoid in asthma), tranexamic acid,
      then procedures (balloon tamponade, uterine artery embolization or ligation, hysterectomy).
  - title: Adenomyosis
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Endometrial glands and stroma within the myometrium.
    presentation: Dysmenorrhea and heavy menstrual bleeding in parous women in their 40s; **symmetrically enlarged, boggy, tender** uterus.
pance:
  - "Postmenopausal bleeding → TVUS and/or **endometrial biopsy** to exclude endometrial cancer."
  - "Enlarged **irregular** uterus = fibroids; **symmetric boggy tender** uterus = adenomyosis."
  - "Most common cause of postpartum hemorrhage = **uterine atony** → massage + oxytocin."
  - "Cervical cancer = HPV 16/18; screening starts at **21**."
  - "Fundal height: at the **umbilicus at ~20 weeks**; in cm ≈ weeks of gestation from about 20–36 weeks."
imaging:
  ultrasound: >-
    **Transvaginal ultrasound** is first-line: uterine size, fibroids, endometrial thickness, adenomyosis, early
    pregnancy. **Saline infusion sonohysterography** shows polyps and submucosal fibroids.
  mri: Maps fibroids before surgery or embolization, and characterizes adenomyosis; stages endometrial and cervical cancer.
  xray: '**Hysterosalpingography (HSG)** shows the uterine cavity (congenital anomalies, synechiae) and tubal patency.'
  keyViews:
    - "Transvaginal ultrasound (sagittal and transverse)"
    - "MRI pelvis (T2) for fibroid mapping and cancer staging"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    The non-pregnant uterus is not palpable abdominally. In pregnancy the fundus reaches the **pubic symphysis at about
    12 weeks** and the **umbilicus at about 20 weeks**; fundal height in cm approximates gestational age from about 20
    to 36 weeks.
  palpation: >-
    **Bimanual exam**: size (in "weeks" equivalent), shape (regular or irregular), consistency, mobility, position
    (anteverted or retroverted) and tenderness. **Cervical motion tenderness** suggests PID or ectopic pregnancy.
  testing: Speculum exam to inspect the cervix and take cervical cytology/HPV samples.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 58-year-old woman, 6 years postmenopausal, has had two episodes of light vaginal bleeding. BMI is 38. What is
      the most appropriate next step?
    choices:
      - Reassurance
      - Start estrogen therapy
      - Transvaginal ultrasound and/or endometrial biopsy
      - Pap smear only
      - Repeat exam in 12 months
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Postmenopausal bleeding** must be evaluated for **endometrial cancer**, especially with obesity (peripheral
      estrogen from adipose tissue). Transvaginal ultrasound (endometrial thickness) and/or **endometrial biopsy** are
      the initial tests.
  - stem: >-
      Twenty minutes after a vaginal delivery, a woman has heavy bleeding. The uterus is soft and boggy above the
      umbilicus. What is the most appropriate first step?
    choices:
      - Hysterectomy
      - Bimanual uterine massage and oxytocin
      - Uterine artery embolization
      - Methylergonovine despite hypertension
      - Observation
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A boggy uterus indicates **uterine atony**, the most common cause of postpartum hemorrhage. First-line treatment
      is **bimanual massage** and **oxytocin**; other uterotonics and procedures follow if bleeding continues.
  - stem: Lymph from the uterine fundus drains mainly to which nodes?
    choices:
      - Superficial inguinal
      - Internal iliac
      - Lumbar (para-aortic)
      - Sacral
      - Deep inguinal
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The fundus and upper body drain along the **ovarian vessels to the lumbar (para-aortic) nodes**. Some fundal lymph
      follows the round ligament to the superficial inguinal nodes, and the cervix drains to the iliac and sacral nodes.
  - stem: >-
      A 44-year-old woman who has had three children has worsening dysmenorrhea and heavy menstrual bleeding. The uterus
      is symmetrically enlarged, boggy and tender. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Leiomyoma
      - Adenomyosis
      - Endometrial cancer
      - Cervical cancer
      - Ectopic pregnancy
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Adenomyosis** (endometrial glands and stroma within the myometrium) causes dysmenorrhea and heavy bleeding in
      parous women in their 40s, with a **symmetrically enlarged, boggy, tender** uterus. Fibroids give an **enlarged,
      irregular, firm** uterus.
  - stem: At about how many weeks of gestation does the uterine fundus reach the level of the umbilicus?
    choices:
      - 12 weeks
      - 16 weeks
      - 20 weeks
      - 28 weeks
      - 36 weeks
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The fundus reaches the **umbilicus at about 20 weeks**. From about 20 to 36 weeks, fundal height in centimeters
      roughly equals the weeks of gestation.
