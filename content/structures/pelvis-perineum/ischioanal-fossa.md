name: Ischioanal fossa
type: space
region: pelvis-perineum
subregion: Perineum
taName: Fossa ischioanalis
aka: [Ischiorectal fossa]
summary: Paired fat-filled wedge-shaped spaces on either side of the anal canal. The pudendal canal lies in their lateral wall, and anorectal abscesses spread through them, sometimes in a horseshoe pattern.
tags: [perineum, abscess]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: In the anal triangle of the [[perineum]], between the [[anal-canal]] and the ischium on each side.
  boundaries:
    lateral: Ischium and obturator internus with its fascia (containing the **pudendal canal**)
    medial: External anal sphincter and the sloping levator ani
    apex (superior): Where levator ani arises from the obturator fascia
    floor (inferior): Perianal skin and superficial fascia
    anterior: Posterior edge of the perineal membrane (the anterior recesses extend forward above it)
    posterior: Sacrotuberous ligament and gluteus maximus
  contents:
    - "**Fat** (the fat body of the ischioanal fossa), which lets the anal canal distend during defecation"
    - "**Pudendal canal** in the lateral wall, containing the [[pudendal-nerve]] and internal pudendal vessels"
    - "**Inferior rectal nerves and vessels**, crossing from the pudendal canal medially to the anal canal"
    - "Perineal branches of the posterior femoral cutaneous nerve and the perforating cutaneous nerve (S2–S3)"
  communications: >-
    The right and left fossae **communicate behind the anal canal** (deep postanal space, above the anococcygeal body),
    so infection can spread from one side to the other (**horseshoe abscess**). Anterior recesses extend forward above
    the perineal membrane.
clinical:
  - title: Ischioanal (ischiorectal) abscess
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Spread of a cryptoglandular infection from an anal gland through the external sphincter into the fossa (or from a perianal abscess).
    presentation: >-
      Deep throbbing buttock/perianal pain, fever, and a tender indurated or fluctuant area lateral to the anus
      (external signs may be subtle because the fossa is deep). Treat with prompt **incision and drainage**; antibiotics
      are added for cellulitis, immunosuppression or diabetes.
  - title: Fistula-in-ano
    highYield: true
    mechanism: A persistent tract between an anal gland at the pectinate line and the perianal skin after an abscess; associated with Crohn disease when complex or multiple.
    presentation: >-
      Chronic intermittent discharge from a perianal opening. **Goodsall rule**: an external opening **anterior** to a
      transverse line through the anus usually has a **straight radial** tract; one **posterior** to it usually curves
      to the **posterior midline**. Treated surgically (fistulotomy, seton), taking care to preserve continence.
pance:
  - "Perianal pain + fever + fluctuance → **incision and drainage** (antibiotics alone are not enough)."
  - "Goodsall rule: anterior openings → straight tracts; posterior openings → curve to the posterior midline."
  - "Horseshoe abscess = spread between the two ischioanal fossae behind the anal canal."
  - "Complex or recurrent fistulas → think **Crohn disease**."
imaging:
  ct: CT pelvis locates deep abscesses (ischioanal, supralevator).
  mri: '**MRI (fistula protocol)** is the gold standard for mapping complex fistula tracts and horseshoe extensions.'
  ultrasound: Endoanal ultrasound (with hydrogen peroxide enhancement) shows fistula tracts relative to the sphincters.
  keyViews:
    - "MRI pelvis fistula protocol (axial and coronal STIR/T2)"
exam:
  landmarks: The fossa lies between the anus and the ischial tuberosity on each side.
  palpation: Inspection and palpation of the buttock and perianal skin for induration and fluctuance; DRE for tenderness or a mass lateral to the anal canal and for an internal opening.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 40-year-old man with diabetes has 4 days of throbbing left buttock pain and fever. There is a tender, indurated,
      fluctuant area 3 cm lateral to the anus. What is the most appropriate management?
    choices:
      - Oral antibiotics and sitz baths only
      - Incision and drainage
      - Hemorrhoidectomy
      - Topical nitroglycerin
      - Colonoscopy before any treatment
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      This is an **ischioanal abscess**. Definitive treatment is **incision and drainage**; antibiotics are an adjunct
      in patients with diabetes, immunosuppression or cellulitis.
  - stem: Which structure runs in the lateral wall of the ischioanal fossa?
    choices:
      - Inferior mesenteric vein
      - Pudendal canal with the pudendal nerve and internal pudendal vessels
      - Sciatic nerve
      - Obturator nerve
      - Superior rectal artery
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **pudendal (Alcock) canal** is a split in the obturator internus fascia on the lateral wall of the ischioanal
      fossa; it carries the pudendal nerve and internal pudendal vessels.
