name: Sacroiliac joint
type: joint
region: pelvis-perineum
subregion: Bony pelvis
taName: Articulatio sacroiliaca
aka: [SI joint]
summary: Strong, nearly immobile joint between the sacrum and ilium that transmits body weight to the pelvis. It is the classic site of inflammatory sacroiliitis in axial spondyloarthritis.
tags: [pelvis, spondyloarthritis]
systems: [musculoskeletal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Between the auricular surfaces of the [[sacrum]] and the ilium, deep to the PSIS on each side.
  classification: >-
    Compound joint: a **synovial** (plane) joint anteriorly between the auricular surfaces, and a **syndesmosis**
    posteriorly (interosseous ligament between the tuberosities).
  bones: "[[sacrum]] and the ilium of the [[hip-bone]]"
  capsule: Thin anteriorly; the articular surfaces are irregular and interlocking, with hyaline cartilage (thicker on the sacral side) and fibrocartilage.
  ligaments:
    - "**Interosseous sacroiliac ligament**: the strongest; fills the space between the sacral and iliac tuberosities"
    - "**Posterior sacroiliac ligaments**: posterior to the interosseous ligament"
    - "**Anterior sacroiliac ligament**: thin, part of the anterior capsule"
    - "**Iliolumbar ligament**: L5 transverse process to the iliac crest"
    - "Accessory: [[sacrotuberous-and-sacrospinous-ligaments|sacrotuberous and sacrospinous ligaments]], which resist upward tilting of the lower sacrum"
  movements:
    - "Very limited gliding and rotation (**nutation** and counternutation of the sacrum), mainly during pregnancy and changes of posture"
  stability: >-
    Body weight tends to drive the sacrum downward and rotate its upper end forward; the interlocking surfaces, the
    interosseous and posterior ligaments and the sacrotuberous/sacrospinous ligaments resist this. **Relaxin** in
    pregnancy loosens the ligaments.
  innervation: "Mainly lateral branches of the dorsal rami of about L5–S3 posteriorly, with anterior contributions from ventral rami {{verify: reported innervation varies (L2/L4–S3)}}"
  bloodSupply: Iliolumbar, superior gluteal and lateral sacral arteries (branches of the [[internal-iliac-artery]]).
  relations: >-
    Anterior: the psoas and the lumbosacral trunk, obturator nerve and internal iliac vessels cross the pelvic brim
    nearby; the ureter crosses the pelvic brim just anterior to the joint. Posterior: erector spinae and gluteus maximus.
clinical:
  - title: Sacroiliitis in axial spondyloarthritis (ankylosing spondylitis)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Immune-mediated enthesitis and synovitis of the SI joints, strongly associated with **HLA-B27**. Also seen with
      IBD-associated, psoriatic and reactive arthritis (these are more often asymmetric).
    presentation: >-
      **Inflammatory back pain** in a young adult (onset before about 40): insidious, lasting over 3 months, **morning
      stiffness over 30 minutes**, night pain, **better with exercise and not with rest**; alternating buttock pain.
      Extra-articular: anterior uveitis, enthesitis (Achilles), dactylitis, reduced chest expansion.
    notes: >-
      First-line treatment is **NSAIDs** plus exercise and physical therapy; **TNF inhibitors** (or IL-17 inhibitors)
      if symptoms persist. Methotrexate does not help axial disease.
  - title: Sacroiliac joint dysfunction
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Mechanical pain from the joint or its ligaments, e.g. after pregnancy, falls onto the buttock, or leg-length discrepancy.
    presentation: Unilateral pain below L5 over the PSIS (the patient points to it with one finger — the Fortin finger test), sometimes radiating to the buttock or posterior thigh but not below the knee.
  - title: Septic sacroiliitis
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Hematogenous infection (Staphylococcus aureus most often), with risk factors such as injection drug use.
    presentation: Fever, buttock or low back pain, limp; easily mistaken for a disc or hip problem. MRI is the key test.
pance:
  - "Young man, chronic low back pain with **morning stiffness better with exercise**, bilateral sacroiliitis → **ankylosing spondylitis** (HLA-B27)."
  - "AS: first-line **NSAIDs**; then **TNF inhibitors**."
  - "AS extra-articular association to remember: **anterior uveitis**."
  - "**MRI (STIR)** detects early sacroiliitis before X-ray changes."
  - "FABER with posterior (SI) pain vs groin pain (hip joint)."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    AP pelvis or angled AP (**Ferguson view**) of the SI joints: erosions, "pseudo-widening", sclerosis, then
    ankylosis, usually **bilateral and symmetric** in AS. Changes may take years to appear. Late spine: squaring of
    vertebral bodies, syndesmophytes, "bamboo spine".
  ct: Best for bony erosions and ankylosis; also used to guide SI joint injections.
  mri: >-
    **STIR/T2 fat-suppressed MRI** shows **bone marrow edema** (active sacroiliitis) before radiographic damage, and
    is the test of choice for suspected early axial spondyloarthritis or septic sacroiliitis.
  keyViews:
    - "AP pelvis or Ferguson view of the SI joints"
    - "MRI of the SI joints (STIR)"
exam:
  landmarks: The SI joint lies deep to the **PSIS** (the "dimples of Venus", at about S2).
  palpation: Tenderness over the sacral sulcus just medial to the PSIS.
  testing: >-
    **Schober test** for lumbar flexion in suspected AS: mark the skin at the level of the dimples of Venus and 10 cm
    above; an increase of less than 5 cm on full forward flexion suggests restricted lumbar motion. Chest expansion is
    also reduced in advanced AS.
  specialTests:
    - name: FABER (Patrick) test
      technique: Supine; the tested leg is placed in **F**lexion, **AB**duction and **E**xternal **R**otation (figure-4, heel on the opposite knee). The examiner presses the knee down while stabilizing the opposite ASIS.
      positive: Pain in the posterior pelvis/SI region (SI joint), or in the groin (hip joint).
      significance: Screens for SI joint and hip pathology; not specific.
    - name: Gaenslen test
      technique: Supine at the edge of the table; one hip is fully flexed toward the chest while the other leg is lowered over the edge into hyperextension.
      positive: Pain in the SI joint.
      significance: SI joint provocation test.
    - name: Thigh thrust (posterior shear) test
      technique: Supine with the hip flexed to 90°; the examiner applies an axial force along the femur toward the table while supporting the sacrum.
      positive: Reproduction of the patient's buttock pain.
      significance: >-
        One of the more sensitive single SI provocation tests. Several positive provocation tests together make SI
        joint pain more likely. {{verify: diagnostic accuracy of provocation-test clusters}}
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 24-year-old man has 8 months of low back and alternating buttock pain. He is stiff for over an hour each morning
      and feels better after exercise. He once had a painful red eye with photophobia. Radiographs are normal. What is
      the best next imaging study?
    choices:
      - CT of the lumbar spine
      - MRI of the sacroiliac joints
      - Bone scan
      - Repeat radiographs in 5 years
      - Myelography
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      This is inflammatory back pain with a history suggesting **anterior uveitis**, pointing to **axial
      spondyloarthritis**. Radiographic sacroiliitis can lag for years; **MRI of the SI joints** shows active bone
      marrow edema early (non-radiographic axial SpA).
  - stem: What is the first-line drug treatment for ankylosing spondylitis?
    choices:
      - Methotrexate
      - Oral glucocorticoids
      - NSAIDs
      - Sulfasalazine
      - Hydroxychloroquine
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **NSAIDs** (with exercise and physical therapy) are first-line and can reduce inflammatory pain. Persistent
      disease is treated with **TNF inhibitors** or IL-17 inhibitors. Conventional DMARDs such as methotrexate and
      sulfasalazine do not help axial disease, and systemic steroids are not used long term.
  - stem: Which is the strongest ligament of the sacroiliac joint?
    choices:
      - Anterior sacroiliac ligament
      - Interosseous sacroiliac ligament
      - Iliolumbar ligament
      - Sacrospinous ligament
      - Inguinal ligament
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **interosseous sacroiliac ligament**, which forms the posterior syndesmotic part of the joint, is the
      strongest and main load-bearing ligament. The anterior ligament is thin.
