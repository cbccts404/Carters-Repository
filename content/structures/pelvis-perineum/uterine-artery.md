name: Uterine artery
type: artery
region: pelvis-perineum
subregion: Vessels, nerves & lymphatics
taName: Arteria uterina
summary: Branch of the internal iliac artery that crosses above the ureter beside the cervix to supply the uterus. It is ligated in hysterectomy, embolized for fibroids and postpartum hemorrhage, and greatly enlarged in pregnancy.
tags: [female, obstetrics, gynecology, vascular]
systems: [reproductive, cardiovascular]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Base of the [[broad-ligament]] (in the cardinal ligament) and then along the lateral margin of the [[uterus]].
  origin: Anterior division of the [[internal-iliac-artery]] (sometimes sharing a trunk with the vaginal or umbilical artery).
  course: >-
    Runs medially across the pelvic floor in the base of the broad ligament and passes **above and in front of the
    [[ureter]]** about 1–2 cm lateral to the supravaginal cervix ("water under the bridge"). It reaches the uterus at
    about the level of the internal os, then ascends **tortuously** along the lateral uterus to the uterine horn.
  branches:
    - "**Vaginal (cervicovaginal) branches** to the cervix and upper vagina"
    - "**Arcuate arteries** in the myometrium → **radial arteries** → **spiral arteries** (to the functional layer of the endometrium, shed at menstruation) and basal arteries"
    - "**Tubal** and **ovarian** branches, which anastomose with the ovarian artery"
  supplies: The uterus, cervix, upper [[vagina]], medial part of the [[uterine-tubes|uterine tube]] and part of the ovary.
  anastomoses: With the **ovarian artery** (from the aorta) near the uterotubal junction; with the vaginal arteries below.
  termination: Ends by anastomosing with the ovarian artery near the uterine horn.
  notes: >-
    In pregnancy the uterine arteries enlarge enormously, and trophoblast remodels the spiral arteries into low-resistance
    vessels; failure of this remodeling is linked to **preeclampsia** and fetal growth restriction. Uterine blood flow
    at term is on the order of 500–750 mL/min, which is why postpartum hemorrhage can be torrential. {{verify: term uterine blood flow estimate}}
clinical:
  - title: Hysterectomy and the ureter
    highYield: true
    mechanism: The uterine vessels are clamped next to the cervix, where the ureter passes beneath them.
    presentation: Clamping, ligation or thermal injury of the ureter → hydronephrosis, urinoma, fistula. Surgeons identify the ureter before clamping.
  - title: Uterine artery embolization
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Catheter-directed embolization through the internal iliac arteries.
    presentation: Treats symptomatic fibroids and postpartum hemorrhage while preserving the uterus. Post-embolization pain and fever are common.
  - title: Uterine artery Doppler in pregnancy
    highYield: false
    mechanism: High resistance and an early diastolic "notch" reflect poor spiral artery remodeling.
    presentation: Used in some settings to predict preeclampsia and fetal growth restriction.
pance:
  - "**Water under the bridge**: uterine artery passes over the ureter beside the cervix."
  - "Spiral arteries supply the functional endometrium; their poor remodeling → **preeclampsia**."
  - "Fibroids and PPH can be treated by **uterine artery embolization**."
imaging:
  ultrasound: Color Doppler shows the uterine arteries lateral to the cervix; pulsatility index and notching are assessed in pregnancy.
  ct: CT angiography locates active pelvic bleeding.
  xray: Catheter angiography for embolization.
exam:
  landmarks: Reaches the uterus about 1–2 cm lateral to the cervix at the level of the internal os; not palpable.
quiz:
  - stem: The uterine artery is a branch of which artery?
    choices:
      - Abdominal aorta
      - External iliac artery
      - Anterior division of the internal iliac artery
      - Inferior mesenteric artery
      - Internal pudendal artery
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **uterine artery** comes from the **anterior division of the internal iliac artery**. The ovarian artery, with
      which it anastomoses, comes from the abdominal aorta.
  - stem: Which vessels supply the functional layer of the endometrium that is shed during menstruation?
    choices:
      - Arcuate arteries
      - Radial arteries
      - Spiral arteries
      - Straight (basal) arteries
      - Ovarian arteries
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Spiral arteries** supply the functional layer; their constriction at the end of the luteal phase causes
      ischemia and shedding. Straight (basal) arteries supply the basal layer, which persists.
