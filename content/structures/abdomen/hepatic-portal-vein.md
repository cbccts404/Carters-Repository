---
name: Hepatic portal vein
type: vein
region: abdomen
subregion: Accessory organs
taName: Vena portae hepatis
aka: [Portal vein, Portal system, Portosystemic anastomoses]
summary: Carries nutrient-rich venous blood from the GI tract, spleen and pancreas to the liver. In portal hypertension its anastomoses with systemic veins cause varices, caput medusae and rectal varices.
tags: [vascular, hepatobiliary, portal-hypertension]
systems: [gastrointestinal, cardiovascular]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Formed behind the neck of the [[pancreas]]; ascends in the free edge of the hepatoduodenal ligament to the porta hepatis.
  formation: >-
    Union of the **superior mesenteric vein** and the **splenic vein** behind the neck of the pancreas (at about L1–L2).
    The **inferior mesenteric vein** usually drains into the splenic vein (sometimes into the SMV or the confluence).
  course: >-
    Ascends behind the 1st part of the [[duodenum]] into the hepatoduodenal ligament, where it lies **posterior** to the
    common bile duct and hepatic artery proper. At the porta hepatis it divides into right and left branches.
  tributaries:
    - Superior mesenteric vein and splenic vein (formation)
    - Left and right gastric veins
    - Cystic veins, paraumbilical veins (via the ligamentum teres), posterior superior pancreaticoduodenal vein
  drainage: Liver sinusoids → hepatic veins → [[inferior-vena-cava]].
  notes: >-
    **Portosystemic anastomoses** (where portal and systemic veins meet; dilate in portal hypertension):
    **(1) Esophageal**: left gastric (portal) ↔ esophageal veins → azygos (systemic) → **esophageal varices**.
    **(2) Rectal**: superior rectal (portal) ↔ middle and inferior rectal (systemic) → **rectal varices** (distinct from
    hemorrhoids). **(3) Paraumbilical**: paraumbilical (portal) ↔ superficial and inferior epigastric (systemic) →
    **caput medusae**. **(4) Retroperitoneal**: colic and other visceral veins ↔ retroperitoneal/lumbar veins. The
    portal system has no functional valves, so flow can reverse.
clinical:
  - title: Portal hypertension
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Increased resistance to portal flow. **Prehepatic**: portal vein thrombosis. **Intrahepatic**: **cirrhosis** (most
      common), schistosomiasis. **Posthepatic**: Budd–Chiari, right heart failure, constrictive pericarditis.
    presentation: >-
      **Splenomegaly** (with thrombocytopenia), **ascites** (SAAG ≥ 1.1 g/dL), **esophageal/gastric varices**
      (life-threatening hematemesis), **caput medusae**, hepatic encephalopathy.
  - title: Variceal hemorrhage
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Rupture of dilated esophageal (or gastric) submucosal veins at the left gastric–azygos anastomosis.
    presentation: >-
      Massive hematemesis and shock in a cirrhotic patient. Resuscitate, give **octreotide** and **prophylactic
      antibiotics**, and perform urgent **endoscopic band ligation**. TIPS for refractory bleeding. Non-selective beta
      blockers and banding for prevention.
  - title: TIPS
    highYield: false
    mechanism: A transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt connects a portal vein branch to a hepatic vein inside the liver, lowering portal pressure.
    presentation: Used for refractory variceal bleeding or refractory ascites. Main complication is **hepatic encephalopathy** (portal blood bypasses the liver).
pance:
  - "Portal vein = **SMV + splenic vein** behind the neck of the pancreas."
  - "Anastomoses: **esophageal (varices)**, **rectal (rectal varices)**, **paraumbilical (caput medusae)**, retroperitoneal."
  - "Variceal bleed: **octreotide + antibiotics + endoscopic banding**; TIPS if refractory."
  - "Most common cause of portal hypertension: **cirrhosis**."
imaging:
  ct: Contrast CT (portal venous phase) shows portal vein thrombosis, collaterals and varices, splenomegaly and ascites.
  mri: MR venography as an alternative.
  ultrasound: >-
    **Doppler ultrasound** is first-line: portal vein patency, diameter, and **flow direction** (normally hepatopetal,
    toward the liver; **hepatofugal**, away from the liver, in advanced portal hypertension). Also splenomegaly,
    ascites and recanalized paraumbilical vein.
  keyViews:
    - "Doppler ultrasound of the portal vein"
    - "Endoscopy (EGD) to detect and treat varices"
exam:
  testing: >-
    Signs of portal hypertension: **caput medusae** (flow away from the umbilicus), splenomegaly, ascites (shifting
    dullness), and a venous hum over the umbilicus (Cruveilhier–Baumgarten) in some patients. Look for stigmata of
    chronic liver disease.
quiz:
  - stem: The hepatic portal vein is formed by the union of which two veins?
    choices:
      - Inferior mesenteric and splenic veins
      - Superior mesenteric and splenic veins
      - Superior mesenteric and inferior mesenteric veins
      - Left gastric and splenic veins
      - Hepatic veins and IVC
    answer: B
    explanation: The **superior mesenteric vein** and **splenic vein** join behind the neck of the pancreas. The IMV usually drains into the splenic vein.
  - stem: A patient with alcohol-related cirrhosis has massive hematemesis. The bleeding varices form at an anastomosis between which veins?
    choices:
      - Superior rectal and inferior rectal veins
      - Left gastric and esophageal veins draining to the azygos system
      - Paraumbilical and superficial epigastric veins
      - Splenic and left renal veins
      - Hepatic veins and IVC
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Esophageal varices** form where the **left gastric vein** (portal) meets **esophageal veins** that drain to the
      **azygos** (systemic). Treat with octreotide, antibiotics and endoscopic band ligation.
  - stem: What is the most common complication after placement of a TIPS?
    choices:
      - Esophageal varices
      - Hepatic encephalopathy
      - Portal vein thrombosis
      - Hepatocellular carcinoma
      - Ascites
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A TIPS diverts portal blood (with ammonia and other toxins) around the liver, so **hepatic encephalopathy** is the
      main complication.
  - stem: Caput medusae forms at the portosystemic anastomosis between which veins?
    choices:
      - Left gastric and esophageal veins
      - Paraumbilical and superficial epigastric veins
      - Superior rectal and inferior rectal veins
      - Splenic and left renal veins
      - Colic and lumbar veins
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Caput medusae** is dilation of the **paraumbilical veins** (portal, via the ligamentum teres) anastomosing with
      the **superficial and inferior epigastric veins** (systemic). The left gastric–esophageal anastomosis forms
      varices, and the superior–inferior rectal anastomosis forms rectal varices.
  - stem: >-
      In the hepatoduodenal ligament, where does the portal vein lie relative to the common bile duct and hepatic
      artery?
    choices:
      - Anterior to both
      - Between them
      - To the left of both
      - Outside the ligament entirely
      - Posterior to both
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      In the free edge of the hepatoduodenal ligament, the **portal vein lies posterior**, with the **common bile duct**
      to the right and the **hepatic artery proper** to the left in front of it.
