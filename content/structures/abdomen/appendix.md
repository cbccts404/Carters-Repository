name: Vermiform appendix
type: organ
region: abdomen
subregion: GI viscera
taName: Appendix vermiformis
aka: [Appendix]
summary: Blind-ending tube arising from the cecum where the teniae coli converge. Acute appendicitis is the most common abdominal surgical emergency.
tags: [gi, midgut]
systems: [gastrointestinal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Right iliac fossa. Its base is on the posteromedial cecum about 2 cm below the ileocecal valve, where the three **teniae coli converge** (a surgical guide to finding it). The tip is variable (most often **retrocecal**, also pelvic, subcecal, pre- or post-ileal).
  parts:
    - Base, body and tip; lumen rich in lymphoid tissue
    - "**Mesoappendix**: a peritoneal fold from the terminal ileum mesentery, containing the appendicular artery"
  relations: The base projects to **McBurney point**, one-third of the way from the right ASIS to the umbilicus. A retrocecal appendix lies against the psoas; a pelvic appendix against the obturator internus, bladder or rectum.
  bloodSupply: Appendicular artery, a branch of the ileocolic artery (from the [[superior-mesenteric-artery]]).
  venousDrainage: Appendicular vein → ileocolic vein → SMV → portal vein (portal pyelophlebitis and liver abscess are rare complications).
  innervation: Visceral afferents with sympathetic fibers to about **T10**, so early pain refers to the **periumbilical** region.
clinical:
  - title: Acute appendicitis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Luminal obstruction (fecalith, lymphoid hyperplasia, rarely tumor) → distension, ischemia, bacterial invasion, and perforation if untreated.
    presentation: >-
      **Periumbilical pain migrating to the RLQ**, then **anorexia**, nausea/vomiting (usually after the pain begins),
      and low-grade fever. **McBurney point tenderness**, rebound and guarding. Signs by position: **Rovsing**, **psoas**
      (retrocecal), **obturator** (pelvic). Leukocytosis. Presentation can be atypical in children, older adults and
      pregnancy (the appendix is displaced upward).
  - title: Appendiceal neoplasms
    highYield: false
    mechanism: '**Neuroendocrine (carcinoid) tumors** are among the most common appendiceal tumors, often found incidentally after appendectomy; also mucinous neoplasms.'
    presentation: Usually incidental. Mucinous tumors can cause pseudomyxoma peritonei.
pance:
  - "Appendicitis sequence: **periumbilical pain → RLQ**, anorexia, then vomiting."
  - "Signs: **McBurney**, **Rovsing** (LLQ pressure → RLQ pain), **psoas** (retrocecal), **obturator** (pelvic)."
  - "Imaging: **CT** in adults; **ultrasound first in children and pregnancy** (MRI if US non-diagnostic in pregnancy)."
  - "Alvarado score helps risk-stratify."
imaging:
  xray: Rarely helpful; an appendicolith may be visible.
  ct: >-
    **CT with IV contrast** is the most accurate test in adults: a **dilated appendix (more than about 6 mm)**, wall
    thickening and enhancement, periappendiceal fat stranding, appendicolith, abscess or perforation.
    {{verify: diameter thresholds vary}}
  mri: Preferred in **pregnancy** when ultrasound is non-diagnostic (no radiation).
  ultrasound: >-
    First-line in **children and pregnant patients** (graded compression): a **non-compressible**, blind-ending tubular
    structure larger than about 6 mm in diameter, a target appearance in cross-section, periappendiceal fluid, and
    focal tenderness.
  keyViews:
    - "Graded-compression ultrasound of the RLQ"
    - "CT abdomen/pelvis with IV contrast"
exam:
  landmarks: "**McBurney point**: one-third of the distance from the right ASIS to the umbilicus."
  palpation: Maximal tenderness at McBurney point; rebound tenderness and guarding with peritoneal involvement.
  specialTests:
    - name: Rovsing sign
      technique: Press deeply in the left lower quadrant.
      positive: Pain felt in the right lower quadrant.
      significance: Appendicitis (peritoneal irritation in the RLQ).
    - name: Psoas sign
      technique: Patient lies on the left side; the examiner passively extends the right hip (or the supine patient flexes the right hip against resistance).
      positive: RLQ pain.
      significance: Inflamed appendix against the psoas, typical of a **retrocecal** appendix. Also positive with psoas abscess.
    - name: Obturator sign
      technique: With the patient supine, flex the right hip and knee to 90° and passively internally rotate the hip.
      positive: Hypogastric or RLQ pain.
      significance: Inflammation adjacent to obturator internus, typical of a **pelvic** appendix.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 21-year-old woman has 18 hours of pain that began around the umbilicus and moved to the right lower quadrant,
      with anorexia and one episode of vomiting. Pressing in the left lower quadrant causes pain in the right lower
      quadrant. What is this sign called?
    choices:
      - Murphy sign
      - Rovsing sign
      - Psoas sign
      - Obturator sign
      - Carnett sign
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Rovsing sign** (LLQ pressure producing RLQ pain) supports **appendicitis**. Murphy sign is for cholecystitis;
      psoas and obturator signs suggest retrocecal and pelvic positions respectively.
  - stem: A 24-year-old pregnant woman at 20 weeks has right-sided abdominal pain and suspected appendicitis. What is the first imaging test?
    choices:
      - CT abdomen with contrast
      - Graded-compression ultrasound
      - Abdominal radiograph
      - HIDA scan
      - Barium enema
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      In **pregnancy** (and children), **ultrasound** is first. If it's non-diagnostic, **MRI** is preferred over CT to
      avoid radiation.
  - stem: Why does early appendicitis cause periumbilical pain?
    choices:
      - The appendix lies directly under the umbilicus
      - Visceral afferents from the appendix enter the spinal cord at about T10
      - The phrenic nerve supplies the appendix
      - The ilioinguinal nerve is compressed
      - The parietal peritoneum is inflamed first
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Visceral afferents from the appendix (midgut) enter at about **T10**, the dermatome of the umbilicus, so early pain
      is referred there before the parietal peritoneum is involved.
  - stem: A surgeon has difficulty finding the appendix. Which feature reliably leads to its base?
    choices:
      - The ileocecal valve
      - Convergence of the teniae coli on the cecum
      - The right colic artery
      - McBurney point on the peritoneal surface
      - The mesentery of the sigmoid colon
    answer: B
    explanation: The three **teniae coli converge** at the base of the appendix on the cecum, a reliable intraoperative guide.
