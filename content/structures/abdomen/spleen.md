summary: Intraperitoneal lymphoid organ in the left upper quadrant under ribs 9–11. It is the organ most often injured in blunt abdominal trauma, and asplenia predisposes to infection with encapsulated bacteria.
tags: [lymphoid, trauma]
systems: [hematologic]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Left upper quadrant (left hypochondrium), deep to the **9th–11th ribs**, with its long axis roughly along the **10th rib**. Normally not palpable.
  parts:
    - "Diaphragmatic and visceral surfaces; hilum (splenic vessels)"
    - "Notched superior border (can help identify an enlarged spleen on exam)"
    - "**Gastrosplenic ligament** (short gastric and left gastro-omental vessels) and **splenorenal ligament** (splenic vessels and the **tail of the pancreas**)"
  relations: >-
    Visceral surface contacts the stomach, left kidney, splenic (left colic) flexure and the tail of the [[pancreas]].
    Separated from the ribs by the diaphragm and pleural costodiaphragmatic recess.
  bloodSupply: '**Splenic artery**, the largest branch of the [[celiac-trunk]], running tortuously along the superior border of the pancreas.'
  venousDrainage: Splenic vein, joined by the inferior mesenteric vein, unites with the superior mesenteric vein behind the neck of the pancreas to form the [[hepatic-portal-vein]].
  lymphatics: Pancreaticosplenic nodes → celiac nodes.
  notes: >-
    **Functions**: filters blood (removes old or abnormal RBCs and inclusions), immune response to blood-borne antigens
    (especially encapsulated bacteria, through IgM and opsonization), and reservoir of platelets.
clinical:
  - title: Splenic injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Most commonly injured organ in blunt abdominal trauma**; also lower left rib fractures. Enlarged spleens (e.g. mononucleosis) rupture more easily.'
    presentation: >-
      LUQ pain, **Kehr sign** (left shoulder pain from diaphragmatic irritation), hypotension, positive FAST. **Unstable
      + positive FAST → laparotomy**. **Stable → CT** grading, often non-operative management or embolization.
  - title: Splenomegaly
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Portal hypertension (congestion), infection (**infectious mononucleosis**, malaria, endocarditis), hematologic malignancy (CML, myelofibrosis, lymphoma), hemolytic anemias, infiltrative disease.
    presentation: >-
      LUQ fullness, early satiety, cytopenias from hypersplenism. **Mononucleosis**: avoid contact sports until cleared,
      commonly for at least 3–4 weeks, because of rupture risk. {{verify: return-to-play timing recommendations vary}}
  - title: Asplenia and post-splenectomy infection
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Loss of splenic filtering and opsonization (splenectomy, sickle cell autosplenectomy).
    presentation: >-
      Risk of overwhelming sepsis from **encapsulated bacteria** (*Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae*
      type b, *Neisseria meningitidis*). **Howell–Jolly bodies** on smear. Vaccinate against these organisms (ideally
      ≥ 2 weeks before elective splenectomy) and counsel about urgent care for fever.

imaging:
  xray: Lower left rib fractures raise concern for splenic injury. An enlarged spleen can displace the gastric bubble and splenic flexure.
  ct: '**Contrast CT** grades splenic injury (laceration, hematoma, active extravasation) in stable trauma patients; shows splenomegaly and infarcts.'
  ultrasound: >-
    **FAST LUQ view** shows free fluid around the spleen and in the splenorenal recess. Ultrasound measures splenic
    length; roughly **more than 12–13 cm** suggests splenomegaly in adults. {{verify: size cut-offs vary}}
  keyViews:
    - "FAST (LUQ view)"
    - "CT abdomen with IV contrast (trauma)"
exam:
  landmarks: Lies under the left 9th–11th ribs posterior to the mid-axillary line. It enlarges toward the right lower quadrant (diagonally, toward the umbilicus).
  palpation: >-
    Start palpation in the **RLQ** and move toward the left costal margin as the patient breathes in (so a massively
    enlarged spleen isn't missed). The **right lateral decubitus** position helps bring the spleen forward. A normal
    spleen is not palpable.
  testing: >-
    **Percussion for splenomegaly**: **Traube space** (bounded by the 6th rib, the left costal margin and the anterior
    axillary line) is normally tympanic; dullness suggests splenomegaly. **Castell sign**: percuss the lowest left
    intercostal space in the anterior axillary line; dullness on full inspiration suggests enlargement.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      After a bicycle handlebar injury to the left upper abdomen, a 16-year-old is tachycardic with BP 82/50 and has left
      shoulder pain. FAST shows free fluid in the LUQ. What is the next step?
    choices:
      - CT abdomen with contrast
      - Exploratory laparotomy
      - Repeat FAST in 6 hours
      - Diagnostic peritoneal lavage
      - Observation with serial hemoglobin
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **hemodynamically unstable** patient with a **positive FAST** needs **laparotomy**. CT is reserved for stable
      patients. Left shoulder pain here is **Kehr sign**.
  - stem: A 19-year-old college athlete has fever, pharyngitis, posterior cervical lymphadenopathy and atypical lymphocytes. What advice is most important?
    choices:
      - Start amoxicillin
      - Avoid contact sports because of splenic rupture risk
      - Begin a daily aspirin regimen
      - Return to football immediately if afebrile
      - Undergo prophylactic splenectomy
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Infectious mononucleosis** causes splenomegaly with a risk of rupture, so the patient should **avoid contact
      sports** for several weeks. Amoxicillin is avoided (it causes a rash in EBV infection).
  - stem: Which organism is a major risk after splenectomy?
    choices:
      - Staphylococcus epidermidis
      - Streptococcus pneumoniae
      - Clostridioides difficile
      - Candida albicans
      - Pseudomonas aeruginosa
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Asplenic patients are at risk from **encapsulated bacteria**: *S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae* type b and *N.
      meningitidis*. They should be vaccinated against all three.
