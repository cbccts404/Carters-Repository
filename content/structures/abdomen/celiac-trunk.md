---
name: Celiac trunk
type: artery
region: abdomen
subregion: Posterior abdominal wall
taName: Truncus coeliacus
aka: [Celiac axis]
summary: Short anterior branch of the abdominal aorta at T12 and the artery of the foregut. It divides into the left gastric, splenic and common hepatic arteries.
tags: [vascular, foregut]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Arises from the anterior aorta just below the aortic hiatus, at about **T12**, above the pancreas.
  origin: "[[abdominal-aorta]]"
  course: A short trunk (1–2 cm) surrounded by the celiac plexus and nodes; it divides almost immediately.
  branches:
    - "**Left gastric artery**: along the lesser curvature of the [[stomach]]; esophageal branches"
    - "**Splenic artery**: tortuous course along the superior border of the [[pancreas]] to the [[spleen]]; gives pancreatic branches, **short gastric**, **left gastro-omental** and posterior gastric arteries"
    - "**Common hepatic artery** → **gastroduodenal artery** (→ right gastro-omental, superior pancreaticoduodenal) and **hepatic artery proper** (→ right gastric, right and left hepatic arteries; the cystic artery usually comes from the right hepatic)"
  supplies: >-
    **Foregut** derivatives: abdominal esophagus, stomach, **duodenum proximal to the major papilla**, liver,
    gallbladder, pancreas (with the SMA), and the spleen (a mesodermal organ in the foregut mesentery).
  anastomoses: Pancreaticoduodenal arcades connect it with the [[superior-mesenteric-artery]], an important collateral route in occlusive disease.
clinical:
  - title: Median arcuate ligament syndrome
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Compression of the celiac trunk by the median arcuate ligament of the diaphragm (worse in expiration).
    presentation: Postprandial epigastric pain, weight loss, and an epigastric bruit that varies with respiration, typically in young thin women. A diagnosis of exclusion.
  - title: Upper GI bleeding from celiac branches
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Ulcers eroding branches: posterior duodenal ulcer → **gastroduodenal artery**; lesser-curvature gastric ulcer →
      **left gastric artery**; posterior gastric ulcer → **splenic artery**.
    presentation: Hematemesis or melena, hemodynamic instability. Managed endoscopically or with angiographic embolization.
  - title: Splenic artery aneurysm
    highYield: false
    mechanism: The most common visceral artery aneurysm; more common in women, and associated with pregnancy and portal hypertension.
    presentation: Usually asymptomatic. **Rupture in pregnancy** carries high maternal and fetal mortality.
pance:
  - "Celiac trunk (T12) → **left gastric, splenic, common hepatic**."
  - "Foregut = esophagus (abdominal) to the proximal duodenum, liver, gallbladder, pancreas (+ spleen)."
  - "Posterior duodenal ulcer bleeds from the **gastroduodenal artery**."
imaging:
  ct: CT angiography shows celiac stenosis (a characteristic hooked narrowing in median arcuate ligament syndrome), aneurysms and variant anatomy.
  ultrasound: Duplex shows elevated celiac velocities in stenosis; in median arcuate ligament syndrome velocities increase in expiration.
  keyViews:
    - "CT angiography (sagittal view of the celiac and SMA origins)"
    - "Catheter angiography for embolization of bleeding branches"
exam:
  testing: Listen for an epigastric bruit (celiac or SMA stenosis). Localize foregut pain to the epigastrium.
quiz:
  - stem: Which of the following is NOT a direct branch of the celiac trunk?
    choices:
      - Left gastric artery
      - Splenic artery
      - Common hepatic artery
      - Gastroduodenal artery
      - None; all are direct branches
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      The celiac trunk gives the **left gastric, splenic and common hepatic** arteries. The **gastroduodenal artery** comes
      from the common hepatic artery.
  - stem: At approximately what vertebral level does the celiac trunk arise?
    choices:
      - T8
      - T10
      - T12
      - L3
      - L4
    answer: C
    explanation: The celiac trunk arises at **T12**, the SMA at L1, and the IMA at L3.
  - stem: The short gastric arteries arise from which vessel?
    choices:
      - Left gastric artery
      - Splenic artery
      - Gastroduodenal artery
      - Right gastric artery
      - Superior mesenteric artery
    answer: B
    explanation: The **short gastric arteries** (supplying the fundus) and the left gastro-omental artery are branches of the **splenic artery**.
