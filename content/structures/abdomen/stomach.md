summary: J-shaped dilated foregut organ in the upper abdomen, supplied by all three branches of the celiac trunk. Peptic ulcers, gastric cancer and pyloric stenosis are its key clinical problems.
tags: [gi, foregut]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Mainly in the left upper quadrant, epigastric and umbilical regions. Position varies with body habitus and filling.
  parts:
    - "**Cardia** (around the gastroesophageal junction), **fundus** (above the cardiac notch), **body**, **pyloric antrum**, **pyloric canal**, **pylorus** (pyloric sphincter, at about the transpyloric plane, L1)"
    - "**Lesser curvature** (with the angular incisure) and **greater curvature**"
    - "Gastric folds (rugae); physiological lower esophageal sphincter at the GE junction"
  relations: >-
    Anterior: left lobe of the liver, diaphragm, anterior abdominal wall. **Posterior (the "stomach bed")**, separated by
    the [[lesser-sac]]: [[pancreas]], left kidney and suprarenal gland, [[spleen]], splenic artery, transverse mesocolon.
    The [[greater-omentum]] hangs from the greater curvature; the lesser omentum runs from the lesser curvature to the
    [[liver]].
  bloodSupply: >-
    All from the [[celiac-trunk]]. **Lesser curvature**: left gastric artery (direct branch of the celiac trunk) and
    right gastric artery (usually from the proper or common hepatic artery). **Greater curvature**: right gastro-omental
    (from the gastroduodenal) and left gastro-omental (from the splenic). **Fundus**: short gastric arteries (from the
    splenic), plus the posterior gastric artery.
  venousDrainage: Gastric veins to the [[hepatic-portal-vein]] system. The left gastric vein anastomoses with esophageal veins (esophageal varices in portal hypertension).
  lymphatics: Gastric and gastro-omental nodes → celiac nodes. Spread can reach the left supraclavicular node (Virchow).
  innervation: >-
    **Parasympathetic**: anterior and posterior vagal trunks (acid secretion, motility). **Sympathetic**: T6–T9 via the
    greater splanchnic nerves and celiac plexus. Visceral pain refers to the **epigastrium** (foregut).
clinical:
  - title: Peptic ulcer disease
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Mucosal injury, most often from ***Helicobacter pylori*** and **NSAIDs** (also Zollinger–Ellison syndrome, smoking).
      Duodenal ulcers are more common than gastric ulcers.
    presentation: >-
      Burning epigastric pain. Classically, **gastric ulcer pain worsens with meals** and **duodenal ulcer pain improves
      with meals** (and occurs at night). Complications: bleeding (a lesser-curvature ulcer can erode the **left gastric
      artery**), perforation (free air), and gastric outlet obstruction. Gastric ulcers need biopsy to exclude cancer.
  - title: Gastric adenocarcinoma
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Risk factors include *H. pylori*, smoked/salted foods, pernicious anemia/atrophic gastritis, and prior partial gastrectomy.
    presentation: >-
      Weight loss, early satiety, epigastric pain, anemia. Signs of spread: **Virchow node** (left supraclavicular),
      **Sister Mary Joseph nodule** (periumbilical), **Krukenberg tumor** (ovary), Blumer shelf (rectouterine pouch).
  - title: Hypertrophic pyloric stenosis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Hypertrophy of the pyloric muscle in early infancy (typically 3–6 weeks old, more common in first-born males).
    presentation: >-
      **Projectile, non-bilious vomiting** after feeds with a hungry infant, a palpable **"olive"** in the epigastrium,
      visible peristalsis, and **hypochloremic, hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis**. Treated with pyloromyotomy after
      correcting fluids and electrolytes.
pance:
  - "Gastric ulcer: pain **worse** with food. Duodenal ulcer: pain **better** with food; more common."
  - "PUD causes: ***H. pylori*** and **NSAIDs**; test and treat *H. pylori*."
  - "Pyloric stenosis: 3–6-week-old, projectile non-bilious vomiting, olive, **hypochloremic hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis**; **ultrasound** to diagnose."
  - "Gastric cancer signs: Virchow node, Sister Mary Joseph nodule, Krukenberg tumor."
  - "Stomach blood supply is entirely from the **celiac trunk**."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    Upright CXR for free air after perforation. Upper GI contrast series shows ulcers, masses, outlet obstruction, and in
    infants the "string sign" of pyloric stenosis (ultrasound is preferred).
  ct: Gastric wall thickening or mass, perforation, cancer staging.
  ultrasound: >-
    **First-line for pyloric stenosis**: a thickened, elongated pyloric channel (quoted thresholds around 3–4 mm muscle
    thickness and about 15 mm or more channel length). {{verify: thresholds vary between sources}}
  keyViews:
    - "Upper endoscopy (EGD) with biopsy: the diagnostic procedure for ulcers and cancer"
    - "Pyloric ultrasound in infants"
exam:
  landmarks: The stomach projects to the left upper quadrant and epigastrium. The pylorus lies near the transpyloric plane (L1), just right of the midline.
  palpation: Epigastric tenderness in PUD. In infants, palpate for the olive in the epigastrium after the stomach is emptied.
  testing: >-
    A **succussion splash** (sloshing heard with a stethoscope while rocking the patient) more than a few hours after a
    meal suggests gastric outlet obstruction.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 4-week-old first-born boy has projectile non-bilious vomiting after every feed and is eager to feed again. Which
      metabolic abnormality is expected?
    choices:
      - Hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis
      - Hypochloremic, hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis
      - Hyperkalemic metabolic acidosis
      - Respiratory acidosis
      - Hypernatremic metabolic acidosis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Hypertrophic pyloric stenosis** causes loss of gastric HCl through vomiting, producing a **hypochloremic,
      hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis**. Ultrasound confirms the diagnosis.
  - stem: A patient with a bleeding ulcer on the lesser curvature of the stomach is most likely bleeding from which artery?
    choices:
      - Gastroduodenal artery
      - Left gastric artery
      - Short gastric arteries
      - Right gastro-omental artery
      - Splenic artery
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Lesser-curvature ulcers typically erode the **left gastric artery**. Posterior duodenal ulcers erode the
      **gastroduodenal artery**.
  - stem: A 45-year-old man has epigastric pain that improves after eating and wakes him at night. Which is the most likely cause?
    choices:
      - Gastric ulcer
      - Duodenal ulcer
      - Acute pancreatitis
      - Biliary colic
      - Esophageal spasm
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Duodenal ulcer** pain classically improves with meals (food buffers acid) and occurs at night. Gastric ulcer
      pain typically worsens with eating.
  - stem: Which branch of the celiac trunk supplies the lesser curvature of the stomach directly?
    choices:
      - Splenic artery
      - Common hepatic artery
      - Left gastric artery
      - Gastroduodenal artery
      - Superior mesenteric artery
    answer: C
    explanation: The **left gastric artery** arises directly from the celiac trunk and runs along the lesser curvature, anastomosing with the right gastric artery.
