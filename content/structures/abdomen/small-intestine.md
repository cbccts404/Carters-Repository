summary: Coiled intraperitoneal small bowel on a mesentery, supplied by the SMA. Small bowel obstruction, Meckel diverticulum, Crohn disease and mesenteric ischemia are its key clinical problems.
tags: [gi, midgut]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Central and lower abdomen. Jejunum mostly in the left upper quadrant; ileum mostly in the right lower quadrant and pelvis. Suspended by the **mesentery**, whose root runs from the duodenojejunal flexure (left of L2) to the ileocecal junction (right sacroiliac joint).
  parts:
    - "**Jejunum** (proximal ~2/5): wider, thicker wall, redder, prominent circular folds (plicae circulares), **long vasa recta**, **few arterial arcades**, less mesenteric fat"
    - "**Ileum** (distal ~3/5): narrower, thinner wall, fewer folds, **short vasa recta**, **many arcades**, more fat, **Peyer patches** (lymphoid aggregates)"
    - "**Terminal ileum**: absorbs **vitamin B12** (with intrinsic factor) and **bile acids**; ends at the ileocecal valve"
  relations: The greater omentum lies anteriorly; the ascending and descending colon frame it laterally and the transverse colon above.
  bloodSupply: Jejunal and ileal branches of the [[superior-mesenteric-artery]] forming arcades and vasa recta; the terminal ileum also from the ileocolic artery.
  venousDrainage: Superior mesenteric vein → [[hepatic-portal-vein]].
  lymphatics: Lacteals → mesenteric nodes → superior mesenteric nodes → intestinal trunk → cisterna chyli.
  innervation: Vagus (parasympathetic) and sympathetic (about T8–T10 via the superior mesenteric plexus). Visceral pain refers to the **periumbilical** region (midgut).
clinical:
  - title: Small bowel obstruction
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Adhesions** from prior surgery (most common in adults), hernias, and malignancy; Crohn strictures; gallstone ileus; intussusception in children.'
    presentation: >-
      Crampy periumbilical pain, **distension**, **vomiting** (early with proximal obstruction), and **obstipation**.
      High-pitched, tinkling bowel sounds early. Fever, tachycardia, peritonitis or constant pain suggest
      strangulation.
  - title: Meckel diverticulum
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Persistent **vitelline (omphalomesenteric) duct**, a true diverticulum on the **antimesenteric** border of the
      ileum. **Rule of 2s**: about 2% of people, about 2 feet from the ileocecal valve, about 2 inches long, 2 types of
      ectopic tissue (gastric, pancreatic), symptoms often before age 2.
    presentation: >-
      **Painless rectal bleeding** in a young child (acid from ectopic gastric mucosa ulcerates the adjacent ileum),
      intussusception, obstruction, or diverticulitis mimicking appendicitis. **Technetium-99m pertechnetate scan**
      (Meckel scan) detects the gastric mucosa.
  - title: Crohn disease
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Transmural granulomatous inflammation that can affect any part of the GI tract but most commonly the **terminal ileum**, with skip lesions.
    presentation: Chronic diarrhea, RLQ pain, weight loss, fistulas, strictures, perianal disease, B12 deficiency and gallstones (from bile acid loss).
  - title: Acute mesenteric ischemia
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Most often **embolus to the SMA** (atrial fibrillation), also thrombosis, non-occlusive ischemia, or venous thrombosis.
    presentation: '**Pain out of proportion to exam** in an older patient with AF or vascular disease, elevated lactate late. CT angiography; emergency revascularization.'
  - title: Intussusception
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Telescoping of bowel (usually ileum into colon, **ileocolic**), most common at 6–36 months; lead points in older children and adults.
    presentation: Intermittent severe colicky pain with drawing up of the legs, vomiting, **"currant jelly" stool** (late), and a **sausage-shaped mass**. Ultrasound shows a **target sign**. Air or contrast enema is diagnostic and therapeutic.
pance:
  - "Small bowel obstruction #1 cause in adults: **adhesions**. Look for air-fluid levels and dilated loops."
  - "**Meckel**: painless rectal bleeding in a toddler; **Tc-99m pertechnetate** scan; rule of 2s."
  - "Crohn: **terminal ileum**, skip lesions, transmural, string sign; B12 malabsorption."
  - "Mesenteric ischemia: pain out of proportion + AF → **CT angiography**."
  - "Intussusception: currant jelly stool, sausage mass, **target sign** on ultrasound; enema reduction."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    Obstruction: **dilated small bowel loops (more than about 3 cm)** with **air-fluid levels** in a step-ladder pattern
    and little colonic gas. Free air with perforation. {{verify: diameter thresholds vary}}
  ct: >-
    CT with IV contrast is the main test for obstruction (a **transition point**, closed loop, signs of ischemia),
    Crohn disease (CT/MR enterography: wall thickening, "comb sign", fistulas), and CT angiography for mesenteric
    ischemia.
  mri: MR enterography for Crohn disease activity and complications without radiation.
  ultrasound: First-line for **intussusception** in children (target or doughnut sign). Can show dilated fluid-filled loops and bowel wall thickening.
  keyViews:
    - "Supine and upright abdominal radiographs"
    - "CT abdomen/pelvis with contrast"
    - "Tc-99m pertechnetate scan for Meckel"
exam:
  landmarks: Jejunum in the left upper and central abdomen; terminal ileum in the right iliac fossa.
  palpation: Distension, tenderness, masses (RLQ mass in Crohn disease; sausage mass in intussusception). Check hernia orifices in every obstruction.
  testing: Auscultate for bowel sounds (high-pitched and tinkling early in obstruction; reduced or absent in ileus or late obstruction). Percuss for tympany.

      A 2-year-old boy has painless passage of a large amount of bright red and maroon blood per rectum. He is
      otherwise well. Which test is most likely to confirm the diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Colonoscopy
      - Technetium-99m pertechnetate scan
      - Barium enema
      - Upper GI series
      - Abdominal ultrasound for target sign
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Meckel diverticulum** with ectopic gastric mucosa causes painless bleeding in young children. A **Tc-99m
      pertechnetate (Meckel) scan** detects the gastric mucosa.
  - stem: A 60-year-old woman with prior hysterectomy has crampy abdominal pain, vomiting, distension and no flatus for 24 hours. What is the most likely cause?
    choices:
      - Colon cancer
      - Adhesions
      - Volvulus of the cecum
      - Gallstone ileus
      - Crohn stricture
    answer: B
    explanation: '**Adhesions** from prior abdominal or pelvic surgery are the most common cause of small bowel obstruction in adults.'
  - stem: Which feature distinguishes the ileum from the jejunum?
    choices:
      - Long vasa recta and few arcades
      - More prominent circular folds
      - Thicker, more vascular wall
      - Many arterial arcades with short vasa recta and Peyer patches
      - Less mesenteric fat
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      The **ileum** has **many arcades, short vasa recta, more fat and Peyer patches**. The jejunum has long vasa recta,
      few arcades, prominent folds and a thicker wall.
  - stem: A 78-year-old man with atrial fibrillation has sudden severe abdominal pain, but his abdomen is soft with only mild tenderness. What is the most appropriate next test?
    choices:
      - Abdominal ultrasound
      - CT angiography of the abdomen
      - Upper GI series
      - Colonoscopy
      - Stool guaiac
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Pain out of proportion to exam** in a patient with AF suggests **acute mesenteric ischemia** from an SMA embolus.
      **CT angiography** is the diagnostic test of choice.
