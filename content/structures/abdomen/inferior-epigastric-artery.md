name: Inferior epigastric artery
type: artery
region: abdomen
subregion: Inguinal region
taName: Arteria epigastrica inferior
summary: Branch of the external iliac artery that ascends into the rectus sheath. It's the landmark separating indirect from direct inguinal hernias and must be avoided during paracentesis and laparoscopic port placement.
tags: [abdominal-wall, hernia, procedures]
systems: [cardiovascular, gastrointestinal]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Deep surface of the lower anterior abdominal wall, running from just above the inguinal ligament into the rectus sheath.
  origin: External iliac artery, just superior to the inguinal ligament.
  course: >-
    Runs superomedially in the extraperitoneal tissue, **medial to the deep inguinal ring**, raising a peritoneal fold
    (the lateral umbilical fold). It pierces the transversalis fascia and enters the rectus sheath near the **arcuate
    line**, ascending on the deep surface of [[rectus-abdominis]].
  branches:
    - Cremasteric artery (to the spermatic cord) or artery of the round ligament
    - Pubic branch (may anastomose with the obturator artery, the "corona mortis")
    - Muscular and cutaneous branches
  supplies: Lower rectus abdominis and the medial anterior abdominal wall.
  anastomoses: With the **superior epigastric artery** (from the internal thoracic) inside the rectus sheath, a collateral route between the subclavian and iliac systems (important in coarctation and aortoiliac occlusion).
  relations: >-
    Forms the **lateral border of the inguinal (Hesselbach) triangle** ([[inguinal-triangle]]). **Indirect** inguinal
    hernias emerge **lateral** to it through the deep ring; **direct** hernias emerge **medial** to it.
clinical:
  - title: Landmark for inguinal hernia type
    highYield: true
    mechanism: The vessels mark the medial edge of the deep inguinal ring.
    presentation: At surgery (especially laparoscopic), a hernia sac lateral to the vessels is indirect and one medial to them is direct.
  - title: Injury during paracentesis or laparoscopy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Needles or trocars placed too medially in the lower abdomen.
    presentation: >-
      Abdominal wall hematoma or hemoperitoneum. **Paracentesis** is performed lateral to the rectus sheath, commonly in
      the **left lower quadrant** about two fingerbreadths medial and superior to the ASIS, ideally with ultrasound
      guidance to avoid the vessels. {{verify: preferred site descriptions vary between protocols}}
  - title: Rectus sheath hematoma
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Rupture of the inferior epigastric artery, often in anticoagulated patients.
    presentation: Tender lower abdominal wall mass (see [[rectus-abdominis]]).
pance:
  - "**Indirect hernia = lateral** to the inferior epigastric vessels; **direct = medial** (Hesselbach triangle)."
  - "Paracentesis: stay **lateral to the rectus sheath** (LLQ preferred) to avoid the inferior epigastric artery."
  - "Superior–inferior epigastric anastomosis = collateral pathway in coarctation or aortoiliac occlusion."
imaging:
  ct: Contrast CT shows the vessel and active bleeding in rectus sheath hematoma; CT angiography before abdominal wall flaps (the DIEP flap in breast reconstruction is based on its perforators).
  ultrasound: Color Doppler identifies the vessel before paracentesis or trocar placement.
  keyViews:
    - "Color Doppler of the lower abdominal wall before procedures"
exam:
  landmarks: Runs roughly from the mid-inguinal point toward the lateral edge of the rectus sheath at the arcuate line. Not palpable.
  testing: Before paracentesis, percuss for dullness laterally and use ultrasound to find fluid and avoid the vessels.
quiz:
  - stem: During laparoscopic hernia repair, a hernia sac is found passing through the abdominal wall medial to the inferior epigastric vessels. What type of hernia is it?
    choices:
      - Indirect inguinal hernia
      - Direct inguinal hernia
      - Femoral hernia
      - Spigelian hernia
      - Obturator hernia
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Direct** hernias pass through the inguinal (Hesselbach) triangle, **medial** to the inferior epigastric vessels.
      Indirect hernias enter the deep ring lateral to them; femoral hernias pass below the inguinal ligament.
  - stem: The inferior epigastric artery arises from which vessel?
    choices:
      - Internal iliac artery
      - External iliac artery
      - Femoral artery
      - Internal thoracic artery
      - Obturator artery
    answer: B
    explanation: It arises from the **external iliac artery** just above the inguinal ligament. The superior epigastric comes from the internal thoracic artery.
  - stem: To reduce the risk of injuring the inferior epigastric artery during paracentesis, where should the needle be placed?
    choices:
      - Through the rectus abdominis just below the umbilicus
      - Lateral to the rectus sheath in the lower quadrant
      - Directly over the pubic symphysis
      - Through the linea semilunaris at the arcuate line
      - At the midclavicular line just below the costal margin
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The artery runs deep to the rectus abdominis, so placing the needle **lateral to the rectus sheath** (typically LLQ)
      avoids it. Ultrasound guidance further reduces risk.
  - stem: >-
      A branch of the inferior epigastric artery sometimes anastomoses with the obturator artery behind the superior
      pubic ramus and can bleed heavily in pelvic surgery. What is this variant called?
    choices:
      - Corona mortis
      - Arc of Riolan
      - Marginal artery of Drummond
      - Circle of Willis
      - Cruciate anastomosis
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      The **pubic branch** of the inferior epigastric artery may anastomose with the obturator artery (the **"corona
      mortis"**), a source of significant bleeding during pelvic and hernia surgery.
  - stem: >-
      A patient on anticoagulation develops a tender, firm lower abdominal wall mass after a coughing fit. The mass
      stays palpable when she tenses her abdominal muscles. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Spigelian hernia
      - Ovarian torsion
      - Rectus sheath hematoma
      - Incarcerated umbilical hernia
      - Appendiceal abscess
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      A **rectus sheath hematoma** follows rupture of the **inferior epigastric artery**, often in anticoagulated
      patients after coughing or straining. A wall mass stays palpable when the rectus contracts (Fothergill sign),
      unlike an intra-abdominal mass.
