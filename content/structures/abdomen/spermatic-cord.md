name: Spermatic cord
type: organ
region: abdomen
subregion: Inguinal region
taName: Funiculus spermaticus
summary: Bundle of structures running between the deep inguinal ring and the testis. It carries the ductus deferens, testicular vessels and pampiniform plexus, and is involved in varicocele, torsion and inguinal hernia.
tags: [inguinal, male-genital]
systems: [genitourinary]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: From the deep inguinal ring, through the [[inguinal-canal]], to the posterior border of the testis in the scrotum.
  parts:
    - "**Ductus (vas) deferens** and the artery of the ductus deferens"
    - "**Testicular artery** (from the abdominal aorta at about L2)"
    - "**Pampiniform venous plexus** → testicular vein"
    - "Cremasteric artery and vein"
    - "**Genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve** (supplies cremaster)"
    - "Sympathetic and visceral afferent fibers; lymphatics (drain to lumbar/para-aortic nodes)"
    - "Remnant of the processus vaginalis"
    - "**Coverings** (derived from abdominal wall layers): **external spermatic fascia** (external oblique), **cremasteric muscle and fascia** (internal oblique), **internal spermatic fascia** (transversalis fascia)"
  relations: >-
    An indirect inguinal hernia sac lies within the coverings of the cord, anterolateral to its contents. The ilioinguinal
    nerve runs on the cord but outside its coverings.
  bloodSupply: Testicular artery, artery of the ductus deferens (from the inferior vesical/umbilical artery), cremasteric artery (from the inferior epigastric artery).
  venousDrainage: >-
    Pampiniform plexus → testicular vein. The **right testicular vein drains into the IVC** at an oblique angle; the
    **left drains into the left renal vein** at a right angle.
  lymphatics: Testicular lymph drains to the **lumbar (para-aortic) nodes**, not the inguinal nodes. Scrotal skin drains to the superficial inguinal nodes.
  innervation: Genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve (L1–L2) to cremaster; testicular plexus (visceral, about T10).
clinical:
  - title: Varicocele
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Dilation of the pampiniform plexus, about 90% on the **left** (the left testicular vein drains at a right angle into
      the left renal vein, which can be compressed between the aorta and SMA). {{verify: quoted left-sided proportion}}
    presentation: >-
      A **"bag of worms"** above the testis that is more prominent standing or with Valsalva and decreases lying down.
      Associated with infertility and testicular atrophy. A **new right-sided or non-decompressing** varicocele (doesn't
      shrink supine) warrants imaging for a retroperitoneal mass (e.g. **renal cell carcinoma**) or IVC obstruction.
  - title: Testicular torsion
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Twisting of the cord, usually in adolescents with a **"bell clapper" deformity** (the testis lacks normal posterior fixation), cutting off testicular blood flow.
    presentation: >-
      **Sudden severe scrotal pain**, nausea/vomiting, a **high-riding, horizontally oriented testis**, and an **absent
      cremasteric reflex**. A surgical emergency: salvage rates fall sharply after about 6 hours. Don't delay surgery for
      imaging when clinical suspicion is high.
  - title: Hydrocele
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Fluid in the tunica vaginalis (communicating, via a patent processus vaginalis, or non-communicating).
    presentation: Painless scrotal swelling that **transilluminates**. Communicating hydroceles change size during the day.
pance:
  - "Varicocele: left > right, bag of worms; **right-sided or non-reducing → image for RCC/IVC obstruction**."
  - "**Torsion**: sudden pain, high horizontal testis, **absent cremasteric reflex** → immediate urology/surgery."
  - "Cremasteric reflex = **L1–L2** (genitofemoral nerve)."
  - "Testicular cancer spreads to **para-aortic** nodes (not inguinal)."
  - "Cord coverings: external spermatic (EO), cremasteric (IO), internal spermatic (transversalis fascia)."
imaging:
  ct: Evaluates the retroperitoneum when a varicocele is right-sided or doesn't decompress supine; staging of testicular cancer (para-aortic nodes).
  ultrasound: >-
    **Scrotal ultrasound with color Doppler** is first-line: absent or reduced flow and a twisted cord ("whirlpool sign")
    in torsion; dilated veins (roughly 3 mm or more) with reflux on Valsalva in varicocele; an anechoic collection
    around the testis in hydrocele. {{verify: vein-diameter thresholds vary}}
  keyViews:
    - "Scrotal ultrasound with color Doppler, supine and with Valsalva/standing"
exam:
  landmarks: The cord is palpable between the finger and thumb at the neck of the scrotum, with the ductus deferens as a firm cord within it.
  palpation: Palpate the cord standing and supine, with and without Valsalva (varicocele). Transilluminate scrotal swellings.
  testing: >-
    **Cremasteric reflex**: stroke the superomedial thigh; the ipsilateral testis should rise. Absence suggests torsion.
    Testicular lie and position (high-riding, horizontal).
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 14-year-old boy wakes with sudden severe left scrotal pain and vomiting. The left testis is high-riding with a
      horizontal lie, and the left cremasteric reflex is absent. What is the next step?
    choices:
      - Antibiotics for epididymitis and follow-up in 1 week
      - Emergency surgical exploration (with urology)
      - Scrotal elevation and NSAIDs
      - Outpatient ultrasound in 48 hours
      - CT abdomen and pelvis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      These findings indicate **testicular torsion**, a surgical emergency. When clinical suspicion is high, proceed to
      **surgical exploration** without delaying for imaging; testicular salvage declines rapidly after about 6 hours.
  - stem: >-
      A 58-year-old man has a new right-sided varicocele that does not decrease when he lies supine. What is the most
      important next step?
    choices:
      - Reassurance
      - Scrotal support
      - Imaging of the abdomen for a retroperitoneal mass
      - Semen analysis
      - Varicocele embolization immediately
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The right testicular vein drains directly into the IVC, so a **new right-sided or non-decompressing** varicocele
      raises concern for **IVC or renal vein obstruction** (e.g. renal cell carcinoma). Image the abdomen.
  - stem: The cremasteric muscle and fascia of the spermatic cord are derived from which layer of the abdominal wall?
    choices:
      - External oblique
      - Internal oblique
      - Transversus abdominis
      - Transversalis fascia
      - Parietal peritoneum
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Internal oblique** → cremasteric muscle and fascia. External oblique → external spermatic fascia. Transversalis
      fascia → internal spermatic fascia.
  - stem: A painless scrotal swelling transilluminates brightly. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Varicocele
      - Testicular torsion
      - Hydrocele
      - Testicular tumor
      - Indirect inguinal hernia containing bowel
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      A **hydrocele** is fluid in the tunica vaginalis and **transilluminates**. A varicocele feels like a "bag of
      worms"; testicular tumors are solid and don't transilluminate.
  - stem: A testicular cancer first spreads to which lymph nodes?
    choices:
      - Superficial inguinal nodes
      - Deep inguinal nodes
      - Internal iliac nodes
      - Axillary nodes
      - Lumbar (para-aortic) nodes
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      The testis develops on the posterior abdominal wall and descends with its vessels, so testicular lymph drains to
      the **lumbar (para-aortic) nodes**. Scrotal **skin** drains to the superficial inguinal nodes.
