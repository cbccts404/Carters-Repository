summary: Muscular tube carrying urine from the renal pelvis to the bladder. Its three narrow points are where stones lodge, and its pelvic course puts it at risk during gynecologic surgery.

status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Retroperitoneal, descending on the **psoas major** from the renal pelvis to the bladder. About 25 cm long.
  parts:
    - "**Abdominal part**: from the ureteropelvic junction down along the psoas, crossing the genitofemoral nerve and passing behind the gonadal vessels"
    - "**Pelvic part**: crosses the pelvic brim at about the **bifurcation of the common iliac artery**, descends on the lateral pelvic wall, then turns medially to enter the bladder obliquely"
    - "**Three physiological constrictions**: (1) **ureteropelvic junction**; (2) where it **crosses the iliac vessels / pelvic brim**; (3) **ureterovesical junction** (the narrowest)"
  relations: >-
    **"Water under the bridge"**: in females the ureter passes **under the uterine artery** near the lateral fornix of the
    cervix; in males it passes **under the ductus deferens**. The gonadal vessels cross anterior to it in the abdomen.
  bloodSupply: Segmental, from the renal artery (upper), gonadal artery and aorta (middle), and internal iliac branches (lower, e.g. vesical and uterine arteries). Longitudinal anastomoses along the adventitia; stripping it risks ischemia.
  innervation: Renal, aortic and hypogastric plexuses (about T11–L2). Pain refers from the **loin to the groin** (and labia or scrotum) as a stone descends.
clinical:
  - title: Ureteral calculus (renal colic)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Stones lodge at the three narrow points, most often the **ureterovesical junction**.
    presentation: >-
      Colicky flank pain radiating to the groin, testis or labia; hematuria; nausea. Distal stones cause urinary urgency
      and frequency. Most stones under about 5 mm pass spontaneously; **alpha-blockers** (tamsulosin) help pass distal
      stones of about 5–10 mm. **Obstruction with infection, a solitary kidney, AKI or intractable pain** needs urgent
      urology (stent or nephrostomy). {{verify: size thresholds for passage and medical expulsive therapy}}
  - title: Iatrogenic ureteral injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Ligation, transection or thermal injury during **hysterectomy** (near the uterine artery), oophorectomy (near the gonadal vessels at the pelvic brim), or colorectal surgery.
    presentation: Postoperative flank pain, fever, urinoma, hydronephrosis, rising creatinine (if bilateral), or urine leaking from the vagina (ureterovaginal fistula).
  - title: Ureteropelvic junction obstruction
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Congenital narrowing or a crossing lower-pole vessel. The most common cause of prenatal hydronephrosis.
    presentation: Hydronephrosis on prenatal ultrasound; in adults, intermittent flank pain after high fluid intake (e.g. alcohol).
pance:

imaging:
  xray: KUB shows radiopaque stones along the ureteral course (often confused with pelvic phleboliths).
  ct: '**Non-contrast CT** for stones (the "rim sign" of ureteral wall around a stone helps distinguish it from a phlebolith). **CT urography** for the collecting system and ureteral injury (contrast leak).'
  ultrasound: Shows **hydronephrosis** and UVJ stones through a full bladder; ureteral jets on color Doppler. Preferred in pregnancy.
  keyViews:
    - "Non-contrast CT (stone protocol)"
    - "CT urography (delayed excretory phase)"
exam:
  landmarks: The ureter runs roughly along the tips of the lumbar transverse processes on radiographs and crosses the pelvic brim near the sacroiliac joint.
  testing: CVA tenderness and suprapubic tenderness. Pain migrating from the flank to the groin suggests a descending stone.
