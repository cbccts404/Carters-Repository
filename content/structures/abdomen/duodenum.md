subregion: GI viscera
taName: Duodenum
summary: C-shaped first part of the small intestine wrapped around the head of the pancreas. It receives bile and pancreatic juice at the major duodenal papilla and is the most common site of peptic ulcers.
tags: [gi, foregut, midgut]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Mostly retroperitoneal, curving around the head of the [[pancreas]] at about L1–L3.
  parts:
    - "**1st (superior) part** (about L1): the proximal 2 cm (duodenal cap/bulb) is intraperitoneal and mobile. **Most duodenal ulcers occur here**"
    - "**2nd (descending) part** (L1–L3): retroperitoneal. The **major duodenal papilla** receives the hepatopancreatic ampulla (common bile duct + main pancreatic duct); the minor papilla receives the accessory pancreatic duct"
    - "**3rd (horizontal) part** (about L3): crosses **anterior to the IVC and aorta**, with the **superior mesenteric vessels** passing anterior to it"
    - "**4th (ascending) part** (L2–L3): ends at the **duodenojejunal flexure**, suspended by the suspensory muscle (**ligament of Treitz**)"
  relations: >-
    1st part: gallbladder neck and liver anteriorly; gastroduodenal artery, common bile duct and portal vein
    posteriorly. 2nd part: right kidney posteriorly, transverse colon anteriorly. 3rd part: between the
    [[superior-mesenteric-artery]] in front and the [[abdominal-aorta]] behind.
  bloodSupply: >-
    The **foregut–midgut junction** lies just distal to the major papilla. **Proximally**: superior pancreaticoduodenal
    arteries (from the gastroduodenal, [[celiac-trunk]] territory) and the supraduodenal artery. **Distally**: inferior
    pancreaticoduodenal arteries (from the [[superior-mesenteric-artery]]). The two form arcades around the pancreatic head.
  venousDrainage: Pancreaticoduodenal veins → superior mesenteric and portal veins.
  innervation: Vagus and sympathetic (celiac and superior mesenteric plexuses). Visceral pain is epigastric.
clinical:
  - title: Duodenal ulcer
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '*H. pylori* and NSAIDs, with acid hypersecretion. Most are in the 1st part.'
    presentation: >-
      Epigastric pain that improves with meals and wakes the patient at night. **Posterior** ulcers can erode the
      **gastroduodenal artery** (major upper GI bleed). **Anterior** ulcers tend to **perforate**, causing free air and
      peritonitis.
  - title: Duodenal atresia
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Failure of recanalization of the duodenal lumen. Strongly associated with **Down syndrome** (trisomy 21).
    presentation: Bilious vomiting in the first day(s) of life (the obstruction is usually distal to the papilla), polyhydramnios prenatally, and a **"double bubble"** sign on radiograph.
  - title: Superior mesenteric artery syndrome
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Compression of the 3rd part between the SMA and aorta when the aortomesenteric angle narrows, typically after **rapid weight loss** (loss of the mesenteric fat pad) or spinal surgery.
    presentation: Postprandial epigastric pain, early satiety, bilious vomiting, weight loss.
  - title: Upper vs lower GI bleeding
    highYield: true
    mechanism: The ligament of Treitz (duodenojejunal flexure) is the conventional dividing point.
    presentation: Bleeding proximal to it is "upper GI" (hematemesis, melena, elevated BUN/creatinine ratio); distal to it is "lower GI" (typically hematochezia).
pance:
  - "Posterior duodenal ulcer → **gastroduodenal artery** hemorrhage; anterior ulcer → **perforation**."
  - "**Double bubble** + bilious vomiting in a newborn with Down syndrome → duodenal atresia."
  - "**Ligament of Treitz** divides upper from lower GI bleeding."
  - "Major duodenal papilla (2nd part) = CBD + main pancreatic duct → gallstone pancreatitis if a stone impacts here."
imaging:
  xray: Newborn abdominal film showing the **double bubble** (gas in the stomach and proximal duodenum) with little distal gas suggests duodenal atresia. Free air with a perforated ulcer.
  ct: Duodenal wall thickening, perforation (extraluminal air near the duodenum), SMA syndrome (narrow aortomesenteric angle and distance with a distended proximal duodenum).
  ultrasound: Prenatal ultrasound can show the double bubble and polyhydramnios.
  keyViews:
    - "EGD for ulcers and bleeding (diagnostic and therapeutic)"
    - "Upper GI contrast series: SMA syndrome, malrotation (abnormal position of the duodenojejunal flexure)"
exam:
  landmarks: The 1st part lies near the transpyloric plane (L1) just right of the midline; the 3rd part crosses the midline at about L3.
  palpation: Epigastric tenderness in ulcer disease. A rigid abdomen with perforation.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 52-year-old man taking daily ibuprofen has melena and hypotension. Endoscopy shows an ulcer on the posterior
      wall of the duodenal bulb with a visible vessel. Which artery is most likely eroded?
    choices:
      - Left gastric artery
      - Gastroduodenal artery
      - Splenic artery
      - Superior mesenteric artery
      - Right gastro-omental artery
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **gastroduodenal artery** runs directly behind the 1st part of the duodenum and is the classic source of
      bleeding from a posterior duodenal ulcer.
  - stem: A newborn with trisomy 21 has bilious vomiting on the first day of life. The radiograph shows a "double bubble." What is the diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Hypertrophic pyloric stenosis
      - Duodenal atresia
      - Hirschsprung disease
      - Meconium ileus
      - Intussusception
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Duodenal atresia** (failure of recanalization) is associated with **Down syndrome** and shows a **double bubble**.
      Pyloric stenosis presents at 3–6 weeks with non-bilious vomiting.
  - stem: Which structure passes anterior to the third part of the duodenum?
    choices:
      - Inferior vena cava
      - Abdominal aorta
      - Superior mesenteric artery and vein
      - Right kidney
      - Common bile duct
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **SMA and SMV** pass anterior to the 3rd part; the IVC and aorta lie behind it. Narrowing of the angle between
      the SMA and aorta can compress the duodenum (SMA syndrome).
