name: Greater omentum
type: organ
region: abdomen
subregion: Peritoneum
taName: Omentum majus
summary: Fatty, apron-like peritoneal fold hanging from the greater curvature of the stomach. It limits the spread of infection and is a common site of peritoneal metastases.
tags: [peritoneum]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Hangs from the greater curvature of the [[stomach]] and proximal [[duodenum]] in front of the small intestine, folding back up to attach to the transverse [[colon]].
  parts:
    - "**Gastrocolic ligament** (between the stomach and transverse colon)"
    - "**Gastrosplenic ligament** (between the stomach and the [[spleen]], containing the short gastric and left gastro-omental vessels)"
    - "**Gastrophrenic ligament**"
    - "Four layers of peritoneum (two descending, two ascending) that fuse, enclosing a potential inferior recess of the lesser sac"
  relations: Lies anterior to the small intestine and the transverse colon. The lesser omentum, by contrast, runs from the lesser curvature to the liver.
  bloodSupply: Right and left gastro-omental (gastroepiploic) arteries along the greater curvature, from the gastroduodenal and splenic arteries respectively.
  lymphatics: Contains milky spots (lymphoid aggregates) involved in peritoneal immune defense.
clinical:
  - title: The "policeman of the abdomen"
    highYield: true
    mechanism: The mobile omentum migrates to and adheres around sites of inflammation (e.g. an inflamed appendix or perforated ulcer).
    presentation: Walls off infection, forming a phlegmon or abscess rather than generalized peritonitis. Found adherent to the inflamed organ at surgery.
  - title: Omental caking (peritoneal carcinomatosis)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Peritoneal spread of malignancy, classically **ovarian cancer**, also gastric, colorectal and pancreatic cancers.
    presentation: Abdominal distension, ascites, early satiety, weight loss. Omental "cake" on imaging.
pance:
  - "Omental caking + ascites in a woman → consider **ovarian cancer** (check CA-125)."
  - "The omentum walls off inflammation (\"policeman of the abdomen\")."
imaging:
  ct: >-
    Normally fatty with fine vessels. **Omental caking** appears as soft-tissue thickening replacing the fat anterior to
    the bowel. Omental infarction appears as a focal fatty mass with stranding, usually in the right abdomen.
  ultrasound: Omental caking may be seen as a hypoechoic layer beneath the abdominal wall, often with ascites.
  keyViews:
    - "CT abdomen/pelvis with contrast"
exam:
  palpation: Extensive omental disease may be palpable as a firm, ill-defined upper or mid-abdominal mass with ascites.
quiz:

    choices:
      - Liver

    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The greater omentum hangs from the greater curvature and folds back to attach to the **transverse colon**
      (gastrocolic ligament). The **lesser** omentum connects the lesser curvature and duodenum to the liver.
  - stem: A 62-year-old woman has abdominal distension and weight loss. CT shows ascites and nodular soft tissue replacing the fat of the greater omentum. What is the most likely primary tumor?
    choices:
      - Renal cell carcinoma
      - Ovarian carcinoma
      - Prostate cancer
      - Glioblastoma
      - Thyroid papillary carcinoma
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Omental caking** with ascites in a woman is classic for **ovarian cancer**, which spreads transcoelomically across
      the peritoneum. Gastric, colorectal and pancreatic cancers can also do this.
  - stem: Which arteries run along the greater curvature of the stomach within the greater omentum?
    choices:
      - Left and right gastric arteries

      - Short gastric arteries only
      - Superior mesenteric branches
      - Middle colic arteries
    answer: B
    explanation: The **right and left gastro-omental (gastroepiploic)** arteries run along the greater curvature. The left and right gastric arteries run along the lesser curvature.
