summary: Pear-shaped sac under the liver that stores and concentrates bile, with the extrahepatic bile ducts. Gallstone disease (biliary colic, cholecystitis, choledocholithiasis, cholangitis) is one of the most tested topics.
tags: [gi, foregut, hepatobiliary]

highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: >-
    In the gallbladder fossa on the visceral surface of the [[liver]]. The **fundus** projects at the **tip of the right
    9th costal cartilage**, where the lateral border of the right rectus abdominis meets the costal margin.
  parts:
    - "Fundus, body, neck (with Hartmann pouch, a common site of impacted stones)"
    - "**Cystic duct** (with spiral folds, the spiral valve of Heister) joins the **common hepatic duct** to form the **common bile duct (CBD)**"
    - "The CBD descends in the hepatoduodenal ligament, passes **posterior to the 1st part of the duodenum**, runs through or behind the head of the [[pancreas]], and joins the main pancreatic duct to form the **hepatopancreatic ampulla (of Vater)**, opening at the **major duodenal papilla** in the 2nd part of the [[duodenum]]. The **sphincter of Oddi** controls flow"
    - "**Cystohepatic triangle (of Calot)**: cystic duct, common hepatic duct, and the inferior surface of the liver. It contains the **cystic artery** (usually from the right hepatic artery) and must be clearly identified in cholecystectomy (the \"critical view of safety\")"
  relations: The fundus contacts the transverse colon; the body contacts the 1st and 2nd parts of the duodenum (the site of cholecystoenteric fistula in gallstone ileus).
  bloodSupply: Cystic artery, usually from the right hepatic artery.
  venousDrainage: Cystic veins and small veins directly into the liver.
  innervation: Celiac plexus (sympathetic, visceral pain in the epigastrium and RUQ), vagus, and the right phrenic nerve (referred pain to the **right shoulder or scapula**).
clinical:
  - title: Biliary colic and acute cholecystitis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      **Biliary colic**: transient obstruction of the cystic duct by a stone. **Acute cholecystitis**: persistent
      obstruction with gallbladder inflammation. Risk factors ("5 F's"): female, fertile, forty, fat (obesity), fair; also
      rapid weight loss, pregnancy, estrogen and hemolysis (pigment stones).
    presentation: >-
      Colic: **postprandial (fatty meal) RUQ/epigastric pain** lasting less than about 6 hours, radiating to the right
      scapula. Cholecystitis: longer pain, **fever**, leukocytosis, **Murphy sign**.
  - title: Choledocholithiasis and ascending cholangitis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Stones in the common bile duct obstruct bile flow; superimposed infection causes cholangitis.
    presentation: >-
      **Jaundice**, elevated bilirubin and **alkaline phosphatase**. **Cholangitis**: **Charcot triad** (fever, RUQ pain,
      jaundice); **Reynolds pentad** adds hypotension and altered mental status. Needs antibiotics and urgent biliary
      drainage (**ERCP**).
  - title: Gallstone pancreatitis and gallstone ileus
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      A stone impacted at the ampulla blocks the pancreatic duct (pancreatitis). A large stone eroding through a
      cholecystoenteric fistula can obstruct the **terminal ileum** (gallstone ileus).
    presentation: >-
      Pancreatitis: epigastric pain radiating to the back with elevated lipase. Gallstone ileus: small bowel obstruction
      with **pneumobilia** and an ectopic stone (Rigler triad) in an older woman.
  - title: Courvoisier sign
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Gradual distal CBD obstruction by malignancy (pancreatic head cancer, cholangiocarcinoma) distends a compliant gallbladder. Chronic gallstone disease causes a fibrotic, non-distensible gallbladder.
    presentation: A **palpable, non-tender gallbladder with painless jaundice** suggests malignancy rather than stones.
pance:
  - "**Ultrasound** is first-line for RUQ pain / suspected cholecystitis; **HIDA** if ultrasound is equivocal (non-filling gallbladder = cholecystitis)."
  - "**Charcot triad**: fever, RUQ pain, jaundice → cholangitis → **ERCP**. Reynolds pentad adds hypotension + confusion."
  - "**Murphy sign**: inspiratory arrest with RUQ palpation."
  - "**Courvoisier**: painless jaundice + palpable non-tender gallbladder → pancreatic head cancer."
  - "Calot triangle: cystic duct, common hepatic duct, liver edge; contains the cystic artery."
imaging:
  xray: "Most gallstones are **not** radiopaque (only about 10–15% are visible). Pneumobilia with gallstone ileus. Porcelain gallbladder (calcified wall). {{verify: percentage of radiopaque stones}}"
  ct: Less sensitive than ultrasound for gallstones. Useful for complications (perforation, emphysematous cholecystitis, abscess), malignancy, and pancreatitis.
  mri: '**MRCP** noninvasively shows the biliary tree and CBD stones before choosing ERCP.'
  ultrasound: >-
    **First-line.** Gallstones are **echogenic with posterior acoustic shadowing** and move with position. **Acute
    cholecystitis**: stones plus **wall thickening (more than about 3 mm)**, **pericholecystic fluid**, gallbladder
    distension and a **sonographic Murphy sign**. A **dilated CBD** (more than about 6 mm, higher after cholecystectomy
    or with age) suggests obstruction. {{verify: CBD diameter thresholds vary with age}}
  keyViews:
    - "RUQ ultrasound"
    - "HIDA (cholescintigraphy) if ultrasound equivocal"
    - "MRCP / ERCP (therapeutic) for CBD stones"
exam:
  landmarks: "The **gallbladder point**: tip of the right 9th costal cartilage, at the lateral border of rectus abdominis (linea semilunaris)."
  palpation: Palpate at the gallbladder point. A palpable gallbladder with jaundice suggests Courvoisier sign.
  specialTests:
    - name: Murphy sign
      technique: Place fingers under the right costal margin at the gallbladder point and ask the patient to breathe in deeply.
      positive: The patient stops inspiring abruptly (catches the breath) because of pain as the inflamed gallbladder descends onto the fingers.
      significance: Acute cholecystitis. Less sensitive in older adults.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 42-year-old woman has 12 hours of constant RUQ pain after a fatty meal, fever of 38.5 °C, and leukocytosis.
      During palpation below the right costal margin she stops inhaling abruptly. What is the best initial imaging?
    choices:
      - CT abdomen
      - RUQ ultrasound
      - HIDA scan
      - ERCP
      - Abdominal radiograph
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Suspected **acute cholecystitis** (positive **Murphy sign**, fever) is evaluated first with **RUQ ultrasound**. HIDA
      is used if ultrasound is equivocal. ERCP is for CBD stones.
  - stem: A 70-year-old woman has fever, RUQ pain and jaundice, and she becomes hypotensive and confused. What is the definitive treatment after resuscitation and antibiotics?
    choices:
      - Laparoscopic cholecystectomy within 72 hours
      - Urgent biliary decompression by ERCP
      - Oral ursodiol
      - IV proton pump inhibitor
      - Observation
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Reynolds pentad** indicates severe **ascending cholangitis**. After antibiotics and fluids, the patient needs
      urgent **biliary drainage (ERCP)**.
  - stem: A 66-year-old man has painless jaundice, weight loss, and a palpable, non-tender gallbladder. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Acute cholecystitis
      - Choledocholithiasis
      - Carcinoma of the head of the pancreas
      - Hepatitis A
      - Gilbert syndrome
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Courvoisier sign** (painless jaundice + palpable non-tender gallbladder) suggests malignant distal CBD obstruction,
      most commonly **pancreatic head carcinoma**.
  - stem: Which structure is found within the cystohepatic triangle (of Calot)?
    choices:
      - Common bile duct
      - Cystic artery
      - Gastroduodenal artery
      - Portal vein
      - Left hepatic duct
    answer: B
    explanation: The triangle (cystic duct, common hepatic duct, inferior liver surface) contains the **cystic artery**, which is ligated during cholecystectomy.
