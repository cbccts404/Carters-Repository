---
name: Superior mesenteric artery
type: artery
region: abdomen
subregion: Posterior abdominal wall
taName: Arteria mesenterica superior
aka: [SMA]
summary: Artery of the midgut, arising from the aorta at L1 behind the pancreas. Its occlusion causes acute mesenteric ischemia, and its angle with the aorta explains SMA and nutcracker syndromes.
tags: [vascular, midgut]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Arises about 1 cm below the celiac trunk at about **L1**, behind the neck of the [[pancreas]].
  origin: "[[abdominal-aorta]]"
  course: >-
    Descends **anterior to the left renal vein**, the uncinate process of the pancreas, and the **3rd part of the
    duodenum**, then enters the root of the mesentery toward the right iliac fossa.
  branches:
    - "Inferior pancreaticoduodenal arteries"
    - "**Jejunal and ileal arteries** (from its left side), forming arcades and vasa recta"
    - "**Middle colic artery** (transverse colon)"
    - "**Right colic artery** (ascending colon)"
    - "**Ileocolic artery** (terminal ileum, cecum, appendix via the appendicular artery)"
  supplies: '**Midgut**: duodenum distal to the major papilla, jejunum, ileum, cecum, appendix, ascending colon, and the proximal two-thirds of the transverse colon.'
  anastomoses: Pancreaticoduodenal arcades with the [[celiac-trunk]]; the marginal artery and middle colic–left colic connection with the [[inferior-mesenteric-artery]].
  relations: >-
    The **left renal vein** passes between the SMA and the aorta; the **3rd part of the duodenum** also passes between
    them. The superior mesenteric vein lies to its right.
clinical:
  - title: Acute mesenteric ischemia
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Embolism** (often from atrial fibrillation or recent MI; typically lodges a few cm beyond the origin, sparing the proximal jejunum), thrombosis of an atherosclerotic origin, non-occlusive ischemia (low-flow states), or mesenteric venous thrombosis.'
    presentation: >-
      Sudden severe abdominal **pain out of proportion to the exam**, nausea, vomiting, possibly bloody stool.
      Lactate rises late. **CT angiography**; treatment is emergency revascularization and resection of dead bowel.
  - title: Chronic mesenteric ischemia
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Atherosclerotic stenosis of usually two or more mesenteric arteries.
    presentation: '"Intestinal angina": **postprandial pain** 15–60 minutes after eating, **food fear**, and **weight loss**, in a patient with vascular disease.'
  - title: SMA syndrome and nutcracker syndrome
    highYield: false
    mechanism: A narrowed aortomesenteric angle compresses the 3rd part of the duodenum (**SMA syndrome**) or the **left renal vein** (**nutcracker syndrome**).
    presentation: SMA syndrome causes postprandial pain and vomiting after rapid weight loss. Nutcracker causes hematuria, left flank pain and a **left varicocele**.
pance:
  - "SMA (L1) = **midgut**: distal duodenum → proximal 2/3 transverse colon."
  - "**Pain out of proportion to exam** + AF → SMA embolus → **CT angiography**."
  - "Chronic mesenteric ischemia: postprandial pain + food fear + weight loss."
  - "Left renal vein and 3rd part of duodenum pass **between SMA and aorta**."
imaging:
  xray: Late signs of ischemia (pneumatosis intestinalis, portal venous gas, thumbprinting). Often normal early.
  ct: >-
    **CT angiography** is the test of choice for acute mesenteric ischemia (filling defect, bowel wall changes,
    pneumatosis). Sagittal images show the SMA origin and the aortomesenteric angle.
  ultrasound: Duplex screens for SMA stenosis (elevated peak systolic velocity) in chronic mesenteric ischemia; limited by bowel gas.
  keyViews:
    - "CT angiography (arterial phase, sagittal reformats)"
exam:
  palpation: A soft abdomen with minimal tenderness despite severe pain is the classic early finding in acute mesenteric ischemia. Peritoneal signs appear late (infarction).
  testing: Listen for an epigastric bruit. Look for atrial fibrillation (irregularly irregular pulse).
quiz:
  - stem: The SMA supplies which part of the colon?
    choices:
      - Descending colon
      - Sigmoid colon
      - Cecum, ascending colon and proximal two-thirds of the transverse colon
      - Rectum
      - Splenic flexure and descending colon
    answer: C
    explanation: The **SMA** supplies the midgut, including the cecum, ascending colon and **proximal two-thirds of the transverse colon** (via the ileocolic, right colic and middle colic arteries).
  - stem: A 64-year-old woman with vascular disease has abdominal pain 30 minutes after meals, has lost 9 kg, and now avoids eating. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Peptic ulcer disease
      - Chronic mesenteric ischemia
      - Irritable bowel syndrome
      - Chronic pancreatitis
      - Biliary colic
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Intestinal angina**: postprandial pain, **food fear** and weight loss from atherosclerotic mesenteric stenosis
      (**chronic mesenteric ischemia**).
  - stem: Which vessel passes between the SMA and the abdominal aorta?
    choices:
      - Right renal artery
      - Left renal vein
      - Splenic vein
      - Inferior mesenteric vein
      - Right gonadal vein
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **left renal vein** crosses anterior to the aorta beneath the SMA. Compression here (**nutcracker syndrome**)
      can cause hematuria and a left varicocele.
