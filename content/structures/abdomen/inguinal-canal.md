name: Inguinal canal
type: space
region: abdomen
subregion: Inguinal region
taName: Canalis inguinalis
summary: Oblique passage through the lower anterior abdominal wall carrying the spermatic cord or round ligament. It's the site of indirect and direct inguinal hernias, the most common hernias overall.
tags: [inguinal, hernia]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Above and parallel to the medial half of the inguinal ligament, about 4 cm long, running inferomedially from the deep ring to the superficial ring.
  boundaries:
    deep ring: Opening in the **transversalis fascia**, just above the midpoint of the inguinal ligament and **lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels**
    superficial ring: Opening in the **external oblique aponeurosis**, just superolateral to the pubic tubercle
    anterior wall: External oblique aponeurosis (reinforced laterally by internal oblique)
    posterior wall: Transversalis fascia (reinforced medially by the conjoint tendon)
    roof: Arching fibers of internal oblique and transversus abdominis
    floor: Inguinal ligament (with the lacunar ligament medially)
  contents:
    - "**Males**: [[spermatic-cord]]"
    - "**Females**: round ligament of the uterus"
    - "**Both**: ilioinguinal nerve (enters the canal partway along, not through the deep ring, and exits the superficial ring); genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve (within the cord in males)"
  communications: From the extraperitoneal space (deep ring) to the scrotum or labium majus (superficial ring).
  notes: >-
    Mnemonic for the walls, **MALT** from superior clockwise: roof **M**uscles (internal oblique, transversus),
    anterior **A**poneurosis (external oblique), floor **L**igament (inguinal), posterior **T**ransversalis fascia. The
    canal's obliquity and the muscle "shutter" mechanism normally resist herniation.
clinical:
  - title: Indirect inguinal hernia
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Viscera enter the **deep ring lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels** and follow the canal, often through a
      **patent processus vaginalis** (congenital). The **most common hernia** in both sexes and in children. Can
      descend into the scrotum.
    presentation: A groin bulge that may extend into the scrotum, enlarging with coughing or straining. In infants, an intermittent groin or scrotal swelling while crying.
  - title: Direct inguinal hernia
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Protrusion through a weak posterior wall (transversalis fascia) in the **inguinal triangle**, **medial** to the inferior epigastric vessels. Acquired, usually in **older men**.
    presentation: A bulge in the medial groin that rarely enters the scrotum. Lower strangulation risk than indirect or femoral hernias.
  - title: Femoral hernia (for contrast)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Passes **below the inguinal ligament** through the femoral ring into the femoral canal, **medial to the femoral vein**. More common in **women**.
    presentation: A small bulge below and lateral to the pubic tubercle. **Highest risk of incarceration and strangulation** of groin hernias, so repair is recommended.
pance:
  - "**Indirect** = through the deep ring, **lateral** to the inferior epigastrics, patent processus vaginalis, **most common** (including in women and children)."
  - "**Direct** = Hesselbach triangle, **medial** to the inferior epigastrics, older men."
  - "**Femoral** = below the inguinal ligament, women, **highest strangulation risk**."
  - "Canal walls: MALT (Muscles, Aponeurosis, Ligament, Transversalis fascia)."
  - "Incarcerated/strangulated hernia (tender, irreducible, erythema, obstruction) → emergency surgery."
imaging:
  ct: Shows hernia contents and complications (bowel obstruction, strangulation). Helpful in obese patients or equivocal exams.
  mri: Occult hernias and athletic groin pain ("sports hernia" or athletic pubalgia) when ultrasound is inconclusive.
  ultrasound: >-
    **Dynamic ultrasound with Valsalva** is first-line imaging for an equivocal groin bulge: it locates the sac relative
    to the inferior epigastric vessels (indirect vs direct) and distinguishes a femoral hernia (below the ligament,
    medial to the femoral vein).
  keyViews:
    - "Dynamic groin ultrasound at rest and with Valsalva"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    **ASIS** and **pubic tubercle** mark the ends of the inguinal ligament. The deep ring lies about 1.5 cm above the
    midpoint of the inguinal ligament. The superficial ring lies above and lateral to the pubic tubercle.
  palpation: >-
    Examine standing. Inspect both groins while the patient coughs. In males, invaginate the loose scrotal skin with a
    finger up toward the superficial ring and ask the patient to cough or bear down; feel for an impulse. Examine for
    femoral hernias below the inguinal ligament.
  testing: >-
    Attempt gentle reduction if not tender. The traditional teaching that an impulse at the fingertip means indirect
    and at the finger pad means direct is **unreliable** on exam; the distinction is made surgically or on imaging.

quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 6-month-old boy has an intermittent swelling in the right groin extending into the scrotum that appears when he
      cries. What is the underlying defect?
    choices:
      - Weakness of the transversalis fascia in the inguinal triangle
      - Patent processus vaginalis
      - Enlarged femoral ring
      - Defect in the linea alba
      - Undescended testis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      An **indirect inguinal hernia** in infants results from a **patent processus vaginalis**, which lets bowel follow the
      path of testicular descent through the deep ring.
  - stem: Which structure forms the posterior wall of the inguinal canal?
    choices:
      - External oblique aponeurosis
      - Inguinal ligament
      - Transversalis fascia
      - Internal oblique muscle fibers
      - Lacunar ligament
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **transversalis fascia** (reinforced medially by the conjoint tendon) forms the posterior wall. Weakness of it
      in the inguinal triangle produces direct hernias.
  - stem: A 70-year-old woman has a small, tender, irreducible bulge just below the inguinal ligament, lateral to the pubic tubercle, with vomiting. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Direct inguinal hernia
      - Indirect inguinal hernia
      - Incarcerated femoral hernia
      - Spigelian hernia
      - Obturator hernia
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      A bulge **below** the inguinal ligament in a woman is a **femoral hernia**, which has the highest risk of
      incarceration and strangulation. This one needs emergency repair.
  - stem: Which nerve travels through the inguinal canal but does NOT pass through the deep inguinal ring?
    choices:
      - Genitofemoral nerve (genital branch)
      - Ilioinguinal nerve
      - Iliohypogastric nerve
      - Femoral nerve
      - Obturator nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **ilioinguinal nerve** enters the canal from its lateral wall (not through the deep ring) and exits the
      superficial ring. It can be injured in open hernia repair, causing groin and scrotal/labial numbness or pain.
