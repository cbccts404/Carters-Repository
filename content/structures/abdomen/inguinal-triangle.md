name: Inguinal triangle
type: space
region: abdomen
subregion: Inguinal region
taName: Trigonum inguinale
aka: [Hesselbach triangle]
summary: Area of the lower anterior abdominal wall, seen from inside, where direct inguinal hernias push through a weak transversalis fascia.
tags: [inguinal, hernia]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Inferomedial part of the anterior abdominal wall, viewed from the internal (peritoneal) surface.
  boundaries:
    lateral: "[[inferior-epigastric-artery|Inferior epigastric vessels]]"
    medial: Lateral border of [[rectus-abdominis]]
    inferior: Inguinal ligament (iliopubic tract)
    floor: Transversalis fascia (reinforced medially by the conjoint tendon)
  contents:
    - No structures pass through it normally; it is an area of relative weakness in the posterior wall of the [[inguinal-canal]]
  notes: 'Mnemonic: "RIP" — **R**ectus abdominis (medial), **I**nferior epigastric vessels (lateral), **P**oupart (inguinal) ligament (inferior).'
clinical:
  - title: Direct inguinal hernia
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Acquired weakness of the transversalis fascia (age, chronic straining, heavy lifting, chronic cough, connective tissue changes).
    presentation: A medial groin bulge in an older man, usually broad-necked, easily reducible, and rarely descending into the scrotum.
pance:
  - "Hesselbach triangle boundaries: **rectus (medial), inferior epigastrics (lateral), inguinal ligament (inferior)**."
  - "Direct hernias go **through** the triangle (medial to the inferior epigastrics)."
imaging:
  ultrasound: Dynamic groin ultrasound shows a sac bulging medial to the inferior epigastric vessels on Valsalva.
  ct: Shows a direct hernia medial to the inferior epigastric vessels.
exam:
  landmarks: Lies medial to the midpoint of the inguinal ligament and just lateral to the rectus sheath, above the pubic tubercle.
  palpation: See the groin hernia examination on [[inguinal-canal]].
quiz:

    answer: C

  - stem: A hernia passing through the inguinal triangle is classified as which type?

      - Direct inguinal
      - Femoral
      - Umbilical

    answer: B
    explanation: The inguinal triangle is the site of **direct** inguinal hernias, medial to the inferior epigastric vessels.
  - stem: Which layer is weak in a direct inguinal hernia?
    choices:
      - External oblique aponeurosis
      - Transversalis fascia

    answer: B
    explanation: The floor of the triangle is the **transversalis fascia** (posterior wall of the inguinal canal); its weakness allows direct herniation.
