---
name: Lumbar plexus
type: nerve
region: abdomen
subregion: Posterior abdominal wall
taName: Plexus lumbalis
summary: Nerve network formed within psoas major from the anterior rami of L1–L4. It supplies the lower abdominal wall, groin and anterior and medial thigh, and its branches are injured in hernia repair and by tight clothing.
tags: [posterior-abdominal-wall, nerves]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Within the substance of [[psoas-major]], on the posterior abdominal wall.
  roots: Anterior rami of **L1–L4** (often with a contribution from T12).
  origin: Anterior rami of L1–L4 in psoas major.
  course: Branches emerge from the lateral border, medial border or anterior surface of psoas and pass to the abdominal wall, groin and thigh.
  branches:
    - "**Iliohypogastric nerve (L1)**: skin over the iliac crest and hypogastrium; internal oblique and transversus"
    - "**Ilioinguinal nerve (L1)**: through the [[inguinal-canal]]; skin of the medial thigh, root of the penis and anterior scrotum or mons pubis and labia majora"
    - "**Genitofemoral nerve (L1–L2)**: genital branch (cremaster, scrotal skin) and femoral branch (skin over the femoral triangle)"
    - "**Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve (L2–L3)**: skin of the lateral thigh; passes under or through the inguinal ligament near the ASIS"
    - "**Femoral nerve (L2–L4, posterior divisions)**: anterior thigh muscles (quadriceps, sartorius, pectineus, with iliacus); skin of the anterior thigh and medial leg (saphenous nerve)"
    - "**Obturator nerve (L2–L4, anterior divisions)**: medial thigh adductors; skin of the medial thigh"
    - "**Lumbosacral trunk (L4–L5)**: joins the sacral plexus"
  motor: Lower abdominal wall muscles (iliohypogastric, ilioinguinal), cremaster, hip flexors, knee extensors (femoral), and hip adductors (obturator).
  sensory: Lower abdominal wall, groin, genitalia (anterior), and the anterior, lateral and medial thigh; medial leg (saphenous).
  notes: 'Mnemonic for the branches from superior to inferior: "**I I G**et **L**unch **O**n **F**ridays" (Iliohypogastric, Ilioinguinal, Genitofemoral, Lateral femoral cutaneous, Obturator, Femoral).'
clinical:
  - title: Meralgia paresthetica
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Compression of the **lateral femoral cutaneous nerve** as it passes under the inguinal ligament near the ASIS (obesity, pregnancy, tight belts or pants, diabetes).
    presentation: Burning, tingling or numbness of the **anterolateral thigh**, **no motor weakness**, normal reflexes. Usually managed conservatively (loosen clothing, weight loss).
  - title: Ilioinguinal and iliohypogastric nerve injury
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Entrapment or injury during **inguinal hernia repair**, Pfannenstiel (low transverse) incisions, or laparoscopic port placement.
    presentation: Chronic groin pain or numbness over the groin, medial thigh, and scrotum or labia.
  - title: Femoral and obturator nerve injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Femoral**: psoas/retroperitoneal hematoma, pelvic surgery (retractor compression), hip surgery. **Obturator**: pelvic surgery or tumor, childbirth.'
    presentation: >-
      **Femoral**: weak knee extension (knee buckling), reduced **patellar reflex (L4)**, numbness of the anterior thigh
      and medial leg. **Obturator**: weak thigh adduction and a wide-based gait, numbness of the medial thigh.
pance:
  - "**Meralgia paresthetica** = lateral femoral cutaneous nerve; lateral thigh burning, **no weakness**."
  - "Femoral nerve (L2–L4): knee extension + **patellar reflex (L4)**."
  - "Obturator nerve: thigh adduction."
  - "Cremasteric reflex: **L1–L2** (genitofemoral)."
imaging:
  ct: CT shows retroperitoneal hematoma or masses compressing plexus branches.
  mri: MR neurography for plexopathy (tumor, inflammation); MRI of the lumbar spine distinguishes radiculopathy from plexopathy.
  ultrasound: Identifies the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve near the ASIS and the femoral nerve lateral to the femoral artery; used for nerve blocks.
  keyViews:
    - "EMG/nerve conduction studies to localize (root vs plexus vs peripheral nerve)"
exam:
  landmarks: The lateral femoral cutaneous nerve passes about 1 cm medial to the ASIS under the inguinal ligament. The femoral nerve lies lateral to the femoral artery in the femoral triangle ("NAVEL", lateral to medial).
  testing: >-
    Hip flexion (L2–L3), knee extension (L3–L4), hip adduction (obturator), patellar reflex (L4), cremasteric reflex
    (L1–L2), and sensory mapping of the thigh.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 32-year-old pregnant woman has burning and numbness of the anterolateral thigh without weakness. Reflexes are
      normal. Which nerve is affected?
    choices:
      - Femoral nerve
      - Obturator nerve
      - Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve
      - Genitofemoral nerve
      - Sciatic nerve
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Meralgia paresthetica** is compression of the purely sensory **lateral femoral cutaneous nerve** under the
      inguinal ligament, common with pregnancy, obesity and tight clothing.
  - stem: After a pelvic operation, a patient has difficulty climbing stairs, a buckling knee and an absent patellar reflex. Which nerve is injured?
    choices:
      - Obturator nerve
      - Femoral nerve
      - Sciatic nerve
      - Superior gluteal nerve
      - Ilioinguinal nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: The **femoral nerve** (L2–L4) supplies the quadriceps; injury weakens knee extension and reduces the **patellar reflex**.
  - stem: Which roots form the lumbar plexus?
    choices:
      - T7–T11
      - L1–L4 (± T12)
      - L4–S3
      - S2–S4
      - C5–T1
    answer: B
    explanation: The lumbar plexus forms within psoas major from the anterior rami of **L1–L4**, often with a T12 contribution. L4–S3 form the sacral plexus.
