name: Abdominal aorta
type: artery
region: abdomen
subregion: Posterior abdominal wall
taName: Pars abdominalis aortae
summary: Continuation of the thoracic aorta from the aortic hiatus (T12) to its bifurcation at L4. It supplies the abdominal organs and is the usual site of aortic aneurysm.
tags: [vascular, posterior-abdominal-wall]
systems: [cardiovascular]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Retroperitoneal, on the anterior surface of the lumbar vertebral bodies, slightly left of the midline, with the [[inferior-vena-cava]] on its right.
  origin: Continuation of the descending [[thoracic-aorta]] at the **aortic hiatus of the diaphragm (T12)**.
  course: Descends anterior to the lumbar vertebrae to bifurcate at about **L4** (roughly at or slightly below the umbilicus) into the right and left common iliac arteries.
  branches:
    - "**Anterior (unpaired, to the gut)**: [[celiac-trunk]] (T12), [[superior-mesenteric-artery]] (L1), [[inferior-mesenteric-artery]] (L3)"
    - "**Lateral (paired)**: inferior phrenic, middle suprarenal, **renal** (about L1–L2), **gonadal** (about L2)"
    - "**Posterior**: lumbar arteries (four pairs), median sacral artery"
    - "**Terminal**: right and left common iliac arteries (L4)"
  supplies: Abdominal viscera, posterior abdominal wall, kidneys, suprarenal glands, gonads, and (via the iliacs) the pelvis and lower limbs.
  relations: >-
    Anterior: pancreas and splenic vein, the **left renal vein** (which crosses between the aorta and the SMA), the 3rd
    part of the [[duodenum]], and the root of the mesentery. Right: IVC, cisterna chyli, azygos vein. The sympathetic
    trunks run on each side of the vertebral bodies.
  termination: Common iliac arteries at L4.
clinical:
  - title: Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Dilation of the aorta to **3.0 cm or more**, usually **infrarenal**. Risk factors: **smoking** (the strongest),
      male sex, age over 65, family history, hypertension, atherosclerosis.
    presentation: >-
      Usually asymptomatic, found on screening or incidental imaging. **Rupture** presents with the triad of **severe
      abdominal or back/flank pain, hypotension and a pulsatile abdominal mass**. Elective repair is generally
      recommended at **5.5 cm or more in men** and **5.0 cm or more in women**, with rapid growth, or when
      symptomatic. Surveillance ultrasound (SVS 2018): every 3 years at 3.0–3.9 cm, every 12 months at 4.0–4.9 cm and
      every 6 months at 5.0–5.4 cm.
  - title: AAA screening
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Screening detects asymptomatic aneurysms before rupture.
    presentation: '**USPSTF**: one-time **abdominal ultrasound for men aged 65–75 who have ever smoked**.'
  - title: Aortoiliac occlusive disease (Leriche syndrome)
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Atherosclerotic occlusion of the distal aorta and/or iliac arteries.
    presentation: Classic triad of **buttock/thigh claudication, erectile dysfunction, and absent or diminished femoral pulses**.
pance:
  - "AAA = **≥ 3.0 cm**; most are **infrarenal**; **smoking** is the strongest risk factor."
  - "Screen: **one-time ultrasound, men 65–75 who ever smoked** (USPSTF)."
  - "Rupture triad: **pain + hypotension + pulsatile mass** → emergency surgery (unstable patients go straight to the OR)."
  - "Leriche: buttock claudication + ED + absent femoral pulses."
  - "Branch levels: celiac T12, SMA L1, renals L1–L2, IMA L3, bifurcation L4."
imaging:
  xray: Calcified aneurysm wall ("eggshell" calcification) on a lateral abdominal film. Not reliable for sizing.
  ct: >-
    **CT angiography** is the test of choice for **stable** patients with suspected rupture and for planning repair
    (open or endovascular). It measures diameter, the neck length below the renal arteries, and iliac involvement.
  ultrasound: >-
    **Screening and surveillance** test (outer-to-outer wall diameter). Point-of-care ultrasound in the ED can quickly
    confirm an aneurysm in an unstable patient (though it cannot reliably show retroperitoneal rupture).
  keyViews:
    - "Abdominal ultrasound (transverse and longitudinal aorta)"
    - "CT angiography abdomen/pelvis"
exam:
  landmarks: The bifurcation lies at about L4, projecting near the level of the iliac crests (supracristal plane), slightly below the umbilicus.
  palpation: >-
    Palpate deeply above and to the left of the umbilicus with both hands to estimate the **width** of the aortic
    pulsation (an expansile pulsation wider than about 3 cm suggests AAA). Sensitivity is limited by body habitus. Don't
    palpate forcefully if a symptomatic aneurysm is suspected.
  testing: Auscultate for bruits (aortic, renal, iliac). Check femoral and distal pulses.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 72-year-old man with a 50-pack-year smoking history has sudden severe back pain, BP 76/40, and a tender pulsatile
      periumbilical mass. What is the next step?
    choices:
      - CT angiography
      - Emergency surgical (or endovascular) repair
      - Abdominal ultrasound in 1 week
      - IV fluids and observation
      - MRI of the lumbar spine
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **rupture triad** (pain, hypotension, pulsatile mass) in an **unstable** patient calls for **immediate repair**.
      CT angiography is only for hemodynamically stable patients.
  - stem: Which patient should be screened for abdominal aortic aneurysm under USPSTF guidance?
    choices:
      - A 55-year-old man who smokes
      - A 68-year-old man who smoked 20 years ago
      - A 70-year-old woman who never smoked
      - An 80-year-old man who never smoked
      - A 45-year-old man with hypertension
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      USPSTF recommends a **one-time ultrasound for men aged 65–75 who have ever smoked**. Screening in women who have
      never smoked isn't recommended.
  - stem: At approximately which vertebral level does the abdominal aorta bifurcate?
    choices:
      - T12
      - L1
      - L2
      - L4
      - S1
    answer: D
    explanation: The aorta bifurcates into the common iliac arteries at about **L4**. The IVC forms at about L5.
  - stem: >-
      A 58-year-old smoker has cramping pain in both buttocks and thighs when walking, erectile dysfunction, and
      diminished femoral pulses. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Lumbar spinal stenosis
      - Superficial femoral artery stenosis
      - Bilateral sciatica
      - Aortoiliac occlusive disease (Leriche syndrome)
      - Abdominal aortic aneurysm
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      **Leriche syndrome** (atherosclerotic occlusion of the distal aorta and/or iliac arteries) causes the triad of
      **buttock/thigh claudication, erectile dysfunction and absent or diminished femoral pulses**. Superficial femoral
      disease causes calf claudication with normal femoral pulses.
  - stem: Which vessel crosses anterior to the abdominal aorta, just below the origin of the superior mesenteric artery?
    choices:
      - Left renal vein
      - Right renal artery
      - Inferior mesenteric vein
      - Splenic artery
      - Right gonadal vein
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      The **left renal vein** crosses anterior to the aorta, between it and the **SMA**. Compression there
      ("nutcracker") can cause hematuria and a left varicocele. The right renal artery passes behind the IVC.
