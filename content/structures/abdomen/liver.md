summary: Largest gland in the body, in the right upper quadrant. It has a dual blood supply (portal vein and hepatic artery) and is divided into eight functional segments; cirrhosis and portal hypertension are its central clinical problems.

anatomy:
  location: Right upper quadrant and epigastrium, under the right dome of the diaphragm, protected by the lower ribs.
  parts:
    - "**Anatomical lobes**: right, left, **caudate** (posterior, between the IVC and ligamentum venosum) and **quadrate** (inferior, between the gallbladder and ligamentum teres)"
    - "**Functional (surgical) lobes**: right and left, separated by **Cantlie line** (from the gallbladder fossa to the IVC). Divided into **eight Couinaud segments**, each with its own portal pedicle and biliary drainage, allowing segmental resection"
    - "**Porta hepatis**: entry of the [[hepatic-portal-vein]] and hepatic artery proper; exit of the hepatic ducts"
    - "**Bare area**: on the posterior surface, in direct contact with the diaphragm (no peritoneum)"
    - "**Peritoneal ligaments**: falciform ligament (with the **ligamentum teres**, the remnant of the umbilical vein, in its free edge), coronary and triangular ligaments, lesser omentum (hepatogastric and hepatoduodenal ligaments)"
  relations: >-
    Diaphragmatic surface under the diaphragm. Visceral surface contacts the stomach, duodenum, hepatic flexure of the
    colon, right kidney and suprarenal gland, and the [[gallbladder]]. The [[inferior-vena-cava]] runs in a groove on
    the posterior surface, receiving the hepatic veins.
  bloodSupply: >-
    **Dual supply**: the **portal vein** (about 75% of inflow; nutrient-rich, relatively deoxygenated) and the **hepatic
    artery proper** (about 25%; oxygenated) from the common hepatic artery ([[celiac-trunk]]). {{verify: commonly quoted
    75/25 split}}
  venousDrainage: Right, middle and left **hepatic veins** → [[inferior-vena-cava]] just below the diaphragm.
  lymphatics: Hepatic nodes at the porta hepatis → celiac nodes; some drain through the diaphragm to thoracic nodes.
  innervation: Hepatic plexus (vagus and sympathetic). The capsule (Glisson) is sensitive to stretch, so hepatomegaly causes RUQ discomfort; diaphragmatic irritation can refer to the right shoulder.
clinical:
  - title: Cirrhosis and portal hypertension
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Chronic injury (alcohol, viral hepatitis B/C, MASLD/NAFLD, hemochromatosis, autoimmune disease) leads to fibrosis
      and nodular regeneration, raising resistance to portal flow.
    presentation: >-
      **Portal hypertension**: ascites, splenomegaly, **esophageal varices**, caput medusae. **Liver failure**: jaundice,
      coagulopathy (raised INR), hypoalbuminemia, hepatic encephalopathy (asterixis), spider angiomas, palmar erythema,
      gynecomastia.
  - title: Hepatocellular carcinoma
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Usually in a cirrhotic liver or with chronic hepatitis B (even without cirrhosis).
    presentation: Often found on **surveillance ultrasound** (every 6 months in at-risk patients, ± AFP). Decompensation of known cirrhosis, weight loss, RUQ mass. Diagnosed on multiphase CT or MRI (arterial enhancement with washout).
  - title: Liver injury in trauma
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Large, relatively fixed solid organ; injured in both blunt and penetrating trauma.
    presentation: RUQ pain, hemoperitoneum (positive FAST), shock. Stable patients are often managed non-operatively after CT grading.
  - title: Budd–Chiari syndrome
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Hepatic venous outflow obstruction (thrombosis), associated with hypercoagulable states (e.g. polycythemia vera, pregnancy, OCPs).
    presentation: Triad of **abdominal pain, ascites and hepatomegaly**. The caudate lobe may enlarge (it drains directly to the IVC).
pance:
  - "Dual supply: **portal vein ~75%**, **hepatic artery ~25%**."
  - "Cirrhosis complications: ascites, varices, encephalopathy, SBP, HCC, hepatorenal syndrome."
  - "HCC surveillance in cirrhosis: **ultrasound every 6 months** ± AFP."
  - "Budd–Chiari: pain + ascites + hepatomegaly; hypercoagulable state."
  - "Ligamentum teres = remnant of the **umbilical vein**; ligamentum venosum = remnant of the **ductus venosus**."
imaging:
  xray: Not useful for the liver itself. Calcifications or gas in the portal venous system (a sign of bowel ischemia) may be seen.
  ct: >-
    **Multiphase (arterial, portal venous, delayed) CT** characterizes liver lesions (HCC: arterial hyperenhancement and
    washout), shows trauma injuries, and features of cirrhosis (nodular contour, caudate hypertrophy, splenomegaly,
    varices, ascites).
  mri: MRI (with liver-specific contrast) is the most accurate for characterizing focal lesions. Quantifies iron and fat.
  ultrasound: >-
    **First-line** for RUQ pain, abnormal liver tests, and HCC surveillance. Cirrhotic liver: coarse echotexture and
    nodular surface. **Doppler** assesses portal vein flow direction and patency and hepatic vein patency (Budd–Chiari).
    **Elastography** estimates fibrosis.
  keyViews:
    - "RUQ ultrasound with Doppler"
    - "Multiphase CT or MRI for focal lesions"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    Upper border at about the 5th intercostal space in the right midclavicular line; lower edge at or just below the
    right costal margin. Normal **liver span** by percussion in the right midclavicular line is roughly **6–12 cm**.
    {{verify: normal span values vary with body size and technique}}
  palpation: >-
    Place the hand below the right costal margin (or start from the RLQ if hepatomegaly is suspected) and ask the
    patient to breathe in deeply; feel for the edge descending onto the fingers. Note consistency (firm, nodular),
    tenderness and pulsatility.
  testing: >-
    **Percussion** from resonant lung downward to dullness (upper border) and from tympanic abdomen upward (lower
    border) in the midclavicular line. The **scratch test** is an alternative for locating the lower edge.
quiz:
  - stem: What proportion of hepatic blood flow is supplied by the portal vein?
    choices:
      - About 10%
      - About 25%
      - About 50%
      - About 75%
      - 100%
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      The **portal vein** provides about **75%** of hepatic blood flow; the hepatic artery provides about 25% but a large
      share of the oxygen.
  - stem: >-
      A 34-year-old woman with polycythemia vera develops RUQ pain, hepatomegaly and ascites over two weeks. Doppler
      ultrasound shows no flow in the hepatic veins. What is the diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Portal vein thrombosis
      - Budd–Chiari syndrome
      - Acute viral hepatitis
      - Right heart failure
      - Cholangitis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Budd–Chiari syndrome** (hepatic vein thrombosis) causes the triad of **pain, ascites and hepatomegaly** and is
      associated with myeloproliferative disorders such as polycythemia vera.
  - stem: A patient with cirrhosis should undergo which surveillance for hepatocellular carcinoma?
    choices:
      - Annual CT chest
      - Ultrasound every 6 months, with or without AFP
      - Colonoscopy every 3 years
      - Liver biopsy every year
      - No surveillance is recommended
    answer: B
    explanation: Patients with cirrhosis (and some with chronic hepatitis B) should have **ultrasound every 6 months**, with or without **AFP**.
  - stem: The functional right and left lobes of the liver are separated by a plane from the gallbladder fossa to which structure?
    choices:
      - Falciform ligament
      - Inferior vena cava
      - Ligamentum venosum
      - Porta hepatis
      - Caudate lobe
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Cantlie line** runs from the gallbladder fossa to the **IVC**, following the middle hepatic vein. The falciform
      ligament marks the anatomical (not functional) division.
  - stem: The ligamentum teres in the free edge of the falciform ligament is the remnant of which fetal structure?
    choices:
      - Ductus venosus
      - Umbilical vein
      - Umbilical artery
      - Ductus arteriosus
      - Vitelline duct
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **ligamentum teres** is the remnant of the **umbilical vein**; the **ligamentum venosum** is the remnant of
      the ductus venosus. The paraumbilical veins run with the ligamentum teres, which is how caput medusae forms.
