name: Lesser sac
type: space
region: abdomen
subregion: Peritoneum
taName: Bursa omentalis
aka: [Omental bursa, Epiploic foramen, Foramen of Winslow]
summary: Part of the peritoneal cavity behind the stomach, entered through the omental (epiploic) foramen. The hepatoduodenal ligament in front of that foramen is compressed in the Pringle maneuver.
tags: [peritoneum, surgery]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Posterior to the [[stomach]] and lesser omentum, anterior to the [[pancreas]], left kidney and suprarenal gland.
  boundaries:
    anterior: Lesser omentum, posterior surface of the stomach, anterior layers of the greater omentum
    posterior: Pancreas, left kidney and suprarenal gland (covered by peritoneum), aorta
    superior: Caudate lobe of the [[liver]] and diaphragm
    inferior: Fused layers of the greater omentum / transverse mesocolon
    left: Spleen and the gastrosplenic and splenorenal ligaments
  contents:
    - A potential space normally containing only a thin film of fluid
  communications: >-
    Connects with the greater sac through the **omental (epiploic) foramen (of Winslow)**. Its boundaries are:
    **anterior**, the free edge of the **hepatoduodenal ligament** (containing the **portal triad**: [[hepatic-portal-vein]]
    posteriorly, hepatic artery proper on the left, **common bile duct** on the right); **posterior**, the
    [[inferior-vena-cava]]; **superior**, the caudate lobe of the liver; **inferior**, the first part of the
    [[duodenum]].
clinical:
  - title: Pringle maneuver
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Compressing the hepatoduodenal ligament (index finger in the omental foramen, thumb in front) occludes the hepatic
      artery and portal vein.
    presentation: >-
      Used to control hepatic bleeding. If bleeding continues despite the maneuver, the source is likely the **hepatic
      veins or retrohepatic IVC** (which aren't controlled by it).
  - title: Collections and perforations in the lesser sac
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      **Pancreatic pseudocysts** and fluid from pancreatitis collect here. A **perforated posterior gastric ulcer** leaks
      into the lesser sac and can erode the pancreas or splenic artery.
    presentation: Epigastric fullness, early satiety or pain. A retrogastric collection on CT.
pance:
  - "Portal triad in the hepatoduodenal ligament: **portal vein (posterior), hepatic artery (left), CBD (right)**."
  - "Pringle maneuver controls inflow; persistent bleeding → hepatic veins/IVC."
  - "Pancreatic pseudocyst classically sits in the lesser sac (behind the stomach)."
imaging:
  ct: Shows retrogastric fluid collections and pseudocysts (well-defined wall, typically 4 or more weeks after acute pancreatitis) and their relation to the stomach.
  ultrasound: Pseudocysts may be seen through the left lobe of the liver or stomach. Endoscopic ultrasound guides drainage into the stomach (cystgastrostomy).
  keyViews:
    - "CT abdomen with contrast"
exam:
  palpation: Large pseudocysts may present as an epigastric mass.
quiz:
  - stem: Which structure forms the posterior boundary of the omental (epiploic) foramen?
    choices:
      - Hepatoduodenal ligament
      - Inferior vena cava
      - Caudate lobe of the liver
      - First part of the duodenum
      - Pancreas
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **IVC** lies behind the omental foramen. The hepatoduodenal ligament is anterior, the caudate lobe superior, and
      the first part of the duodenum inferior.
  - stem: >-
      During a trauma laparotomy, a surgeon compresses the hepatoduodenal ligament, but brisk bleeding continues from
      behind the liver. What is the most likely source?
    choices:
      - Proper hepatic artery
      - Portal vein
      - Retrohepatic IVC or hepatic veins
      - Cystic artery
      - Gastroduodenal artery
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **Pringle maneuver** occludes the hepatic artery and portal vein (inflow). Continued bleeding suggests injury to
      the **hepatic veins or retrohepatic IVC**, which drain the liver (outflow).
  - stem: Within the hepatoduodenal ligament, which structure lies most posteriorly?
    choices:
      - Common bile duct
      - Proper hepatic artery
      - Portal vein
      - Cystic duct
      - Right gastric artery
    answer: C
    explanation: The **portal vein** is posterior, with the **common bile duct** to the right and the **hepatic artery proper** to the left anteriorly.
