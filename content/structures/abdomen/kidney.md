anatomy:
  location: >-
    Retroperitoneal, on the posterior abdominal wall beside the vertebral column from about **T12 to L3**. The **right
    kidney lies slightly lower** (displaced by the liver). The **hila** lie at about **L1** (transpyloric plane). The
    kidneys move a few centimeters with respiration.
  parts:
    - "Cortex, medulla (renal pyramids), renal columns; minor and major calyces → **renal pelvis** → ureter"
    - "**Hilum**, anterior to posterior: renal **vein**, renal **artery**, renal **pelvis** (VAP)"
    - "**Coverings**, inside to out: fibrous capsule, perirenal fat, **renal (Gerota) fascia** (also encloses the suprarenal gland), pararenal fat"
  relations: >-
    **Posterior (both)**: diaphragm, **12th rib** (and 11th on the left), psoas major, quadratus lumborum, transversus
    abdominis; subcostal, iliohypogastric and ilioinguinal nerves. **Anterior, right**: liver, 2nd part of the duodenum,
    hepatic flexure. **Anterior, left**: stomach, spleen, tail of the pancreas, splenic flexure, jejunum. Superiorly, the
    [[suprarenal-gland]].
  bloodSupply: >-
    **Renal arteries** from the [[abdominal-aorta]] at about L1–L2 (the right is longer, passing behind the IVC).
    Segmental arteries are **end arteries** (so occlusion causes segmental infarction). Accessory renal arteries are common.
  venousDrainage: >-
    Renal veins → [[inferior-vena-cava]]. The **left renal vein** is longer, crosses anterior to the aorta beneath the
    SMA, and receives the left gonadal and left suprarenal veins.
  lymphatics: Lumbar (para-aortic) nodes.
  innervation: Renal plexus (sympathetic from the lesser/least splanchnic nerves, about T10–L1). Pain from capsular or pelvic distension refers to the **flank ("loin") and groin**.
clinical:
  - title: Nephrolithiasis (kidney stones)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Calcium oxalate stones are most common; also calcium phosphate, uric acid (radiolucent), struvite (urease-producing organisms, staghorn), and cystine.
    presentation: >-
      Sudden severe **colicky flank pain radiating to the groin**, restlessness, nausea, **hematuria**. Stones lodge at
      the ureter's narrow points (see [[ureter]]). Infection above an obstructing stone is a **urologic emergency**
      (decompression needed).
  - title: Pyelonephritis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Ascending bacterial infection (most often *E. coli*) from the bladder.
    presentation: Fever, flank pain, **costovertebral angle tenderness**, nausea, with or without cystitis symptoms. Urinalysis shows pyuria and **WBC casts**.
  - title: Renal cell carcinoma
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Most common renal malignancy in adults. Risk factors include smoking, obesity, hypertension and von Hippel–Lindau disease.
    presentation: >-
      Often incidental on imaging. Classic (uncommon) triad: **hematuria, flank pain, palpable mass**. Paraneoplastic
      syndromes: **polycythemia** (EPO), hypercalcemia, hypertension. Invasion of the left renal vein can cause a **left
      varicocele**; tumor thrombus may extend into the IVC.
  - title: Horseshoe kidney
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Fusion of the lower poles; ascent is arrested by the [[inferior-mesenteric-artery]]. Associated with **Turner syndrome**.
    presentation: Usually asymptomatic; increased risk of obstruction, stones, infection and trauma.
pance:
  - "Hilum order anterior → posterior: **vein, artery, pelvis (VAP)**."
  - "Stone workup: **non-contrast CT** (ultrasound in pregnancy and young patients); uric acid stones are radiolucent."
  - "Pyelonephritis: fever + CVA tenderness + **WBC casts**."
  - "RCC: hematuria + flank pain + mass; **polycythemia**; left varicocele."
  - "Right kidney sits lower than the left (liver)."
imaging:
  xray: KUB shows radiopaque stones (calcium, struvite, cystine); **uric acid stones are radiolucent**. Staghorn calculi fill the collecting system.
  ct: >-
    **Non-contrast CT** is the most sensitive test for stones (size, location, hydronephrosis). **CT urography**
    (multiphase with contrast) evaluates hematuria and masses. Renal masses are characterized by enhancement on
    multiphase CT or MRI.
  mri: Characterizes indeterminate renal masses and renal vein/IVC tumor thrombus; avoids iodinated contrast.
  ultrasound: >-
    First-line for **hydronephrosis**, renal size, cysts vs solid masses, and stones in pregnancy or children. Normal
    adult kidney length is roughly 10–12 cm. {{verify: normal size ranges vary}} Doppler for renal artery stenosis and
    transplant perfusion.
  keyViews:
    - "Non-contrast CT abdomen/pelvis (stone protocol)"
    - "Renal ultrasound"
    - "CT urogram for hematuria"
exam:
  landmarks: The kidneys lie under the 11th–12th ribs posteriorly. The **costovertebral angle** (between the 12th rib and the vertebral column) overlies the kidney.
  palpation: >-
    **Bimanual palpation (ballottement)**: one hand posteriorly under the flank, the other anteriorly below the costal
    margin; push up from behind during deep inspiration. The lower pole of the right kidney may be palpable in thin
    people.
  testing: >-
    **CVA tenderness**: place one hand over the costovertebral angle and strike it with the ulnar side of the other
    fist. Pain suggests pyelonephritis or obstruction. Auscultate for a **renal artery bruit** (hypertension workup).

    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Renal cell carcinoma** can secrete EPO (polycythemia) and invade the **left renal vein**, which receives the left
