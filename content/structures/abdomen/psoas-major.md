---
name: Psoas major
type: muscle
region: abdomen
subregion: Posterior abdominal wall
taName: Musculus psoas major
summary: Long muscle beside the lumbar spine that joins iliacus to flex the hip. The lumbar plexus forms within it, and the psoas sign reflects inflammation of structures lying on it.
tags: [posterior-abdominal-wall, hip]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Posterior abdominal wall, beside the lumbar vertebral bodies, passing under the inguinal ligament into the thigh.
  group: Posterior abdominal wall muscles (with iliacus and quadratus lumborum). Psoas + iliacus = **iliopsoas**.
  origin: Sides of the vertebral bodies of **T12–L5** and the intervening discs; transverse processes of the lumbar vertebrae.
  insertion: '**Lesser trochanter of the femur** (via a common tendon with iliacus).'
  innervation: "Anterior rami of lumbar nerves **L1–L3** (from the [[lumbar-plexus]])"
  bloodSupply: Lumbar arteries, iliolumbar artery (psoas branch) and branches of the external iliac/femoral arteries.
  action:
    - Flexes the thigh at the hip (with iliacus), the main hip flexor
    - Flexes the trunk on the thigh (sitting up from supine) and flexes the lumbar spine laterally
  relations: >-
    The [[lumbar-plexus]] forms **within** the muscle: the femoral nerve emerges from its **lateral** border, the
    obturator nerve from its **medial** border, and the genitofemoral nerve from its **anterior** surface. The
    [[ureter]] and gonadal vessels descend on its anterior surface. The kidney lies on its upper part.
clinical:
  - title: Psoas abscess
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Primary**: hematogenous spread (*Staphylococcus aureus*, often in people who inject drugs, immunocompromised). **Secondary**: spread from adjacent disease such as **vertebral osteomyelitis/TB (Pott disease)**, **Crohn disease**, diverticulitis or appendicitis.'
    presentation: Fever, back or flank pain, **limp**, and the hip held flexed; **pain on hip extension** (psoas sign). CT confirms it; treated with drainage and antibiotics.
  - title: Psoas sign in appendicitis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: An inflamed **retrocecal appendix** lies against the right psoas.
    presentation: RLQ pain on passive extension of the right hip or on active flexion against resistance.
  - title: Retroperitoneal (psoas) hematoma
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Bleeding in anticoagulated patients or after femoral arterial access.
    presentation: Flank or groin pain, hip flexion, falling hemoglobin, and **femoral nerve palsy** (weak knee extension, reduced patellar reflex, anterior thigh numbness).
pance:
  - "Psoas = main **hip flexor**; inserts on the **lesser trochanter**; L1–L3."
  - "**Psoas sign** → retrocecal appendicitis or psoas abscess."
  - "Psoas abscess: fever, back pain, limp, flexed hip; think *S. aureus*, Pott disease, Crohn."
  - "Femoral nerve emerges from its **lateral** border; obturator from its **medial** border."
imaging:
  xray: Loss of the psoas shadow on an abdominal film is a nonspecific sign of retroperitoneal disease.
  ct: '**CT with contrast** is the test of choice for psoas abscess or hematoma (a rim-enhancing collection within an enlarged muscle).'
  mri: Best for associated spinal osteomyelitis/discitis.
  keyViews:
    - "CT abdomen/pelvis with contrast"
exam:
  landmarks: Not palpable directly; its tendon passes deep to the inguinal ligament lateral to the femoral vessels.
  testing: Resisted hip flexion in sitting (L2–L3 myotome).
  specialTests:
    - name: Psoas sign
      technique: With the patient lying on the left side, passively extend the right hip; or have the supine patient flex the right hip against resistance.
      positive: Lower abdominal or flank pain.
      significance: Irritation of the psoas (retrocecal appendicitis, psoas abscess or hematoma).

    choices:
      - Greater trochanter
      - Lesser trochanter

      - Ischial tuberosity

    answer: B
    explanation: Psoas major and iliacus insert together on the **lesser trochanter** of the femur.
  - stem: Which nerve emerges from the lateral border of psoas major?
    choices:
      - Obturator nerve

      - Sciatic nerve
      - Subcostal nerve
    answer: C
    explanation: The **femoral nerve** emerges laterally, the **obturator** medially, and the **genitofemoral** nerve through its anterior surface.
