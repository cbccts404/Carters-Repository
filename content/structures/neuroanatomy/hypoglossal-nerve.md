---
name: Hypoglossal nerve (CN XII)
type: nerve
region: neuroanatomy
subregion: Cranial nerves
taName: Nervus hypoglossus
aka: [CN XII, Cranial nerve XII]
summary: Motor nerve to the tongue. A lower motor neuron lesion makes the protruded tongue deviate toward the weak side, with atrophy and fasciculations.
tags: [cranial-nerves, tongue]
systems: [neurologic]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  origin: '**Hypoglossal nucleus** in the floor of the fourth ventricle in the [[medulla-oblongata]]; rootlets emerge in the **preolivary sulcus** between the pyramid and the olive.'
  course: >-
    Leaves the skull through the **hypoglossal canal** → descends between the internal carotid artery and internal
    jugular vein → loops forward around the occipital artery → **crosses superficial to the internal and external
    carotid arteries** above the bifurcation (see [[carotid-arteries]]) → runs forward on hyoglossus above the hyoid, below
    the submandibular duct, into the tongue. C1 fibers travel with it (superior root of the ansa cervicalis, and to
    thyrohyoid and geniohyoid; see [[cervical-plexus]]).
  motor: '**All intrinsic and extrinsic muscles of the [[tongue]] except palatoglossus** (CN X) — genioglossus, hyoglossus, styloglossus.'
  relations: At risk in **carotid endarterectomy**, submandibular gland excision and neck dissection.
clinical:
  - title: Hypoglossal nerve lesion
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**LMN**: skull-base tumors, carotid dissection, **carotid endarterectomy**, medial medullary stroke, ALS (bulbar, with fasciculations). **UMN**: contralateral cortical/corticobulbar stroke.'
    presentation: >-
      **LMN**: tongue **deviates toward the side of the lesion** on protrusion (the intact genioglossus pushes it over),
      with **ipsilateral atrophy and fasciculations**, dysarthria. **UMN**: the tongue deviates **away from the cortical
      lesion** (toward the weak side of the body) without atrophy; often mild because of partly bilateral input.
  - title: Medial medullary syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Occlusion of the **anterior spinal artery** or vertebral branches supplying the medial medulla.
    presentation: '**Ipsilateral tongue deviation/atrophy (XII)**, **contralateral hemiparesis** (pyramid) and **contralateral loss of vibration and proprioception** (medial lemniscus).'
pance:
  - "Tongue **deviates toward** a CN XII LMN lesion (\"lick your wounds\")."
  - "Tongue atrophy + fasciculations → LMN (e.g. **ALS**)."
  - "Ipsilateral tongue weakness + contralateral hemiparesis + contralateral loss of proprioception → **medial medullary syndrome**."
  - "Tongue deviation after carotid endarterectomy → CN XII injury."
imaging:
  mri: MRI brain and skull base (hypoglossal canal), with MRA or CTA of the neck when carotid dissection is possible.
exam:
  testing: >-
    Inspect the tongue **at rest in the mouth** (atrophy, fasciculations — look at rest, since protrusion causes normal
    tremor), then on **protrusion** (deviation), side-to-side movement and pushing against the cheek; speech ("la-la-la").
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A patient has sudden right-sided weakness and loss of vibration sense in the right limbs. Her tongue deviates to the
      left on protrusion. Where is the lesion?
    choices:
      - Right medial medulla
      - Left medial medulla
      - Left lateral medulla
      - Right internal capsule
      - Left cerebellar hemisphere
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Left medial medullary syndrome**: left CN XII (tongue deviates to the left), left pyramid (right hemiparesis — the
      pyramids decussate below) and left medial lemniscus (right-sided loss of vibration/proprioception — already
      crossed).
  - stem: Which tongue muscle is NOT supplied by the hypoglossal nerve?
    choices:
      - Genioglossus
      - Hyoglossus
      - Styloglossus
      - Palatoglossus
      - Intrinsic longitudinal muscles
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      **Palatoglossus** is supplied by the **vagus (pharyngeal plexus)**; all other tongue muscles are supplied by CN XII.
