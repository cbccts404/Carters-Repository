name: Internal capsule
type: organ
region: neuroanatomy
subregion: Cerebrum
taName: Capsula interna
aka: [Posterior limb of internal capsule, Lacunar infarct, Lacunar stroke]
summary: Compact band of white matter carrying motor and sensory fibers between the cortex and the brainstem. A small (lacunar) infarct here can cause a pure motor hemiparesis of the face, arm and leg.
tags: [cerebrum, stroke, white-matter]
systems: [neurologic]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Deep in each hemisphere, V-shaped in horizontal section, between the **caudate and thalamus** (medially) and the **lentiform nucleus** (laterally) — see [[basal-ganglia]], [[thalamus]].
  parts:
    - "**Anterior limb** (between caudate and lentiform): frontopontine and thalamocortical (anterior/mediodorsal) fibers"
    - "**Genu**: **corticobulbar** fibers (face, head)"
    - "**Posterior limb** (between thalamus and lentiform): **corticospinal** fibers (arm anterior, leg posterior) and **thalamocortical sensory radiations** (from VPL/VPM)"
    - "**Retrolenticular and sublenticular parts**: **optic radiations** and auditory radiations"
    - "Continues below as the **cerebral peduncle** of the [[midbrain]] and above as the corona radiata"
  bloodSupply: '**Lenticulostriate arteries** (from the **MCA**) — most of the capsule; **anterior choroidal artery** (from the ICA) — lower posterior limb; **recurrent artery of Heubner** (from the ACA) — anterior limb and genu. See [[cerebral-arteries]].'
  notes: Contains the [[corticospinal-tract]] and the sensory projections from the thalamus.
  relations: Because motor fibers for the whole half-body are packed tightly together, a **small lesion causes a large deficit** — contralateral face, arm and leg equally.
clinical:
  - title: Lacunar infarcts
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Occlusion of a single deep penetrating artery (lipohyalinosis) from **chronic hypertension** and **diabetes**; infarcts under 1.5 cm.
    presentation: >-
      Classic syndromes, **without cortical signs** (no aphasia, neglect or visual field loss): **pure motor
      hemiparesis** (posterior limb or basis pontis — contralateral face, arm and leg equally); **pure sensory stroke**
      (thalamus); **sensorimotor stroke** (thalamus + capsule); **ataxic hemiparesis** and **dysarthria–clumsy hand
      syndrome** (capsule or pons).
  - title: Hypertensive intracerebral hemorrhage
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Rupture of lenticulostriate vessels in the putamen or thalamus extending into the capsule.
    presentation: Sudden contralateral hemiplegia and sensory loss, headache, vomiting, declining consciousness; very high blood pressure. Noncontrast CT; lower BP, reverse anticoagulation.
pance:
  - "Pure motor hemiparesis of **face, arm and leg equally**, no cortical signs → **lacunar infarct** of the posterior limb."
  - "Lacunes → **hypertension and diabetes** (small vessel disease)."
  - "Cortical signs (aphasia, neglect, field cut, gaze deviation) → cortical (large vessel) stroke, not lacunar."
imaging:
  ct: Noncontrast CT excludes hemorrhage; small acute lacunes are often invisible early.
  mri: '**DWI MRI** shows acute lacunar infarcts; T2/FLAIR shows chronic small vessel disease (white matter hyperintensities, old lacunes).'
exam:
  testing: 'Compare face, arm and leg strength (equal involvement suggests capsular lesion; face/arm > leg suggests MCA cortex); check for cortical signs — language, neglect, visual fields, cortical sensation; upper motor neuron signs (hyperreflexia, Babinski).'
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 66-year-old with long-standing hypertension develops weakness of the left face, arm and leg to the same degree.
      Sensation, language, visual fields and attention are normal. Where is the most likely lesion?
    choices:
      - Right MCA cortex
      - Right posterior limb of the internal capsule
      - Left thalamus
      - Right ACA territory
      - Left lateral medulla
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Pure motor hemiparesis** of the face, arm and leg equally, with no cortical signs, is a **lacunar infarct** of the
      **posterior limb of the internal capsule** (lenticulostriate branch of the MCA).
  - stem: Which fibers pass through the genu of the internal capsule?
    choices:
      - Optic radiations
      - Corticobulbar fibers
      - Corticospinal fibers to the leg
      - Auditory radiations
      - Thalamocortical fibers from VPL
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **genu** carries **corticobulbar** fibers to cranial nerve motor nuclei. Corticospinal fibers and sensory
      radiations are in the posterior limb; optic radiations are retrolenticular.
flashcards:

  - front: Fibers in the posterior limb?
    back: Corticospinal and thalamocortical sensory fibers
  - front: Risk factors for lacunar infarcts?
    back: Hypertension and diabetes
