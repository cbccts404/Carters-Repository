---
name: Spinothalamic tract
type: nerve
region: neuroanatomy
subregion: Spinal tracts
taName: Tractus spinothalamicus
aka: [Anterolateral system, Lateral spinothalamic tract, Syringomyelia]
summary: Ascending pathway for pain, temperature and crude touch. Its fibers cross within one or two segments of entering the cord, so cord lesions cause contralateral loss; syringomyelia damages the crossing fibers.
tags: [spinal-tracts, sensation, pain]
systems: [neurologic]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  origin: First-order neurons in the **dorsal root ganglia**; small, thinly myelinated (Aδ) and unmyelinated (C) fibers.
  course: >-
    Enter the cord, ascend or descend **1–2 segments in Lissauer tract**, and synapse in the **dorsal horn** (substantia
    gelatinosa and nucleus proprius). Second-order fibers **cross in the anterior white commissure** and ascend in the
    **contralateral anterolateral column** as the spinothalamic tract → through the lateral brainstem (lateral medulla,
    beside the spinal trigeminal tract) → **VPL of the [[thalamus]]** → somatosensory cortex.
  sensory: '**Pain, temperature** and **crude (non-discriminative) touch** and pressure from the contralateral body.'
  relations: >-
    **Somatotopy**: sacral fibers lie **most lateral** and cervical fibers most medial — expanding intramedullary tumors
    may spare sacral sensation ("sacral sparing"). Supplied by the **anterior spinal artery** (lost in anterior cord
    syndrome — see [[spinal-cord]]).
  notes: Pain and temperature from the **face** travel separately in the **spinal trigeminal tract**, which descends ipsilaterally in the lateral medulla — hence crossed face/body loss in Wallenberg syndrome (see [[medulla-oblongata]]).
clinical:
  - title: Syringomyelia
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Fluid-filled cavity (syrinx) in the central cord, usually cervical, often with **Chiari I malformation** or after trauma; damages the **crossing fibers in the anterior white commissure**.
    presentation: '**Bilateral "cape-like" loss of pain and temperature** over the shoulders and arms with **preserved touch and vibration** (dissociated sensory loss); painless burns of the hands; later LMN weakness and atrophy of the hands (ventral horns) and UMN signs in the legs. MRI of the cervical cord.'
  - title: Brown-Séquard and anterior cord syndromes
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Cord hemisection; anterior spinal artery infarction.
    presentation: '**Brown-Séquard**: **contralateral** pain/temperature loss starting 1–2 segments below the lesion, with ipsilateral weakness and dorsal column loss. **Anterior cord**: bilateral pain/temperature loss and paralysis below the lesion with preserved vibration/proprioception.'
  - title: Lateral medullary syndrome
    highYield: false
    mechanism: PICA/vertebral occlusion affecting the spinothalamic tract and spinal trigeminal nucleus.
    presentation: Contralateral body and ipsilateral face loss of pain and temperature.
pance:
  - "Spinothalamic fibers cross **within 1–2 segments** → cord lesions give **contralateral** pain/temp loss."
  - "Cape-like bilateral loss of pain/temp in the arms with preserved touch → **syringomyelia** (often Chiari I)."
  - "Brown-Séquard: ipsilateral motor + vibration loss, **contralateral** pain/temp loss."
  - "Face pain/temp = spinal trigeminal (ipsilateral in the medulla)."
imaging:
  mri: '**MRI of the cervical and thoracic cord** (and craniocervical junction) — syrinx, Chiari malformation, tumors, infarcts.'
exam:
  testing: >-
    **Pinprick** (disposable pin) and **temperature** (cold tuning fork) — map the level and side of loss, comparing
    dermatomes; look for a **sensory level** on the trunk (cord lesion) and for dissociated loss (pain lost, touch
    preserved).
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 30-year-old has painless burns on both hands. Pain and temperature sensation is lost over both shoulders and arms,
      but light touch and vibration are normal. MRI shows a Chiari I malformation. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Brown-Séquard syndrome
      - Syringomyelia
      - Anterior spinal artery syndrome
      - Subacute combined degeneration
      - Carpal tunnel syndrome
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A central cervical **syrinx** interrupts the **crossing spinothalamic fibers** in the anterior white commissure,
      causing **bilateral cape-like** pain/temperature loss with preserved dorsal column sensation.
  - stem: A right-sided hemisection of the cord at T10 causes loss of pain and temperature where?
    choices:
      - Right side below T10
      - Left side starting about T11–T12
      - Both sides below T10
      - Right side above T10
      - Left side above T10
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Spinothalamic fibers cross within 1–2 segments of entry, so a right hemisection causes **contralateral (left)** pain
      and temperature loss starting **1–2 levels below** the lesion.
  - stem: Where do the second-order spinothalamic fibers cross the midline?
    choices:
      - In the medulla
      - In the internal capsule
      - In the anterior white commissure of the spinal cord
      - In the thalamus
      - They don't cross
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      Second-order fibers cross in the **anterior white commissure** within 1–2 segments and ascend in the contralateral
      anterolateral column. A central syrinx damages these crossing fibers.
  - stem: Why can an expanding intramedullary spinal cord tumor spare sacral pain and temperature sensation?
    choices:
      - Sacral fibers cross higher in the cord
      - Sacral sensation travels in the dorsal columns
      - Sacral fibers use the spinal trigeminal tract
      - Sacral fibers bypass the thalamus
      - Sacral fibers lie most laterally in the spinothalamic tract
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      In the spinothalamic tract, **sacral fibers lie most laterally** and cervical fibers most medially, so a tumor
      expanding from the center compresses cervical fibers first (**sacral sparing**).
  - stem: >-
      Before synapsing in the dorsal horn, pain and temperature fibers travel up or down 1–2 segments in which tract?
    choices:
      - Fasciculus gracilis
      - Dorsolateral tract (of Lissauer)
      - Medial longitudinal fasciculus
      - Lateral corticospinal tract
      - Spinocerebellar tract
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Pain and temperature fibers run 1–2 segments in **Lissauer tract** before synapsing, which is why the
      contralateral sensory level in a hemisection starts 1–2 segments below the lesion.
