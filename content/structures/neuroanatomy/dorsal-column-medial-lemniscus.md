---
name: Dorsal column–medial lemniscus pathway
type: nerve
region: neuroanatomy
subregion: Spinal tracts
taName: Funiculus posterior; Lemniscus medialis
aka: [DCML, Dorsal columns, Posterior columns, Fasciculus gracilis, Fasciculus cuneatus, Medial lemniscus]
summary: Ascending pathway for fine touch, vibration and proprioception. It runs up the same side of the spinal cord and crosses in the medulla, so cord lesions cause ipsilateral loss and a positive Romberg sign.
tags: [spinal-tracts, sensation]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  origin: First-order neurons in the **dorsal root ganglia**; large myelinated fibers from skin, joints and muscle spindles.
  course: >-
    Enter the cord and ascend **ipsilaterally without synapsing** in the dorsal columns: **fasciculus gracilis** (medial
    — lower body, below about T6) and **fasciculus cuneatus** (lateral — upper body, above T6) → synapse in the
    **nucleus gracilis and nucleus cuneatus** of the lower [[medulla-oblongata]] (second-order) → fibers **cross as the
    internal arcuate fibers (sensory decussation)** → ascend as the **medial lemniscus** → **VPL of the [[thalamus]]**
    (third-order) → posterior limb of the [[internal-capsule]] → **primary somatosensory cortex** (postcentral gyrus; see
    [[cerebral-cortex]]).
  sensory: '**Fine (discriminative) touch, two-point discrimination, vibration, conscious proprioception** (joint position).'
  relations: Supplied by the **posterior spinal arteries** (spared in anterior spinal artery syndrome — see [[spinal-cord]]).
  notes: Rule of thumb — the pathway crosses in the **medulla**, so a **spinal cord** lesion causes **ipsilateral** loss, while a lesion **above the medulla** (brainstem, thalamus, cortex) causes **contralateral** loss.
clinical:
  - title: Subacute combined degeneration
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Vitamin B12 deficiency** (pernicious anemia, metformin, PPIs, vegan diet, gastric surgery) or nitrous oxide abuse, copper deficiency — demyelination of the **dorsal columns and lateral corticospinal tracts**.'
    presentation: 'Symmetric distal **loss of vibration and proprioception**, **sensory ataxia** with a **positive Romberg**, plus UMN signs (spasticity, Babinski) with reduced ankle reflexes (neuropathy); macrocytic anemia, raised methylmalonic acid. Treat with B12.'
  - title: Tabes dorsalis
    highYield: false
    mechanism: '**Tertiary syphilis** damaging the dorsal roots and columns.'
    presentation: Sensory ataxia, positive Romberg, lightning pains, absent reflexes, **Argyll Robertson pupils** (accommodate but do not react to light), Charcot joints.
  - title: Brown-Séquard syndrome (hemisection)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Hemisection of the cord (penetrating trauma, tumor, MS).
    presentation: '**Ipsilateral** loss of vibration and proprioception and **ipsilateral** UMN weakness below the lesion, with **contralateral** loss of pain and temperature starting 1–2 levels below (see [[spinothalamic-tract]]).'
pance:
  - "Loss of vibration/proprioception + positive Romberg + UMN signs + macrocytosis → **B12 deficiency** (subacute combined degeneration)."
  - "DCML crosses in the **medulla** → cord lesions give **ipsilateral** loss."
  - "Romberg positive → **sensory (proprioceptive) ataxia**, not cerebellar."
  - "Anterior spinal artery infarct **spares** the dorsal columns."
imaging:
  mri: '**MRI of the spinal cord** — T2 hyperintensity in the dorsal columns ("inverted V" sign) in B12 deficiency; MS plaques; compressive lesions.'
  keyViews:
    - "Serum B12, methylmalonic acid, homocysteine; syphilis serology"
    - "Somatosensory evoked potentials"
exam:
  testing: >-
    **Vibration** with a **128 Hz tuning fork** on the great toe, then more proximal bony points until felt;
    **proprioception** (move the great toe up or down with the patient's eyes closed); two-point discrimination;
    **Romberg**; tandem gait.
  specialTests:
    - name: Romberg test
      technique: Feet together, arms at sides; observe with eyes open, then closed for about 30 seconds.
      positive: Balance is maintained with eyes open but lost with eyes closed.
      significance: Loss of **proprioception** (dorsal columns or large-fiber peripheral neuropathy).
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 62-year-old vegan woman has numb feet, unsteadiness in the dark, loss of vibration sense at the toes and a positive
      Romberg sign. She has brisk knee reflexes and extensor plantar responses. MCV is 110 fL. What is the most likely
      cause?
    choices:
      - Folate deficiency
      - Vitamin B12 deficiency
      - Diabetic neuropathy
      - Multiple sclerosis
      - Cerebellar degeneration
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Subacute combined degeneration** — **dorsal column** loss (vibration, proprioception, Romberg) plus **lateral
      corticospinal** signs, with macrocytosis — from **B12 deficiency**. Folate deficiency causes macrocytosis without
      these neurologic signs.
  - stem: A stab wound hemisects the left side of the spinal cord at T8. Where is vibration sense lost?
    choices:
      - Left side below T8
      - Right side below T8
      - Both sides below T8
      - Right side starting at T10
      - Left side at T8 only
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      The dorsal columns ascend **ipsilaterally** and cross only in the medulla, so a left hemisection causes **left-sided
      loss of vibration and proprioception** below the lesion. Pain and temperature are lost on the **right**.
flashcards:
  - front: Where does the DCML pathway cross?
    back: In the medulla (internal arcuate fibers, sensory decussation)
  - front: Fasciculus gracilis vs cuneatus?
    back: Gracilis = lower body (medial); cuneatus = upper body (lateral, above T6)
  - front: Modalities carried by the dorsal columns?
    back: Fine touch, vibration, proprioception, two-point discrimination
