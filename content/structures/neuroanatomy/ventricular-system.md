anatomy:
  location: Within the brain — two **lateral ventricles** in the cerebral hemispheres, the **third ventricle** between the thalami, the **cerebral aqueduct** through the midbrain and the **fourth ventricle** between the pons/medulla and cerebellum.
  boundaries:
    lateral ventricles: Frontal horn, body, occipital horn and **temporal horn** (the hippocampus lies in its floor); the caudate forms the lateral wall of the body
    third ventricle: Thalami (lateral walls), hypothalamus (floor), lamina terminalis (front)
    fourth ventricle: Pons and medulla (floor, rhomboid fossa), cerebellum (roof)
  contents:
    - "**CSF** — about **150 mL** total, produced at about **500 mL/day** (replaced 3–4 times daily), mainly by the **choroid plexus** (lateral, third and fourth ventricles)"
    - "Choroid plexus (ependymal cells over capillaries)"
  communications: >-
    Lateral ventricles → **interventricular foramina (of Monro)** → **third ventricle** → **cerebral aqueduct (of
    Sylvius)** → **fourth ventricle** → **median aperture (Magendie)** and two **lateral apertures (Luschka)** →
    **subarachnoid space** and cisterns (and the central canal of the cord) → absorbed through **arachnoid granulations**
    into the **superior sagittal sinus** (see [[dural-venous-sinuses]], [[cranial-meninges]]).
  notes: "CSF cushions the brain (buoyancy), removes metabolites and maintains a stable chemical environment. Normal opening pressure on lumbar puncture is about 6–20 cm H₂O (lateral decubitus). {{verify: upper limit quoted as 20 or 25 cm H₂O}}"
clinical:
  - title: Hydrocephalus
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Noncommunicating (obstructive)** — blockage within the ventricles: aqueductal stenosis, tumors (posterior fossa, colloid cyst of the third ventricle), Chiari II/Dandy–Walker. **Communicating** — impaired absorption at the arachnoid granulations: after **subarachnoid hemorrhage** or **meningitis**; normal pressure hydrocephalus.'
    presentation: '**Adults**: headache (worse in the morning), vomiting, papilledema, declining consciousness, CN VI palsy. **Infants**: **enlarging head circumference**, bulging fontanelle, splayed sutures, "**sunset eyes**" (upgaze palsy). Treat with a ventriculoperitoneal shunt or endoscopic third ventriculostomy; acute cases need an external ventricular drain.'
  - title: Normal pressure hydrocephalus
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Communicating hydrocephalus in older adults, often idiopathic; normal opening pressure on LP.
    presentation: 'Triad "**wet, wobbly and wacky**": **urinary incontinence**, **gait apraxia** (magnetic, shuffling, wide-based gait — usually first), and **dementia**. CT/MRI: ventricles enlarged out of proportion to atrophy. Improvement after **large-volume lumbar puncture** predicts benefit from a **VP shunt** — a reversible dementia.'
  - title: Colloid cyst of the third ventricle
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Benign cyst near the foramen of Monro that can intermittently block CSF outflow.
    presentation: Positional headaches, drop attacks and sudden death from acute hydrocephalus.
pance:
  - "Gait apraxia + incontinence + dementia → **NPH** → large-volume LP → **VP shunt**."
  - "Infant with rapidly enlarging head, bulging fontanelle and sunset eyes → **hydrocephalus** → ultrasound through the fontanelle."
  - "Hydrocephalus after SAH or meningitis → **communicating** (arachnoid granulations)."
  - "CSF path: lateral → Monro → third → aqueduct → fourth → Luschka/Magendie → subarachnoid → arachnoid granulations → superior sagittal sinus."
imaging:
  ct: Noncontrast CT — ventricular size, periventricular edema (transependymal flow), shunt problems.
  mri: MRI — cause of obstruction (aqueductal stenosis, tumor), CSF flow studies, NPH (enlarged ventricles, narrow high-convexity sulci).
  ultrasound: '**Cranial ultrasound** through the anterior fontanelle in infants.'
  keyViews:
    - "Shunt series (X-rays along the tubing) for suspected shunt malfunction"
exam:
  landmarks: '**Lumbar puncture** below the conus — at **L3–L4 or L4–L5** (the iliac crests mark about L4; see [[spinal-meninges]]).'
  testing: 'Head circumference (plot on growth charts), fontanelle, fundi (papilledema), gait (NPH: timed walk before and after LP), cognition; examine for shunt malfunction (headache, vomiting, lethargy).'
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 74-year-old man has had progressive urinary urgency and incontinence, forgetfulness, and a slow, shuffling gait
      in which his feet seem stuck to the floor. CT shows enlarged ventricles without proportional sulcal widening. What
      is the most appropriate next step?
    choices:
      - Start donepezil
      - Large-volume lumbar puncture with gait assessment
      - Levodopa trial only
      - Emergency craniotomy
      - Brain biopsy
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      This is **normal pressure hydrocephalus** (wet, wobbly, wacky). Clinical improvement after **large-volume CSF
      removal** supports a **VP shunt**, which can reverse symptoms.
  - stem: >-
      An MRI shows dilated lateral and third ventricles and a normal-sized fourth ventricle. Where is the obstruction most
      likely?
    choices:
      - Foramen of Monro on one side
      - Cerebral aqueduct
      - Foramina of Luschka and Magendie
      - Arachnoid granulations
      - Central canal of the spinal cord
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Dilation **proximal** to the aqueduct (lateral and third ventricles) with a **normal fourth ventricle** localizes the
      block to the **cerebral aqueduct** (aqueductal stenosis).
  - stem: Where is most cerebrospinal fluid produced?
    choices:
      - Arachnoid granulations
      - Superior sagittal sinus
      - Choroid plexus of the ventricles
      - Ependyma of the central canal only
      - Dura mater
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      CSF (about 500 mL/day, about 150 mL at a time) is made mainly by the **choroid plexus** of the lateral, third and
      fourth ventricles and absorbed through arachnoid granulations into the superior sagittal sinus.
  - stem: Hydrocephalus after subarachnoid hemorrhage or meningitis is usually caused by what?
    choices:
      - Aqueductal stenosis
      - Overproduction of CSF
      - A colloid cyst
      - Impaired absorption at the arachnoid granulations (communicating hydrocephalus)
      - Brain atrophy
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      Blood or pus impairs **absorption at the arachnoid granulations**, causing **communicating** hydrocephalus. Blocks
      within the ventricular system cause noncommunicating hydrocephalus.
  - stem: Which finding in an infant suggests hydrocephalus?
    choices:
      - Sunken fontanelle
      - Rapidly enlarging head circumference with "sunset eyes"
      - Early fontanelle closure
      - Small head circumference
      - Brisk Moro reflex
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Infant hydrocephalus causes **rapidly enlarging head circumference**, a bulging fontanelle, splayed sutures and
      **"sunset eyes"** (upgaze palsy). Ultrasound through the fontanelle is the first test.
