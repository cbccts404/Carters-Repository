name: Trochlear nerve (CN IV)
type: nerve
region: neuroanatomy
subregion: Cranial nerves
taName: Nervus trochlearis
aka: [CN IV, Cranial nerve IV, Fourth nerve palsy]
summary: Thinnest cranial nerve and the only one to exit the back of the brainstem. It supplies the superior oblique; a palsy causes vertical diplopia worse on looking down and a compensatory head tilt away from the affected side.
tags: [cranial-nerves, eye]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  origin: '**Trochlear nucleus** in the [[midbrain]] at the level of the inferior colliculus.'
  course: >-
    Fibers **decussate** and emerge from the **dorsal surface** of the brainstem just below the inferior colliculus —
    the only cranial nerve to do so — so each nucleus supplies the **contralateral** superior oblique. The nerve winds
    around the cerebral peduncle (the **longest intracranial course**), runs in the **lateral wall of the cavernous
    sinus** (see [[dural-venous-sinuses]]) and enters the [[orbit]] through the **superior orbital fissure outside
    the common tendinous ring**.
  motor: '**Superior oblique** only (see [[extraocular-muscles]]) — **depresses the adducted eye**, and intorts and abducts it.'
  relations: Its long, thin intracranial course along the tentorial edge makes it vulnerable to **head trauma**.
clinical:
  - title: Fourth nerve palsy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Head trauma** (most common acquired cause), congenital (decompensates in adulthood), microvascular ischemia, and less often tumors.'
    presentation: >-
      **Vertical (and oblique) diplopia**, worst when **looking down and in** — reading or **walking down stairs**. The
      affected eye is **higher (hypertropia)**, especially in adduction. Patients **tilt the head away from the affected
      side** to compensate (intorting the unaffected eye). Old photos showing a longstanding head tilt suggest a congenital palsy.
pance:
  - "Diplopia going **down stairs** or reading → **CN IV** (superior oblique)."
  - "Head tilt **away** from the affected side."
  - "CN IV: only nerve exiting the **dorsal** brainstem; crosses → supplies the **contralateral** SO."
  - "Most common acquired cause → **head trauma**."
imaging:
  mri: MRI brain and orbits if there is no trauma or vascular explanation, or if other neurologic signs are present.
exam:
  testing: >-
    Ask the patient to look **down and in** (toward the nose) — isolates the superior oblique. Note hypertropia on the
    cover test.
  specialTests:
    - name: Parks–Bielschowsky three-step test
      technique: 'Identify (1) which eye is higher in primary gaze, (2) whether the deviation worsens on looking left or right, and (3) whether it worsens with head tilt to the left or right.'
      positive: For a right CN IV palsy — right hypertropia, worse on **left gaze** and on **right head tilt**.
      significance: Localizes a vertical deviation to a single paretic muscle, most often the superior oblique.
