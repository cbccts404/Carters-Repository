name: Olfactory nerve (CN I)
type: nerve
region: neuroanatomy
subregion: Cranial nerves
taName: Nervus olfactorius
aka: [CN I, Cranial nerve I, Olfactory bulb, Olfactory tract]
summary: Special sensory nerve for smell. Its fibers pass through the cribriform plate to the olfactory bulb, so head trauma and anterior fossa tumors cause anosmia.
tags: [cranial-nerves, special-senses]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  origin: >-
    Bipolar olfactory receptor neurons in the **olfactory epithelium** in the roof of the [[nasal-cavity]]. Their axons
    (about 20 bundles, the "olfactory nerves" proper) pass through the **cribriform plate of the ethmoid** to synapse in the
    **olfactory bulb**.
  course: >-
    Olfactory bulb → **olfactory tract** along the orbital surface of the frontal lobe → lateral olfactory stria →
    primary olfactory cortex (piriform cortex, uncus/entorhinal area of the medial temporal lobe) and the amygdala.
    **Olfaction is the only sense that does not relay through the thalamus before reaching the cortex.**
  sensory: Smell (special visceral afferent).
  relations: The olfactory bulb and tract lie in the olfactory sulcus under the frontal lobe, close to the olfactory groove (a site of meningiomas) and above the cribriform plate.
  notes: Olfactory receptor neurons are replaced throughout life, one of the few neurons in the adult to regenerate.
clinical:
  - title: Anosmia
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Most commonly after **viral URI** (including COVID-19) and chronic rhinosinusitis/nasal polyps (conductive loss);
      **head trauma** shearing the fibers at the cribriform plate; early **Parkinson disease** and **Alzheimer
      disease**; **Kallmann syndrome** (anosmia + hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, failed GnRH neuron migration).
    presentation: Loss of smell and often reduced "taste" (flavor depends on smell). Patients may not notice gas leaks or spoiled food — a safety issue.
  - title: CSF rhinorrhea
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Fracture of the **cribriform plate** (anterior skull base) tearing the dura.
    presentation: Clear watery nasal discharge after head trauma, worse leaning forward; positive for **beta-2 transferrin**; risk of meningitis. Often with anosmia and periorbital ecchymosis ("raccoon eyes").
  - title: Olfactory groove meningioma (Foster Kennedy syndrome)
    highYield: false
    mechanism: A mass under the frontal lobe compressing the olfactory tract and one optic nerve while raising intracranial pressure.
    presentation: '**Anosmia**, **ipsilateral optic atrophy** and **contralateral papilledema**; personality change from frontal lobe compression.'
pance:
  - "Anosmia + hypogonadotropic hypogonadism → **Kallmann syndrome**."
  - "Clear nasal discharge after head trauma → **CSF rhinorrhea** (cribriform plate) → beta-2 transferrin."
  - "Loss of smell can precede motor signs in **Parkinson disease**."
  - "Smell is the only sense that bypasses the thalamus."
imaging:
  ct: Thin-section CT of the skull base for cribriform plate fractures and CSF leak localization.
  mri: MRI brain with attention to the olfactory bulbs and frontal lobes (tumors, Kallmann syndrome — absent or small bulbs).
exam:
  testing: >-
    Test **each nostril separately** with the eyes closed, occluding the other nostril, using a familiar
    **non-irritating** scent (coffee, soap, peppermint). Avoid ammonia or alcohol pads, which stimulate trigeminal pain
    fibers (CN V) rather than smell. Examine the nose first to exclude obstruction.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 17-year-old boy has not entered puberty. He has never been able to smell. LH and FSH are low. What is the most
      likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Klinefelter syndrome
      - Kallmann syndrome
      - Prolactinoma
      - Constitutional delay only
      - Androgen insensitivity syndrome
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Kallmann syndrome** — failed migration of GnRH neurons (which travel with olfactory neurons from the olfactory
      placode) — causes **hypogonadotropic hypogonadism with anosmia**. Klinefelter causes high LH/FSH.
  - stem: Why should ammonia not be used to test the olfactory nerve?
    choices:
      - It damages the olfactory epithelium permanently
      - It stimulates trigeminal (CN V) pain fibers in the nasal mucosa
      - It is only detected by the glossopharyngeal nerve
      - It is too weak to smell
      - It stimulates the vagus nerve and causes bradycardia
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Irritants like ammonia are sensed by **trigeminal** fibers, so a patient with complete anosmia may still react.
      Use a familiar, non-irritating scent.
