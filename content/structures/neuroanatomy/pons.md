summary: Middle part of the brainstem that links the cerebrum to the cerebellum. It holds the nuclei of CN V–VIII and horizontal gaze centers; basilar artery occlusion causes locked-in syndrome.
tags: [brainstem, eye-movements, stroke]
systems: [neurologic]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Between the [[midbrain]] and the [[medulla-oblongata]], in front of the [[cerebellum]] and the fourth ventricle, resting on the clivus.
  parts:
    - "**Basilar (ventral) pons**: descending **corticospinal** and corticobulbar fibers, **pontine nuclei**, and transverse pontocerebellar fibers forming the **middle cerebellar peduncle**"
    - "**Tegmentum (dorsal)**: nuclei of **CN V** (motor and principal sensory), **VI**, **VII** and **VIII** (vestibular and cochlear); the **PPRF** (paramedian pontine reticular formation — horizontal gaze center); **MLF**; medial lemniscus and spinothalamic tract; **locus coeruleus** (norepinephrine)"
    - "Floor of the **fourth ventricle** with the **facial colliculus** (CN VII fibers looping around the CN VI nucleus)"
    - "Respiratory centers (pneumotaxic and apneustic) that modulate medullary rhythm"
  bloodSupply: '**Basilar artery** — paramedian and short circumferential pontine branches; **AICA** (lateral lower pons) and **SCA** (upper pons) — see [[vertebrobasilar-system]].'
  venousDrainage: Pontine veins → superior petrosal sinus and basal veins.
  notes: Emergence of CN V (lateral pons), CN VI (pontomedullary junction, medially), CN VII and VIII (cerebellopontine angle) — see [[trigeminal-nerve]], [[abducens-nerve]], [[facial-nerve]], [[vestibulocochlear-nerve]].
  relations: The basilar artery runs in the basilar groove on its anterior surface.
clinical:
  - title: Locked-in syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Basilar artery occlusion** (or hemorrhage) destroying the **ventral pons** — corticospinal and corticobulbar tracts — while sparing the tegmentum (reticular activating system) above.'
    presentation: '**Quadriplegia and anarthria** with **preserved consciousness**; **vertical eye movements and blinking are preserved** (midbrain) and used to communicate. Sudden basilar occlusion can present with coma, and needs emergency thrombectomy.'
  - title: Osmotic demyelination syndrome (central pontine myelinolysis)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Overly rapid correction of chronic hyponatremia** (risk: alcohol use disorder, malnutrition, liver disease, hypokalemia).'
    presentation: 'Days after correction: dysarthria, dysphagia, **quadriparesis**, locked-in state. Prevent by correcting sodium no faster than about **8 mEq/L per 24 hours** in high-risk patients (10–12 limit otherwise).'
  - title: Pontine hemorrhage and gaze palsies
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Hypertensive hemorrhage from paramedian perforators; lesions of the PPRF, abducens nucleus or MLF.
    presentation: 'Pontine hemorrhage: coma, quadriplegia, **pinpoint reactive pupils**, hyperthermia. **PPRF/abducens nucleus lesion** → ipsilateral horizontal gaze palsy. **MLF** → internuclear ophthalmoplegia (MS). "**One-and-a-half syndrome**" → both.'
pance:
  - "Awake, quadriplegic, communicates by vertical eye movements/blinking → **locked-in syndrome** (ventral pons, basilar)."
  - "Rapid correction of hyponatremia → **osmotic demyelination** → ≤ 8 mEq/L/24 h in high-risk patients."
  - "Coma + **pinpoint pupils** → pontine hemorrhage (or opioids)."
  - "Pons houses nuclei of **CN V, VI, VII, VIII**."
imaging:
  mri: '**MRI with DWI** for pontine infarcts and demyelination (a "trident" or "bat-wing" T2 lesion in osmotic demyelination, which may appear only after 1–2 weeks); MRA/CTA for basilar occlusion.'
  ct: CT shows pontine hemorrhage; small infarcts are often missed in the posterior fossa.
exam:
  testing: Corneal reflex, facial strength, horizontal gaze (PPRF, CN VI), hearing and vestibular function; limb strength and reflexes for corticospinal involvement; level of consciousness and pupil size.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 60-year-old man is found unable to move his limbs or speak. He is alert and answers yes/no questions by looking
      up and down, but cannot move his eyes horizontally. Which artery is most likely occluded?
    choices:
      - Middle cerebral artery
      - Basilar artery
      - Posterior inferior cerebellar artery
      - Anterior cerebral artery
      - Anterior spinal artery
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Locked-in syndrome** from **basilar artery** occlusion infarcts the ventral pons (motor tracts) and the horizontal
      gaze centers, sparing consciousness and midbrain-controlled vertical gaze and blinking.
  - stem: >-
      A woman with alcohol use disorder has a sodium of 108 mEq/L, which rises to 128 mEq/L in 24 hours. Four days later
      she develops dysarthria and weakness of all limbs. What is the diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Wernicke encephalopathy
      - Osmotic demyelination syndrome
      - Guillain–Barré syndrome
      - Hepatic encephalopathy
      - Cerebellar degeneration
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Correcting chronic hyponatremia by **20 mEq/L in 24 hours** is far too fast and causes **osmotic demyelination
      (central pontine myelinolysis)** — dysarthria, dysphagia and quadriparesis days later.
  - stem: >-
      A comatose patient has pinpoint but reactive pupils and quadriplegia after sudden collapse. Where is the likely
      lesion (if opioids are excluded)?
    choices:
      - Midbrain
      - Pons (hemorrhage)
      - Medulla
      - Occipital lobe
      - Thalamus
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Pontine hemorrhage** causes coma, quadriplegia and **pinpoint reactive pupils** (interrupted descending
      sympathetic fibers). Opioid overdose is the main mimic.
  - stem: The locus coeruleus in the pons is the main source of which neurotransmitter?
    choices:
      - Dopamine
      - Serotonin
      - Acetylcholine
      - Norepinephrine
      - GABA
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      The **locus coeruleus** is the brain's main source of **norepinephrine**. The substantia nigra supplies dopamine
      and the raphe nuclei serotonin.
  - stem: What is the function of the paramedian pontine reticular formation (PPRF)?
    choices:
      - Horizontal gaze center
      - Vertical gaze center
      - Respiratory rhythm generator
      - Chemoreceptor trigger zone
      - Taste relay
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      The **PPRF** is the **horizontal gaze center**, driving the ipsilateral abducens nucleus. Vertical gaze is
      controlled in the midbrain.
