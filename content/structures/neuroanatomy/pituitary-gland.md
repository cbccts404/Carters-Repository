summary: Master endocrine gland in the sella turcica below the optic chiasm, with anterior (hormone-producing) and posterior (ADH, oxytocin) lobes. Adenomas cause hormone excess, hypopituitarism and bitemporal hemianopia.
tags: [endocrine, skull-base]

highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: In the **sella turcica (hypophyseal fossa)** of the sphenoid bone, roofed by the **diaphragma sellae**, connected to the [[hypothalamus]] by the infundibular stalk.
  parts:
    - "**Anterior lobe (adenohypophysis)** — from **Rathke pouch** (oral ectoderm): **GH**, **prolactin**, **ACTH**, **TSH**, **FSH** and **LH**; controlled by hypothalamic releasing hormones via the **hypophyseal portal system** (prolactin is mainly under **inhibitory dopamine** control)"
    - "**Posterior lobe (neurohypophysis)** — from neuroectoderm (downgrowth of the diencephalon): stores and releases **ADH** and **oxytocin** made in the hypothalamus"
    - "Intermediate lobe — rudimentary in humans"
  bloodSupply: '**Superior and inferior hypophyseal arteries** (from the internal carotid). The anterior lobe receives most of its blood through the **portal veins** — it has a vulnerable, low-pressure supply (Sheehan syndrome).'
  venousDrainage: Hypophyseal veins → **cavernous sinuses** (inferior petrosal sinus sampling is used to localize ACTH secretion).
  innervation: Posterior lobe — hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract (axons of supraoptic and paraventricular neurons); anterior lobe — hormonal, not neural, control.
  relations: >-
    **Above**: the **optic chiasm** (see [[optic-nerve]]). **Laterally**: the **cavernous sinuses** with the internal
    carotid arteries and **CN III, IV, V1, V2 and VI** (see [[dural-venous-sinuses]]). **Below**: the sphenoid sinus
    (transsphenoidal surgical approach; see [[paranasal-sinuses]]).
clinical:
  - title: Pituitary adenomas
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Benign tumors — **prolactinoma** (most common functioning adenoma), GH-secreting (acromegaly/gigantism), ACTH-secreting (**Cushing disease**), nonfunctioning macroadenomas; MEN 1.
    presentation: >-
      **Hormone excess**: prolactinoma — galactorrhea, amenorrhea, infertility, low libido, ED; GH — enlarged hands and
      feet, coarse facies, jaw growth, sweating (check **IGF-1**, confirm with failure of GH suppression on OGTT); ACTH —
      Cushing syndrome. **Mass effect** (macroadenomas, 1 cm or larger): headache, **bitemporal hemianopia**,
      **hypopituitarism**, CN palsies (cavernous sinus), mildly raised prolactin from **stalk compression**.
      **Prolactinoma → dopamine agonist (cabergoline) first**; most others → **transsphenoidal surgery**.
  - title: Pituitary apoplexy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Sudden hemorrhage or infarction of a (often unknown) pituitary adenoma.
    presentation: '**Sudden severe headache**, visual loss, **ophthalmoplegia** (cavernous sinus), altered consciousness and **acute adrenal insufficiency** (hypotension). Emergency: **IV hydrocortisone**, then decompression if vision is threatened.'
  - title: Sheehan syndrome and hypopituitarism
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Postpartum hemorrhage** → ischemic necrosis of the enlarged anterior pituitary; also tumors, surgery, radiation, trauma.'
    presentation: '**Failure to lactate**, amenorrhea, fatigue, hypotension, hypothyroidism, loss of pubic/axillary hair after a complicated delivery. Replace **cortisol first**, then thyroid hormone.'
pance:
  - "Galactorrhea + amenorrhea → check **prolactin** (and pregnancy, TSH, medications) → **cabergoline** for prolactinoma."
  - "Big hands/feet, coarse features → **acromegaly** → IGF-1, then OGTT GH suppression."
  - "Sudden headache + visual loss + ophthalmoplegia + hypotension → **pituitary apoplexy** → IV hydrocortisone."
  - "No lactation after postpartum hemorrhage → **Sheehan syndrome**."
  - "Hypopituitarism → give **glucocorticoid before thyroid hormone**."
imaging:
  mri: '**MRI of the pituitary (sella) with gadolinium** — microadenomas (under 1 cm) and macroadenomas, stalk, chiasm and cavernous sinus invasion.'
  ct: CT for hemorrhage in suspected apoplexy if MRI is unavailable, and bone anatomy before surgery.
  keyViews:
    - "Dedicated pituitary protocol MRI (thin coronal slices)"
    - "Formal visual field testing"
exam:
  testing: '**Visual fields by confrontation** (bitemporal defects), eye movements (cavernous sinus), signs of hormone excess (acromegaly, Cushing, galactorrhea) or deficiency (hypotension, hypothyroidism, hypogonadism).'
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 28-year-old woman has amenorrhea and milky nipple discharge. Pregnancy test is negative, TSH is normal, and prolactin
      is 180 ng/mL. MRI shows a 7-mm pituitary lesion. What is the first-line treatment?
    choices:
      - Transsphenoidal surgery
      - Cabergoline
      - Radiation therapy
      - Octreotide
      - Observation only
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **prolactinoma** is treated first with a **dopamine agonist (cabergoline)**, which shrinks the tumor and
      normalizes prolactin in most patients. Surgery is for intolerance or resistance.
  - stem: >-
      A 45-year-old man has a sudden severe headache, double vision and hypotension. MRI shows hemorrhage into a pituitary
      macroadenoma. What is the most urgent treatment?
    choices:
      - Levothyroxine
      - IV hydrocortisone
      - Cabergoline
      - Lumbar puncture
      - Oral desmopressin
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Pituitary apoplexy** causes **acute ACTH deficiency**; hypotension requires **stress-dose IV hydrocortisone**.
      Surgical decompression follows if vision or consciousness is affected.
