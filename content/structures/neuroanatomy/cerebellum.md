subregion: Brainstem & cerebellum
taName: Cerebellum
aka: [Cerebellar vermis, Cerebellar peduncles, Dentate nucleus, Cerebellar ataxia]
summary: Coordinates movement, balance and eye movements. Hemisphere lesions cause ipsilateral limb ataxia, vermis lesions cause truncal and gait ataxia, and cerebellar strokes can swell and compress the brainstem.
tags: [cerebellum, movement, stroke]
systems: [neurologic]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: In the **posterior cranial fossa** under the tentorium cerebelli, behind the [[pons]], [[medulla-oblongata]] and the fourth ventricle.
  parts:
    - "**Vermis** (midline) — trunk, posture, gait (spinocerebellum); anterior vermis damaged by alcohol"
    - "**Hemispheres** (lateral) — planning and coordination of **ipsilateral** limb movements (cerebrocerebellum)"
    - "**Flocculonodular lobe** — balance and eye movements (vestibulocerebellum)"
    - "**Deep nuclei** (lateral to medial): **dentate**, emboliform, globose, fastigial (\"Don't Eat Greasy Foods\")"
    - "**Peduncles**: **superior** (mainly output — dentate → contralateral red nucleus and **thalamus (VL)** → motor cortex); **middle** (input from the contralateral pontine nuclei — the largest); **inferior** (input from the spinal cord, vestibular nuclei and inferior olive)"
    - "**Cortex**: Purkinje cells — the sole output of the cortex, **inhibitory (GABA)** onto the deep nuclei; **tonsils** above the foramen magnum"
  bloodSupply: '**Superior cerebellar artery (SCA)**, **anterior inferior cerebellar artery (AICA)** and **posterior inferior cerebellar artery (PICA)** — see [[vertebrobasilar-system]].'
  venousDrainage: Cerebellar veins → straight, transverse and petrosal sinuses.
  relations: The **fourth ventricle** lies in front — cerebellar swelling obstructs it, causing hydrocephalus and brainstem compression.
  notes: Because output crosses to the opposite thalamus and cortex, whose corticospinal fibers cross back, **each cerebellar hemisphere controls the same side of the body** (lesions → **ipsilateral** signs).
clinical:
  - title: Cerebellar stroke or hemorrhage
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Infarction (PICA, SCA, AICA) or hypertensive hemorrhage.
    presentation: >-
      Sudden **vertigo, vomiting, headache, inability to stand or walk**, ipsilateral limb ataxia, nystagmus, dysarthria.
      Can mimic peripheral vertigo (use HINTS). Swelling over 2–4 days can compress the **fourth ventricle and brainstem**
      → declining consciousness → **emergency suboccipital decompression** or ventricular drain.
  - title: Alcohol-related cerebellar degeneration
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Chronic alcohol use (± thiamine deficiency) → atrophy of the **anterior superior vermis**.
    presentation: '**Wide-based, unsteady gait and truncal ataxia** with relatively normal arm coordination.'
  - title: Posterior fossa tumors in children
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Medulloblastoma** (vermis, most common malignant brain tumor of childhood), **pilocytic astrocytoma** (hemisphere, cystic, benign), ependymoma (fourth ventricle).'
    presentation: Morning headache and vomiting (hydrocephalus), truncal ataxia (vermis) or limb ataxia (hemisphere), papilledema.
pance:
  - "Cerebellar lesions → **ipsilateral** signs."
  - "Vermis → truncal/gait ataxia; hemisphere → limb ataxia, intention tremor, dysdiadochokinesia."
  - "Vertigo + vomiting + can't walk → rule out **cerebellar stroke** (MRI, not CT)."
  - "Alcohol → **anterior vermis** → wide-based gait."
  - "Romberg is **negative** in pure cerebellar ataxia (unsteady with eyes open too); positive in dorsal column/sensory ataxia."
  - "Child with morning headaches, vomiting and ataxia → **medulloblastoma** (vermis)."
imaging:
  mri: '**MRI with DWI** is best for posterior fossa strokes and tumors.'
  ct: CT detects hemorrhage and hydrocephalus but can miss small posterior fossa infarcts.
exam:
  testing: >-
    **Finger-to-nose** (dysmetria, intention tremor), **heel-to-shin**, **rapid alternating movements**
    (dysdiadochokinesia), rebound; **gait** and **tandem walking** (truncal ataxia); speech (scanning dysarthria);
    **nystagmus** (gaze-evoked). Signs mnemonic **DANISH**: Dysdiadochokinesia, Ataxia, Nystagmus, Intention tremor,
    Slurred speech, Hypotonia.
  specialTests:
    - name: Romberg test
      technique: Patient stands with feet together, arms at the sides; observe with the eyes open, then closed for about 30 seconds.
      positive: Loses balance only when the eyes are **closed**.
      significance: '**Proprioceptive (dorsal column or peripheral sensory) ataxia** — e.g. B12 deficiency, tabes dorsalis, neuropathy. Patients with cerebellar ataxia are unsteady even with the eyes open, so Romberg is not a test of the cerebellum.'
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 58-year-old has sudden vertigo and vomiting. He cannot walk, and finger-to-nose testing shows marked overshooting
      with the right hand. Which area is affected?
    choices:
      - Left cerebellar hemisphere
      - Right cerebellar hemisphere
      - Cerebellar vermis only
      - Right vestibular nerve
      - Left internal capsule
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Right limb ataxia** points to the **right cerebellar hemisphere** — cerebellar lesions cause **ipsilateral**
      signs. Sudden onset with vomiting and inability to walk suggests a **cerebellar stroke**: obtain MRI and watch for
      swelling.
  - stem: >-
      A 55-year-old with chronic alcohol use has a wide-based unsteady gait. Finger-to-nose testing is normal. Which part of
      the cerebellum is most affected?
    choices:
      - Flocculonodular lobe
      - Lateral hemispheres
      - Anterior vermis
      - Dentate nucleus
      - Middle cerebellar peduncle
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      Alcohol preferentially damages the **anterior (superior) vermis**, which controls the trunk and gait, so gait ataxia
      occurs with relatively normal limb coordination.
