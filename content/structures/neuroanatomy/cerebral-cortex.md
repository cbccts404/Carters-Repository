summary: The folded gray matter of the cerebral hemispheres, organized into frontal, parietal, temporal and occipital lobes with specialized areas for movement, sensation, language and vision. Lesion location predicts the deficit.
tags: [cerebrum, language, stroke]

anatomy:
  location: Surface of the cerebral hemispheres in the anterior and middle cranial fossae; the **central sulcus** separates frontal from parietal lobes, the **lateral (Sylvian) fissure** separates the temporal lobe below.
  parts:
    - "**Frontal lobe**: **primary motor cortex** (precentral gyrus), premotor and supplementary motor areas, **frontal eye fields** (voluntary saccades to the opposite side), **prefrontal cortex** (executive function, judgment, personality), **Broca area** (inferior frontal gyrus, dominant hemisphere — speech production)"
    - "**Parietal lobe**: **primary somatosensory cortex** (postcentral gyrus); **dominant** inferior parietal lobule (angular and supramarginal gyri — reading, writing, calculation); **nondominant** parietal lobe (spatial attention — lesion → hemineglect)"
    - "**Temporal lobe**: **primary auditory cortex** (Heschl gyrus), **Wernicke area** (posterior superior temporal gyrus, dominant — language comprehension), medial temporal memory structures ([[limbic-system]]), Meyer loop of the optic radiation"
    - "**Occipital lobe**: **primary visual cortex (V1)** around the calcarine sulcus (see [[optic-nerve]])"
    - "**Arcuate fasciculus** connects Wernicke and Broca areas"
    - "**Homunculus**: the leg and foot lie on the **medial** surface (paracentral lobule); the trunk, arm, hand and face lie on the **lateral** surface, face lowest"
  bloodSupply: '**Anterior cerebral artery** (medial frontal and parietal — leg), **middle cerebral artery** (lateral surface — face, arm, language), **posterior cerebral artery** (occipital lobe, inferior temporal lobe) — see [[cerebral-arteries]].'
  venousDrainage: Superficial cerebral veins → superior sagittal and other [[dural-venous-sinuses]].
  relations: The **left hemisphere is dominant for language** in about 95% of right-handers and most left-handers.
clinical:
  - title: Aphasias
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Lesions of the dominant (usually left) hemisphere — most often **MCA** territory strokes.
    presentation: >-
      **Broca (expressive)**: **nonfluent**, effortful telegraphic speech, **comprehension intact**, poor repetition,
      patient aware and frustrated; often with right face/arm weakness (superior MCA division). **Wernicke
      (receptive)**: **fluent** but meaningless speech (paraphasias, neologisms), **poor comprehension**, poor
      repetition, patient unaware; often no weakness (inferior division). **Conduction** (arcuate fasciculus): fluent,
      comprehension intact, **repetition poor**. **Global**: all impaired (large MCA stroke).
  - title: Parietal lobe syndromes
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Stroke, tumor or trauma of the parietal lobe.
    presentation: '**Nondominant (right)**: **left hemineglect** (ignores the left side of space and body), anosognosia, constructional apraxia. **Dominant (left) angular gyrus**: **Gerstmann syndrome** — agraphia, acalculia, finger agnosia, left–right confusion. Either side: cortical sensory loss (astereognosis, agraphesthesia).'
  - title: Frontal lobe syndrome
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Frontal tumors (e.g. meningioma), trauma, frontotemporal dementia, ACA stroke.
    presentation: Personality change, disinhibition or apathy, poor judgment and planning, primitive reflexes (grasp), urinary incontinence, gaze deviation toward the lesion (frontal eye field) in acute strokes.
  - title: Focal seizures
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Abnormal cortical discharge; symptoms reflect the involved cortex.
    presentation: Motor cortex → clonic jerking that can "march" from hand to face to leg (**Jacksonian march**), followed by transient weakness (**Todd paralysis**); occipital → flashing lights; temporal → déjà vu, smells, automatisms.
pance:
  - "Nonfluent speech + intact comprehension → **Broca** (inferior frontal gyrus)."
  - "Fluent nonsense + poor comprehension → **Wernicke** (superior temporal gyrus)."
  - "Poor repetition only → **conduction aphasia** (arcuate fasciculus)."
  - "Left-sided neglect → **right parietal** lesion."
  - "Agraphia, acalculia, finger agnosia, L–R confusion → **Gerstmann** (dominant angular gyrus)."
  - "Leg > arm weakness → **ACA**; face/arm > leg + aphasia → **MCA**."
imaging:
  ct: Noncontrast CT first in acute neurologic deficits (exclude hemorrhage before thrombolysis).
  mri: '**MRI with DWI** shows acute infarcts within minutes; MRI with contrast for tumors; functional MRI maps language before epilepsy surgery.'
  keyViews:
    - "CT head without contrast (stroke code)"
    - "MRI brain with DWI"
    - "EEG for seizures"
exam:
  testing: >-
    **Language**: fluency, comprehension (one- and multi-step commands), repetition ("no ifs, ands or buts"), naming,
    reading, writing. **Cortical sensation**: stereognosis, graphesthesia, two-point discrimination, extinction to
    double simultaneous stimulation (neglect). Visual fields. Frontal release signs. Cognitive screening (MoCA/MMSE).
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      After a stroke, a 67-year-old man speaks in short, halting phrases ("Walk... dog... park"). He follows commands
      correctly and is clearly frustrated. His right face and arm are weak. Where is the lesion?
    choices:
      - Left superior temporal gyrus
      - Left inferior frontal gyrus
      - Right parietal lobe
      - Left occipital lobe
      - Left arcuate fasciculus only
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Nonfluent speech with **preserved comprehension** and awareness is **Broca aphasia**, from the **left inferior
      frontal gyrus** (superior MCA division), often with right face and arm weakness.
  - stem: >-
      A 72-year-old woman after a stroke eats only from the right side of her plate, ignores people on her left and denies
      that anything is wrong. Where is the lesion?
    choices:
      - Left parietal lobe
      - Right parietal lobe
      - Left frontal lobe
      - Right occipital lobe only
      - Cerebellar vermis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Hemineglect** of the left side with **anosognosia** (denial of deficit) is typical of a **right (nondominant)
      parietal** lesion.
  - stem: A lesion of the medial surface of the right precentral gyrus would cause weakness mainly of which part of the body?
    choices:
      - Left face
      - Left hand
      - Left leg
      - Right leg
      - Right face
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **leg and foot** are represented on the **medial** surface of the motor cortex (ACA territory). Motor output
      crosses, so a right-sided lesion weakens the **left leg**.
