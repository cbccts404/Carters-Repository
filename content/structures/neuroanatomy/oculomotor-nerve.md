name: Oculomotor nerve (CN III)
type: nerve
region: neuroanatomy
subregion: Cranial nerves
taName: Nervus oculomotorius
aka: [CN III, Cranial nerve III, Third nerve palsy]
summary: Supplies four extraocular muscles, levator palpebrae and the pupillary constrictor. A palsy gives a "down and out" eye with ptosis; a blown pupil signals compression (aneurysm or herniation) rather than diabetic ischemia.
tags: [cranial-nerves, eye, emergency]
systems: [neurologic, eent]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  origin: >-
    **Oculomotor nucleus** (somatic motor) and **Edinger–Westphal nucleus** (parasympathetic) in the
    [[midbrain]] at the level of the superior colliculus. The nerve emerges in the **interpeduncular fossa**, medial to
    the cerebral peduncle.
  course: >-
    Passes **between the posterior cerebral and superior cerebellar arteries**, runs forward beside the **posterior
    communicating artery**, crosses the edge of the tentorium near the **uncus**, then runs in the **lateral wall of the
    cavernous sinus** (see [[dural-venous-sinuses]]) and enters the [[orbit]] through the **superior orbital fissure
    within the common tendinous ring**, dividing into superior and inferior divisions.
  branches:
    - "**Superior division**: superior rectus, levator palpebrae superioris"
    - "**Inferior division**: medial rectus, inferior rectus, inferior oblique, and the parasympathetic root to the **ciliary ganglion**"
  motor: >-
    [[extraocular-muscles]] — **superior, inferior and medial recti and inferior oblique**; **levator palpebrae
    superioris**. **Parasympathetic** (via the ciliary ganglion and short ciliary nerves): **sphincter pupillae**
    (miosis) and **ciliary muscle** (accommodation).
  relations: The **parasympathetic fibers lie superficially** (dorsomedial) on the nerve, so compression affects the pupil early, while ischemia (vasa nervorum, core of the nerve) tends to spare it.
clinical:
  - title: Third nerve palsy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Compressive**: **posterior communicating artery aneurysm**, **uncal (transtentorial) herniation**, tumor. **Ischemic (microvascular)**: diabetes, hypertension. Also midbrain stroke, cavernous sinus lesions, trauma.'
    presentation: >-
      Eye **"down and out"** (unopposed lateral rectus and superior oblique), **ptosis**, and — if the parasympathetic
      fibers are involved — a **fixed dilated pupil** and loss of accommodation; diplopia. **Pupil-involving** palsy
      (especially painful, sudden) = **aneurysm until proven otherwise** → emergency CTA/MRA. **Pupil-sparing** palsy in
      an older diabetic = usually microvascular ischemia, recovers over about 3 months.
  - title: Uncal herniation
    highYield: true
    mechanism: A supratentorial mass (e.g. epidural or subdural hematoma) pushes the medial temporal lobe (uncus) over the tentorium, compressing CN III.
    presentation: '**Ipsilateral blown pupil** followed by third nerve palsy, decreasing consciousness, and **contralateral hemiparesis** (or ipsilateral from Kernohan notch compression of the opposite peduncle). Neurosurgical emergency.'
  - title: Weber syndrome
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Paramedian midbrain infarct (branches of the PCA) involving the CN III fascicles and the cerebral peduncle.
    presentation: '**Ipsilateral CN III palsy** with **contralateral hemiparesis**.'
pance:
  - "\"Down and out\" eye + ptosis → **CN III palsy**."
  - "CN III palsy **with a dilated pupil** → **PComm aneurysm** (or herniation) → emergency CTA."
  - "**Pupil-sparing** CN III palsy in a diabetic → microvascular ischemia."
  - "Unilateral blown pupil in a head-injured patient → **uncal herniation**."
  - "Ipsilateral CN III palsy + contralateral hemiparesis → **Weber syndrome** (midbrain)."
imaging:
  ct: Noncontrast CT head for trauma/herniation; **CT angiography** for suspected PComm aneurysm.
  mri: MRI with MRA to evaluate the midbrain, cavernous sinus and aneurysm; catheter angiography if noninvasive imaging is negative but suspicion is high.
exam:
  testing: >-
    Inspect for ptosis and eye position at rest; **H-pattern** eye movements (adduction, elevation, depression);
    **pupil size, direct and consensual light reflex** and accommodation (near response). See the H-test diagram on
    [[extraocular-muscles]].
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 52-year-old woman has a sudden severe headache and drooping of the left eyelid. The left eye is deviated down and
      out, and the left pupil is 7 mm and unreactive. What is the most likely cause?
    choices:
      - Diabetic microvascular ischemia
      - Posterior communicating artery aneurysm
      - Myasthenia gravis
      - Horner syndrome
      - Trochlear nerve palsy
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **pupil-involving** third nerve palsy with sudden headache indicates **compression** of the superficial
      parasympathetic fibers, classically by a **posterior communicating artery aneurysm** — obtain emergency CTA.
  - stem: >-
      A 68-year-old man with poorly controlled diabetes has double vision and right ptosis. The right eye is down and out;
      the pupils are equal and reactive. What is the most likely mechanism?
    choices:
      - Compression by an aneurysm
      - Microvascular ischemia of the nerve
      - Uncal herniation
      - Cavernous sinus thrombosis
      - Demyelination
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **pupil-sparing** CN III palsy in a patient with vascular risk factors is usually **microvascular ischemia**,
      which affects the core of the nerve and spares the superficial pupillary fibers. It typically recovers within
      about 3 months.
  - stem: Why does a compressive lesion of CN III affect the pupil early, while ischemic lesions often spare it?
    choices:
      - The pupil fibers travel with CN IV
      - The pupil fibers are the thickest in the nerve
      - The parasympathetic pupillary fibers lie superficially on the nerve
      - Ischemia affects only the pupillary fibers
      - The pupil is controlled only by sympathetic fibers
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **parasympathetic fibers lie on the surface** of the nerve, so external compression (aneurysm, herniation)
      affects the pupil early, while microvascular ischemia of the nerve's core tends to spare it.
  - stem: Between which two arteries does the oculomotor nerve emerge?
    choices:
      - Anterior cerebral and middle cerebral arteries
      - Internal carotid and ophthalmic arteries
      - Vertebral and basilar arteries
      - Anterior and posterior inferior cerebellar arteries
      - Posterior cerebral and superior cerebellar arteries
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      CN III emerges from the interpeduncular fossa and passes **between the posterior cerebral and superior cerebellar
      arteries**, then runs alongside the posterior communicating artery.
  - stem: >-
      A patient with a large left subdural hematoma has a blown left pupil but weakness on the LEFT side. What explains
      the ipsilateral weakness?
    choices:
      - A second stroke in the right hemisphere
      - Compression of the opposite cerebral peduncle against the tentorium (Kernohan notch)
      - Spinal cord injury
      - Conversion disorder
      - Uncrossed corticospinal fibers
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      In uncal herniation, the midbrain can be pushed so that the **opposite cerebral peduncle** is compressed against
      the tentorial edge (**Kernohan notch**), causing weakness on the **same side** as the lesion, a false localizing
      sign.
