name: Thalamus
type: organ
region: neuroanatomy
subregion: Cerebrum
taName: Thalamus
aka: [Diencephalon, VPL, VPM, Lateral geniculate nucleus, Medial geniculate nucleus, Thalamic pain syndrome]
summary: Paired relay station of the diencephalon. Almost all sensory information (except smell) and motor loops pass through it to the cortex; thalamic strokes cause contralateral sensory loss and sometimes severe pain.
tags: [cerebrum, sensation, stroke]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: On each side of the **third ventricle**, medial to the posterior limb of the [[internal-capsule]], above the [[hypothalamus]] and [[midbrain]].
  parts:
    - "**VPL** (ventral posterolateral) — body sensation: dorsal columns/medial lemniscus and spinothalamic tract → somatosensory cortex"
    - "**VPM** (ventral posteromedial) — **face** sensation (trigeminal) and **taste**"
    - "**LGN** (lateral geniculate) — **vision** (optic tract → optic radiations); \"**L**ateral = **L**ight\""
    - "**MGN** (medial geniculate) — **hearing** (inferior colliculus → auditory cortex); \"**M**edial = **M**usic\""
    - "**VL and VA** — motor: input from the **cerebellum** (VL) and **basal ganglia** (VA/VL) → motor and premotor cortex"
    - "**Anterior nucleus** — Papez circuit (mammillothalamic tract → cingulate gyrus); **mediodorsal nucleus** — prefrontal cortex, memory and emotion"
    - "**Pulvinar** (visual attention), **intralaminar and reticular nuclei** (arousal, thalamocortical rhythms)"
  bloodSupply: '**Thalamoperforating (thalamogeniculate) branches of the posterior cerebral artery** and the **posterior communicating artery**, plus the anterior choroidal artery — see [[cerebral-arteries]] and [[circle-of-willis]].'
  venousDrainage: Internal cerebral veins → great cerebral vein (of Galen) → straight sinus.
  notes: Relay nuclei connecting ascending tracts ([[dorsal-column-medial-lemniscus]], [[spinothalamic-tract]]) with the [[cerebral-cortex]].
  relations: Forms the lateral walls of the third ventricle; the two thalami are often joined by the interthalamic adhesion.
clinical:
  - title: Thalamic (lacunar) stroke
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Occlusion of a thalamoperforating branch of the PCA (hypertension, diabetes — small vessel disease).
    presentation: '**Pure sensory stroke** — **contralateral** loss of all sensory modalities over the face, arm and leg (VPL + VPM) without weakness.'
  - title: Thalamic pain syndrome (Dejerine–Roussy)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Weeks to months after a thalamic (VPL) stroke.
    presentation: '**Severe, burning contralateral pain** and allodynia in the area of sensory loss; treated with neuropathic agents (amitriptyline, gabapentinoids).'
  - title: Thalamic hemorrhage
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Hypertensive rupture of thalamic perforators (second most common site after the putamen).
    presentation: Contralateral sensory loss and hemiparesis (internal capsule), **eyes deviated downward**, and hydrocephalus from rupture into the third ventricle.
pance:
  - "Pure contralateral sensory loss (face, arm, leg) → **thalamic lacunar stroke** (VPL/VPM)."
  - "Burning pain on the numb side after a stroke → **thalamic pain syndrome**."
  - "VPL = body; VPM = face and taste; LGN = vision; MGN = hearing."
  - "Olfaction is the only sense that bypasses the thalamus."
imaging:
  mri: MRI with DWI for small thalamic infarcts; bilateral paramedian thalamic infarcts suggest an **artery of Percheron** occlusion.
  ct: CT for thalamic hemorrhage and intraventricular extension.
exam:
  testing: Compare light touch, pinprick, temperature, vibration and proprioception on both sides of the face, arm and leg; a sharp midline split (including the face) suggests a thalamic lesion.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 64-year-old with hypertension develops numbness of the entire right side of the face, arm and leg. Strength,
      language and vision are normal. Which structure is most likely affected?
    choices:
      - Left thalamus
      - Right thalamus
      - Left internal capsule, posterior limb
      - Left lateral medulla
      - Right parietal cortex
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      A **pure sensory stroke** of the whole contralateral half of the body including the face is a **lacunar infarct of
      the left thalamus** (VPL and VPM). A posterior limb lesion would cause mainly weakness.
  - stem: Which thalamic nucleus relays visual information?
    choices:
      - Medial geniculate nucleus
      - Lateral geniculate nucleus
      - Ventral posteromedial nucleus
      - Anterior nucleus
      - Ventral lateral nucleus
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **lateral geniculate nucleus** receives the optic tract and projects via the optic radiations to V1. The medial
      geniculate relays hearing.
