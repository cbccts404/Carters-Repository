aka: [Medulla, Wallenberg syndrome, Lateral medullary syndrome, Pyramidal decussation, Area postrema]
summary: Lowest part of the brainstem, continuous with the spinal cord at the foramen magnum. It contains the cardiorespiratory centers, the nuclei of CN IX–XII and the pyramidal decussation; PICA strokes cause the lateral medullary (Wallenberg) syndrome.
tags: [brainstem, stroke, autonomic]
systems: [neurologic]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: From the lower border of the [[pons]] to the **foramen magnum**, where it becomes the [[spinal-cord]]; in front of the [[cerebellum]] and the lower fourth ventricle.
  parts:
    - "**Pyramids** (ventral): corticospinal tracts; about **85–90% cross in the pyramidal decussation** at the medulla–cord junction (see [[corticospinal-tract]])"
    - "**Olives** (inferior olivary nucleus → cerebellum via the inferior cerebellar peduncle)"
    - "**Nuclei**: hypoglossal (XII), dorsal motor nucleus of X, **nucleus ambiguus** (IX, X — swallowing, voice), **nucleus of the solitary tract** (taste, visceral afferents), spinal trigeminal nucleus and tract (**ipsilateral face pain/temperature**), vestibular nuclei, gracile and cuneate nuclei (dorsal columns → **sensory decussation** → medial lemniscus)"
    - "**Cardiovascular and respiratory centers** (reticular formation); **area postrema** — chemoreceptor trigger zone for vomiting (outside the blood–brain barrier)"
    - "Descending **sympathetic** fibers in the lateral medulla (lesion → Horner syndrome)"
  bloodSupply: '**Vertebral arteries**, **PICA** (lateral medulla), **anterior spinal artery** (medial medulla) — see [[vertebrobasilar-system]].'
  venousDrainage: Medullary veins → inferior petrosal sinus and spinal veins.
  notes: CN IX, X (postolivary sulcus), XI (lateral), XII (preolivary sulcus) emerge here — see [[glossopharyngeal-nerve]], [[vagus-nerve]], [[accessory-nerve]], [[hypoglossal-nerve]].
  relations: The **cerebellar tonsils** lie just above the foramen magnum behind the medulla.
clinical:
  - title: Lateral medullary (Wallenberg) syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Occlusion of the **vertebral artery or PICA** (dissection, atherosclerosis).
    presentation: >-
      **Ipsilateral** loss of pain/temperature on the **face** (spinal trigeminal) with **contralateral** loss on the
      **body** (spinothalamic); **dysphagia, hoarseness, reduced gag** (nucleus ambiguus); ipsilateral **Horner
      syndrome**; vertigo, nystagmus, nausea (vestibular); ipsilateral limb **ataxia**; hiccups. **No limb weakness.**
  - title: Medial medullary syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Occlusion of the **anterior spinal artery** or vertebral paramedian branches.
    presentation: '**Contralateral hemiparesis** (pyramid), **contralateral loss of proprioception/vibration** (medial lemniscus), **ipsilateral tongue deviation** (XII).'
  - title: Tonsillar herniation and Chiari I malformation
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Cerebellar tonsils pushed through the foramen magnum, compressing the medulla (raised ICP, posterior fossa mass, or lumbar puncture with a mass lesion); Chiari I — congenital tonsillar descent.
    presentation: '**Herniation**: neck stiffness, **Cushing triad** (hypertension, bradycardia, irregular respirations), then respiratory arrest. **Chiari I**: occipital headache worse with cough/Valsalva, syringomyelia (cape-like loss of pain/temperature).'
pance:
  - "Ipsilateral face + contralateral body pain/temp loss + dysphagia/hoarseness + Horner → **Wallenberg** (PICA/vertebral)."
  - "Contralateral hemiparesis + contralateral proprioception loss + ipsilateral tongue → **medial medullary** (anterior spinal)."
  - "**Cushing triad**: hypertension, bradycardia, irregular breathing → impending herniation."
  - "Medulla = CN **IX, X, XI, XII** + cardiorespiratory centers."
  - "Area postrema = chemoreceptor trigger zone (vomiting)."
imaging:
  mri: '**MRI with DWI** — medullary infarcts (often missed on CT); sagittal MRI for Chiari malformation and syrinx; MRA/CTA for vertebral dissection.'
  ct: CT for hemorrhage and signs of raised ICP; image before lumbar puncture when a mass is possible.
exam:
  testing: Palate elevation, gag, voice and swallow (IX, X), tongue (XII), SCM/trapezius (XI); facial and body pinprick/temperature (crossed sensory loss), pupils and ptosis (Horner), limb coordination, nystagmus.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 48-year-old has sudden vertigo, hoarseness and difficulty swallowing. Exam shows a small left pupil with ptosis,
      reduced pain sensation on the left face and the right side of the body, and left limb ataxia. Strength is normal.
      Which artery is occluded?
    choices:
      - Left anterior spinal artery
      - Left posterior inferior cerebellar artery (or vertebral artery)
      - Right middle cerebral artery
      - Basilar artery
      - Left anterior inferior cerebellar artery
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Lateral medullary (Wallenberg) syndrome** from **left PICA/vertebral** occlusion: crossed pain/temperature loss,
      nucleus ambiguus signs (dysphagia, hoarseness), ipsilateral Horner and ataxia, with no weakness.
  - stem: >-
      A patient with a large cerebellar hemorrhage becomes hypertensive with a heart rate of 44/min and irregular
      breathing. What is happening?
    choices:
      - Vasovagal reaction
      - Tonsillar herniation compressing the medulla
      - Hypovolemic shock
      - Autonomic dysreflexia
      - Seizure
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **Cushing triad** signals raised ICP with **brainstem (medullary) compression** — impending tonsillar
      herniation. Posterior fossa masses need urgent neurosurgical decompression.
  - stem: The area postrema of the medulla has which function?
    choices:
      - Chemoreceptor trigger zone for vomiting
      - Control of voluntary swallowing
      - Relay for hearing
      - Pain modulation
      - Horizontal gaze center
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      The **area postrema** lies outside the blood–brain barrier and acts as the **chemoreceptor trigger zone** for
      vomiting.
  - stem: >-
      A 30-year-old has occipital headaches worse with coughing and a cape-like loss of pain and temperature over the
      shoulders. MRI shows the cerebellar tonsils below the foramen magnum. What is the diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Tonsillar herniation from a tumor
      - Dandy–Walker malformation
      - Multiple sclerosis
      - Normal pressure hydrocephalus
      - Chiari I malformation with syringomyelia
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      **Chiari I malformation** is congenital descent of the cerebellar tonsils, causing occipital headache with
      cough/Valsalva and often a **syrinx** (cape-like pain/temperature loss).
  - stem: In lateral medullary syndrome, damage to which nucleus causes dysphagia and hoarseness?
    choices:
      - Hypoglossal nucleus
      - Nucleus of the solitary tract
      - Nucleus ambiguus
      - Dorsal motor nucleus of the vagus
      - Spinal trigeminal nucleus
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **nucleus ambiguus** supplies the pharyngeal and laryngeal muscles (CN IX, X), so its damage causes
      **dysphagia, hoarseness** and a reduced gag. The spinal trigeminal nucleus causes the ipsilateral face
      pain/temperature loss.
