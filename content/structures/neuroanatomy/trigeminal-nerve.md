name: Trigeminal nerve (CN V)
type: nerve
region: neuroanatomy
subregion: Cranial nerves
taName: Nervus trigeminus
aka: [CN V, Cranial nerve V, Ophthalmic nerve, Maxillary nerve, Mandibular nerve, V1, V2, V3, Trigeminal neuralgia]
summary: Largest cranial nerve — sensation from the face, scalp, eye, nose and mouth in three divisions, plus motor supply to the muscles of mastication. Trigeminal neuralgia and shingles of V1 are the classic problems.
tags: [cranial-nerves, face, pain]
systems: [neurologic]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  origin: >-
    Leaves the lateral [[pons]] as a large sensory root and a small motor root. Nuclei: **principal sensory** (pons —
    touch), **spinal nucleus** (down into the cervical cord — **pain and temperature**), **mesencephalic** (proprioception,
    jaw jerk) and **motor** nuclei. The sensory cell bodies form the **trigeminal (semilunar) ganglion** in **Meckel
    cave** on the petrous apex.
  course: >-
    The ganglion gives three divisions: **V1 ophthalmic** — lateral wall of the cavernous sinus → **superior orbital
    fissure**; **V2 maxillary** — lateral wall of the cavernous sinus → **foramen rotundum** → pterygopalatine fossa;
    **V3 mandibular** (with the whole motor root) → **foramen ovale** → infratemporal fossa.
    Mnemonic "**S**tanding **R**oom **O**nly" (SOF, rotundum, ovale).
  branches:
    - "**V1 ophthalmic** (sensory): frontal (supraorbital, supratrochlear), lacrimal, **nasociliary** (cornea, tip of the nose) — forehead, upper eyelid, cornea, dorsum of nose"
    - "**V2 maxillary** (sensory): infraorbital, zygomatic, superior alveolar, palatine and nasal branches — cheek, lower eyelid, upper lip, upper teeth, palate, nasal cavity"
    - "**V3 mandibular** (sensory + motor): auriculotemporal, buccal, **lingual** (anterior 2/3 of tongue), **inferior alveolar** (lower teeth, chin via the mental nerve) and motor branches"
  motor: '**V3 only**: [[muscles-of-mastication]] (masseter, temporalis, medial and lateral pterygoids), mylohyoid, anterior belly of digastric, **tensor tympani**, tensor veli palatini.'
  sensory: Face and anterior scalp to the vertex, cornea and conjunctiva, nasal and oral cavities, teeth, anterior two-thirds of the tongue (general sensation), most of the supratentorial dura. **The angle of the jaw is supplied by C2–C3 (great auricular nerve), not CN V.**
  relations: V1 and V2 run in the lateral wall of the cavernous sinus with CN III and IV (see [[dural-venous-sinuses]]). Parasympathetic fibers from CN VII and IX "hitchhike" on V branches to the lacrimal, nasal, palatine and parotid glands.
clinical:
  - title: Trigeminal neuralgia
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Usually **vascular compression** of the root near the pons (superior cerebellar artery); in a **young patient or with bilateral symptoms, think multiple sclerosis**.
    presentation: >-
      Brief (seconds), **electric, stabbing unilateral facial pain**, usually **V2 or V3**, triggered by chewing,
      talking, brushing teeth, touching the face or wind; normal neurologic exam. **Carbamazepine** (or oxcarbazepine)
      is first-line; microvascular decompression or ablation if refractory. MRI to exclude secondary causes.
  - title: Herpes zoster ophthalmicus
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Reactivation of varicella-zoster virus in the trigeminal ganglion, **V1** distribution.
    presentation: 'Painful vesicular rash on the forehead and upper eyelid; **Hutchinson sign** (vesicles on the **tip of the nose** — nasociliary branch) predicts **eye involvement** (keratitis, uveitis). Oral antivirals within 72 hours and urgent ophthalmology.'
  - title: Motor (V3) lesion
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Tumors at the skull base or foramen ovale, trauma.
    presentation: Weak jaw clench and masseter/temporalis wasting; the **jaw deviates toward the weak side** on opening (unopposed contralateral lateral pterygoid).
pance:
  - "Lightning-like facial pain triggered by chewing/touch → **trigeminal neuralgia** → **carbamazepine**."
  - "Trigeminal neuralgia in a young adult → consider **MS**."
  - "Zoster with vesicles on the nose tip (**Hutchinson sign**) → eye involvement → ophthalmology."
  - "Corneal reflex: afferent **V1**, efferent **VII**."
  - "Jaw deviates **toward** a V3 lesion."
  - "SOF (V1), rotundum (V2), ovale (V3) — \"Standing Room Only\"."
imaging:
  mri: '**MRI brain with thin-section imaging of the trigeminal root** (e.g. FIESTA/CISS) to show vascular compression or exclude tumor or MS plaques.'
  ct: CT of the skull base foramina for tumors or fractures.
exam:
  landmarks: Supraorbital (V1), infraorbital (V2) and mental (V3) foramina lie on a vertical line through the pupil.
  palpation: Palpate masseter and temporalis while the patient clenches the teeth.
  testing: >-
    Light touch and pinprick (± temperature) in **each of the three divisions on both sides** (forehead, cheek, jaw —
    not the angle of the jaw). Motor: clench teeth (palpate masseter/temporalis), open the jaw against resistance
    (deviation). **Jaw jerk** (brisk in upper motor neuron lesions above the pons).
  specialTests:
    - name: Corneal reflex
      technique: Touch the cornea (not the conjunctiva) from the side with a wisp of cotton while the patient looks away.
      positive: Absent blink in both eyes when one cornea is touched.
      significance: 'Afferent **V1 (nasociliary)**, efferent **CN VII** (orbicularis oculi). Loss with preserved blink on stimulating the other eye → V1 lesion (e.g. acoustic neuroma compressing V).'
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 62-year-old woman has attacks of stabbing pain in the right cheek lasting a few seconds, set off by brushing her
      teeth. Neurologic exam is normal. What is the first-line treatment?
    choices:
      - Gabapentin only after surgery
      - Carbamazepine
      - Sumatriptan
      - Prednisone
      - High-flow oxygen
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Brief, triggered, electric pain in V2 with a normal exam is **trigeminal neuralgia**; **carbamazepine** is
      first-line. Obtain MRI to exclude a secondary cause.
  - stem: The motor fibers of the trigeminal nerve exit the skull through which foramen?
    choices:
      - Superior orbital fissure
      - Foramen rotundum
      - Foramen ovale
      - Foramen spinosum
      - Jugular foramen
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      All motor fibers travel with **V3 (mandibular)**, which leaves through the **foramen ovale**. The foramen spinosum
      transmits the middle meningeal artery.
  - stem: >-
      A patient has a vesicular rash over the left forehead and upper eyelid, with vesicles on the tip of the nose. What
      does the nasal involvement indicate?
    choices:
      - Involvement of V2
      - High risk of ocular involvement
      - Facial nerve involvement (Ramsay Hunt syndrome)
      - Disseminated zoster requiring isolation
      - Contact dermatitis rather than zoster
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Hutchinson sign** — the nasociliary branch of V1 supplies both the nose tip and the eye, so nasal vesicles predict
      **ocular zoster**. Urgent ophthalmology and antivirals.
