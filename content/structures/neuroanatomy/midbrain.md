summary: Uppermost part of the brainstem, around the cerebral aqueduct. It contains the CN III and IV nuclei, the substantia nigra and the corticospinal tracts in the cerebral peduncles, and is compressed in transtentorial herniation.

status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Between the diencephalon above and the [[pons]] below, passing through the **tentorial notch**.
  parts:
    - "**Tectum** (dorsal, behind the aqueduct): **superior colliculi** (visual reflexes, vertical gaze) and **inferior colliculi** (auditory pathway)"
    - "**Cerebral aqueduct** (of Sylvius) connecting the third and fourth ventricles, surrounded by the **periaqueductal gray** (pain modulation)"
    - "**Tegmentum**: nuclei of **CN III** (with Edinger–Westphal) and **CN IV**, the **red nucleus**, medial lemniscus, spinothalamic tract, MLF, reticular formation"
    - "**Substantia nigra** (dopaminergic neurons of the pars compacta → striatum; see [[basal-ganglia]])"
    - "**Cerebral peduncles (crus cerebri)**: descending **corticospinal** and corticobulbar fibers; the **interpeduncular fossa** between them, where [[oculomotor-nerve]] emerges"
    - "**Pretectal area**: pupillary light reflex relay (bilateral to both Edinger–Westphal nuclei → consensual response)"
  bloodSupply: Branches of the **posterior cerebral artery**, **superior cerebellar artery** and top of the **basilar artery** (see [[vertebrobasilar-system]]).
  venousDrainage: Basal veins → great cerebral vein (of Galen) → straight sinus.
  notes: Contains the nuclei of CN III and IV; the trochlear nerve exits dorsally below the inferior colliculus ([[trochlear-nerve]]).
  relations: The **uncus** of the temporal lobe and CN III lie against the free tentorial edge beside the peduncle; the **pineal gland** sits above the superior colliculi.
clinical:
  - title: Parkinson disease
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Loss of dopaminergic neurons in the **substantia nigra pars compacta** (Lewy bodies, alpha-synuclein) → reduced dopamine in the striatum.
    presentation: '**Resting (pill-rolling) tremor, rigidity (cogwheel), bradykinesia, postural instability**; shuffling gait, masked face, micrographia; often asymmetric onset. Clinical diagnosis; levodopa-carbidopa.'
  - title: Parinaud (dorsal midbrain) syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Compression of the dorsal midbrain/superior colliculi — **pineal tumor** (germinoma in young males), hydrocephalus, dorsal midbrain stroke.
    presentation: '**Upward gaze palsy**, **light–near dissociation** (pupils react to accommodation but not light), **convergence–retraction nystagmus**, eyelid retraction (Collier sign); often obstructive hydrocephalus from aqueduct compression.'
  - title: Weber syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Infarct of the **ventral (basal) midbrain** — PCA/paramedian branches.
    presentation: '**Ipsilateral CN III palsy** + **contralateral hemiparesis** (cerebral peduncle).'
  - title: Transtentorial (uncal) herniation and aqueductal stenosis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: A supratentorial mass pushes the uncus through the tentorial notch, compressing CN III, the peduncle and the PCA; congenital or acquired narrowing of the aqueduct blocks CSF flow.
    presentation: 'Herniation: ipsilateral fixed dilated pupil, contralateral (or ipsilateral — **Kernohan notch**) hemiparesis, declining consciousness. Aqueductal stenosis: **noncommunicating hydrocephalus** with dilated lateral and third ventricles but a normal fourth ventricle.'
pance:
  - "Substantia nigra degeneration → **Parkinson disease** (TRAP: tremor, rigidity, akinesia, postural instability)."
  - "Pineal tumor → **Parinaud syndrome** (upgaze palsy, light–near dissociation) + hydrocephalus."
  - "Ipsilateral CN III + contralateral hemiparesis → **Weber** (midbrain)."
  - "Dilated lateral and third ventricles, normal fourth → **aqueductal stenosis**."
imaging:
  mri: '**MRI** is best for the brainstem (CT has bone artifact in the posterior fossa); DaT-SPECT can support a diagnosis of parkinsonism in unclear cases.'
  ct: Noncontrast CT for herniation, hemorrhage and hydrocephalus.
exam:
  testing: 'Pupils (light and near response), vertical and horizontal eye movements, CN III/IV function; tone, tremor and bradykinesia (finger tapping), gait and pull test for parkinsonism.'
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 15-year-old boy has headaches, cannot look upward, and his pupils constrict when he looks at a near object but not
      to light. MRI shows a mass above the superior colliculi with dilated lateral and third ventricles. What is the
      diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Wallenberg syndrome
      - Parinaud syndrome from a pineal tumor
      - Weber syndrome
      - Argyll Robertson pupils from neurosyphilis
      - Internuclear ophthalmoplegia
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **pineal region tumor** compressing the **dorsal midbrain** causes **Parinaud syndrome** (upgaze palsy,
      light–near dissociation, convergence–retraction nystagmus) and obstructive hydrocephalus at the aqueduct.
  - stem: A 66-year-old has a resting tremor of the right hand, cogwheel rigidity and slow finger tapping. Degeneration of which structure explains this?
    choices:
      - Caudate nucleus
      - Substantia nigra pars compacta
      - Subthalamic nucleus
      - Cerebellar vermis
      - Red nucleus
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Parkinson disease** results from loss of dopaminergic neurons in the **substantia nigra pars compacta**. Caudate
      atrophy is Huntington disease; a subthalamic lesion causes hemiballismus.
  - stem: Weber syndrome (ipsilateral CN III palsy with contralateral hemiparesis) results from a lesion where?
    choices:
      - Dorsal midbrain (tectum)
      - Ventral midbrain (cerebral peduncle)
      - Lateral medulla
      - Basilar pons
      - Cerebellar vermis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Weber syndrome** is a **ventral (basal) midbrain** infarct involving the **CN III fascicles** and the **cerebral
      peduncle** (corticospinal fibers). Dorsal midbrain lesions cause Parinaud syndrome.
  - stem: On MRI, which pattern of ventricular enlargement indicates aqueductal stenosis?
    choices:
      - All four ventricles dilated
      - Only the fourth ventricle dilated
      - Only one lateral ventricle dilated
      - Lateral and third ventricles dilated with a normal fourth ventricle
      - No dilation
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      Obstruction at the **cerebral aqueduct** dilates everything **upstream** (lateral and third ventricles) while the
      **fourth ventricle stays normal**, a noncommunicating hydrocephalus.
  - stem: >-
      Which midbrain area relays the pupillary light reflex to both Edinger–Westphal nuclei, producing the consensual
      response?
    choices:
      - Pretectal area
      - Red nucleus
      - Substantia nigra
      - Inferior colliculus
      - Periaqueductal gray
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      The **pretectal nuclei** receive optic tract input and project to **both** Edinger–Westphal nuclei, so light in
      one eye constricts both pupils (direct and consensual responses).
