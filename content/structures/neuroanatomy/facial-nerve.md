name: Facial nerve (CN VII)
type: nerve
region: neuroanatomy
subregion: Cranial nerves
taName: Nervus facialis
aka: [CN VII, Cranial nerve VII, Bell palsy, Chorda tympani, Nervus intermedius]
summary: Motor to the muscles of facial expression, with taste from the anterior tongue and parasympathetic supply to the lacrimal and submandibular/sublingual glands. Whether the forehead is spared separates a central (stroke) from a peripheral (Bell) palsy.
tags: [cranial-nerves, face]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  origin: >-
    **Facial motor nucleus** in the caudal [[pons]] (its fibers loop around the abducens nucleus — the facial
    colliculus); **superior salivatory nucleus** (parasympathetic); **nucleus of the solitary tract** (taste). Emerges at
    the **cerebellopontine angle** (pontomedullary junction) as the motor root and the **nervus intermedius**.
  course: >-
    With CN VIII into the **internal acoustic meatus** → **facial canal** in the petrous temporal bone → bends at the
    **geniculate ganglion** (knee) → runs in the medial wall of the [[middle-ear]] → descends behind it → exits the
    skull through the **stylomastoid foramen** → enters the [[parotid-gland]], where it divides into its terminal branches
    (it does not supply the gland).
  branches:
    - "**Greater petrosal nerve** (at the geniculate ganglion) → pterygopalatine ganglion → **lacrimal gland** and nasal/palatine glands"
    - "**Nerve to stapedius** (damps loud sounds — lesion causes **hyperacusis**)"
    - "**Chorda tympani** → crosses the middle ear → joins the lingual nerve (V3): **taste from the anterior two-thirds of the tongue** and parasympathetic to the **submandibular and sublingual glands** (via the submandibular ganglion)"
    - "After the stylomastoid foramen: posterior auricular, posterior belly of digastric, stylohyoid"
    - "**Five terminal branches in the parotid**: temporal, zygomatic, buccal, marginal mandibular, cervical (\"**T**o **Z**anzibar **B**y **M**otor **C**ar\")"
  motor: '[[muscles-of-facial-expression]] (including orbicularis oculi and oris, buccinator, platysma), stapedius, posterior belly of digastric, stylohyoid.'
  sensory: Taste from the anterior two-thirds of the tongue (chorda tympani) and a small area of skin of the external ear.
  relations: The **upper face** (forehead) receives **bilateral** corticobulbar input, whereas the lower face receives input only from the **contralateral** motor cortex.
clinical:
  - title: Bell palsy (peripheral, lower motor neuron)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Idiopathic, probably viral (HSV-1) inflammation of the nerve in the facial canal. Also Lyme disease (often bilateral), Ramsay Hunt syndrome, parotid tumors, trauma, otitis media.
    presentation: >-
      Acute (under 72 hours) weakness of the **entire half of the face, including the forehead** — cannot raise the
      eyebrow or close the eye (**Bell phenomenon**: the eye rolls up on attempted closure), drooping mouth; may have
      hyperacusis, loss of taste, reduced tearing, ear pain. Treat with **oral corticosteroids within 72 hours** (±
      antiviral) and **eye protection** (lubricants, taping at night). Most recover within weeks to months.
  - title: Central facial palsy (upper motor neuron)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Stroke or tumor affecting the contralateral motor cortex or corticobulbar fibers (internal capsule).
    presentation: Weakness of the **contralateral lower face** with **forehead sparing** (bilateral upper-face innervation), often with arm weakness or aphasia — a stroke until proven otherwise.
  - title: Ramsay Hunt syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Varicella-zoster reactivation in the **geniculate ganglion**.
    presentation: 'Facial palsy with **ear pain and vesicles in the external ear canal/auricle**, ± hearing loss and vertigo (CN VIII). Treat with antivirals and steroids; recovery is worse than Bell palsy.'
pance:
  - "**Forehead involved** → peripheral (LMN) lesion (Bell palsy); **forehead spared** → central (UMN) — stroke."
  - "Bell palsy → **prednisone within 72 h** ± valacyclovir + **eye protection**."
  - "Facial palsy + vesicles in the ear → **Ramsay Hunt** (VZV, geniculate ganglion)."
  - "**Bilateral** facial palsy → Lyme disease, sarcoidosis, Guillain–Barré."
  - "Corneal reflex efferent = **CN VII** (orbicularis oculi)."
  - "Facial palsy after parotid surgery or with a parotid mass → malignant tumor until proven otherwise."
imaging:
  mri: MRI with contrast (internal auditory canal, temporal bone, parotid) for atypical, recurrent, slowly progressive (over 3 weeks) or non-recovering palsies — look for tumors (schwannoma, parotid malignancy).
  ct: Temporal bone CT after trauma (fracture through the facial canal). Typical Bell palsy needs no imaging.
exam:
  landmarks: The nerve exits at the stylomastoid foramen, behind the ear lobe and anterior to the mastoid; its branches fan out from the anterior border of the parotid.
  testing: >-
    Observe symmetry at rest (nasolabial folds, palpebral fissures). Ask the patient to **raise the eyebrows** (frontalis
    — distinguishes central from peripheral), **close the eyes tightly** against resistance, show the teeth, puff out
    the cheeks, whistle. Test taste on the anterior tongue if relevant; ask about hyperacusis and tearing. Look in the
    ear for vesicles.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 35-year-old woman woke with a drooping right face. She cannot wrinkle her right forehead or fully close her right
      eye. There are no other neurologic deficits and no ear vesicles. What is the most appropriate management?
    choices:
      - Emergency thrombolysis
      - Oral prednisone and eye protection
      - Surgical decompression immediately
      - MRI brain before any treatment
      - Observation only; eye care is unnecessary
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Weakness of the **whole half-face including the forehead** is a peripheral (LMN) lesion — **Bell palsy**. Give
      **corticosteroids within 72 hours** (± antiviral) and protect the eye. Imaging is for atypical features.
  - stem: >-
      A 70-year-old man has sudden weakness of the left lower face and left arm. He can wrinkle both sides of his forehead.
      Where is the lesion most likely?
    choices:
      - Left facial nerve in the facial canal
      - Right motor cortex or internal capsule
      - Left parotid gland
      - Right stylomastoid foramen
      - Left geniculate ganglion
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Forehead sparing** with contralateral lower facial and arm weakness is an **upper motor neuron** lesion — a
      **right hemispheric stroke** (the forehead has bilateral cortical input). Activate the stroke pathway.
