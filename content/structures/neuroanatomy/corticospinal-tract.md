name: Corticospinal tract
type: nerve
region: neuroanatomy
subregion: Spinal tracts
taName: Tractus corticospinalis
aka: [Pyramidal tract, Upper motor neuron, UMN, Lateral corticospinal tract, Babinski sign]
summary: Main voluntary motor pathway from the motor cortex to the spinal cord. It crosses in the lower medulla; damage causes upper motor neuron signs — weakness, spasticity, hyperreflexia and an extensor plantar response.
tags: [spinal-tracts, motor, stroke]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  origin: '**Upper motor neurons** in the primary motor cortex (precentral gyrus, including the giant Betz cells of layer V), premotor and supplementary motor areas, and parietal cortex.'
  course: >-
    Corona radiata → **posterior limb of the [[internal-capsule]]** → middle of the **cerebral peduncle** ([[midbrain]])
    → scattered bundles in the **basilar [[pons]]** → **pyramids** of the [[medulla-oblongata]] → **85–90% cross in the
    pyramidal decussation** and descend in the **lateral corticospinal tract** of the contralateral cord → synapse on
    **lower motor neurons** in the ventral horn (directly or via interneurons). The uncrossed 10–15% descend in the
    **anterior corticospinal tract** (axial muscles) and mostly cross at segmental levels.
  motor: Voluntary skilled movement, especially of the distal limbs (fine finger movements); lower motor neurons carry the signal to muscle via the spinal nerves.
  relations: '**Corticobulbar** fibers accompany it to cranial nerve motor nuclei (most receive **bilateral** input, except the lower face and — partly — CN XII, which are mainly contralateral).'
  notes: A lesion **above the decussation** (cortex, capsule, brainstem) causes **contralateral** weakness; a **spinal cord** lesion causes **ipsilateral** weakness below it.
clinical:
  - title: Upper vs lower motor neuron lesions
    highYield: true
    mechanism: UMN — damage anywhere along the corticospinal tract (stroke, MS, cord compression, B12 deficiency). LMN — ventral horn cell, root, plexus or peripheral nerve.
    presentation: >-
      **UMN**: weakness in a pyramidal pattern (arm extensors, leg flexors), **spasticity** (velocity-dependent,
      "clasp-knife"), **hyperreflexia**, **clonus**, **Babinski sign** (extensor plantar), Hoffmann sign, little
      atrophy. Acutely (spinal shock) tone and reflexes may be reduced. **LMN**: weakness, **atrophy**,
      **fasciculations**, **hypotonia**, **hyporeflexia**, flexor plantar.
  - title: Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Degeneration of **both upper and lower motor neurons** (corticospinal tracts and ventral horn/brainstem motor nuclei); sensation spared.
    presentation: '**Mixed UMN and LMN signs** in the same limb — weakness, atrophy, fasciculations with hyperreflexia and Babinski; bulbar dysarthria and dysphagia; no sensory loss. EMG; riluzole; respiratory failure is the usual cause of death.'
  - title: Cervical spondylotic myelopathy
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Degenerative narrowing of the cervical canal compressing the cord (older adults).
    presentation: Clumsy hands, gait imbalance, **UMN signs in the legs** (and arms below the level), positive Hoffmann sign, bladder urgency; neck pain may be minimal. MRI cervical spine; surgical decompression if progressive.
pance:
  - "UMN: spasticity, **hyperreflexia**, clonus, **Babinski**; LMN: atrophy, **fasciculations**, hyporeflexia."
  - "UMN + LMN signs, no sensory loss → **ALS**."
  - "Crosses at the **pyramidal decussation** (lower medulla) → brain lesions give contralateral weakness, cord lesions ipsilateral."
  - "Older adult with clumsy hands, unsteady gait, Hoffmann sign → **cervical myelopathy** → MRI."
imaging:
  mri: '**MRI brain or spine** at the suspected level — stroke (DWI), demyelination, cord compression; EMG/nerve conduction studies to confirm LMN disease (ALS).'
  ct: Noncontrast CT head in acute weakness (stroke protocol).
exam:
  testing: '**Pronator drift** (arms outstretched, palms up, eyes closed — the weak arm pronates and drifts down); strength grading (0–5); tone (spasticity); reflexes; **plantar response**; clonus at the ankle; **Hoffmann sign**; gait (circumduction).'
  specialTests:
    - name: Babinski sign (plantar response)
      technique: Stroke the lateral sole firmly from the heel toward the little toe and across the ball of the foot.
      positive: '**Extension (dorsiflexion) of the great toe**, with fanning of the other toes.'
      significance: '**Upper motor neuron lesion** (corticospinal tract). Normal in infants under about 1–2 years.'
    - name: Hoffmann sign
      technique: Flick the nail of the relaxed middle finger downward.
      positive: Flexion of the thumb and index finger.
      significance: Suggests an upper motor neuron lesion affecting the arms, e.g. **cervical myelopathy** (can occur in some normal people).
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 58-year-old has progressive weakness of the right hand with wasting and fasciculations, brisk reflexes in all
      limbs, and bilateral Babinski signs. Sensation is normal. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Cervical radiculopathy
      - Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis
      - Guillain–Barré syndrome
      - Multiple sclerosis
      - Myasthenia gravis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Combined UMN (hyperreflexia, Babinski) and LMN (atrophy, fasciculations) signs without sensory loss** are the
      hallmark of **ALS**.
  - stem: Which finding indicates an upper motor neuron lesion?
    choices:
      - Fasciculations
      - Marked muscle atrophy
      - Extensor plantar response
      - Hyporeflexia
      - Flaccid tone in chronic stage
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      An **extensor plantar response (Babinski sign)** indicates **corticospinal tract (UMN)** dysfunction. The other
      findings suggest a lower motor neuron lesion.
