name: Dural venous sinuses
type: vein
region: neuroanatomy
subregion: Cerebral vasculature
taName: Sinus durae matris
aka: [Superior sagittal sinus, Cavernous sinus, Transverse sinus, Sigmoid sinus, Straight sinus, Cerebral venous sinus thrombosis, Cavernous sinus thrombosis]
summary: Valveless venous channels between the layers of the dura that drain the brain into the internal jugular veins. Thrombosis of the sagittal or cavernous sinus is an important, easily missed cause of headache and cranial nerve palsies.
tags: [cerebral-vessels, venous-thrombosis]
systems: [cardiovascular]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  formation: Endothelium-lined spaces between the periosteal and meningeal layers of the dura (see [[cranial-meninges]]); they receive the cerebral, cerebellar, diploic and emissary veins and CSF from the arachnoid granulations.
  course: >-
    **Superior sagittal sinus** (along the attached border of the falx; receives CSF via arachnoid granulations) and
    **straight sinus** (formed by the inferior sagittal sinus and the **great cerebral vein of Galen**) meet at the
    **confluence of sinuses** (near the internal occipital protuberance) → **transverse sinuses** → **sigmoid sinuses**
    (grooving the mastoid part of the temporal bone) → **internal jugular vein** at the jugular foramen. The **cavernous
    sinuses** on each side of the sella drain via the **superior and inferior petrosal sinuses**.
  tributaries:
    - "Superior cerebral veins (bridging veins) → superior sagittal sinus"
    - "Great cerebral vein (of Galen) → straight sinus"
    - "**Superior and inferior ophthalmic veins** → **cavernous sinus** (connecting it with the facial veins)"
    - "Emissary veins (scalp ↔ sinuses) and diploic veins"
  drainage: '**[[internal-jugular-vein]]** (via the sigmoid sinus and inferior petrosal sinus).'
  relations: >-
    **Cavernous sinus** contents: **internal carotid artery** and **CN VI** run **through** the sinus; **CN III, IV,
    V1 and V2** run in its **lateral wall** (see [[oculomotor-nerve]], [[trochlear-nerve]], [[trigeminal-nerve]],
    [[abducens-nerve]]); the [[pituitary-gland]] lies medially.
  notes: The **valveless facial veins** (angular → ophthalmic) connect the "**danger triangle**" of the face (nose and upper lip) with the cavernous sinus; scalp emissary veins connect scalp infections with the intracranial sinuses (see [[scalp]]).
clinical:
  - title: Cerebral venous sinus thrombosis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Prothrombotic states** — **oral contraceptives**, **pregnancy and postpartum**, thrombophilias, cancer, dehydration, infection (mastoiditis → sigmoid/transverse sinus).'
    presentation: >-
      **Headache** (often progressive, worse lying down), **papilledema**, **seizures**, focal deficits, altered
      consciousness; **venous (often hemorrhagic) infarcts** not matching arterial territories. Diagnose with **MR or CT
      venography** (**empty delta sign** on contrast CT). Treat with **anticoagulation (heparin) even if there is
      hemorrhage**.
  - title: Cavernous sinus thrombosis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Septic thrombosis spreading from the **face (danger triangle)**, **sphenoid/ethmoid sinusitis**, orbit or teeth; usually *S. aureus*.
    presentation: 'Fever, headache, **proptosis**, **chemosis**, periorbital edema, **ophthalmoplegia (CN VI often first)**, reduced sensation in V1/V2, and spread to the **other eye**. IV antibiotics ± anticoagulation; MRI/CT venography.'
  - title: Carotid–cavernous fistula
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Abnormal connection between the ICA (or its branches) and the cavernous sinus — trauma or ruptured cavernous aneurysm.
    presentation: '**Pulsatile exophthalmos**, orbital **bruit**, chemosis, arterialized conjunctival veins, ophthalmoplegia; endovascular repair.'
pance:
  - "Headache + papilledema + seizures in a woman on OCPs or postpartum → **venous sinus thrombosis** → MRV → **heparin** (even with hemorrhage)."
  - "Facial/sinus infection → proptosis + chemosis + ophthalmoplegia + fever → **cavernous sinus thrombosis**."
  - "CN VI is the first/most affected nerve in the cavernous sinus."
  - "Pulsatile exophthalmos + bruit → **carotid–cavernous fistula**."
  - "Sinus drainage: superior sagittal → confluence → transverse → sigmoid → **IJV**."
imaging:
  ct: Noncontrast CT may show a hyperdense sinus ("cord sign") or venous infarcts; **CT venography** shows filling defects (empty delta sign).
  mri: '**MRI with MR venography** — the test of choice for venous sinus thrombosis; contrast MRI of the orbits and cavernous sinus.'
  keyViews:
    - "MR venography"
    - "CT venography"
exam:
  testing: 'Fundoscopy (papilledema), eye movements (CN III, IV, VI), corneal reflex and V1/V2 sensation, proptosis and chemosis, facial skin and sinuses for an infective source.'
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 29-year-old woman 2 weeks postpartum has a worsening headache for 5 days, blurred vision and a seizure. Fundi show
      bilateral papilledema. CT shows a small hemorrhagic infarct in the left parietal lobe. What is the most likely
      diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Postpartum preeclampsia only
      - Cerebral venous sinus thrombosis
      - Migraine with aura
      - Pituitary apoplexy
      - Subarachnoid hemorrhage from an aneurysm
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Postpartum** hypercoagulability, headache, **papilledema**, seizures and a **hemorrhagic venous infarct** suggest
      **cerebral venous sinus thrombosis**. Confirm with MR/CT venography; treat with **anticoagulation** despite the
      hemorrhage.
  - stem: >-
      A 35-year-old squeezed a boil on the side of his nose. Three days later he has fever, proptosis, chemosis and
      cannot abduct the right eye. Which structure is involved?
    choices:
      - Superior sagittal sinus
      - Cavernous sinus
      - Sigmoid sinus
      - Straight sinus
      - Middle meningeal artery
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Infection from the **danger triangle** spreads through the valveless **facial and ophthalmic veins** to the
      **cavernous sinus**. **CN VI**, which runs freely within the sinus, is often affected first.
  - stem: >-
      MR venography confirms cerebral venous sinus thrombosis in a postpartum woman with a small hemorrhagic venous
      infarct. What is the appropriate treatment?
    choices:
      - Withhold anticoagulation because of the hemorrhage
      - Aspirin only
      - Observation
      - Anticoagulation with heparin despite the hemorrhage
      - Emergency craniotomy for all patients
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      Venous sinus thrombosis is treated with **anticoagulation (heparin) even when there is a hemorrhagic venous
      infarct**, because restoring venous outflow is key.
  - stem: >-
      Weeks after a head injury, a patient has a pulsating, protruding eye with an audible orbital bruit and
      arterialized conjunctival vessels. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Orbital cellulitis
      - Carotid–cavernous fistula
      - Thyroid eye disease
      - Retrobulbar hemorrhage
      - Cavernous sinus thrombosis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **carotid–cavernous fistula** (an abnormal connection between the ICA and the cavernous sinus, after trauma or a
      ruptured cavernous aneurysm) causes **pulsatile exophthalmos**, an **orbital bruit**, chemosis and
      ophthalmoplegia.
  - stem: What is the usual order of venous drainage from the superior sagittal sinus?
    choices:
      - Confluence of sinuses → transverse sinus → sigmoid sinus → internal jugular vein
      - Straight sinus → cavernous sinus → external jugular vein
      - Inferior sagittal sinus → petrosal sinus → vertebral vein
      - Cavernous sinus → ophthalmic vein → facial vein
      - Transverse sinus → straight sinus → subclavian vein
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      The superior sagittal and straight sinuses meet at the **confluence**, then drain through the **transverse** and
      **sigmoid** sinuses to the **internal jugular vein** at the jugular foramen.
