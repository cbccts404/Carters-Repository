name: Circle of Willis
type: artery
region: neuroanatomy
subregion: Cerebral vasculature
taName: Circulus arteriosus cerebri
aka: [Cerebral arterial circle, Anterior communicating artery, Posterior communicating artery, Berry aneurysm, Saccular aneurysm]
summary: Arterial ring at the base of the brain joining the carotid (anterior) and vertebrobasilar (posterior) circulations. It provides collateral flow, and its branch points are where berry aneurysms form.
tags: [cerebral-vessels, aneurysm, stroke]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  origin: Formed by the terminal **internal carotid arteries** ([[carotid-arteries]]) and the **basilar artery** ([[vertebrobasilar-system]]).
  course: >-
    Lies in the **interpeduncular cistern** (subarachnoid space) around the **optic chiasm**, infundibulum and
    mammillary bodies. Components: the two **anterior cerebral arteries** (A1 segments) joined by the **anterior
    communicating artery**; the two **internal carotid arteries**; the two **posterior communicating arteries**
    connecting each ICA to the **posterior cerebral artery** (P1 segments) from the basilar bifurcation.
  branches:
    - "**Anterior cerebral arteries** and **middle cerebral arteries** (see [[cerebral-arteries]])"
    - "**Anterior communicating artery (AComm)**"
    - "**Posterior communicating arteries (PComm)** — beside [[oculomotor-nerve]]"
    - "**Posterior cerebral arteries**"
    - "Small **central (perforating) branches** to the hypothalamus, thalamus, internal capsule and basal ganglia; **anterior choroidal artery** (from the ICA)"
  supplies: The whole brain via its branches; the perforators supply deep structures ([[hypothalamus]], [[thalamus]], [[internal-capsule]]).
  anastomoses: "Allows flow from one side or from the posterior circulation to compensate for a slowly narrowing carotid; a **complete, symmetric circle is present in only a minority** of people, so collateral capacity varies. {{verify: proportion with a complete circle varies widely between studies}}"
  relations: The AComm lies above the **optic chiasm**; the PComm runs alongside **CN III**.
clinical:
  - title: Saccular (berry) aneurysms
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Thin-walled outpouchings at arterial **branch points** — most at the **AComm** junction, then the **PComm–ICA** junction and the MCA bifurcation. Risk factors — hypertension, **smoking**, **ADPKD**, Ehlers–Danlos, coarctation of the aorta, family history.
    presentation: >-
      Rupture → **subarachnoid hemorrhage** (thunderclap headache; see [[cranial-meninges]]). Unruptured aneurysms can
      compress nearby structures: **PComm aneurysm → pupil-involving CN III palsy**; **AComm aneurysm → visual field
      defects** (chiasm). Treated by endovascular coiling or surgical clipping.
  - title: Collateral failure and watershed infarction
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Incomplete circle or severe hypotension.
    presentation: Carotid occlusion may be silent with a good circle or cause a large MCA infarct without one; profound hypotension causes **watershed (border-zone) infarcts** between territories.
pance:
  - "Most common berry aneurysm site → **anterior communicating artery**."
  - "PComm aneurysm → **CN III palsy with a dilated pupil**."
  - "Berry aneurysms + kidney cysts → **ADPKD**."
  - "Circle of Willis lies in the **subarachnoid space** → aneurysm rupture = SAH."
imaging:
  ct: '**CT angiography** — aneurysms and vessel occlusion; noncontrast CT for SAH first.'
  mri: MRA (time-of-flight, no contrast needed) for screening, e.g. in ADPKD with a family history of aneurysm.
  keyViews:
    - "Digital subtraction (catheter) angiography — gold standard and route for coiling"
exam:
  testing: 'Pupils and eye movements (CN III compression), visual fields (chiasm); in suspected SAH — level of consciousness and neck stiffness.'
quiz:
  - stem: A ruptured berry aneurysm most commonly arises from which artery?
    choices:
      - Middle cerebral artery trifurcation
      - Anterior communicating artery
      - Basilar tip
      - Posterior inferior cerebellar artery
      - Vertebral artery
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      About a third of saccular aneurysms arise at the **anterior communicating artery**, followed by the posterior
      communicating–ICA junction and the MCA bifurcation.
  - stem: >-
      A 38-year-old with autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease has a mother who died of a brain hemorrhage. What
      screening test is most appropriate?
    choices:
      - Carotid duplex ultrasound
      - MR angiography of the brain
      - Lumbar puncture
      - EEG
      - Transcranial Doppler only
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **ADPKD** carries an increased risk of **intracranial berry aneurysms**; with a family history of aneurysm or SAH,
      screening with **MRA** (or CTA) is recommended.
