aka: [Basilar artery, Posterior circulation, PICA, AICA, Superior cerebellar artery, Subclavian steal syndrome]
summary: The posterior circulation — the vertebral arteries join to form the basilar artery, which supplies the brainstem, cerebellum and (through the PCAs) the occipital lobes. Its strokes cause vertigo, crossed signs and, with basilar occlusion, coma or locked-in syndrome.
tags: [cerebral-vessels, stroke, brainstem]
systems: [neurologic, cardiovascular]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  origin: '**Vertebral arteries** ([[vertebral-artery]]), first branches of the subclavian arteries, ascending through the transverse foramina of C6–C1 and entering the skull through the **foramen magnum**.'
  course: >-
    The two vertebral arteries run up the front of the [[medulla-oblongata]] and **join at the pontomedullary junction
    to form the basilar artery**, which ascends in the groove on the front of the [[pons]] and **divides into the two
    posterior cerebral arteries** at the upper border of the pons/midbrain.
  branches:
    - "**Vertebral**: **anterior spinal artery** (joins its partner — medial medulla, anterior cord), posterior spinal arteries, **PICA** (lateral medulla, inferior cerebellum)"
    - "**Basilar**: **AICA** (lateral lower pons, anterior-inferior cerebellum; often gives the **labyrinthine artery** to the inner ear), **pontine** paramedian and circumferential branches, **superior cerebellar artery** (upper pons, superior cerebellum)"
    - "**Posterior cerebral arteries** (terminal branches; see [[cerebral-arteries]])"
  supplies: '[[medulla-oblongata]], [[pons]], [[midbrain]], [[cerebellum]], inner ear, [[thalamus]], occipital and inferior temporal lobes, upper spinal cord.'
  anastomoses: Joins the anterior circulation through the **posterior communicating arteries** (see [[circle-of-willis]]).
  termination: Bifurcates into the posterior cerebral arteries at the midbrain.
  relations: CN III emerges between the PCA and SCA; CN VI runs up alongside the basilar artery.
clinical:
  - title: Posterior circulation stroke
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Embolism, atherosclerosis of the vertebral/basilar arteries, **vertebral artery dissection** (neck trauma or manipulation).
    presentation: >-
      "**5 Ds**": **dizziness/vertigo, diplopia, dysarthria, dysphagia, dystaxia** (ataxia), plus nausea, nystagmus and
      **crossed signs** (ipsilateral cranial nerve + contralateral limb deficits). **PICA** → lateral medullary
      (Wallenberg); **AICA** → lateral pontine syndrome (ipsilateral facial palsy, **hearing loss**, vertigo, ataxia,
      face sensory loss, contralateral body pain/temp loss); **SCA** → ipsilateral ataxia.
  - title: Basilar artery occlusion
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Thrombosis or embolism — high mortality without reperfusion.
    presentation: Fluctuating or sudden **coma**, quadriparesis, pinpoint pupils, bilateral signs, or **locked-in syndrome**; "**top of the basilar**" syndrome — visual loss, eye movement abnormalities, altered consciousness. Emergency CTA and **thrombectomy**.
  - title: Subclavian steal syndrome
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Stenosis of the proximal subclavian artery** (before the vertebral origin, usually left) → blood flows **retrograde down the ipsilateral vertebral artery** to supply the arm during exercise.'
    presentation: '**Arm claudication** and **vertebrobasilar symptoms (dizziness, syncope) with arm exercise**; **arm BP difference > 15–20 mm Hg** and a weaker radial pulse on the affected side. Duplex shows reversed vertebral flow.'
pance:
  - "Vertigo + diplopia + dysarthria + ataxia + crossed signs → **posterior circulation stroke** → MRI (CT misses it)."
  - "Coma or locked-in state + pinpoint pupils → **basilar occlusion** → CTA + thrombectomy."
  - "Dizziness when using one arm + unequal arm BPs → **subclavian steal**."
  - "Vertigo + sudden unilateral hearing loss → **AICA** (labyrinthine artery) stroke."
  - "Neck pain + posterior circulation stroke in a young patient → **vertebral dissection**."
imaging:
  ct: Noncontrast CT has **poor sensitivity in the posterior fossa**; **CTA** for vertebral/basilar occlusion and dissection.
  mri: '**MRI with DWI** is the test of choice for posterior circulation infarcts; MRA and fat-saturated T1 for dissection.'
  ultrasound: Duplex of the vertebral arteries shows reversed flow in subclavian steal.
exam:
  testing: 'Eye movements and nystagmus, **HINTS** exam, facial sensation and strength, hearing, palate and tongue, limb ataxia and gait, crossed sensory findings; **blood pressure in both arms** and bilateral radial pulses.'
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 66-year-old has dizziness and near-fainting whenever he chops wood with his left arm. BP is 150/85 in the right
      arm and 118/70 in the left. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Carotid sinus hypersensitivity
      - Left subclavian steal syndrome
      - Thoracic outlet syndrome
      - Aortic stenosis
      - Vertebrobasilar migraine
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Proximal left subclavian stenosis** makes the arm "steal" blood by reversing flow down the **left vertebral
      artery** during exercise, causing posterior circulation symptoms and a large inter-arm BP difference.
  - stem: >-
      A 58-year-old has sudden vertigo, vomiting, right-sided hearing loss, right facial weakness and right limb ataxia.
      Which artery is most likely occluded?
    choices:
      - Right PICA
      - Right AICA
      - Right MCA
      - Left SCA
      - Right anterior spinal artery
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **AICA** supplies the lateral lower pons (facial nucleus, vestibular/cochlear nuclei) and usually the **labyrinthine
      artery** — vertigo with **hearing loss** and facial palsy suggests the **lateral pontine (AICA) syndrome**.
