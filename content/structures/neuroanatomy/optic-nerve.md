name: Optic nerve and visual pathway (CN II)
type: nerve
region: neuroanatomy
subregion: Cranial nerves
taName: Nervus opticus
aka: [CN II, Cranial nerve II, Optic chiasm, Optic tract, Optic radiation, Visual pathway, Meyer loop]
summary: Carries vision from the retina to the occipital cortex through the chiasm, tracts and radiations. The pattern of a visual field defect localizes the lesion, and CN II is the afferent limb of the pupillary light reflex.
tags: [cranial-nerves, special-senses, visual-fields]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  origin: Axons of **retinal ganglion cells**, converging at the optic disc of the [[eyeball]].
  course: >-
    **Optic nerve** (orbital part, surrounded by meninges and CSF) → **optic canal** (with the ophthalmic artery) →
    **optic chiasm** above the pituitary, where fibers from the **nasal half of each retina cross** (carrying the
    temporal visual fields) → **optic tract** (ipsilateral temporal + contralateral nasal retina = the contralateral
    visual field) → **lateral geniculate nucleus** of the [[thalamus]] → **optic radiations**: the inferior fibers loop
    through the **temporal lobe (Meyer loop)** and carry the **contralateral superior quadrant**; the superior fibers
    pass through the **parietal lobe** and carry the **inferior quadrant** → **primary visual cortex (V1)** on the
    banks of the calcarine sulcus of the occipital lobe (see [[cerebral-cortex]]).
  branches:
    - "Fibers from the optic tract to the **pretectal nuclei** (pupillary light reflex) and superior colliculus"
    - "Retinohypothalamic fibers to the suprachiasmatic nucleus (circadian rhythm; see [[hypothalamus]])"
  sensory: Vision (special somatic afferent); afferent limb of the pupillary light and accommodation reflexes.
  relations: >-
    Inside the [[orbit]] it lies within the cone of the extraocular muscles; the **central retinal artery** enters it
    about 1 cm behind the eyeball. The chiasm sits above the [[pituitary-gland]] and diaphragma sellae, with the internal
    carotid arteries lateral and the anterior cerebral/anterior communicating arteries above (see [[circle-of-willis]]).
  notes: Developmentally the optic nerve is a **tract of the CNS** — myelinated by oligodendrocytes and wrapped in meninges — which is why it is affected by MS and why raised intracranial pressure causes papilledema.
clinical:
  - title: Visual field defects by lesion site
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Crossing of the nasal fibers at the chiasm and the split of the radiations between temporal and parietal lobes.
    presentation: >-
      **Optic nerve** → monocular vision loss (ipsilateral). **Chiasm** (pituitary adenoma, craniopharyngioma) →
      **bitemporal hemianopia**. **Optic tract** → contralateral **homonymous hemianopia** (often incongruous). **Meyer
      loop (temporal lobe)** → contralateral **superior quadrantanopia** ("pie in the sky"). **Parietal radiations** →
      contralateral **inferior quadrantanopia** ("pie on the floor"). **Occipital cortex (PCA stroke)** → contralateral
      **homonymous hemianopia with macular sparing** (dual MCA/PCA supply of the occipital pole).
  - title: Optic neuritis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Inflammatory demyelination — often the first presentation of **multiple sclerosis**; also neuromyelitis optica (aquaporin-4) and MOG antibody disease.
    presentation: 'Subacute **painful** monocular vision loss, **pain with eye movement**, reduced **color vision** (red desaturation), a **relative afferent pupillary defect**, central scotoma; the disc is often normal (retrobulbar). MRI brain; **IV methylprednisolone** speeds recovery.'
  - title: Papilledema
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Raised intracranial pressure** transmitted along the optic nerve sheath (tumor, hemorrhage, venous sinus thrombosis, **idiopathic intracranial hypertension**).'
    presentation: '**Bilateral optic disc swelling** with blurred margins, loss of venous pulsations, flame hemorrhages; transient visual obscurations, headache worse lying down, enlarged blind spot. Image before lumbar puncture.'
  - title: Central retinal artery occlusion
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Embolus (carotid, cardiac) or giant cell arteritis blocking the central retinal artery.
    presentation: '**Sudden painless monocular vision loss**, RAPD, **cherry-red spot** with a pale, swollen retina. A stroke equivalent — urgent stroke workup; check ESR/CRP in older patients.'
pance:
  - "Bitemporal hemianopia → **chiasm** (pituitary adenoma; craniopharyngioma in children)."
  - "\"Pie in the sky\" → **temporal lobe** (Meyer loop); \"pie on the floor\" → **parietal**."
  - "Homonymous hemianopia with **macular sparing** → **PCA stroke** (occipital)."
  - "Painful monocular vision loss + pain on eye movement + RAPD → **optic neuritis** (MS)."
  - "Pupillary light reflex: **afferent CN II**, efferent **CN III**."
  - "Bilateral disc swelling = **papilledema** = raised ICP until proven otherwise."
imaging:
  mri: '**MRI brain and orbits with contrast** — optic neuritis (enhancing nerve, white matter lesions), pituitary and chiasmal lesions, tumors along the pathway.'
  ct: Emergency CT for trauma, hemorrhage and mass effect; CT venography when venous sinus thrombosis is suspected.
  keyViews:
    - "Formal perimetry (Humphrey visual fields) documents and follows defects"
    - "Optical coherence tomography (OCT) of the retinal nerve fiber layer"
exam:
  landmarks: The optic disc on fundoscopy lies nasal to the macula; the physiologic blind spot lies about 15° temporal to fixation.
  testing: >-
    **Visual acuity** (Snellen chart, each eye, with glasses or a pinhole); **color vision** (Ishihara plates); **visual
    fields by confrontation** (each eye, four quadrants); **pupils** — direct and consensual light reflex and
    accommodation; **fundoscopy** (disc margins, cup-to-disc ratio, venous pulsations, retina).
  specialTests:
    - name: Swinging flashlight test
      technique: Swing a bright light back and forth between the eyes, about 2–3 seconds on each, in a dim room.
      positive: '**Relative afferent pupillary defect (Marcus Gunn pupil)** — both pupils dilate when the light moves to the affected eye.'
      significance: Asymmetric **optic nerve** (or extensive retinal) disease — optic neuritis, ischemic optic neuropathy, CRAO, severe glaucoma. Not caused by cataract or refractive error.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 45-year-old woman has headaches and keeps bumping into door frames on both sides. Confrontation testing shows loss
      of the temporal half of the visual field in each eye. Where is the lesion?
    choices:
      - Right optic nerve
      - Optic chiasm
      - Left optic tract
      - Right Meyer loop
      - Left occipital cortex
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Bitemporal hemianopia** results from compression of the **crossing nasal fibers at the chiasm**, classically by a
      **pituitary macroadenoma**. Next step: MRI of the sella and pituitary hormone testing.
  - stem: >-
      A 28-year-old woman has 3 days of blurred vision in the right eye with pain on eye movement. When a light is swung
      from the left eye to the right, both pupils dilate. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Acute angle-closure glaucoma
      - Optic neuritis
      - Central retinal vein occlusion
      - Retinal detachment
      - Cataract
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Painful vision loss with **pain on eye movement** and a **relative afferent pupillary defect** in a young woman is
      **optic neuritis**, often the first sign of **multiple sclerosis** — obtain MRI.
  - stem: >-
      After a right temporal lobectomy, a patient has a visual field defect. Which defect is expected?
    choices:
      - Right superior quadrantanopia
      - Left superior quadrantanopia
      - Left inferior quadrantanopia
      - Bitemporal hemianopia
      - Right monocular blindness
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **Meyer loop** in the temporal lobe carries fibers for the **contralateral superior quadrant**, so a right
      temporal lesion causes a **left superior quadrantanopia** ("pie in the sky").
