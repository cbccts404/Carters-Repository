name: Hypothalamus
type: organ
region: neuroanatomy
subregion: Cerebrum
taName: Hypothalamus
aka: [Supraoptic nucleus, Paraventricular nucleus, Suprachiasmatic nucleus, Mammillary bodies, Diabetes insipidus]
summary: Small part of the diencephalon that controls the pituitary, autonomic nervous system, temperature, hunger, thirst and circadian rhythm. Its disorders include central diabetes insipidus and craniopharyngioma.
tags: [cerebrum, endocrine, autonomic]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Below the [[thalamus]], forming the floor and lower lateral walls of the **third ventricle**; the **optic chiasm** lies in front and the **mammillary bodies** behind; the **infundibulum (stalk)** connects it to the [[pituitary-gland]].
  parts:
    - "**Supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei** — make **ADH (vasopressin)** and **oxytocin**, transported down axons to the **posterior pituitary**"
    - "**Arcuate nucleus** (and others) — releasing hormones (GnRH, TRH, CRH, GHRH, somatostatin) and **dopamine** (inhibits prolactin) into the **hypophyseal portal system**"
    - "**Suprachiasmatic nucleus** — circadian rhythm (retinal input; melatonin via the pineal)"
    - "**Lateral area** — hunger (lesion → anorexia); **ventromedial nucleus** — satiety (lesion → hyperphagia, obesity)"
    - "**Anterior hypothalamus** — heat loss (cooling), parasympathetic; **posterior hypothalamus** — heat conservation, sympathetic"
    - "**Preoptic area** — thermoregulation (fever set point via prostaglandin E2), sexual behavior"
    - "**Mammillary bodies** — memory (Papez circuit; see [[limbic-system]])"
    - "Thirst centers and osmoreceptors (organum vasculosum of the lamina terminalis)"
  bloodSupply: Small perforating branches of the [[circle-of-willis]] (anterior and posterior communicating, anterior and posterior cerebral arteries).
  venousDrainage: Into the basal and internal cerebral veins; the **hypophyseal portal veins** carry releasing hormones to the anterior pituitary.
  notes: Receives limbic, visceral and retinal input; outputs to the pituitary, brainstem autonomic nuclei and spinal cord.
  relations: The optic chiasm lies anteroinferiorly — hypothalamic/suprasellar tumors cause visual field defects.
clinical:
  - title: Central diabetes insipidus
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Deficient ADH — pituitary/hypothalamic surgery, **head trauma**, tumors (craniopharyngioma, germinoma), infiltrative disease, idiopathic.
    presentation: >-
      **Polyuria of dilute urine and polydipsia**, high-normal or high serum sodium, **low urine osmolality**. Water
      deprivation test: urine stays dilute; **urine osmolality rises after desmopressin** (central) — no rise in
      **nephrogenic** DI (lithium, hypercalcemia). Treat central DI with **desmopressin**.
  - title: SIADH
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Excess ADH — CNS disease (meningitis, hemorrhage, trauma), small cell lung cancer, drugs (SSRIs, carbamazepine), pulmonary disease, postoperative.
    presentation: '**Euvolemic hypotonic hyponatremia** with inappropriately concentrated urine (urine osmolality > 100) and urine Na > 30. Fluid restriction; hypertonic saline if severe symptoms (correct slowly).'
  - title: Craniopharyngioma
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Benign tumor from **Rathke pouch** remnants in the suprasellar region; peak in **children** (also adults 50s–60s).
    presentation: 'Headache, **bitemporal hemianopia**, **growth failure**/hypopituitarism, **diabetes insipidus**; imaging shows a **calcified, cystic suprasellar mass**.'
pance:
  - "Polyuria + dilute urine + rise in urine osm after desmopressin → **central DI**."
  - "No response to desmopressin → **nephrogenic DI** (lithium, hypercalcemia)."
  - "Euvolemic hyponatremia + concentrated urine → **SIADH**."
  - "Child + bitemporal hemianopia + calcified suprasellar mass → **craniopharyngioma**."
  - "Supraoptic/paraventricular nuclei → ADH and oxytocin."
imaging:
  mri: '**MRI of the sella and hypothalamus** — loss of the posterior pituitary "bright spot" on T1 in central DI; thickened stalk (germinoma, histiocytosis, hypophysitis); suprasellar masses.'
  ct: CT shows calcification in craniopharyngioma.
exam:
  testing: Visual fields (chiasm), growth and pubertal assessment in children, volume status and weight, signs of hypopituitarism; strict intake/output and serum/urine osmolality in suspected DI.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 25-year-old develops polyuria of 6 L/day after a motorcycle crash with head injury. Serum sodium is 148 mEq/L and
      urine osmolality is 120 mOsm/kg, rising to 450 after desmopressin. What is the diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Primary polydipsia
      - Central diabetes insipidus
      - Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus
      - SIADH
      - Osmotic diuresis from hyperglycemia
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Dilute urine with high-normal sodium that **concentrates after desmopressin** indicates **central DI** — here from
      head trauma damaging the hypothalamus or stalk.
  - stem: >-
      An 8-year-old boy has headaches, short stature and bitemporal visual field loss. CT shows a calcified cystic mass
      above the sella. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Pituitary prolactinoma
      - Craniopharyngioma
      - Medulloblastoma
      - Pineal germinoma
      - Meningioma
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **calcified cystic suprasellar mass** in a child with growth failure and **bitemporal hemianopia** is a
      **craniopharyngioma** (Rathke pouch remnant).
