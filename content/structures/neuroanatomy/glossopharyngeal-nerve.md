---
name: Glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX)
type: nerve
region: neuroanatomy
subregion: Cranial nerves
taName: Nervus glossopharyngeus
aka: [CN IX, Cranial nerve IX, Carotid sinus nerve, Glossopharyngeal neuralgia]
summary: Mainly sensory — posterior tongue, oropharynx and middle ear, plus the carotid sinus and body. It is the afferent limb of the gag reflex and supplies the parotid gland (parasympathetic) and stylopharyngeus.
tags: [cranial-nerves, pharynx]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  origin: >-
    Emerges from the [[medulla-oblongata]] in the **postolivary sulcus**, above the vagus. Nuclei: **nucleus ambiguus**
    (motor), **inferior salivatory nucleus** (parasympathetic), **nucleus of the solitary tract** (taste and visceral
    sensation), spinal trigeminal nucleus (general sensation).
  course: >-
    Leaves the skull through the **jugular foramen** (with CN X and XI and the internal jugular vein), with superior and
    inferior ganglia; descends between the internal carotid artery and internal jugular vein, curves forward around
    **stylopharyngeus**, and passes between the internal and external carotid arteries (see [[carotid-arteries]]) to reach
    the tonsillar bed and the posterior tongue.
  branches:
    - "**Tympanic nerve** → tympanic plexus (middle ear sensation) → **lesser petrosal nerve** → **otic ganglion** → auriculotemporal nerve (V3) → **parotid gland** secretion"
    - "**Carotid sinus nerve** → carotid sinus (**baroreceptor**) and carotid body (**chemoreceptor**)"
    - "Nerve to stylopharyngeus"
    - "Pharyngeal branches (to the pharyngeal plexus), tonsillar and lingual branches"
  motor: '**Stylopharyngeus** only (elevates the pharynx during swallowing).'
  sensory: >-
    **General sensation and taste from the posterior third of the [[tongue]]**, the **oropharynx** and
    [[palatine-tonsils]] (afferent limb of the **gag reflex**), the middle ear and pharyngotympanic tube; carotid sinus
    and body.
  relations: Leaves the jugular foramen with CN X and XI, so lesions there (tumors, glomus jugulare) affect all three.
clinical:
  - title: Jugular foramen syndrome (Vernet syndrome)
    highYield: false
    mechanism: A mass at the jugular foramen (glomus jugulare tumor, schwannoma, metastasis, basilar skull fracture).
    presentation: Palsies of **CN IX, X and XI** — loss of the gag reflex and taste on the posterior tongue, dysphagia, hoarseness, uvula deviated away from the lesion, weak SCM and trapezius.
  - title: Glossopharyngeal neuralgia
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Usually vascular compression of the nerve root (analogous to trigeminal neuralgia).
    presentation: Brief, severe, stabbing pain in the **throat, tonsil, base of tongue and ear**, triggered by swallowing, talking or yawning; occasionally with bradycardia or syncope (carotid sinus connection). Carbamazepine first-line.
  - title: Referred ear pain
    highYield: true
    mechanism: CN IX supplies both the oropharynx and the middle ear.
    presentation: Otalgia after tonsillectomy or with pharyngitis, tonsillar abscess or **oropharyngeal cancer** — always examine the throat in a patient with ear pain and a normal ear exam.
pance:
  - "Gag reflex: **afferent CN IX**, **efferent CN X**."
  - "Taste and sensation from the **posterior 1/3 of the tongue** → CN IX."
  - "Carotid sinus (baroreceptor) and body (chemoreceptor) → **CN IX**."
  - "Ear pain with a normal ear exam → look at the **oropharynx** (referred via CN IX/X)."
imaging:
  mri: MRI of the skull base and jugular foramen for lower cranial nerve palsies.
  ct: CT of the skull base shows erosion of the jugular foramen by glomus tumors.
exam:
  testing: >-
    **Gag reflex** (touch each side of the posterior pharynx — compare sides; many normal people have a weak gag);
    palatal elevation on saying "ah" (mainly CN X); taste on the posterior tongue is rarely tested. Examine the ear and
    the throat together.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      Touching the left posterior pharyngeal wall produces no gag, but touching the right side produces a gag with
      bilateral palatal elevation. Which nerve is most likely damaged?
    choices:
      - Left vagus nerve
      - Left glossopharyngeal nerve
      - Right hypoglossal nerve
      - Left facial nerve
      - Right glossopharyngeal nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The palate elevates normally (efferent CN X intact on both sides) when the right side is stimulated, but the left
      stimulus is not sensed — the **afferent limb (left CN IX)** is damaged.
  - stem: Parasympathetic fibers to the parotid gland travel initially in which cranial nerve?
    choices:
      - Facial nerve
      - Glossopharyngeal nerve
      - Vagus nerve
      - Trigeminal nerve
      - Hypoglossal nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Preganglionic fibers from the inferior salivatory nucleus travel in **CN IX** (tympanic → lesser petrosal nerves)
      to the **otic ganglion**; postganglionic fibers reach the parotid via the auriculotemporal nerve (V3).
