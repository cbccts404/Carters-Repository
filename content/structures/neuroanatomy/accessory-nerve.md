name: Accessory nerve (CN XI)
type: nerve
region: neuroanatomy
subregion: Cranial nerves
taName: Nervus accessorius
aka: [CN XI, Cranial nerve XI, Spinal accessory nerve]
summary: Motor nerve to sternocleidomastoid and trapezius, arising from the upper cervical cord. It crosses the posterior triangle superficially, where lymph node biopsy can injure it and cause shoulder droop.
tags: [cranial-nerves, neck, shoulder]
systems: [neurologic]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  origin: >-
    **Spinal accessory nucleus** in the ventral horn of the upper cervical cord (about **C1–C5**). The rootlets ascend
    beside the [[spinal-cord]] through the **foramen magnum** into the skull. (A small "cranial root" from the nucleus
    ambiguus joins the vagus and is usually considered part of CN X.)
  course: >-
    Exits through the **jugular foramen** with CN IX and X → descends, often crossing in front of or behind the internal
    jugular vein → pierces and supplies [[sternocleidomastoid]] → crosses the **posterior triangle** superficially on
    levator scapulae, just deep to the investing fascia (emerging about a third of the way down the posterior border of
    SCM, above the nerve point) → supplies [[trapezius]] from its deep surface (see [[triangles-of-the-neck]]).
  motor: '**Sternocleidomastoid** (turns the face to the opposite side) and **trapezius** (shrugs, elevates and retracts the scapula, rotates it upward for abduction above 90°). C2–C4 fibers from the cervical plexus add proprioception.'
  relations: In the posterior triangle it lies superficially among the occipital and posterior cervical (level V) lymph nodes.
clinical:
  - title: Spinal accessory nerve injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: '**Iatrogenic** — posterior triangle **lymph node biopsy**, neck dissection; also penetrating trauma.'
    presentation: >-
      Injury in the posterior triangle spares SCM (supplied higher up) but paralyzes **trapezius**: **shoulder droop**,
      weak shrug, difficulty abducting above 90°, **lateral scapular winging** (the inferior angle moves laterally —
      contrast with the medial winging of a long thoracic nerve palsy), shoulder pain.
  - title: Proximal (jugular foramen) lesions
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Tumors at the jugular foramen or skull base (with CN IX and X palsies).
    presentation: Weak SCM (weak head turning to the **opposite** side) and trapezius on the same side as the lesion.
pance:
  - "Shoulder droop and weak shrug after a posterior triangle node biopsy → **CN XI injury**."
  - "Trapezius palsy → **lateral** scapular winging; long thoracic nerve (serratus anterior) → **medial** winging."
  - "Weak head turning to the **left** → **right** SCM / right CN XI."
imaging:
  mri: MRI of the skull base and neck for unexplained XI palsy; EMG/nerve conduction confirms denervation of trapezius.
exam:
  testing: >-
    **SCM**: turn the head against resistance (turning to the left tests the right SCM) and look/palpate for
    contraction. **Trapezius**: shrug the shoulders against resistance; look for asymmetry, wasting and scapular
    position; abduct the arms above 90°.
