---
name: Vagus nerve (CN X)
type: nerve
region: neuroanatomy
subregion: Cranial nerves
taName: Nervus vagus
aka: [CN X, Cranial nerve X, Vagal trunks, Pharyngeal plexus]
summary: Longest cranial nerve — motor to the palate, pharynx and larynx, and parasympathetic supply to the thoracic and abdominal viscera down to the distal transverse colon. A lesion causes hoarseness, dysphagia and a uvula deviated away from the lesion.
tags: [cranial-nerves, autonomic, larynx]
systems: [neurologic, eent]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  origin: >-
    Emerges from the postolivary sulcus of the [[medulla-oblongata]] below CN IX. Nuclei: **nucleus ambiguus** (motor to
    pharynx, larynx, palate), **dorsal motor nucleus of the vagus** (parasympathetic), **nucleus of the solitary tract**
    (visceral sensation, taste from the epiglottis), spinal trigeminal nucleus (ear).
  course: >-
    Leaves through the **jugular foramen** (superior and inferior ganglia) → descends in the **carotid sheath** between and
    behind the internal/common carotid artery and [[internal-jugular-vein]] → **right vagus** crosses in front of the
    right subclavian artery (giving the right recurrent laryngeal nerve) → **left vagus** crosses the **aortic arch**
    (giving the left recurrent laryngeal nerve) → both pass **behind the lung roots** → form the **esophageal plexus** →
    **anterior (mainly left) and posterior (mainly right) vagal trunks** pass through the **esophageal hiatus (T10)** →
    abdominal viscera.
  branches:
    - "Auricular branch (Arnold nerve) — external ear canal (ear-canal cough reflex)"
    - "**Pharyngeal branches** → pharyngeal plexus (pharynx and soft palate)"
    - "**Superior laryngeal nerve** — internal (sensation above the vocal folds) and external (cricothyroid)"
    - "**[[recurrent-laryngeal-nerve]]** — all other intrinsic laryngeal muscles"
    - "Cardiac branches (slow the heart — mainly SA and AV nodes), pulmonary branches (bronchoconstriction, secretion)"
    - "Esophageal, gastric, hepatic, celiac and renal branches — parasympathetic to the **foregut and midgut** (to about the distal third of the transverse colon)"
  motor: >-
    Muscles of the **soft palate** (except tensor veli palatini — V3), **pharynx** (except stylopharyngeus — IX),
    **larynx** ([[larynx]]), palatoglossus, and the upper esophagus. **Parasympathetic** to the heart, lungs and gut.
  sensory: Laryngopharynx and larynx (cough reflex), part of the external ear, visceral afferents from thoracic and abdominal organs (most vagal fibers are afferent), aortic arch baroreceptors and chemoreceptors.
  relations: In the neck in the carotid sheath; in the thorax, related to the [[thoracic-aorta]], [[esophagus]] and lung roots; in the abdomen, the trunks lie on the [[stomach]].
clinical:
  - title: Vagus nerve lesion
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Jugular foramen tumors, lateral medullary (Wallenberg) stroke, skull-base surgery, carotid dissection; distal lesions affect only the recurrent laryngeal branch.
    presentation: '**Uvula deviates away from the lesion** (the intact side pulls it), the ipsilateral palate fails to elevate, **hoarseness** (cord paralysis), **dysphagia**, nasal regurgitation, reduced cough and gag (efferent).'
  - title: Vasovagal (reflex) syncope and vagal maneuvers
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Excess vagal outflow (with sympathetic withdrawal) → bradycardia and vasodilation; vagal maneuvers slow AV nodal conduction.
    presentation: '**Vasovagal syncope** — prodrome (warmth, nausea, pallor) after pain, fear or prolonged standing, rapid recovery. **Valsalva** or **carotid sinus massage** can terminate AV-nodal re-entrant SVT.'
  - title: Vagal effects in the gut
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Vagal stimulation increases gastric acid and motility; vagotomy (historical ulcer surgery) and vagal injury during fundoplication or esophagectomy impair gastric emptying.
    presentation: Gastroparesis, diarrhea (dumping) after vagal injury; vagus nerve stimulation is used for refractory epilepsy and depression.
pance:
  - "Uvula deviates **away** from a CN X lesion (toward the normal side)."
  - "Hoarseness + dysphagia + absent gag + ipsilateral Horner + crossed sensory loss → **Wallenberg** (lateral medulla)."
  - "Gag reflex efferent = **CN X**."
  - "Vagal maneuvers (**Valsalva**, carotid massage) → first step for stable SVT."
  - "Vagal trunks pass through the esophageal hiatus at **T10**."
imaging:
  ct: Contrast CT of the neck and chest along the course of the vagus and recurrent laryngeal nerve (skull base to aortopulmonary window) for unexplained vocal cord paralysis.
  mri: MRI brainstem and skull base for medullary strokes and jugular foramen lesions.
exam:
  testing: >-
    Ask the patient to say "**ah**": watch the soft palate rise symmetrically and the uvula stay midline. Gag reflex
    (efferent limb), voice quality (hoarseness, nasal speech), cough strength, swallowing (water swallow test for
    aspiration risk).
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      When a patient says "ah," the uvula deviates to the right and the left soft palate does not elevate. Which nerve is
      affected?
    choices:
      - Right vagus nerve
      - Left vagus nerve
      - Left glossopharyngeal nerve
      - Right hypoglossal nerve
      - Left trigeminal nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      With a **left vagus** lesion, the intact **right** palatal muscles pull the uvula toward the right — the uvula
      deviates **away** from the side of the lesion.
  - stem: >-
      A hemodynamically stable 25-year-old has a narrow-complex regular tachycardia at 190/min. What is the first step?
    choices:
      - Synchronized cardioversion
      - Vagal maneuvers such as a modified Valsalva
      - IV amiodarone
      - IV diltiazem before anything else
      - Emergency catheter ablation
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      For **stable SVT**, start with **vagal maneuvers** (increase vagal tone at the AV node); if unsuccessful, give
      **adenosine**. Unstable patients need synchronized cardioversion.
  - stem: Which nerve carries the efferent limb of the gag reflex?
    choices:
      - Glossopharyngeal nerve
      - Vagus nerve
      - Hypoglossal nerve
      - Facial nerve
      - Trigeminal nerve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The gag reflex has its **afferent** limb in **CN IX** and its **efferent** limb in **CN X** (pharyngeal and
      palatal muscles).
  - stem: How far along the gut does vagal parasympathetic supply extend?
    choices:
      - Only to the stomach
      - To the rectum
      - To the ileocecal valve only
      - Only to the esophagus
      - Foregut and midgut, to about the distal third of the transverse colon
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      The vagus supplies the **foregut and midgut**, to about the distal third of the transverse colon. Beyond that, the
      pelvic splanchnic nerves (S2–S4) take over.
  - stem: Which pharyngeal muscle is NOT supplied by the vagus nerve?
    choices:
      - Stylopharyngeus
      - Superior constrictor
      - Palatopharyngeus
      - Inferior constrictor
      - Levator veli palatini
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      **Stylopharyngeus** is supplied by **CN IX**. The vagus supplies the other pharyngeal muscles and the soft palate
      except tensor veli palatini (V3).
